GFRC Facade Panels

TECHNOFORMA Group produces facade panels from glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC), flat, textured and 3D, and installs them on ventilated rainscreen facades. These panels make it possible to clad both plain surfaces and complex sculptural facades with a single material.

What GFRC panels are

GFRC panels are thin-walled cladding elements made of glass fibre reinforced concrete that are fixed to a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure. Glass fibre reinforces the panel throughout its volume, so despite its small thickness it is strong and relatively light. The material is non-combustible (Russian fire class NG), which matters when choosing non-combustible panels for the facade of a multi-storey or public building.

Types of panels

Flat

For large-format cladding with a smooth, ground or textured surface.

Textured and 3D

3D facade panels with a relief pattern, ornament or parametric geometry; each mould is made from a digital model.

Stone-textured panels

Reproduce the pattern of natural stone, including rustication.

Corner and reveal panels

For neat corners, reveals and parapets.

Comparison with other panels

Composite, aluminium, metal or linear panel cladding gives a high-tech but flat look. Fibre cement panels are cheaper but limited in form. GFRC facade panels achieve volume, relief and texture close to stone and architectural concrete. They can be combined with porcelain stoneware, as on the Dominanta residential complex, or with FRONTEK, as on RUBLEVO BUSINESS PARK.

Installation

Panels are installed on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure with insulation and a wind barrier; see the ventilated facade installation page. Insulating the facade of a panel building during refurbishment is handled the same way: insulation beneath the ventilated facade cladding.

Texture, colour and quality

The surface of GFRC products can be smooth, ground, acid-etched or textured to imitate natural stone; colour is achieved with integral pigmentation or painting. Models and moulds are made in the company's own mould shops, and quality is controlled at every stage, from mixing to packing. Products from one batch are checked against the drawing so that there is no visible difference in tone or geometry on the facade. A sample can be made before series production by agreement.

  5. Delivery. Shipment from the production base in the Moscow Region, via the company's warehouses if needed.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division of more than 100 specialists installs the substructure, insulation and cladding; installation supervision is also available.

  • texture and colour;
  • volume and delivery schedule;
  • scope of works: design, delivery, installation.
  • panel format, number of unique 3D moulds, substructure type.

Dimensions, thickness and weight of GFRC facade panels

GFRC facade panels are made to suit a specific layout, so there is no standard range of sizes. The limits are set by transport, the lifting capacity of the plant on site and the spacing of the load-bearing members of the subframe. Flat panels are generally produced with a side of up to 3–4 m, while three-dimensional elements are split along the lines of the relief so that the joint reads as part of the pattern.

A panel consists of a thin skin and reinforcement on the rear face — stiffening ribs or a steel frame. This arrangement determines the weight per square metre and therefore the load on the wall. Indicative parameters are given in the table; exact values are established by calculation, and more detail can be found in the article on the properties of glass fibre reinforced concrete.

How are GFRC panels fixed to the ventilated facade subframe?

A panel is hung on the subframe by means of cast-in fixings installed at the factory. There is no need to drill or cut the skin on site for the sake of fixings: all bearing points are set out in the drawing and coincide with the rails.

One of three arrangements is normally used:

  • concealed fixing on cast-in inserts — anchor sockets or plates in the panel ribs are connected to the brackets and rails, and no fixings are visible from the face;
  • panel on a steel frame — the skin is tied to the frame with flexible anchors, and the frame itself bears on the subframe; the arrangement suits large formats and deep relief;
  • hanging on clips or hooks — for small standard slabs where speed of installation matters.

In each arrangement some supports are load-bearing and others are sliding, so that the panel can change size freely as it heats and cools. The build-up of the wall is described in the article on the ventilated facade with GFRC cladding. In seismic areas the fixings are additionally checked for horizontal actions.

What to prepare for a panel estimate

For a preliminary estimate, dimensioned elevations and an idea of the desired finish are sufficient. The more complete the input data, the more accurate the layout and the fewer the changes at working-drawing stage.

  • architectural elevations, sections and plans with levels and, where available, a 3D model;
  • information on the load-bearing substrate: wall material, floor-to-floor spacing, presence of a cast-in-situ frame;
  • the required insulation thickness or a thermal calculation;
  • the region of construction and the height of the building, which govern wind loads;
  • preferences for colour, texture and joint pattern, and reference samples;
  • the construction programme and the conditions for delivering materials to site.

Where no documentation is available, the layout, details and schedule are produced by the company's design department.

What mistakes are made when choosing glass fibre reinforced concrete panels?

Most problems are built in before production — at the stage of setting out the panels and agreeing the details. Correcting them on finished products is expensive, so the list is worth checking in advance.

  • Too many unique formats. Each new mould adds to the budget and the lead time; it pays to reduce the layout to repeating types.
  • Joints unrelated to the architecture. The joint grid should coincide with window axes, string courses and corners, otherwise the facade looks arbitrary.
  • Weight not accounted for. The load from the cladding and subframe is checked against the bearing capacity of the wall and the results of anchor tests.
  • Rigid fixing at every point. Without sliding supports, thermal movement leads to cracking.
  • Approving colour from a photograph. Tone and texture are approved only from a physical sample in daylight.

Which projects use GFRC panels

GFRC panels are used where a facade needs depth and texture with a limited load on the structure. The set of solutions depends on the type of building.

  • Residential complexes. Standard flat panels on typical floors and relief accents at entrance areas and the crowning part of the building. At the Dubai Houses residential complex in Grozny, GFRC cladding covers 70,000 m².
  • Business centres. Large formats, a strict joint grid and combination with glazing and other types of cladding.
  • Hotels and public buildings. Ornamental and 3D panels that create a recognisable image.
  • Refurbishment. The low weight makes it possible to renew a facade without strengthening the existing walls, provided this is confirmed by a survey.

The panel surfaces are usually complemented with architectural ornament in the same material — cornices, string courses and bands, opening surrounds — so that colour and texture match across the whole facade.

How to look after a facade of GFRC panels

GFRC cladding does not require regular maintenance: periodic inspection and washing with water at moderate pressure are sufficient. Every few years the condition of joints, sills and abutments is checked, and the protective water-repellent treatment is renewed as necessary. Detailed recommendations are collected in the article on caring for GFRC products.

FAQ

How much do GFRC facade panels cost per m²?

The price depends on format, relief, texture, volume and scope of works; the estimate is based on drawings.

Are GFRC panels non-combustible?

Yes, GFRC is classified as non-combustible (Russian fire class NG).

Can you make 3D panels from our own model?

Yes, moulds are made in our own mould shops from the architect's digital model.

What are the panels fixed to?

To a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure.

What is the maximum size of a GFRC panel?

There is no fixed standard. Flat panels are usually made with a side of up to 3–4 m; larger elements are reinforced with a steel frame or divided into parts. The limiting format is determined by calculation, transport conditions and the lifting capacity of the plant on site.

Can GFRC panels be installed in winter?

Yes. Hanging panels on a subframe is a dry process that does not depend on temperature in the way that rendering or adhesive work does. The restrictions relate to strong wind, icing of the scaffolding and the requirements of sealant manufacturers where joints are filled.

How long do GFRC facade panels last?

With a correctly selected mix, alkali-resistant fibre and properly designed fixings, the service life of the cladding is measured in decades. The actual life depends on the climate, the quality of installation and timely inspection of the facade.
