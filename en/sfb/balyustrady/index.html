GFRC Balustrades and Balusters

A balustrade is a railing made of balusters, a handrail and pedestals that adorns balconies, terraces, roofs, stairs and parapets. TECHNOFORMA Group manufactures balusters from glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) and complete balustrade sets for facades and landscaping.

GFRC balusters

Concrete balusters are traditionally heavy; GFRC products are thin-walled and noticeably lighter, yet just as durable outdoors: the material is frost-resistant and non-combustible (Russian fire class NG). This matters on balconies and roofs, where every extra load is accounted for in structural calculations.

Balustrade components

  • balusters: vase-shaped, double-sided, square, ornamented;
  • handrail: the top element of the railing;
  • base rail: the plinth;
  • pedestals (newel posts): supports between spans, often topped with balls or urns.

Railing height and baluster spacing are determined by safety regulations and the purpose of the railing; this is taken into account at the design stage.

Applications

Balconies and loggias of residential buildings, terraces, roof parapets, external stairs and ramps, landscape railings, including together with planters and street furniture.

Balusters we do not make

The company does not produce wooden, metal or wrought-iron stair balusters, only GFRC products for outdoor and facade use.

Texture, colour and quality

The surface of GFRC products can be smooth, ground, acid-etched or textured to imitate natural stone; colour is achieved with integral pigmentation or painting. Models and moulds are made in the company's own mould shops, and quality is controlled at every stage, from mixing to packing. Products from one batch are checked against the drawing so that there is no visible difference in tone or geometry on the facade. A sample can be made before series production by agreement.

How ordering works

  1. Drawings and brief. You send architectural drawings, a sketch or photos of the facade; if there is no design, our design department will prepare one.
  2. Estimate. We calculate the product range and quantities and agree the scope of works and delivery sequence.
  3. Design. Design engineers and 3D visualisers issue working documentation, layouts and fixing details.
  4. Production. Models and moulds are made in our own mould shops, with quality control at every stage.
  5. Delivery. Shipment from the production base in the Moscow Region, via the company's warehouses if needed.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division of more than 100 specialists installs the substructure, insulation and cladding; installation supervision is also available.

Standard sizes of balusters and balustrade elements

GFRC balustrades are made to the dimensions of the project, but most solutions fall within established proportions. The table gives the reference values from which an architect usually starts.

ElementTypical dimensionsNote
Balusterheight 400–800 mm, cross-section 100–200 mmproportions are linked to the height of the barrier and the scale of the facade
Handrailwidth 150–300 mm, sections up to 1.5–2.5 m longthe top is made with a fall for water run-off
Plinth (base rail)width close to that of the handrailraises the balusters above the drainage plane
Pedestalcross-section 250–500 mmplaced at corners, at ends and between bays
Bayusually 1.5–3 m between pedestalsdepends on the load and the layout along the facade

The baluster spacing is usually taken so that the clear gap is less than or equal to the width of the baluster itself. Individual shapes can be selected using the article on baluster shapes and sizes.

How a GFRC balustrade is fixed

The barrier is assembled on steel components that transfer the load to the base; the balusters themselves act as infill. The load-bearing role is taken by the pedestals and the handrail.

  1. Anchors and starter bars for the pedestals and balusters are installed in the base, whether a slab, a parapet or a step.
  2. The plinth is installed and checked for level and fall.
  3. The balusters are placed over the bars or fixed through embedded parts, and the cavity around the bar is filled with mortar.
  4. Pedestals with a metal core or frame are fixed to the base; they take the horizontal load from the handrail.
  5. The handrail is installed, connecting the sections to one another and to the pedestals; the joints are sealed.

The base beneath the balustrade must have waterproofing and organised drainage, otherwise water stands at the plinth. The junction with the roof is described in the piece on parapets.

Which safety requirements should the barrier design take into account?

A decorative balustrade on an accessible area is at the same time a guarding and must comply with the regulations on height, clear gaps and handrail load. The requirements differ for residential and public buildings and for facilities used by children, so the parameters are set by the designer.

The height of a classical baluster is often less than the required height of the barrier. In such cases the balustrade is placed on a solid parapet, the plinth is raised or the handrail is supplemented with a metal railing. The gaps between balusters are checked separately: in residential buildings and childcare facilities they are limited. The horizontal load on the handrail is taken by the steel frame, and its calculation is included in the working documentation.

A balustrade on a balcony, roof and terrace: what is the difference

The construction of the barrier is the same, but the constraints depend on the location: on a balcony the load on the slab is decisive, on a roof it is wind and waterproofing, on a staircase it is the slope.

  • Balconies and loggias. The main constraint is the load-bearing capacity of the slab. Thin-walled elements reduce the load; fixing is made into the slab or into the wall at the ends.
  • Roof parapet. The balustrade completes the facade and usually works together with the crowning cornice; wind load and the junction with the roof covering are important.
  • Terraces and staircases. The barrier follows the slope of the flight, so balusters with inclined bearing surfaces or a stepped plinth are needed.
  • Landscaping. Barriers on embankments, retaining walls and parterres are complemented by planters and small architectural forms in the same texture.

On classical facades the balustrade is coordinated in proportion with columns and capitals. GFRC architectural decoration has been carried out, among other projects, at the Dynasty club house in Kislovodsk.

A balustrade in GFRC, concrete or stone: which to choose

The choice of material for a balustrade is determined by the load on the base, the complexity of the shape and the size of the order. GFRC has the advantage where low weight and repeatability of a complex profile matter.

MaterialWeightShape and detailingParticular features
GFRClow, thin-walled elementsany profiles and ornament from a mouldembedded parts and steel frame components are needed
Wet-cast concretehigh, solid elementsstandard shapesrequires a check of the load-bearing capacity of balconies and parapets
Natural stonehighturned shapes, natural patternlong production time, high cost of complex parts
Polyurethane and expanded polystyrenevery lowanynot suitable for barriers that take load

In practice the materials are combined: stone steps and landings are complemented by a GFRC barrier with a texture imitating the same stone.

What to prepare for a balustrade quotation

To quote for a set, the length of the barrier, its configuration and information about the base are needed. The more accurate the input data, the fewer make-up pieces and the less fitting on site.

  • a plan with the dimensions of the runs, the angles of turn and the junctions with walls;
  • a section through the base: slab, parapet or stair flight, presence of waterproofing and screed;
  • the required height of the barrier and the purpose of the area;
  • a sketch or sample of the shape of the balusters, handrail and pedestals;
  • texture and colour, and the relationship to the rest of the facade decoration;
  • for staircases, the slope of the flight and the dimensions of the steps.

From these data the designer divides the barrier into bays, determines the number of balusters, pedestals and handrail sections, and assigns the fixing details. If the balustrade is repeated on many balconies, the bay dimensions are standardised where possible: identical sections speed up both production and installation. For the refurbishment of historic buildings, moulds are taken from surviving elements or recreated from archive drawings.

Typical mistakes when ordering and care of the balustrade

Most problems with balustrades are built in before production: when the proportions and the bearing detail are chosen.

  • The balusters are chosen from a catalogue picture without checking the barrier height and gaps.
  • No embedded parts are provided in the slab or parapet, so the fixing has to be resolved on site.
  • The bay length is not coordinated with the facade axes, which results in make-up sections of different lengths.
  • A handrail without a fall and a drip: water leaves streaks on the balusters.
  • There are no movement joints in a long barrier.

In service a balustrade needs periodic inspection of joints and fixings, cleaning with mild agents and renewal of the water-repellent impregnation as required. Recommendations are gathered in the article on the care of GFRC.

FAQ

Why are GFRC balusters better than concrete ones?

They are lighter with comparable durability, which reduces the load on balcony slabs and makes installation easier.

Can I buy balusters in Moscow?

Production is located in the Moscow Region; products are made to order from drawings and delivered to site.

Do you make wooden or wrought-iron balusters?

No, only GFRC balusters.

How high should a balustrade on a balcony be?

The height is set by the regulations for the specific type of building, and it is often greater than the height of a classical baluster. The missing height is made up by a parapet, a plinth or an additional handrail; the solution is recorded in the design.

Can a balustrade be installed on an existing balcony or parapet?

Yes, if the base is able to take the load. Before the calculation, information on the construction of the slab or parapet is required; fixing is made with chemical or mechanical anchors in accordance with the design.

Will a GFRC balustrade hold if people lean on it?

The load on the handrail is taken by steel components inside the pedestals and the handrail, designed for the prescribed actions. The balusters themselves are relieved of this load.
