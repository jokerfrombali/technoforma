GFRC for facades: element manufacturing

TECHNOFORMA manufactures GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) for facades — architectural mouldings, cladding and 3D panels, small architectural forms — and installs them on site. The company has worked with GFRC for more than 10 years and describes itself as the largest GFRC manufacturer in Russia and the CIS; production capacity is more than 20,000 m² per month on a site of more than 2 ha near Moscow.

What is GFRC

GFRC is glass fibre reinforced concrete: a fine-grained concrete reinforced with alkali-resistant glass fibre. According to the company, it consists of four components — glass fibre, sand, high-grade cement and water. The fibre acts as reinforcement throughout the whole volume, so an element can have a thin wall and be light. By comparison, fibre-reinforced concrete is a general name for concretes with any fibre (steel, polymer, basalt); fibre-reinforced concrete for foundations and floors and GFRC for facades are different materials for different tasks.

  • Non-combustible. Classed as non-combustible (NG under Russian standards) — unlike expanded polystyrene mouldings, which need a protective coat and are restricted by regulations.
  • Frost resistance. High, and further improved with admixtures.
  • Freedom of form. The material reproduces complex profiles, ornament and mould texture.
  • Lasting appearance. A GFRC facade looks like stone or architectural concrete but puts less load on the wall.

What we manufacture

GFRC facades are assembled from elements, each made to the project drawings:

Architectural mouldings

GFRC cornices, columns and capitals, balustrades and balusters, window surrounds, pilasters and rustication, mouldings, string courses and bands. They are a modern replacement for gypsum and polystyrene facade ornament: GFRC mouldings below a window, over an entrance or along a cornice need no protective coating and are not affected by frost.

Panels

GFRC facade panels — flat, relief and 3D, including large-format panels for ventilated rainscreen facades.

Small architectural forms

Street furniture and planters — planters, vases, benches and landscaping elements in the same style as the facade.

How an order works

  1. Drawings and brief. You send architectural drawings, a sketch or photos of the facade; if there is no design, our design department will prepare one.
  2. Quote. We calculate the range of elements and volumes and agree the scope of work and delivery schedule.
  3. Design. Design engineers and 3D visualisers issue the working documentation, layout and fixing details.
  4. Production. Models and moulds are made in our own mould shops, with quality control at every stage.
  5. Delivery. Shipping from the production base near Moscow, via company warehouses if needed.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division of more than 100 specialists fits the substructure, insulation and cladding; installation supervision is also available.

Each stage can be ordered separately: for example, production only to your documentation, or elements with installation supervision.

What drives the cost

We are often asked about GFRC prices. There is no single price per square metre; it depends on several factors:

  • range of elements: how many different profiles and how many repeated elements — repetition reduces the share of mould costs;
  • complexity of shape and depth of relief;
  • size and thickness of elements, embedded fixings;
  • texture and colour — smooth, stone-effect, acid-etched, integral colour or painted;
  • total volume and delivery schedule;
  • scope of work: design, delivery, substructure, installation.

We calculate the exact cost from the facade drawings.

FAQ

How much does GFRC cost?

The price depends on the range of elements, complexity of shapes, texture, volume and scope of work. Send us your drawings and we will prepare a quote.

Are you a GFRC manufacturer or a reseller?

A manufacturer: our own site of more than 2 ha near Moscow, mould shops and production capacity of more than 20,000 m² per month.

Why is a GFRC facade better than polystyrene mouldings?

GFRC is non-combustible, frost-resistant and needs no protective coat; polystyrene mouldings are combustible and vulnerable to mechanical damage.

Can I order elements to my own sketch?

Yes. We make models and moulds in our own mould shops from the architect's drawings or sketch.

Do you install the elements?

Yes, with our own installation division (more than 100 specialists); installation supervision is also available.
