GFRC for facades: element manufacturing

TECHNOFORMA manufactures GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) for facades — architectural mouldings, cladding and 3D panels, small architectural forms — and installs them on site. The company has worked with GFRC for more than 10 years and describes itself as the largest GFRC manufacturer in Russia and the CIS; production capacity is more than 20,000 m² per month on a site of more than 2 ha near Moscow.

What is GFRC

GFRC is glass fibre reinforced concrete: a fine-grained concrete reinforced with alkali-resistant glass fibre. According to the company, it consists of four components — glass fibre, sand, high-grade cement and water. The fibre acts as reinforcement throughout the whole volume, so an element can have a thin wall and be light. By comparison, fibre-reinforced concrete is a general name for concretes with any fibre (steel, polymer, basalt); fibre-reinforced concrete for foundations and floors and GFRC for facades are different materials for different tasks.

  • Non-combustible. Classed as non-combustible (NG under Russian standards) — unlike expanded polystyrene mouldings, which need a protective coat and are restricted by regulations.
  • Frost resistance. High, and further improved with admixtures.
  • Freedom of form. The material reproduces complex profiles, ornament and mould texture.
  • Lasting appearance. A GFRC facade looks like stone or architectural concrete but puts less load on the wall.

What we manufacture

GFRC facades are assembled from elements, each made to the project drawings:

Architectural mouldings

GFRC cornices, columns and capitals, balustrades and balusters, window surrounds, pilasters and rustication, mouldings, string courses and bands. They are a modern replacement for gypsum and polystyrene facade ornament: GFRC mouldings below a window, over an entrance or along a cornice need no protective coating and are not affected by frost.

Panels

GFRC facade panels — flat, relief and 3D, including large-format panels for ventilated rainscreen facades.

Small architectural forms

Street furniture and planters — planters, vases, benches and landscaping elements in the same style as the facade.

How an order works

  1. Drawings and brief. You send architectural drawings, a sketch or photos of the facade; if there is no design, our design department will prepare one.
  2. Quote. We calculate the range of elements and volumes and agree the scope of work and delivery schedule.
  3. Design. Design engineers and 3D visualisers issue the working documentation, layout and fixing details.
  4. Production. Models and moulds are made in our own mould shops, with quality control at every stage.
  5. Delivery. Shipping from the production base near Moscow, via company warehouses if needed.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division of more than 100 specialists fits the substructure, insulation and cladding; installation supervision is also available.

Each stage can be ordered separately: for example, production only to your documentation, or elements with installation supervision.

What drives the cost

We are often asked about GFRC prices. There is no single price per square metre; it depends on several factors:

  • range of elements: how many different profiles and how many repeated elements — repetition reduces the share of mould costs;
  • complexity of shape and depth of relief;
  • size and thickness of elements, embedded fixings;
  • texture and colour — smooth, stone-effect, acid-etched, integral colour or painted;
  • total volume and delivery schedule;
  • scope of work: design, delivery, substructure, installation.

We calculate the exact cost from the facade drawings.

Properties of glass fibre reinforced concrete: reference values for design

For the designer, glass fibre reinforced concrete is a thin-walled composite with a density close to that of ordinary concrete but with noticeably higher flexural and impact strength. Exact figures depend on the mix and the forming method, so the table gives typical ranges.

Spray or premix: how the production method affects the element

GFRC elements are made by two main methods: pneumatic spraying and casting of a premixed compound (premix). The choice is determined by the size, shape and load on the element.

  • Spraying. The mix and chopped fibre are applied to the mould in layers. The fibre content is higher and the fibre is oriented in the plane of the wall, so the method suits large panels and long elements.
  • Premix. The fibre is introduced into the mix in advance, and the mix is then poured or vibration-cast. This is how small parts of complex volume are made: balusters, capitals, rosettes, brackets.

In both cases the element gains strength in the mould and is cured before dispatch. The stages are described in detail in the article on GFRC production technology.

How GFRC elements are fixed to the facade

The elements are fixed mechanically, through embedded parts cast into the body of the element at the factory. Adhesive and mortar are used only as a supplement for small parts.

Large-format GFRC panels usually receive a steel frame connected to the skin by flex anchors: it provides stiffness and serves for hanging on brackets. In a ventilated facade system the panels are set on rails with adjustment in three planes. Cornices, string courses and surrounds are installed on anchors and cantilever brackets fixed to the load-bearing wall or floor slab; the joints between elements are filled with sealant. For each type of element the design provides a detail calculated for self-weight, wind and thermal movement.

Where is GFRC appropriate, and where is another material better?

GFRC is justified where complex modelling, a large format or a low weight of non-combustible cladding is needed. For simple planes without relief, sheet materials may prove more economical.

  • Residential complexes: repeated decorative elements and panels; 70,000 m² of the material was used on 30 blocks of the Dubai Houses residential complex.
  • Business centres and public buildings: large panels with a carefully set joint grid, 3D relief.
  • Hotels and club houses: classical decoration, columns, balustrades.
  • Refurbishment: recreation of lost mouldings with less load on old walls.
  • Private houses: window surrounds, cornices and entrance portals to an individual sketch.

The plinth, steps and areas of heavy wear are traditionally made of natural stone. A comparison of the materials by weight, lead time and freedom of form is given in the article GFRC or stone.

What to prepare for a quotation and which mistakes to avoid

Elevations with dimensions and a list of elements are enough for a quotation; the fuller the input data, the more accurate the estimate.

  • facade elevations, sections and details in DWG or PDF;
  • material and thickness of the load-bearing walls, presence of insulation;
  • wishes regarding texture and colour, a sample or reference;
  • the site address and the desired delivery schedule.

Typical mistakes are ordering decorative elements after the walls are finished, when embedded parts and cantilevers can no longer be provided, an excessive number of unique profiles, and joints set without regard to element length. These are removed by early facade design with layout and standardisation: identical elements are cast in one mould, and joints fall on the axes of openings and pilasters.

How the quality of elements is controlled and what to check on acceptance

The quality of GFRC elements is determined by the consistency of the mix and compliance with the curing regime, so control is carried out at the factory and not only on site. When accepting a batch, the client need only check a few clear parameters.

  • geometry: length, profile cross-section, panel diagonals, position of embedded parts;
  • surface: absence of chips, blowholes and cracks, uniformity of texture;
  • colour: conformity with the approved reference sample in daylight;
  • marking and completeness against the schedule and the installation drawing;
  • packaging and the condition of the elements after transport.

Slight differences in shade between batches are typical of any cement-based material; they are reduced by using the same raw materials and producing the elements of one elevation consecutively. The elements are stored on spacers, in the position specified by the manufacturer, and are not rested on the face surface.

Operation of a GFRC facade: care and repair

A GFRC facade does not require regular renewal of the finish: periodic inspections and cleaning are sufficient. Service life is determined mainly by the condition of the joints, the fixings and the water drainage.

An inspection is usually carried out once a year and after strong winds: the sealant in the joints, the flashings and the junctions with the roof and windows are checked. Dirt is washed off with water and neutral detergents; aggressive acid compounds and abrasives should not be used. The water-repellent coating is renewed as the water-repellent effect is lost. Local chips are restored with a colour-matched repair compound, and in the event of serious damage the element is replaced: mechanical fixing allows it to be removed separately from the adjacent ones.

FAQ

How much does GFRC cost?

The price depends on the range of elements, complexity of shapes, texture, volume and scope of work. Send us your drawings and we will prepare a quote.

Are you a GFRC manufacturer or a reseller?

A manufacturer: our own site of more than 2 ha near Moscow, mould shops and production capacity of more than 20,000 m² per month.

Why is a GFRC facade better than polystyrene mouldings?

GFRC is non-combustible, frost-resistant and needs no protective coat; polystyrene mouldings are combustible and vulnerable to mechanical damage.

Can I order elements to my own sketch?

Yes. We make models and moulds in our own mould shops from the architect's drawings or sketch.

Do you install the elements?

Yes, with our own installation division (more than 100 specialists); installation supervision is also available.

How thick are GFRC elements?

The wall is usually 10–20 mm with thickenings at the embedded parts. The overall thickness of a panel with its frame is greater and is determined by the design.

What panel sizes are possible?

The size is limited by transport and lifting conditions, frame stiffness and the joint layout. Panels several metres long are common practice; the maximum format is clarified from the drawings.

Does the GFRC surface need protection after installation?

A water-repellent impregnation reduces soiling and makes cleaning easier; the need for it and the renewal interval depend on the texture and service conditions.
