What a ventilated facade is

A ventilated facade is a system in which the cladding is fixed to the wall on a metal substructure, leaving an air gap between the cladding and the insulation. Moisture from the wall and insulation escapes through the gap, while the cladding protects them from precipitation and sun. NVF is the Russian abbreviation for a ventilated rainscreen facade, the standard designation of such systems in Russian design documentation.

Components of a ventilated facade

  • substructure: brackets, rails, fixings made of aluminium, galvanised or stainless steel;
  • insulation: non-combustible mineral wool boards;
  • wind barrier membrane;
  • cladding: GFRC panels, stone, porcelain stoneware, metal cassettes, etc.;
  • fire barriers and junctions with windows, roof and plinth.

Ventilated facade substructure

The substructure is selected to suit the cladding and building height: heavy stone and large GFRC panels need reinforced brackets and closer spacing, while porcelain stoneware uses standard solutions. The substructure is calculated for cladding and wind loads, taking into account anchor test results; an online calculator cannot replace a design engineer here. The substructure manufacturer and type are selected for the specific project.

Stages of ventilated facade installation: from survey to cladding

Ventilated facade installation follows a strict sequence: first the substrate and geometry of the building are checked, then the frame and insulation are installed, and only after the concealed works have been accepted is the cladding hung. Breaking the sequence is a frequent cause of rework, because a fault in a lower layer can no longer be seen once it is covered with slabs.

  1. Geodetic survey of the facades. Actual deviations of the walls from the vertical and from plane are established; the results are used to set the bracket projection and adjust the cladding layout.
  2. Anchor testing. The pull-out capacity of the fixings is checked on every type of substrate. The method and reporting are described in the article on anchor pull-out testing.
  3. Setting out and installing brackets. Fixing points are marked on the wall according to the design, and brackets are installed over thermal break pads.
  4. Insulation. Boards are laid tightly, with no voids around the brackets, and secured with disc dowels; where required, a wind barrier membrane is installed on top.
  5. Rails. Profiles are aligned in plane, with thermal breaks left at the points specified in the design.
  6. Fire barriers and opening surrounds. Installed before the cladding, with a check that the air cavity is closed off where required.
  7. Cladding. Panels, slabs or cassettes are hung from the bottom upwards or in sections according to the method statement, keeping to the specified joint width.
  8. Junctions. Sill flashings, parapet copings, plinth details, joints with curtain walling and the roof.

A detailed breakdown of each operation is given in the article on the stages of ventilated facade installation. On large projects the stages run in parallel on different sections: while cladding is hung on one section, the frame and insulation are installed on the next.

Before work begins, incoming inspection of materials is carried out: the marking of brackets, profiles and fasteners is checked against the specification, the accompanying documents for insulation and cladding are verified, and the thickness of the metal and its protective coating is measured on a sample basis. A non-conformity found in the store is dealt with by replacing the batch; the same non-conformity found after installation means dismantling the area. At the same time a mock-up of the system is assembled on a small section of wall, on which the client and the technical supervisor agree the appearance of joints, corners and reveals.

How does the cladding affect the frame and fixing details?

The weight and format of the cladding determine the type of brackets, the rail spacing and the method of hanging. The same facade clad in porcelain stoneware and in natural stone will need different frames, so the facing material is chosen before the subframe design begins.

CladdingApproximate weightFixingFrame features
GFRC panels and decorative elementsDepends on thickness and relief, usually 30–60 kg/m²Cast-in fixings in the body of the element, adjustable bracketsHanging points tied to the cast-in fixings; large formats and three-dimensional shapes are possible
Natural stoneAbout 55–85 kg/m² at a thickness of 20–30 mmCramps and dowels in the edges, anchors in the backReinforced brackets, reduced spacing, corrosion-resistant fasteners
Porcelain stonewareAbout 20–30 kg/m²Clips or concealed fixingStandard vertical or cross-rail arrangements
Metal cassettesUsually up to 10–15 kg/m²Slides, pins, visible fastenersGeometric accuracy is important: a cassette follows every irregularity of the frame
Fibre cement boardsAbout 15–25 kg/m²Rivets, screws or adhesive fixing according to the system documentationThermal movement accommodated through sliding and fixed points

The values are indicative and are refined against the specification of the particular material. With a combined solution — for example, GFRC panels on the piers and porcelain stoneware on ordinary wall areas — the frame is designed as a single system with different brackets in different areas.

The frame arrangement is also chosen to suit the substrate. A vertical arrangement fixed to the wall is suitable for in-situ concrete and brick walls with sufficient load-bearing capacity. A cross-rail arrangement of horizontal and vertical profiles is convenient when the cladding layout does not coincide with the permissible fixing points. A floor-to-floor arrangement transfers the load to the edges of the floor slabs and is used on infill of lightweight blocks, where an anchor in the wall does not reach the design load; in this case the profiles span longer distances and have a larger cross-section.

What input data is needed for a ventilated facade design

A ventilated facade design is developed on the basis of the architectural solutions, information on the load-bearing structure and site conditions. Without this data the frame cannot be calculated correctly and an accurate specification cannot be produced.

  • Architectural facade drawings, plans and sections; for refurbishment — a measured survey and a wall condition survey.
  • Material and thickness of the external walls, position of floor slabs, columns and movement joints.
  • Construction region: wind and snow load, seismicity of the site, building height.
  • Thermal calculation or requirements for the thermal resistance of the wall.
  • Chosen cladding: material, format, colour, preferred type of fixing.
  • Fire safety requirements: functional fire hazard class and building height.
  • Position of windows and curtain walling: the location of frames within the wall thickness determines the depth of the reveals.

On the basis of this data the design department produces the structural calculation, cladding layout, details and bill of materials. If the architectural concept has not yet been fixed, this is also a convenient stage at which to compare several cladding options by weight, frame spacing and labour input.

The completed design generally includes an explanatory note, the structural calculation of the frame and fasteners, a thermal calculation taking thermal bridging into account, bracket and rail setting-out drawings, the cladding layout for each facade, a set of details and a bill of materials. For GFRC elements, drawings of each element type showing the position of cast-in fixings are also issued: production works from them, and the installers receive a hanging scheme with element numbers.

What is checked when ventilated facade installation works are accepted?

Inspection is carried out in stages: each layer is accepted before it is covered by the next. Records are drawn up for concealed works, and measurement results are entered in the as-built documentation.

StageWhat is inspected
SubstrateAnchor test reports, strength and condition of the wall, absence of loose finishes
BracketsType and spacing as designed, anchorage depth, presence of thermal breaks, distance from the edge of the substrate
InsulationTightness of fit, staggered joints, number of dowels per board, no gaps at openings
RailsVerticality and plane, thermal gaps, fixing to brackets with the designed number of rivets
Fire barriers and revealsPosition as designed, material and thickness of the steel, closure of the cavity
CladdingJoint width, flatness, absence of chips, secure fixing of every element
Air cavityClear cross-section over the full height, open inlet at the bottom and outlet at the parapet

The cavity width and permitted deviations are set by the system documentation; as a rule, the cavity is not less than 40–60 mm. On projects where the company carries out both design and installation, the designers take part in acceptance, so changes on site are agreed immediately.

In addition to visual and instrumental inspection, repeat checks are carried out in critical areas: installed anchors are proof-loaded on a sample basis, and individual locations are opened up to assess the insulation. Remarks are recorded in a log with photographs and grid references, and their rectification is confirmed before moving on to the next section. This discipline is particularly important on high-rise blocks, where returning to a closed area means erecting scaffolding or a hoist again.

Typical mistakes in ventilated facade installation and their consequences

A ventilated facade is designed for decades of service, but only if the system is assembled from approved components and in accordance with the design. Failures usually begin with minor departures.

  • Substituted components. Anchors, brackets or rivets are replaced with cheaper equivalents. The calculated load-bearing capacity is not confirmed, and the system no longer corresponds to its technical certificate.
  • Contact between dissimilar metals. An aluminium profile is fixed with fasteners of unsuitable steel without isolation. Galvanic corrosion develops, especially in coastal and industrial areas.
  • Insulation with voids. Gaps between boards and around brackets act as thermal bridges and reduce the calculated thermal performance.
  • A combustible wind barrier, or none where one is needed. In the first case fire requirements are breached; in the second, insulation fibres are carried away by the airflow.
  • Blocked cavity. Construction debris, sagging insulation or an incorrectly installed flashing stops ventilation, and the construction becomes damp.
  • Rigid fixing without thermal joints. The cladding warps, cracks appear and noise occurs on heating.
  • Working without a geodetic survey. The bracket projection is insufficient and the frame is assembled with extensions not covered by the calculation.

Most of these problems are eliminated when design, supply and installation are in the same hands and departures from the documentation are processed through the designer.

Scaffolding, hoists, season: how facade works are organised

The method of access to the facade is chosen according to building height, cladding weight and site constraints. Heavy panels and stone are, as a rule, installed from scaffolding or with a crane and mast climbers; lightweight slabs on high-rise blocks can be installed from suspended platforms. The options are compared in the article scaffolding or cradles for facade works.

The method statement defines the work sections, storage areas, the scheme for delivering materials to the floors and the sequence relative to adjacent trades — roofers, window installers, services. The cladding of the lower storeys is usually left to the final stage so that it is not damaged by construction plant.

Assembly of the frame and hanging of the cladding involve no wet processes, so they are carried out all year round. Restrictions relate to wind, icing of the scaffolding and work with sealants and adhesives, for which the manufacturer sets a minimum temperature. Until it is covered by the cladding, the insulation is protected from precipitation.

Cladding logistics require separate planning. GFRC panels and decorative elements arrive from the factory in individual packaging, marked in accordance with the installation scheme; they are delivered in hanging order so as not to overload the site or handle the elements twice. Stone and porcelain stoneware are stored on pallets under cover, aluminium profiles — without contact with the ground or cement mortars. On constrained urban sites, deliveries are tied to the crane schedule and the permitted hours for goods vehicles.

Where ventilated facades are used: solutions by building type

The system is universal, but the requirements placed on it vary with the purpose of the building. This affects the choice of cladding, the type of fixing and the organisation of the works.

  • Residential complexes. Large areas, repeating elements, a tight handover programme. Ordinary wall areas are covered with slab materials, while window surrounds, string courses and cornices are produced in series from GFRC — as at the Dubai Houses residential complex.
  • Business centres. Large format, concealed fixing, precise joints with curtain walling and entrance areas; ease of repair of the cladding is important.
  • Hotels and public buildings. Expressive modelling, natural stone in the entrance area, higher requirements for sound insulation and fire safety.
  • Refurbishment. The substrate is non-uniform, the geometry is far from the design, and the building often cannot be taken out of use. The wall survey and fastener testing are of particular importance.
  • Seismic regions. Lightweight claddings and fixings that allow relative movement of elements without failure are preferred.

After handover the facade requires minimal but regular attention. The facilities management team receives the as-built documentation and a facade passport stating the system, the materials and the inspection intervals. The cladding is usually inspected annually, and also after storm-force winds and seismic events: the position of the elements, the condition of joints, flashings and parapets, and the absence of corrosion on visible fasteners are checked. Under this regime the service life of the system is determined primarily by the corrosion resistance of the frame and fasteners and is measured in decades.

FAQ

How much does a ventilated facade cost per m²?

The cost is determined after a site inspection and depends on the cladding, substructure, insulation and number of storeys.

What does NVF mean in construction?

It is the Russian abbreviation for a ventilated rainscreen facade: cladding on a substructure with an air gap over the insulation.

Which substructure should I choose?

It is selected by calculation to suit the cladding, building height and wind zone.

Can a ventilated facade be installed in winter?

Yes, a ventilated facade is a dry technology, so work is possible in the cold season subject to safety requirements.

How long does ventilated facade installation take?

The duration depends on the area, number of storeys, type of cladding, number of installation crews and readiness of the building. It is set in the works programme once the design has been developed; on large buildings work proceeds in parallel on several sections.

Can a ventilated facade be installed on an aerated concrete wall?

Yes, provided anchor tests confirm sufficient load-bearing capacity. With a weak substrate, special fasteners are used, the bracket spacing is reduced or a floor-to-floor arrangement is adopted, with the frame fixed to the floor slabs.

Is a wind barrier membrane needed in every ventilated facade?

Not always. The decision is taken according to the facade system documentation and the type of insulation: boards with a dense outer layer or a facing are often used without a membrane. If a membrane is used, it must meet the fire requirements of the design.
