What facade works include

Facade works are the full set of operations to build a building's outer envelope: substrate preparation, substructure fixing, insulation and wind barrier, installation of cladding and mouldings, junctions with windows and roof. For a residential complex, business centre or hotel it matters that this work follows a single design and is done by one team — that makes it easier to meet deadlines and take responsibility for the result.

What we do

Ventilated facade installation

Ventilated facade substructures for any cladding — ventilated rainscreen facade installation.

Insulation

Mineral wool board insulation with a wind barrier as part of a ventilated facade — facade insulation.

Cladding

With GFRC of our own production, natural stone, porcelain stoneware and other materials — for example, GFRC and FRONTEK were used at RUBLEVO BUSINESS PARK.

Facade cladding options

The choice of cladding depends on the building's appearance, height, budget and fire safety requirements:

  • GFRC — architectural mouldings and panels, non-combustible, freedom of form;
  • natural stone — durability and a prestigious look;
  • porcelain stoneware — standardised tiles for plain wall areas;
  • combinations: GFRC for mouldings and accents, porcelain stoneware or stone for plain areas — as at the Dominanta residential complex (14,000 m² of GFRC and 13,000 m² of porcelain stoneware).

How the work proceeds

  1. Drawings and brief. You send architectural drawings, a sketch or photos of the facade; if there is no design, our design department will prepare one.
  2. Quote. We calculate the range of elements and volumes and agree the scope of work and delivery schedule.
  3. Design. Design engineers and 3D visualisers issue the working documentation, layout and fixing details.
  4. Production. Models and moulds are made in our own mould shops, with quality control at every stage.
  5. Delivery. Shipping from the production base near Moscow, via company warehouses if needed.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division of more than 100 specialists fits the substructure, insulation and cladding; installation supervision is also available.

What drives the cost

The price of facade works per m² in Moscow, the Moscow Region and other regions is determined after a site inspection. It depends on:

  • area, number of storeys and complexity of the facade geometry;
  • condition of the substrate and anchor pull-out test results;
  • type of substructure and insulation;
  • chosen cladding and amount of mouldings;
  • access method — scaffolding or cradles, and the season;
  • scope of design work.

What should be prepared for an estimate of facade works?

For an initial assessment, facade drawings or photographs of the building with the main dimensions are sufficient. The more that is known about the substrate and the desired cladding, the closer the preliminary calculation will be to the final estimate.

  • architectural drawings: elevations, plans, sections, details of openings and the roof;
  • information on the wall material and its condition; for existing buildings, the survey results;
  • thermal performance requirements and the type of insulation, if already decided;
  • the cladding chosen or under consideration — for example, GFRC panels or natural stone — the presence of ornament and its approximate composition;
  • the construction stage, readiness of windows and roof, and site conditions: access roads, storage areas, connection points;
  • the desired start and completion dates and the handover sequence.

If there is no design, the calculation is based on measurements, and the missing solutions are developed by the design department. In that case the estimate is refined once the working documentation has been issued.

Substrate preparation and anchor testing

Any facade works on a rainscreen system begin with checking the wall: its material and condition determine the type of anchor, the bracket spacing and ultimately the reliability of the whole assembly. Skipping this stage is one of the common causes of rework.

Before installation starts, an as-built survey of the facade is carried out to establish the actual deviations of the plane from the design, and anchor pull-out tests are performed in representative areas — in concrete, masonry and block infill. Bracket lengths and the choice of fixings are adjusted according to the results. On buildings under refurbishment, the condition of the old finish is also assessed and a decision is taken on whether to remove or retain it.

Access to the facade and seasonality of works

The method of access is chosen according to the height of the building, the configuration of the facade and the weight of the components to be installed. Scaffolding is convenient where there is a great deal of ornament and work proceeds on several tiers at once; mast climbers and cradles suit high-rise buildings with flat planes. A comparison is given in the article "scaffolding or cradles".

Installation of the subframe, insulation and rainscreen cladding is possible all year round, since it involves no wet trades. Limitations arise from strong wind, icing and work with mortars and sealants, for which manufacturers specify a minimum temperature. In planning, such operations are scheduled for the warm period where possible, with the frame and hanging carried out in winter. For each project the programme is drawn up with regard to the climate of the region and the handover dates.

How is quality controlled and the facade accepted?

Facade quality is ensured not by a final inspection but by stage-by-stage control: most critical details are no longer visible once the cladding is installed. The subframe, insulation and fire-stopping elements are therefore accepted before they are covered.

  1. Incoming inspection of materials and components: documents, marking, appearance.
  2. Inspection of concealed works — anchoring, frame, insulation, fire barriers.
  3. Geometry checks: plane, plumb, joint width and evenness.
  4. Final acceptance with handover of as-built documentation.

What the client checks and which tolerances should be agreed in advance is described in the article on acceptance of facade works.

What sections make up a facade cost estimate

A facade cost estimate is made up of several separate sections, and contractors' offers can only be compared fairly on an identical scope. It usually distinguishes:

  • design work — working documentation, layout, details and, where necessary, calculations;
  • frame materials and fixings, insulation, membrane, fire-stopping elements;
  • cladding and ornament, including the making of models and moulds for new components;
  • installation by type of work, including junctions, reveals and sills;
  • access equipment, hoisting of materials, delivery and storage;
  • testing, survey support and as-built documentation.

A frequent cause of discrepancies between offers is items that one bidder has included in the price while another has left "at actual cost": scaffolding, reveals, make-up elements, waste removal. Before comparing, it is therefore worth bringing the bills of quantities to a common form and clarifying exactly what each line includes.

Typical mistakes in organising work on the facade

Programme overruns and rework on the facade are more often linked to organisation than to the materials themselves. The most common mistakes are:

  • starting installation before working documentation is issued and samples are approved;
  • ordering components to design dimensions without measuring the actual geometry;
  • splitting design, supply and installation between unconnected contractors without a shared programme;
  • starting on the facade before completion of works that may damage the cladding — roofing, glazing, external services;
  • saving on the subframe and fixings in disregard of the calculation;
  • the absence of a reference area against which the remaining zones are accepted.

Most of these risks are removed by a single design and a supply and installation programme agreed before work starts. When one contractor both manufactures and installs the components, changes from site reach production faster, and the client deals with one party responsible for the result.

FAQ

How much do facade works cost per m²?

The cost is determined after a site inspection and depends on the cladding, substructure, number of storeys and scope of work.

Do you work in the Moscow Region?

Yes, the company's production base is in the Moscow Region; we work in Moscow, the region and elsewhere in Russia.

Can I order installation only?

Yes, if the design and materials are available; installation supervision is also possible.

Which facade cladding options do you install?

GFRC, natural stone, porcelain stoneware and other materials on ventilated rainscreen facade substructures.

Can facade works be carried out in winter?

Yes, the subframe, insulation and rainscreen cladding are installed at sub-zero temperatures as well. Operations with mortars and sealants are restricted, as is work in strong wind and icing conditions. Seasonality is taken into account when the programme is drawn up.

How does installation supervision differ from ordinary installation?

In ordinary installation all work is done by the contractor's own crews. Under installation supervision the cladding is installed by the client's contractor, while the company's specialists provide technical guidance: they brief the crews, check the subframe and the first areas, and take part in acceptance.

What documents does the client receive on completion of the works?

As a rule this is the as-built documentation: certificates of inspection of concealed works, as-built drawings, and documents for the materials and components used. The exact content is determined by the contract and the requirements of the client's technical team.
