Facade Insulation

Why insulate a facade externally

External insulation moves the dew point into the insulation layer, protects the wall from freezing and reduces the building's heat loss. For multi-storey residential and public buildings it is a mandatory part of the design under thermal protection requirements; for refurbishment it is a way to cut heating costs and extend the life of the wall.

Facade insulation with mineral wool

Ventilated facades use non-combustible mineral wool: stone wool boards of the required density. They are fixed to the wall with insulation anchors in one or two layers (two-layer insulation with staggered joints reduces heat loss through the joints), and a wind barrier membrane is laid on top. The insulation thickness is determined by a thermal calculation.

Insulated facade cladding

Cladding with insulation is a single system: the cladding on the substructure protects the insulation from precipitation and wind, and the air gap removes moisture. Cladding options:

  • GFRC panels;
  • natural stone;
  • porcelain stoneware.

Decorative insulation panels and wet render systems (ETICS) are separate technologies; a ventilated system is more durable and allows heavy cladding to be used.

Which buildings we insulate

Residential complexes, apartment buildings, business centres, hotels, public buildings. Insulating timber and log houses requires separate solutions; the company's core focus is masonry and monolithic concrete buildings.

Junctions and thermal bridges

The effectiveness of insulation is determined by the junctions: substructure brackets are installed with thermal isolation pads, the insulation is fitted tightly to reveals and floor slabs, and fire barriers are installed according to the design. Design engineers detail the junctions with windows, balconies and the roof to avoid thermal bridges and condensation. The work is carried out by the company's own installation division.

How the work is done

  1. Drawings and brief. A design or facade drawings, photos of the building, cladding requirements.
  2. Estimate. Site inspection, quantities, scope of works; the cost is determined after the inspection.
  3. Design. Design of the substructure, insulation and cladding, load calculations, junction details.

  5. Delivery. From the production base in the Moscow Region or via the company's warehouses.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division of more than 100 specialists: substructure, insulation, cladding.

How to choose insulation for a ventilated facade?

Ventilated systems use stone wool boards, which are non-combustible, keep their shape in the vertical position and are permeable to water vapour. The choice of a specific grade is determined by four parameters: thermal conductivity, density, resistance to air washing and the ability not to settle over time. Roll materials and soft mats are not used on facades: they slump and leave areas of wall uncovered.

ParameterTypical values for facade boardsWhy it matters
Thermal conductivityAbout 0.034–0.040 W/(m·K)It determines the design thickness of the layer
Density in a single-layer arrangementRoughly 70–90 kg/m³Rigidity of the board and resistance to the airflow in the cavity
Density in a two-layer arrangementInner layer about 30–50 kg/m³, outer layer 80–100 kg/m³The soft layer fills irregularities in the wall, the dense one protects against air washing
Combustibility classNon-combustibleA mandatory condition for systems on tall buildings
Vapour permeabilityHighMoisture from the wall escapes into the ventilated cavity
Water absorptionLow, boards are water-repellentThe material does not take up water in driving rain during installation

Exact characteristics are taken from the manufacturer's documentation and the technical certificate for the facade system: the insulation must be on the list of materials with which the system has been tested. Substituting a grade with a similar name that has not been approved is a breach that is picked up at acceptance.

A single-layer arrangement is chosen for thicknesses up to 100–120 mm and an even substrate: it involves fewer operations and fewer fixings. A two-layer arrangement is rational for greater thicknesses and on uneven walls: the soft inner layer fits tightly against the substrate, and the total weight and cost are lower than for a single dense board of the same thickness. There are also dual-density boards, in which the rigid outer and soft inner layers are combined in the factory; they reduce labour but require careful orientation during installation — marked side outwards.

What thickness of facade insulation is needed in different climate zones

The thickness of the layer is determined by a thermal calculation, not by an averaged recommendation. The input data are the climate of the construction region, the purpose of the building, and the material and thickness of the wall. The calculation compares the required thermal resistance with the actual one, which is the sum of the resistances of all layers of the wall.

For an initial assessment, the ranges established in design practice can be used as a guide. They do not replace the calculation and are given for walls of in-situ reinforced concrete and masonry.

Climatic conditionsApproximate thickness of mineral wool
Southern Russia, the Black Sea and Caspian coasts80–120 mm
Central Russia, the Volga region120–180 mm
The Urals, Siberia, northern regions150–250 mm

An important correction is thermal uniformity. Frame brackets, dowels, reveals and floor slab edges conduct heat better than the insulation, so the actual thermal performance of the wall is lower than that of the wool layer on its own. The heavier the cladding and the closer the brackets, the more noticeable the losses, and the thickness has to be increased or stainless steel brackets with lower thermal conductivity used. More on how these factors affect running costs can be found in the article on facade energy efficiency.

Increasing the thickness beyond the calculated value is not always worthwhile. Every additional centimetre of wool increases the bracket projection, and with it the bending moment on the anchor and the consumption of metal. Window reveals also become deeper, which affects daylight in the rooms and the cost of the surrounds. For this reason several options are compared at design stage: a thicker layer with standard brackets or a moderate layer with brackets that lose less heat. The optimum depends on the price of heat, the period of ownership of the building and the weight of the cladding.

The process: how insulation is installed behind the cladding

The insulation is installed after the brackets and before the rails. The boards are slit in place and fitted over the brackets so that no voids are left around the metal.

  1. The substrate is cleaned of mortar runs and loose finishes, and through gaps in the masonry are filled.
  2. The first row rests on a plinth profile or starter support so that the layer does not slip.
  3. Boards are laid from the bottom upwards with staggered vertical joints, tight against one another; gaps wider than a few millimetres are filled with strips of the same material.
  4. In a two-layer arrangement the outer boards are offset so as to cover the joints of the inner layer both horizontally and vertically.
  5. Each board is secured with disc dowels; the number and pattern are set by the design, usually several dowels per board with the corners and centre always fixed.
  6. At window openings and building corners the boards are cut so that no joint falls on the line of the opening corner.
  7. If the design provides for a wind barrier membrane, it is rolled out over the insulation with overlapping sheets and held by the same dowels.

The insulation must not be left exposed for long. In practice it is covered with cladding one or two work sections behind, and during a break in the works it is protected from precipitation. Boards that have become wet are dried or replaced before being covered.

Around the perimeter of window and door openings, and on tall buildings at each floor level as well, the steel fire protection elements specified in the system documentation are installed. The insulation is brought tightly up to them, with no voids into which fire or cold air could enter. Where air-conditioning brackets, downpipe fixings and lighting pass through the layer, the boards are likewise cut in place and the openings filled with offcuts of wool.

Ventilated or rendered system: which to choose for external insulation?

External facade insulation is carried out in two main ways: as part of a ventilated facade system or as a rendered system, where the insulation is bonded to the wall and covered with a thin reinforced coat. The choice depends on building height, cladding requirements and working conditions.

CriterionVentilated facade systemRendered system
CladdingGFRC, stone, porcelain stoneware, metalDecorative render, lightweight tiles
Moisture removalThrough the air cavityBy diffusion through the finishing coat
Seasonality of worksAll year roundLimited by the application temperature of adhesives and renders
Impact resistanceDetermined by the cladding, as a rule highLower, especially on the lower storeys
RepairReplacement of an individual elementLocal repair is visible; the whole surface often has to be repainted
Load on the wallHigher, transferred through anchorsLower
Initial costHigherLower

For multi-storey residential and public buildings with heavy or large-format cladding, the ventilated system remains the main solution. The rendered option is justified on low-rise buildings and where the architecture does not call for slab materials. A detailed comparison is given in the article rendered or ventilated facade.

Insulation mistakes that cannot be seen once the cladding is on

Defects in the insulation layer are hidden behind the cladding and show up after one or two heating seasons: as cold corners, condensation on reveals and higher heat consumption. Correcting them means dismantling the facade, so the layer is accepted under a concealed works record before it is covered.

  • Gaps between boards and at brackets. Even narrow through gaps noticeably reduce the thermal resistance of the area.
  • Too few dowels. The board comes away from the wall, air moves behind it and the layer stops working.
  • Insulation not returned into the reveal. The window unit ends up in the cold zone and condensation forms around its perimeter.
  • Uninsulated edges of floor and balcony slabs. The longest thermal bridges in the building.
  • A soft board in the outer layer. Fibres are carried away by the airflow and the layer loses thickness over time.
  • Film instead of a membrane. A vapour-tight material traps moisture in the insulation.
  • Installing wet material. Damp wool insulates less well and dries slowly in a closed construction.

Inspection includes a visual check of every work section, a check of the number of fixings and sample thickness measurements. Once the building is in use, the quality of the layer can be assessed by a thermal imaging survey in the cold season.

One design-level mistake deserves separate mention — a calculation that ignores thermal bridging. In such a design the wool thickness is set from the resistance of the plain layer, while the actual heat loss through brackets, reveals and balcony junctions turns out higher than expected. This can be checked in the thermal calculation section: it should state the uniformity coefficient or include a calculation of the reduced resistance detail by detail.

Insulation in refurbishment and on different wall types

The wall material affects not so much the choice of insulation as the method of fixing and the preparation of the substrate. The solutions differ for new in-situ concrete frames with infill and for existing brick buildings.

  • In-situ reinforced concrete. A reliable substrate for anchors; the main attention goes to floor slab edges and the junctions between infill and frame.
  • Brick masonry. Load-bearing capacity depends on the brick grade and the condition of the joints; special dowels and anchors are used in hollow brick.
  • Aerated concrete and other lightweight blocks. The wall already has appreciable thermal resistance, so the calculated wool thickness is usually smaller, but the fasteners are selected on the basis of test results.
  • Large-panel buildings. The insulation covers the joints between panels — the main source of heat loss and leaks; fixing is made into the load-bearing layers of the panels.

When working on an existing building, a survey is carried out first: the strength of the walls, moisture content and the condition of old finishes and canopies are assessed, and services on the facade that will need to be re-routed are recorded. Weak render layers are removed. The procedure for such works is described in the article on facade refurbishment. Residential projects designed and installed by the company include the Na Sovetskoy residential complex.

External insulation also solves related problems. The stone wool layer together with the cladding lowers the level of street noise indoors, which matters for buildings next to main roads. The load-bearing wall sits in the zone of positive temperatures and does not undergo freeze–thaw cycles, so its service life increases. In existing buildings the heating system settings are usually reviewed after insulation; otherwise the heat saving turns into overheated rooms.

What is needed for an estimate and what the insulation design includes

To estimate insulation and cladding, facade drawings and data on the wall construction and construction region are required. On this basis the layer thickness, the type of frame and the material quantities are determined.

  • Elevations, plans and sections with dimensions; for an existing building — a measured survey and photographs.
  • Build-up of the external walls: material, thickness, any internal insulation or finishes.
  • The city and purpose of the building — for climatic parameters and regulatory requirements.
  • Preferred cladding: its weight sets the bracket spacing and therefore the correction for thermal bridging.
  • The client's requirements for the energy efficiency class, if they exceed the regulatory ones.

The design includes the thermal calculation, the layout of boards and dowels, junction details at windows, plinth, parapet and balconies, and the bill of materials. Where the facade has GFRC panels or large decorative elements, the calculation allows for the reinforced brackets under these elements. The estimate separates materials and labour by layer, so the contribution of insulation to the overall cost of the facade appears as a separate line.

FAQ

How much does external house facade insulation cost?

The cost is determined after an inspection and depends on the area, insulation thickness and chosen cladding.

What insulation is used?

Non-combustible stone wool mineral boards.

Why insulate in two layers?

Staggering the joints between layers reduces heat loss through the board joints.

Can an apartment building be insulated?

Yes, as part of a ventilated rainscreen facade according to the design.

What thickness of insulation does a facade need?

The thickness is determined by a thermal calculation for the specific city, building type and wall construction. As a guide, it is 80–120 mm of mineral wool for southern regions, 120–180 mm for central Russia and 150–250 mm for the Urals and Siberia.

Can a facade be insulated in winter?

As part of a ventilated system — yes: the boards are fixed mechanically, without adhesives or renders. The insulation must be protected from snow and rain until it is covered by the cladding, and wet material must not be installed.

Does a facade need insulating if the walls are aerated concrete?

The calculation shows this. An aerated concrete wall has thermal resistance of its own, and in a warm climate it is sometimes sufficient. In most regions a layer of mineral wool is still required, but it is thinner than for an in-situ concrete or brick wall.
