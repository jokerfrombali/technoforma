Fire Barriers and Ventilated Facade Fire Requirements

Fire barriers in a ventilated facade are steel elements that close off the air gap between the insulation and the cladding around window and door openings and, in some systems, at every floor. Their job is to stop flames from a burning room getting into the ventilation gap and spreading up the facade as if through a chimney.

The key criterion is that fire barriers must match the album of technical solutions of a system that has passed fire tests: material, thickness, projection, fixing spacing and locations are not chosen by eye. The topic matters to designers, technical supervisors and general contractors, as well as to clients who will go through design review and commissioning.

The general construction of rainscreen facades is in the Ventilated rainscreen facades section.

Requirements for rainscreen facades

The fire safety of a rainscreen facade system is determined for the construction as a whole, not for individual materials. The main requirements in Russia are set by Federal Law No. 123-FZ "Technical Regulations on Fire Safety Requirements" and the applicable codes of practice for fire protection.

  • The fire hazard class of the external wall with a rainscreen facade must match the building's structural fire hazard class. Most multi-storey residential and public buildings require K0 — a non-fire-hazardous construction.
  • Confirmation by testing. The class is determined by fire tests on a full-scale facade mock-up with an opening under Russian standard GOST 31251. The result applies only to the tested configuration: the same cladding, insulation, membrane, substructure and fire barriers.
  • Insulation in a rainscreen facade is non-combustible.
  • Cladding and membrane must have fire properties permissible for the given system and building class.
  • Fire barriers and opening surrounds follow the album of technical solutions.

Requirements depend on building height, use and fire resistance degree, so for a specific project they are set in the design and checked in the design review.

Fire barriers

Fire barriers are the main structural element that interrupts the path of fire in the ventilation gap.

  • Material. Galvanised or stainless steel of the thickness specified in the system album. Aluminium is not used for fire barriers: it melts at fire temperatures.
  • Locations. Above window openings and around their perimeter — mandatory; horizontal barriers at every floor — if required by the system or design. On high-rise and complex buildings, vertical barriers are added at internal corners.
  • Construction. The profile closes the ventilation gap fully or with perforation that allows air movement in normal operation but stops flame. A steel box surround is formed at the opening: reveals, sill and head element.
  • Fixing. To the substrate with steel anchors, at the spacing given in the album. Fixing a barrier to an aluminium substructure does not replace fixing to the wall.

Fire barriers are installed together with the insulation and accepted with a concealed works record: once cladding is on, they cannot be seen.

K0 class materials

The fire hazard class of a facade is the class of the external wall construction with the rainscreen system, under the Russian classification: K0 (non-fire-hazardous), K1 (low fire hazard), K2 (moderate fire hazard), K3 (fire hazardous). The class is determined by tests based on how the mock-up behaves: presence of burning, damage to materials, heat effect.

ElementTypical requirement for K0 class systems
CladdingNon-combustible materials (e.g. GFRC of the NG group, porcelain stoneware, stone) or materials confirmed by testing as part of the system
InsulationNon-combustible mineral wool
Wind barrierNon-combustible or approved by system tests
SubstructureSteel or aluminium as tested; in high-rise buildings — steel elements at openings
Fire barriers and opening surroundsSteel, per the album of technical solutions

A non-combustible cladding material makes the task easier, but it is the system that gets the K0 class, not the panel. According to the company, the GFRC produced by TECHNOFORMA belongs to the NG (non-combustible) group.

Documents

Design review, technical supervision and acceptance require a set of documents:

  • technical certificate for the facade system stating its scope of application;
  • album of technical solutions with fire barrier and opening surround details;
  • a conclusion on the system's fire hazard class based on fire tests;
  • certificates or test reports on the combustibility of the cladding, insulation and membrane;
  • the fire safety section of the design;
  • concealed works records for the installation of fire barriers and insulation.

All documents must relate to the same system. A common mistake is cladding from one manufacturer, substructure from another and insulation from a third, with no tests for that combination.

Inspection

Technical supervision checks the fire safety of the rainscreen facade at every stage:

  • before work begins — conformity of the design and materials with the system documents;
  • during insulation — insulation and membrane brands against delivery notes and markings;
  • during fire barrier installation — material, thickness, locations, fixing to the substrate, closure of the ventilation gap;
  • during cladding — integrity of fire barriers, no combustible inserts, correct opening surrounds;
  • at acceptance — completeness of concealed works records and photographs.

Typical violations: barriers above only some windows, aluminium instead of steel, barriers fixed to rails without anchoring into the wall, barriers missing at balcony doors.

Design changes during construction need particular attention. Replacing the cladding with a cheaper one, a different insulation, a different barrier spacing or a new opening shape may take the system outside the tested configuration. Any such change should be agreed with the designer and checked against the technical certificate before materials are bought, not after installation. Junctions with curtain walls, entrance groups and ventilation grilles are also checked: barriers are often interrupted at these points.

In service, fire barriers need no maintenance, but their integrity must be restored when the cladding is repaired or windows are replaced. If the facade is partly dismantled, before the panels are rehung it is checked that the opening surrounds and steel elements are in place and fixed to the substrate.

How fire spreads through the ventilated cavity

In a room fire the glazing fails and a flame plume emerges from the window, rising along the wall. If there is no barrier above the opening, hot gases are drawn into the air cavity behind the cladding, where a draught operates as in a chimney. This is why fire barriers in a ventilated facade are installed first and foremost around openings: that is where the fire enters the system.

What happens next depends on what is in and around the cavity:

  • a combustible or poorly fixed breather membrane may ignite and produce burning droplets;
  • aluminium parts of the subframe lose strength when heated, and the cladding above the opening may lose its support;
  • cladding slabs crack under thermal shock and fall away, exposing the insulation;
  • through an unprotected reveal the flame reaches the joint between the window frame and the wall and the installation foam.

The steel reveal lining and the barrier above the window perform several tasks at once: they deflect the plume away from the plane of the facade, close the entry to the cavity and hold the insulation in place in the heated zone. The other components of the system, namely non-combustible insulation and a breather membrane approved by testing, deprive the fire of fuel beyond the opening.

Types of fire barriers and what each is for

Technical solution albums contain several types of element that are commonly referred to by one word. The set required for a particular building is determined by the system and the design, not by the installer's choice.

ElementWhere it is installedWhat it does
Window surround (steel lining)Around the perimeter of window and door openings: head, jambs and sillCloses the edge of the system at the opening, deflects the plume and protects the joint between window and wall
Barrier above the openingAbove the head, projecting beyond the cladding plane or within it, as the album specifiesBlocks the entry of flame into the air cavity
Horizontal barrier at floor levelAt floor slab level along the full length of the facade, where the system requires itDivides the cavity over the height and limits the draught
Vertical barrierAt internal corners, at the boundaries of fire compartments and at junctions with other systemsPrevents fire from spreading along the facade
Fire break within the insulationOnly in systems with combustible insulation, which do not belong to ventilated facades of class K0A different solution; not to be confused with cavity barriers

Horizontal barriers may be solid or perforated. A solid barrier divides the cavity completely and requires air inlets and outlets at every tier; a perforated one preserves through ventilation. Which option is permitted is stated in the system documents, and replacing one with the other on site alters the tested configuration.

Barriers with GFRC and stone cladding and decorative elements

With heavy cladding and three-dimensional decor the principle is the same but the details are more complex: the cladding stands further from the wall, and architraves, pediments and sill elements appear around the windows. The steel lining of the opening must be worked into these details so that the decor neither interrupts nor replaces it.

  • Larger cavity and stand-off. The further the cladding is from the wall, the wider the barrier has to be and the more its stiffness and fixing spacing to the substrate matter.
  • Decor around windows. GFRC window surrounds are non-combustible, but they are not a barrier in themselves: the steel lining runs beneath or behind them, and the junction is shown on a separate detail.
  • Frames of large units. Cornices, string courses and panels on their own steel frames are fixed so that the frame does not cross the barrier or leave unclosed cut-outs in it.
  • Open and closed joints. With open joints between slabs, flame and hot gases enter the cavity more easily, so barrier solutions in such systems are generally stricter.

The details around openings are developed together with the cladding layout, not after it. The general principles of such junctions are described in the article on details at windows and roofs; fire elements are shown on them as a separate item with the steel grade and thickness.

How the height and use of a building affect the requirements for barriers

The taller the building and the more people it holds, the stricter the requirements for the facade system and the more thoroughly the fire protection elements are detailed. Specific solutions are set by the design, but the general logic is the same.

  • Low-rise buildings. As a rule, lining of the openings in accordance with the system album is sufficient; additional measures are seldom specified.
  • Multi-storey residential buildings. A class K0 system is required, with barriers above all openings, including balcony doors and stairwell windows; floor-level barriers follow the system documents.
  • High-rise buildings. Standard solutions are supplemented with reinforced steel elements around openings and additional horizontal and vertical barriers; the solutions are often fixed in project-specific technical conditions.
  • Schools, hospitals, hotels and shopping centres. The heightened requirements relate to the number of occupants and the conditions of evacuation; particular attention is paid to the zones above exits.

Areas above escape exits and walkways are considered separately: here it is important not only to stop the fire but also to prevent fragments of cladding from falling onto the routes used by people and fire crews. The fixing of slabs and decor in these zones is therefore checked with heating taken into account, and aluminium elements are replaced with steel ones where necessary.

In what order fire barriers are installed

Barriers are fitted after the brackets and insulation but before the rails and cladding in the area of the opening; otherwise access to the substrate is closed off. A typical sequence is as follows:

  1. Checking the window unit: the window is installed, the perimeter joint is completed and covered, and the position of the frame corresponds to the design.
  2. Setting-out to the album details: level of the upper barrier, projection and position of the side elements.
  3. Installing the insulation around the opening without voids or gaps at the reveals.
  4. Fitting the surround elements and the barrier with steel anchors into the substrate; joints between elements are lapped or made with connecting pieces as the album specifies.
  5. Inspection: metal thickness, anchor spacing, closure of the cavity and absence of gaps at the corners.
  6. Photographic record and a certificate for concealed works, after which the area may be closed with cladding.

Cutting and bending barriers on site is permitted only within the limits stated in the album. Improvised elements made from random sheet are a frequent cause of comments from the technical supervisor. It is more practical for the works to be carried out by the same crew that performs the ventilated facade installation: the barriers then do not fall into a gap in responsibility between the window fitters and the facade installers.

What to check in the design before purchasing materials

Most fire-related comments on a ventilated facade can be resolved at design stage, before the materials are bought. The check takes little time if a list is followed.

  • The scope of application in the technical approval covers the height and use of the building.
  • The cladding, insulation, membrane and subframe in the design match those named in the fire test report.
  • The facade elevations show all barriers: window, floor-level and vertical, with setting-out and references.
  • There are details for non-standard locations: balcony doors, curtain walling, entrances, ventilation grilles, the parapet and the plinth.
  • The steel grade and thickness, anchor type and spacing, and coating are specified.
  • Barriers are quantified as a separate line in the schedule rather than included in the subframe without a breakdown.
  • The programme provides for handover of the barriers under a certificate before the cladding is hung.

If even one item raises questions, they are put to the system developer before deliveries begin. A written reply from the system holder on the admissibility of a specific solution is normal practice, and the expert review takes such letters into account.

It is convenient to combine this check with the issue of the working documentation for the cladding. When the slab layout, the decor drawings and the details around openings are released as a single package, discrepancies between the architectural and the fire-related parts are visible at once: for example, a pediment that covers the projection of a barrier, or a cladding joint that falls on the junction of steel elements. Correcting such places on paper is incomparably easier than on an installed facade.

FAQ

Where are fire barriers installed in a ventilated facade?

Mandatorily above and around window and door openings; at every floor and at corners — if required by the system or design.

What are fire barriers made of?

Galvanised or stainless steel of the thickness given in the album of technical solutions. Aluminium is not used.

What does K0 class mean for a facade?

A non-fire-hazardous construction under the Russian classification: in fire tests of a mock-up there is no fire spread and no unacceptable damage. The class is confirmed for the system as a whole.

Does non-combustible cladding guarantee the fire safety of a rainscreen facade?

No. Non-combustible insulation, an approved membrane, fire barriers and confirmation of the system by testing are also needed.

Who checks the fire safety of a rainscreen facade?

The design review, the client's technical supervision during installation and the regulatory authorities at commissioning.

Can floor-level barriers be omitted if window barriers are installed?

It depends on the system. In some technical solution albums the window surrounds and the barriers above the openings are sufficient; in others horizontal barriers at floor slab level are mandatory, particularly with a larger cavity or open joints. The answer is given by the documentation for the specific tested system.

Do fire barriers obstruct ventilation of the cavity?

Not when correctly executed. Perforated barriers let air through, and with solid ones the system provides air inlets and outlets at every tier. Problems arise when solid elements are fitted where the design calls for perforated ones.

Are barriers needed on a low-rise building?

The window surrounds and barriers form part of the tested system regardless of the number of storeys, so when a certified ventilated facade is installed they are fitted on low buildings as well. The extent of additional measures depends on the class of the building and is determined by the design.
