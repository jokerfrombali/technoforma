Ventilated rainscreen facades

What is a ventilated rainscreen facade? The cladding is fixed to the wall on a metal substructure, and an air gap is left between the cladding and the insulation through which moisture escapes. Such a facade protects the wall and insulation, allows cladding to be installed in a wide range of weather conditions and lets individual elements be replaced. Russian project documentation often lists it (NVF) next to SPK — glazed translucent structures; together they form the building envelope.

This section has articles on ventilated facade build-ups and substructures made of aluminium, galvanised steel and stainless steel; on cladding with GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete), stone, porcelain stoneware, composite and fibre cement; on insulation, wind barriers and fire breaks; on load calculation and anchor testing; and on facades for high-rise housing, business centres, hotels and public buildings.

What layers make up a ventilated rainscreen facade

A ventilated facade is not a single material but a system of several layers, each with its own function. A mistake in any of them affects the whole assembly, so the system is designed and accepted as a whole rather than item by item.

LayerFunctionWhat to check
Substrate (wall)takes the load from the facadewall material and load capacity of the anchors
Brackets and railstransfer cladding weight and wind load to the wallmetal, spacing, allowance for thermal movement
Insulationreduces heat lossnon-combustibility, density, number of fixings per slab
Wind barrier membraneprotects the insulation from wind washing and moisturecombustibility class and whether the design requires it
Air cavityremoves moisture from the assemblywidth, free air inlet and outlet
Claddingshapes the appearance and sheds rainweight, fixing method, format

When is a ventilated facade a better choice than render?

A rainscreen system is justified where a long cladding service life, installation that does not depend on the warm season and the option of local repair are important. On multi-storey residential buildings, business centres and public buildings it is usually the main option, whereas a rendered facade is more often chosen for low-rise buildings and limited budgets.

The rainscreen assembly has its own price: it requires structural calculation, anchor testing and more skilled installation, and the subframe has a noticeable effect on the cost estimate. A comparison by programme, repairability and limitations is given in the article "rendered or ventilated facade".

A separate argument in favour of the rainscreen system is freedom in the choice of cladding. Standard slabs and three-dimensional ornament are combined on one frame, and during refurbishment the cladding can be replaced while keeping the insulation and most of the subframe, provided its condition has been confirmed by a survey.

What input data are needed for a ventilated facade design

For design purposes it is enough to understand the geometry of the building, the wall material and the desired cladding; the rest is clarified during the survey. The more complete the input data, the fewer changes have to be made on site.

  • architectural facade drawings, plans and sections, window and roof details;
  • information on the material and condition of the walls, results of anchor pull-out tests;
  • the construction region and building height — these determine the wind load and bracket spacing;
  • thermal performance requirements and the type of insulation;
  • the type of cladding, the format and weight of elements, the presence of ornament.

These data form the basis for the ventilated facade calculation and for the working documentation used for the subsequent installation of the ventilated facade.

Typical mistakes in ventilated facade construction

Most defects are caused not by materials but by deviations from the design and skipped inspection steps. The mistakes that occur most often are listed below.

  • installing brackets without anchor tests on the actual substrate;
  • replacing subframe components or fasteners with cheaper ones without recalculation;
  • a blocked or narrowed air cavity, which prevents the insulation from drying out;
  • missing fire barriers and opening surrounds required by the system;
  • rigid fixing of rails without expansion joints;
  • loosely laid insulation and gaps between slabs.

What such faults lead to in service is described in the article on the causes of cladding failure.

FAQ

What cladding can be installed on a ventilated facade?

Almost any cladding with a proven fixing detail: GFRC, natural stone, porcelain stoneware, fibre cement boards, composite cassettes. The choice is determined by the architecture of the building, the weight of the cladding, fire requirements and the budget.

Can a ventilated facade be installed in winter?

Yes. A rainscreen system involves no wet trades, so the subframe, insulation and cladding can be installed at sub-zero temperatures as well. The limitations relate to wind, icing of the scaffolding and the requirements of individual material manufacturers.

Is a wind barrier membrane always needed in a ventilated facade?

No. Whether a membrane is needed is determined by the design and the technical documents for the specific system: it depends on the type of insulation, the building height and fire requirements. It must not be added or omitted on site without a design decision.
