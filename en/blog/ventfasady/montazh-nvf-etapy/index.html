Ventilated Facade Installation: Stages of Work

Installing a ventilated rainscreen facade is a sequence of work in which each layer covers the previous one: preparation and survey, brackets, insulation and membrane, rails, cladding, acceptance. A mistake made at an early stage cannot be corrected later without dismantling, so quality is determined not by how fast panels are hung but by control of concealed works.

The main criterion for choosing a contractor is whether they work to the design and the system's technical details manual and hand over concealed works with signed inspection records. This article is useful for clients, general contractors and technical supervision planning and accepting rainscreen facades on multi-storey and public buildings.

The system itself is described in Ventilated rainscreen facades.

Preparation and survey

Work starts long before the first anchor.

  • Design and documents. Working drawings of the rainscreen facade with the cladding layout, details and substructure calculation; the system's technical certificate and details manual; a method statement covering scaffolding or cradles and storage.
  • Substrate survey. Wall type, condition, voids and cracks. For weak substrates, a decision to fix to the floor slabs.
  • Anchor pull-out tests. Carried out on site in different areas of the wall; the results confirm the anchor type and bracket spacing.
  • Geodetic survey of the facade. Deviations of the wall from vertical and plane, positions of openings, corners and parapets are recorded. The survey sets the bracket projection and the installation layout is drawn from it.
  • Setting-out. Reference axes, levels and bracket positions are marked on the wall.

TECHNOFORMA starts with a site inspection and measurements: the cost of work is determined after inspecting the building, and the cladding layout is based on its actual geometry.

Work organisation is also decided at the preparation stage: how to work at height (scaffolding, facade cradles, lifts, rope access), where to store insulation and cladding under cover, how to lift large elements, and in what order to move through work zones. For multi-storey buildings the typical scheme is several zones across the elevations, each with its own crew: one sets brackets, another insulates, a third hangs cladding. This way adjacent operations do not get in each other's way, and concealed works are accepted before the next layer covers them.

Brackets

Brackets are the load-bearing elements that transfer the cladding load to the wall. They are installed to the setting-out, starting with the reference rows.

  • Holes are drilled for the anchor specified in the design, observing depth and edge distance.
  • A thermal break pad is placed under the bracket foot to reduce the thermal bridge.
  • Load-bearing brackets that carry the cladding weight go where the design says, often at floor slabs; restraint brackets take wind load.
  • Bracket projection is chosen from the survey so that the facade plane is true once the rails are fitted.

After the brackets are installed, technical supervision checks anchor type, spacing and fixing and signs a concealed works record — the brackets will then be partly covered by insulation.

Substructures differ by material: aluminium (light and corrosion-resistant but with high thermal expansion, hence sliding connections), galvanised steel with a polymer coating (stronger and cheaper, but the protective coating at cut edges must be checked) and stainless steel (high strength and fire resistance, used on tall and critical buildings, more expensive). Different metals must not be mixed in one connection without isolating pads, as this causes galvanic corrosion. The choice depends on cladding weight, building height, fire safety requirements and the specific system covered by the technical certificate and details manual.

Insulation and membrane

Insulation is mineral wool boards approved for rainscreen facades. They are laid from the bottom up with staggered joints, tight to the wall and to each other, cut around the brackets.

  • With two-layer insulation, the joints of the layers are offset.
  • Each board is fixed with insulation dowels in the number and pattern given in the design.
  • The wind barrier membrane, if the system includes one, is laid with overlaps and fixed with the same dowels.
  • Insulation is not left exposed to rain: wet mineral wool loses its thermal performance and dries slowly.

Fire breaks are installed at the same time around openings and at the levels shown in the design. They are also accepted with a concealed works record.

Insulation thickness and number of layers come from the thermal calculation, not crew habit: a shortfall of 20–30 mm over a large area noticeably reduces thermal protection, while extra thickness increases bracket projection and anchor load. If the thickness changes during installation, the substructure design is recalculated.

Rails

Rails are vertical (sometimes horizontal) profiles on which the cladding is hung. They are mounted on the brackets and brought into one plane using stringlines or a laser level.

  • A rail is fixed rigidly to the load-bearing bracket and to restraint brackets through sliding slots so the profile can expand when heated.
  • Rail joints along the height have a thermal gap; a cladding element must not bridge this joint.
  • Rail spacing follows the cladding layout.
  • Fasteners are rivets or bolts made of materials compatible with the profile.

The rail plane is the last chance to straighten the facade. Once cladding is hung it cannot be corrected, so an as-built survey of the rails is done before cladding starts and deviations are removed by adjusting the connections.

For heavy cladding — natural stone and large GFRC elements — the substructure is strengthened: bracket spacing is reduced, heavier profiles are used and more load-bearing points are placed at floor slabs. The substructure type must not be changed on site without recalculation: swapping a profile or bracket for an "equivalent" without the designer's approval is one of the most common causes of supervision remarks.

Cladding

Cladding is hung from the bottom up, to the layout, with each element secured by clips, runners, anchors or cast-in fixings depending on the material.

  • Porcelain stoneware and fibre cement — standard-format slabs; installation is quick.
  • Natural stone — heavy slabs, numbered layout, control of shade variation.
  • GFRC — elements to a bespoke layout with factory cast-in fixings; large elements are lifted mechanically.

Reveals, sills, parapet cappings and window junctions are installed in parallel. At TECHNOFORMA the substructure, insulation and cladding are installed by its own installation teams — over 100 specialists — so elements from its own production go onto a substructure designed within the same team.

Cladding work is limited by weather: in strong wind, hanging large elements from cradles and cranes stops; in rain, insulation is not closed up without protection; sealants and adhesives are used only within the manufacturer's temperature range. In winter, installing the substructure and dry-fixed cladding is possible but slower, and wet processes at junctions are postponed or carried out in heated enclosures.

Acceptance

Accepting a rainscreen facade is not an inspection of the finished facade but the sum of acceptances at every stage.

  • concealed works records: bracket anchoring, insulation and dowels, membrane, fire breaks, rails;
  • anchor test reports;
  • as-built geodetic survey;
  • data sheets and certificates for materials and products, system documents;
  • cladding check: plane, width and evenness of joints, fixing of every element, no chips, clean surface.

Snags are recorded and fixed before the final certificate is signed. Good practice is selective removal of a cladding element in a disputed area for inspection.

What turnkey rainscreen installation usually includes and what drives cost. Scope: design and layout, survey and anchor tests, geodesy, supply of substructure and insulation, installation of all layers, reveals, sills, parapets, junctions, as-built documentation. Cost is affected by:

  • cladding type and weight — stone and three-dimensional elements cost more to install than porcelain stoneware;
  • building height and access method — scaffolding, cradles, lifts;
  • wall condition and material — weak substrates require fixing to floor slabs and more anchors;
  • architectural complexity — number of openings, corners, projections, curved areas;
  • insulation thickness from the thermal calculation;
  • season and schedule — winter work and tight programmes increase costs.

For these reasons an "average market" price per square metre says little about a specific building: at TECHNOFORMA the cost of construction and installation work is determined after inspecting the site.

Stages of ventilated facade installation over time: what takes longest

Time is distributed unevenly across the stages: hanging the cladding, which is commonly regarded as the main work, usually takes less than half of the total duration. The remainder goes on preparation, the subframe, insulation and details.

StageApproximate share of the programmeWhat it depends on
Preparation, surveying, anchor tests5–10%Condition of the walls, readiness of working documentation
Setting out and brackets15–20%Substrate material, fixing spacing, building height
Insulation and membrane15–20%Number of layers, weather, number of openings
Rails10–15%Wall irregularities, complexity of the layout
Cladding30–40%Format and mass of elements, share of cutting
Details, junctions, handover10–15%Number of windows, corners, parapets

The shares are indicative and given for guidance: on a facade with a dense window grid the contribution of details and reveals grows noticeably, while on blank planes the cladding proceeds quickly and the overall duration is determined by the subframe. In addition, the stages do not follow one another strictly. On a project with several work sections, brackets are fixed on one elevation while slabs are already being hung on another.

The calendar duration is also affected by what happens off site: manufacture of the cladding, delivery of profiles, approval of changes. How to put together a realistic programme that takes production and weather into account is discussed in the article on facade installation times.

When planning, a reserve for weather and for dealing with the technical supervisor's comments is added to the calculated duration. For the autumn and winter period it is larger: short daylight hours reduce output, and days with strong wind or precipitation drop out of the programme entirely. The time for erecting and repositioning scaffolding or hoists is shown as a separate line — on a tall building this work is comparable in duration to one of the main stages.

How the work is organised: sections, teams and flow

Installation is carried out by the flow-line method: the facade is divided into work sections and the crew into teams, each of which performs its own operation and moves on to the next section. In this way the stages follow one another with a minimal gap, and exposed insulation does not stand in the rain for weeks.

A section is usually defined vertically — from corner to corner or between movement joints, to the height of a scaffold lift or the travel of a hoist. A typical set of teams:

  • setting-out operatives with a surveyor — transfer axes, levels and fixing points;
  • the subframe team — drills, installs anchors and brackets, and later fits the rails;
  • the insulation team — lays the slabs, fixes dowels, covers with the membrane;
  • the cladding team — hangs slabs or cassettes and sets the joints;
  • sheet-metal workers — make reveals, sills, parapets and junctions.

The order of sections is chosen so that elevations facing landscaping and entrance areas are closed earlier and release the work front to adjacent contractors. The means of access for each section are determined in advance; what to use at a particular height and building configuration is described in the article on scaffolding and cradles.

The size of the teams is selected so that their output is comparable. If the subframe gets far ahead, brackets and insulation stand exposed for a long time; if it lags behind, the cladding team is idle. The balance is checked weekly against the square metres actually closed at each stage, and people are redistributed if necessary. On large projects this is the task of a dedicated site manager rather than a foreman.

Handover of a section between teams is accompanied by interim acceptance. The foreman and a representative of the technical supervisor inspect the completed operation before the next layer covers it, and the same inspection becomes the basis for the concealed works certificate. If this procedure is not maintained, defects in anchoring and insulation are discovered only under the cladding.

Incoming inspection and storage of materials on site

Every batch of materials is checked before it reaches the scaffolding: against documents, by appearance and for compliance with the design. This step is rarely singled out as a separate stage, but a considerable share of complaints about a finished facade begin with an unchecked delivery.

What is checked on receipt:

  • Anchors and fixings — grade, diameter, length, material and coating; they must match those that passed testing on site.
  • Brackets and profiles — metal thickness, type of coating or alloy grade, geometry, presence of thermal break pads.
  • Insulation — grade, density, thickness, integrity of packaging; wet slabs are not used.
  • Membrane — compliance with the type stated in the design, including combustibility.
  • Cladding — dimensions, shade and calibre of the batch, absence of chips and cracks, marking of elements according to the layout.

Storage is no less important than acceptance. Insulation is kept under cover on pallets, profiles on spacers without contact with soil or mortar, cladding in factory packaging, upright or in the position prescribed by the manufacturer. Slabs from different batches are not mixed: a difference in shade becomes visible only on the wall, when correcting it is already expensive.

For elements made to the design, the order of delivery to a section is set by the marking scheme. Elements are brought in sets for a specific area of the facade, so that the site does not turn into a store of hundreds of items in which finding the right part takes longer than fixing it.

The results of incoming inspection are recorded in a log, and copies of data sheets, certificates and test reports are attached to the as-built documentation. Substituting a material with an equivalent is permitted only with the agreement of the designer and the client: even an anchor or insulation with similar characteristics may not be part of the system for which the technical approval was issued. An unapproved substitution is one of the most frequent grounds for refusing acceptance.

Which deviations are checked at each stage and how are they measured?

Geometry is controlled with instruments at every stage, and the permissible values are taken from the design and the technical documentation for the system. Measurements after the cladding is hung show only the result; it can be corrected only at the level of brackets and rails.

StageWhat is checkedInstrument
Geodetic surveyActual deviations of the wall from vertical and from planeTotal station, laser scanner or level
AnchoringHole diameter and depth, edge distance, tightening torqueTemplate, depth gauge, torque wrench
BracketsSpacing, vertical alignment, projectionLaser plane generator, tape measure
InsulationTightness of fit, gaps between slabs, number of dowelsVisual check, feeler gauge, count on a control area
RailsVerticality, plane, thermal gaps at jointsStraightedge, level, plane generator
CladdingJoint width and pattern, steps between elements, air gapJoint template, feeler gauge, rule

Anchor pull-out force is checked separately, with a dedicated device and to an approved programme; the procedure is described in the article on anchor pull-out tests. The results of all measurements are entered in the works log and the as-built drawings. In a disputed situation it is these records that show at which stage a deviation arose and who is responsible for it.

If a measurement reveals a deviation beyond tolerance, work on that area is stopped pending the designer's decision. Typical measures are repositioning a bracket, using an extension piece, adding a fixing point or changing the layout in that area. The plane must not be corrected with packers under the cladding or by bending the clips: such methods alter the design behaviour of the fixing and in time lead to loosening of the elements.

Details that are built separately from the standard plane

The standard plane is installed quickly and uniformly, while most of the attention and a considerable share of the labour go on details. They should be shown as separate lines in the programme, otherwise the handover date will be understated.

  • Window and door openings. Surrounds, reveals, sills and fire boxes are fitted before the cladding is hung around the opening; the insulation and membrane terminate on them.
  • External and internal corners. These require additional brackets and rails, and the cladding layout starts precisely from the corner.
  • Plinth. The lower air inlet to the cavity is closed with a perforated profile; the cladding is raised above the paving and protected from splashes.
  • Parapet. The upper air outlet, a coping with a drip edge and a fall towards the roof, the junction with the waterproofing.
  • Building movement joints. The subframe and cladding are interrupted along the joint line; the joint must not be rigidly bridged with a profile.
  • Penetrations and surface-mounted equipment. Air-conditioner brackets, luminaires, signs and downpipes are fixed to the wall or to reinforced subframe elements, but not to the cladding.

Design options for these locations are collected in the article on ventilated facade junction details. From the installation point of view there is one main requirement: every detail must be drawn in the working documentation with dimensions and a schedule of trim elements. A detail invented on the scaffolding almost always solves the problem of appearance and misses the problem of water drainage.

Fire protection elements deserve particular attention — barriers in the air cavity and steel surrounds to openings. Their location and construction are set by the system and the design, and they are fitted at the same time as the insulation, before the rails or together with them. A missed barrier cannot be installed after the cladding is hung without dismantling the area, so its presence is checked at interim acceptance and recorded in the certificate.

How the stages differ with heavy cladding — stone and GFRC

The sequence of work remains the same, but the content of the preparation and the way elements are delivered to the facade change. Heavy and three-dimensional cladding moves part of the installation decisions to the design and manufacturing stage.

The main differences:

  1. Detailed documentation for the elements. A drawing with embedded parts and fixing points is produced for each element, and a marking scheme for the facade. TECHNOFORMA carries out this work as part of facade design.
  2. Reinforced subframe. Brackets and rails are selected by calculation for the mass of the cladding; for large elements part of the load is transferred to the floor slabs.
  3. Installation samples. Before mass hanging, a control fragment is assembled on the wall, on which colour, texture, joints and details are agreed.
  4. Lifting and rigging. Elements are raised by crane, winch or hoist using spreader beams and lifting loops; manual delivery via scaffolding is acceptable only for small parts.
  5. Adjustment in three planes. The fixing details allow the element to be set in height, projection and level after hanging.
  6. Joint treatment. Joints are left open or filled with sealant as designed; for decorative elements, matching of the profile at the joints is important.

Large GFRC facade panels require fewer fixing points for the same area than a small-format slab, so installation is faster, but the cost of a setting-out error is higher. For stone, preparation of the slabs is critical: kerfs and holes for fixings are made in production, and only the cutting provided for in the design is permitted on site.

The logistics of heavy elements are planned together with the installation programme. Elements are dispatched from production in the sequence in which they will be hung and, where possible, are lifted from the lorry straight onto the facade, bypassing intermediate storage. This reduces the number of handling operations, during which chipped edges and corners most often occur, and removes the question of a storage area on a constrained site.

What must be ready on site before ventilated facade installation begins

Facade works begin at a certain level of construction readiness of the building, and its absence is a frequent cause of downtime and rework. Before the contractor moves in, the client and the general contractor should go through a short list.

  • The load-bearing and enclosing structures are erected, the masonry has gained strength, and the wall geometry is recorded by an as-built survey.
  • Window and door units are installed, or the procedure for working alongside the window crew and the junction detail are agreed.
  • The roof or temporary waterproofing of the parapets is in place, and water from the roof does not reach the wall.
  • Embedded parts and outlets for surface-mounted equipment, lighting, signs and downpipes are installed or marked in the design.
  • Internal wet processes are largely complete, so that the walls do not release excess moisture into the insulation.
  • A storage area, a working zone for the crane or hoist and power connection points are allocated.
  • The working documentation has been issued for construction, and the method statement is approved.
  • Those responsible for technical supervision and the procedure for inspecting concealed works are appointed.

Handover of the work front is formalised by a certificate to which the as-built survey of the walls is attached. From that moment it is clear which substrate deviations the contractor has accepted and allowed for in the design, and which must be rectified before installation begins. The same approach is applied when finished areas of the facade are handed over to adjacent trades — for paving, roof junctions or external lighting.

If some of the conditions are not met, installation is possible section by section, but this decision is recorded in the programme. A full-cycle contractor relieves the client of coordination between the designer, the factory and the installers; how this is arranged in the company is described on the page installation of ventilated facades.

FAQ

What is the installation technology for a ventilated facade?

Preparation and survey, anchor tests, bracket installation, insulation and wind barrier, fire breaks, rails, cladding, acceptance. Each stage of concealed works is accepted with a signed record.

What is a method statement for a ventilated facade?

A document describing the sequence of operations, materials, equipment, and quality and safety requirements for a specific system. It is prepared as part of the work execution plan.

How do rainscreen systems from different manufacturers, such as TechnoNICOL, differ?

In the composition of the substructure, insulation, membranes and details, confirmed by a technical certificate. Installation must follow the details manual of the chosen system strictly.

Is the method different for a metal cassette rainscreen facade?

The stages are the same; hanging the cladding differs: cassettes are hung on runners or fixed through the return, with allowance for thermal expansion.

Who accepts a ventilated facade?

The client's technical supervision and the general contractor — on the basis of concealed works records, test reports, the as-built survey and inspection of the cladding.

Can a ventilated facade be installed in winter?

Yes, the substructure and mechanically fixed cladding can be installed in winter, but more slowly: wind, icing and protecting insulation from precipitation are taken into account, and sealants and adhesives are used only within the manufacturer's temperature range.

Which stage of ventilated facade installation is most often carried out with errors?

Anchoring of brackets and insulation. Both stages are covered by subsequent layers, so breaches of drilling depth, anchor type or number of dowels, or gaps between slabs, are discovered late. The safeguard is interim acceptance of each section with photographic records.

Can stages be run in parallel to shorten the programme?

Yes, with flow-line organisation the stages overlap: brackets are fixed on one section while cladding is hung on another. The sequence on a single area must not be broken — for example, rails must not be fitted before the insulation has been accepted.

Is a control area of the facade needed before the main works?

It is advisable, and for stone and GFRC cladding and non-standard systems it is effectively mandatory. Details, joints, colour and ease of installation are checked on the fragment, after which the documentation is adjusted and the elements go into series production.
