Ventilated Facade Insulation: Materials and Thickness

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

Insulation for a ventilated facade system uses non-combustible mineral wool boards fixed to the wall with insulation dowels and, where necessary, covered with a wind barrier membrane. A ventilated gap is left above the insulation through which moisture escapes, so the board stays dry and keeps its thermal performance.

The key selection criterion is the density and intended use of the board (for rainscreen facades, not for render facades or roofs) and a thickness confirmed by thermal calculation for the specific region and wall. This article is useful for designers, clients and technical supervisors of multi-storey and public buildings, and for private homeowners choosing an insulated ventilated facade.

The general logic of the systems is in the Ventilated rainscreen facades section.

Mineral wool

Rainscreen facades use mineral wool boards based on stone (basalt) wool. Glass wool is used less often and only in systems that provide for it.

Values depend on manufacturer and brand; exact figures are in the documents for the specific product. Insulation for a ventilated facade must be marked as suitable for rainscreen use and come from the list in the system's technical certificate: substituting a similar board of another brand breaks the system's conditions of use.

A two-layer scheme — a dense outer layer over a lighter inner one — saves money and fills wall irregularities better, but requires offset joints and increases labour.

Calculating thickness

Facade insulation thickness is determined by thermal calculation according to the requirements for thermal performance of buildings (Russian code of practice SP 50.13330). The calculation includes:

  • climate zone and heating degree-days;
  • building use and the required thermal resistance;
  • material and thickness of the load-bearing wall;
  • thermal bridges — brackets, dowels, reveals. Each bracket is a thermal bridge, so the actual thermal resistance of a wall with a rainscreen facade is lower than the insulation thickness alone suggests.

In practice, residential buildings in central Russia end up with around 100–150 mm, more in northern regions and less in southern ones. This is a guideline, not a standard: thickness may vary on the same building depending on the wall.

The total depth of a ventilated facade — from the wall to the face of the cladding — is the sum of insulation, ventilation gap (usually at least 40 mm, the exact value per design and system album), substructure depth and cladding thickness. For GFRC and stone the depth of the fixing connections is added. This parameter affects reveals, window junctions and facade alignment.

Fixing with dowels

Each insulation board is fixed with insulation dowels — plastic or with a metal expansion pin. The number and pattern are set by the design and the system's album of technical solutions.

  • A typical pattern is at least 2 dowels per board for the inner layer and more for the outer one; more at building corners and in zones of increased wind load.
  • Dowel length is the insulation thickness plus the anchoring depth in the substrate, which depends on the wall material.
  • The dowel plate must sit flush with the board without crushing it.
  • Dowels with a metal pin are thermal bridges; models with a thermal cap are used.

In hollow and lightweight substrates, dowels with an extended expansion zone are used; the type is confirmed by trial pull-out tests.

Wind barrier

The wind barrier protects the insulation from fibre erosion and from being cooled by the airflow in the ventilation gap. It is a vapour-permeable membrane that lets vapour out of the insulation but keeps out wind and water droplets.

  • The membrane is laid over the insulation with overlaps and fixed with the insulation dowels.
  • Some systems do not use it: they use faced boards or dense water-repellent boards that can work without a membrane. This must be reflected in the system documents.
  • The membrane's combustibility affects the fire performance of the system.

The decision "with or without a membrane" is made not on site but when the system is chosen. If a membrane is specified, it cannot be omitted to save money; if the system allows working without it, it is important that the insulation is exactly the brand for which this is confirmed. On tall buildings, where air speed in the gap is higher, wind barrier requirements are usually stricter.

A separate issue is protecting the insulation during construction. If cladding installation is delayed, exposed mineral wool loses its properties in rain and wind, and the membrane degrades under UV. So insulating in sections that are immediately covered with cladding is not a formality but a condition of durability.

Mistakes

Typical insulation mistakes that are discovered only after cladding:

  • gaps between boards and around brackets — the gaps act as thermal bridges;
  • too few dowels — boards pull away from the wall and air circulates between them and the substrate;
  • wet insulation left behind the cladding;
  • insulation of the wrong brand or density;
  • a blocked ventilation gap — cladding or rails press on the insulation and moisture cannot escape;
  • no fire barriers around openings;
  • holes through the membrane not taped after bracket installation.

All these defects are concealed, which is why insulation is accepted with a signed record before the rails are installed. Thermal imaging during the heating season helps to detect them later: thermal bridges and areas with detached boards show up as zones of higher outer surface temperature. But repair on a finished facade means removing the cladding, so it is cheaper to check each section before it is closed.

FAQ

Can a ventilated facade be built without insulation?

Yes, on unheated buildings or if the wall is already insulated. The substructure and cladding remain; the bracket offset changes.

What insulation is needed for a ventilated facade?

Non-combustible stone wool boards intended for rainscreen facades and listed in the documents for the chosen system.

How thick should facade insulation be?

As per thermal calculation for the region and wall; for residential buildings in central Russia the guideline is 100–150 mm.

Is permission needed to insulate the facade of an apartment building?

Under Russian rules the facade is common property of the owners, so a decision of the general meeting and approval from the management company are needed; in some cities, approval of the architectural appearance as well. Check the procedure with the local administration.

How much does facade insulation cost?

It depends on the area, insulation thickness and brand, facade access method and scope of work. TECHNOFORMA determines the cost after a site inspection.
