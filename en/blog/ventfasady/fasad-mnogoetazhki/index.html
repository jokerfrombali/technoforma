Facades for Multi-Storey Residential Buildings: Requirements and Solutions

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

The facade of a multi-storey building is its external envelope: insulation, fixing system and cladding that must simultaneously withstand wind loads at height, meet fire safety requirements and keep their appearance for decades. Multi-storey buildings most often use ventilated rainscreen facades, less often render systems.

The main selection criterion is not appearance alone but a combination of three conditions: non-combustibility and fire classification of the system for the given height, reliable fixing designed for wind, and access for maintenance. The architectural image is set by the cladding, whether porcelain stoneware, glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) or stone, within these constraints.

This article is for developers, architects and property management companies. For an overview of systems, see Ventilated rainscreen facades.

Codes for tall buildings

Legally, the facade of an apartment building in Russia is an enclosing load-bearing and non-load-bearing structure that forms part of the owners' common property. This means any change to its appearance, from insulation to replacing the cladding, is decided at a general meeting of owners and approved by the city's architecture authorities under local rules.

From a design point of view, the facade of a tall residential building must meet several groups of requirements:

  • Fire safety. The current Russian fire safety codes (SP) restrict combustible materials in external walls and require the rainscreen system to have a confirmed fire hazard class; requirements are stricter for high-rise buildings, and fire barriers in the air gap are also regulated.
  • Loads. The substructure and fixings are designed for wind load, which increases with height, the self-weight of the cladding and ice loads.
  • Thermal protection. Insulation thickness is determined by a thermal calculation for the local climate.
  • Safety in use. Cladding elements must not fall: secondary safety fixings are designed and thermal movement is taken into account.

In the design documentation the facade is shown on elevation drawings (orthogonal views with levels, materials and colour), sections and details: junctions with windows, parapet and plinth. For a rainscreen facade, a cladding layout and a substructure scheme are also issued.

Decorative elements at height

Decorative elements on a multi-storey building (cornices, string courses, window surrounds, pilasters, bay window surrounds) work in harsher conditions than on a low-rise building: stronger wind, more serious consequences if a fragment falls, and harder access for repairs.

  • Ornament is made from light and strong materials; glass fibre reinforced concrete is suitable thanks to its thin-wall design and glass fibre reinforcement.
  • Each element is fixed to the load-bearing structure through embedded plates and brackets, not only to the cladding.
  • Horizontal projections are designed with a slope and a drip so that water does not collect or run down the wall.
  • The scale of ornament is chosen with height in mind: fine profiling on the 15th floor cannot be read from the ground.

TECHNOFORMA designs the ornament, makes models and moulds in its own mould-making workshops and installs the elements with its own crew, so fixing details are coordinated with the substructure at the design stage.

Maintenance

The facade of an apartment building is maintained by the management company or homeowners' association, funded by the owners or as part of a major repair programme. The maintenance schedule for rainscreen systems usually includes:

  • periodic visual inspection of cladding and ornament, including after strong winds;
  • checking fixings and the condition of the substructure in accessible areas;
  • cleaning drainage elements, sills and parapet cappings;
  • replacing damaged cladding elements, which on a rainscreen facade is done individually.

Work at height uses facade cradles, lifts or industrial rope access. If the design provides anchor points for cradles on the roof from the start, maintenance is cheaper and safer.

The management company should obtain as-built documentation from the contractor: the cladding layout with element marking, substructure drawings and material data sheets. With these, ordering and replacing a damaged panel several years later takes days, not months.

Typical solutions

Typical solutions for multi-storey residential buildings in current practice:

  • Classical look: GFRC with a stylised order, a rusticated plinth, pilasters and a strong crowning cornice. Suits club houses and premium residential complexes.
  • Combined facade: porcelain stoneware on typical floors, GFRC on the plinth, entrances and top tier.
  • Contemporary minimalism: large-format porcelain stoneware or fibre cement panels, lighting, vertical accents.
  • Stone on the lower floors: granite on the plinth and entrances, where mechanical loads are higher.

The solution is set in the architectural concept and then refined in the working documentation with the cladding layout. The earlier the cladding manufacturer is involved, the fewer changes are needed at the installation stage.

FAQ

Is the facade common property of an apartment building?

Yes. In Russia, enclosing structures including the facade belong to the common property of the owners. Changes to its appearance are decided at a general meeting and approved under city rules.

Who is responsible for insulating the facade of an apartment building?

The owners decide at a general meeting; the work is funded by them, including through major repair programmes, and carried out to a design. Insulating a single flat from the outside without approval is not allowed.

What is the best facade for a multi-storey building?

Most often a ventilated rainscreen facade with non-combustible cladding: porcelain stoneware, GFRC or stone as part of a system with a confirmed fire hazard class.

What does a facade drawing of a multi-storey building show?

Orthogonal views of the walls with levels, openings, materials and cladding colour. For a rainscreen facade, a panel layout, substructure scheme and junction details are issued separately.

Can an antenna or air conditioner be mounted on the facade without approval?

The facade is common property, so placing equipment usually requires the owners' consent and compliance with the city's rules.
