Porcelain Stoneware Ventilated Facade

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

Porcelain stoneware cladding in a rainscreen system means fixing porcelain tiles to a metal substructure with an air gap over the insulation. Porcelain stoneware is dense fired ceramic with very low water absorption, so it resists frost and precipitation, and a flat tile of standard format makes installation predictable.

The key selection criterion is the combination of format, tile thickness and fixing method: it determines the look of the facade and the weight and cost of the substructure. Porcelain stoneware is chosen for residential complexes, office and public buildings that need a flat, maintainable facade without complex modelling, and also for plinths and individual planes combined with GFRC or stone.

General principles of rainscreen systems are in the Ventilated rainscreen facades section.

Properties of porcelain stoneware

Porcelain stoneware is made by pressing a mixture of clays, feldspars and quartz, followed by high-temperature firing. The result is a sintered material with no open porosity. For a facade this means several practical properties.

PropertyWhat it means for a rainscreen facade
Water absorption — fractions of a percentHigh frost resistance, no efflorescence or darkening from moisture
Non-combustible materialCladding does not support combustion; requirements for the system as a whole still apply
Brittle under impact and bendingCorrect clips, pads and a format limited by calculation are needed
Stable geometryEven joints, simple layout, quick tile replacement
Wide choice of finishesStone, concrete and wood effects, plain surfaces

Special facade series are produced, including travertine-, marble- and slate-effect porcelain stoneware. Sizes and patterns vary between manufacturers, so the collection is chosen together with the layout. Exact properties — water absorption, flexural strength, frost resistance — are taken from the passport of the specific series.

The installation sequence for a porcelain stoneware ventilated facade is standard: setting-out and survey, bracket installation, insulation with dowels, wind barrier if required, rails, cladding. Components — brackets, rails, clips, rivets or screws, paronite pads — must belong to one system with supporting documents. A ventilated porcelain stoneware plinth is also possible, usually with reinforced fixing and protection against mechanical damage.

Ventilated facades clad with porcelain stoneware without an insulation layer are used on unheated buildings, when re-cladding over an already insulated wall, and on individual areas. Cost estimates separate such work into "with insulation" and "without insulation" items; under the Russian GESN estimating standards, adhesive-fixed ceramic tile cladding and a porcelain stoneware rainscreen facade are priced under different rates. Adhesive-fixed porcelain stoneware cladding is possible on low-rise private houses but is practically not used in multi-storey construction.

Visible and concealed fixing

Porcelain stoneware tiles are fixed in two main ways.

  • Visible fixing. The tile rests on clips — steel holders with tabs that grip the edges of adjacent tiles. The tabs are visible in the horizontal joints and are often painted to match the cladding. This is the most common and affordable option, and a tile is easy to replace.
  • Concealed fixing. Holes for expansion anchors are drilled in the back of the tile, or a kerf is cut in the edge; brackets fixed to the tile hook onto horizontal profiles. Another option is adhesive fixing to the rails using special facade adhesive systems. The face is free of metal, but requirements for accuracy and for confirmation of the system by testing are higher.

The concealed method is more expensive, needs thicker tiles and a more precise substructure, but gives clean facades in architecture with large formats. The visible method is easier to maintain. What matters is that the chosen option is described in the system's album of technical solutions rather than improvised on site.

Tile format

Tile format affects appearance, layout and substructure design.

  • 600×600, 600×1200 mm — the most common formats for rainscreen facades, usually 10–12 mm thick. They fit easily to a standard rail spacing.
  • Large formats (1200×1200, 1200×2400 mm and larger) — impressive, but heavier, harder to install at height, require concealed fixing and additional wind calculation.
  • Narrow tiles and slats — for linear architecture, often laid horizontally.

The layout starts by aligning joints with openings and corners: cutting tiles around windows increases waste and the number of details. Wind pressure also matters for format: on upper floors and at building corners, the permissible tile size for the same fixing is smaller.

Substructure

The substructure for porcelain stoneware is one of the most mature in the industry. It consists of load-bearing and support brackets, vertical rails (T- and L-profiles), clips or concealed-fixing profiles, fasteners and pads.

  • Material — aluminium alloys, galvanised or stainless steel; the choice depends on building height, fire safety requirements and budget.
  • Rail spacing is set by tile format: each tile must bear on at least two rails.
  • Bracket spacing and anchor type are calculated for weight and wind, with anchor pull-out tests in the substrate.
  • Rails are connected to brackets via sliding connections to accommodate thermal expansion.

Cost

Publicly listed prices are usually per square metre "turnkey", but the real estimate is made up of several items, and their proportions vary greatly from project to project.

  • porcelain stoneware: collection, format, thickness, cutting waste;
  • substructure: material, offset, bracket spacing, fixing type;
  • insulation, membrane, dowels;
  • labour: height, number of storeys, facade complexity, number of openings and corners;
  • scaffolding or suspended cradles, design, anchor tests.

At TECHNOFORMA, the cost is determined after a site inspection and development of the design solution. A ready-made "per metre" price without input data does not reflect real costs.

FAQ

What is ventilated facade cladding with porcelain stoneware panels?

It is a rainscreen system: porcelain stoneware tiles are fixed with clips or concealed fixings to a metal substructure, with a ventilated gap between them and the insulation.

Which porcelain stoneware is suitable for a ventilated facade?

Facade series, usually from 10 mm thick, with low water absorption and proven frost resistance. Properties are taken from the passport of the specific series.

Can a porcelain stoneware ventilated facade be built without insulation?

Yes, for unheated buildings or over an already insulated wall. The substructure and cladding stay the same; the bracket offset changes.

How does visible fixing of porcelain stoneware differ from concealed?

With visible fixing the clips show in the joints, and installation is simpler and cheaper. With concealed fixing the face has no metal, but thicker tiles and a precise substructure are needed.

How much does porcelain stoneware rainscreen cladding cost?

The cost depends on the collection, format, substructure, height and facade complexity. At TECHNOFORMA it is determined after a site inspection.
