Hotel Facades: Balancing Image and Operation

A hotel facade has to sell rooms and keep working without interruption: guests see the building in booking photos before they walk into the lobby, and the owner cannot close the hotel to repair the cladding. That is why hotel construction almost always uses ventilated rainscreen facades with durable cladding: glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC), natural stone or porcelain stoneware.

The main criterion is how well the cladding keeps its image with minimal maintenance in a given climate. A seaside resort hotel, a city hotel and a mountain complex each require different choices of materials and protection.

This article is for hotel developers, architects and operators. General information on the systems is in Ventilated rainscreen facades.

Image and brand

For a hotel, the facade is part of the product. It sets guest expectations and affects how the category is perceived. The architectural image is usually built from the brand concept or the location:

  • Classical or palatial style: orders, columns, balustrades, cornices, arched windows. Typical of resort and prestige hotels.
  • Regional style: motifs of local architecture such as ornament, carving, characteristic arches and stone colour.
  • Contemporary style: large planes, rhythmic fins, panoramic glazing.

It is important that the image can be produced in series: a hotel has many identical windows, loggias and balconies, and each repeated element is more economical to make from one mould. This is exactly where glass fibre reinforced concrete is convenient: one mould yields dozens of identical pieces.

The image must also work at scale. Hotel complexes often consist of several buildings, and their facades need to be designed as an ensemble: common cornice lines, a shared window rhythm, repeated details, with emphasis on the main building and entrance. Cladding colour is chosen for the local light: under the southern sun, light warm shades look different from how they look in central Russia.

Material characteristics depend on the mix and the quarry; exact values are stated in the product data sheet and test reports.

For hotels, as for other public buildings, the rainscreen system must comply with the current Russian fire safety codes (SP), and the cladding must be non-combustible. Details around guest room windows and fire barriers in the ventilated cavity are checked separately. Noise protection should also be addressed early: on busy streets and near roads, cladding and insulation work together with the windows.

Ornament

Ornament on a hotel facade works for photographs and for the close-up impression: guests walk past the entrance columns and look at the balustrade from their balcony.

  • Entrance: a portico with columns, a canopy, a large pediment or arch; GFRC and natural stone are often combined here.
  • Balconies and loggias: balusters, handrails, brackets, all serial products from one mould.
  • Window surrounds: architraves, pediments, keystones.
  • Crowning elements: cornices, parapet urns, attics.

TECHNOFORMA makes models and moulds in its own mould-making workshops, so ornament can be developed for a specific project rather than chosen from a catalogue. The colour and texture of GFRC are agreed using samples: the product can imitate limestone or travertine, or have a smooth surface for painting.

Climate

Climate determines which solutions will stand up to use:

  • Seaside: salty air and wind accelerate corrosion; the substructure needs materials and coatings with appropriate protection, and the cladding needs dense materials with low water absorption.
  • Mountains and continental climate: many freeze-thaw cycles. The key property is the frost resistance of the cladding; for GFRC it is high and further improved with additives, for natural stone it depends on the stone type.
  • Hot climate: heating of dark surfaces and thermal movement; expansion joints and light tones on sunny elevations are needed.

Horizontal decorative elements are designed with slopes and drips so that water does not stand on cornices or leave streaks.

Another climatic factor is wind. Coastal and mountain hotels often stand on exposed sites where wind loads are higher than in urban areas. The substructure and fixings of large decorative elements are designed for the specific site, not from standard tables.

Maintenance

A hotel operates all year round, so facade maintenance is planned so as not to disturb guests:

  • regular cleaning and inspection, preferably in the low season, with cradle anchors provided on the roof;
  • water-repellent treatment of porous stone and concrete surfaces so that dirt and salts are absorbed less;
  • a reserve of cladding and ornament from the same batch, or retained moulds for recasting;
  • as-built documentation: layout, details, material data sheets.

A rainscreen facade has the advantage that a damaged element is replaced individually without closing the rooms beneath. Facade work above entrances and terraces is carried out at times of lowest occupancy and with the danger zone fenced off.

The operating budget should be considered when choosing materials. Cladding that needs repainting every few years costs a hotel more than it seems: besides the work itself, there is scaffolding in front of guest room windows, noise and lower guest ratings. Durable cladding on a rainscreen system costs more up front but is easier to maintain.

How a hotel facade affects quiet and comfort in the guest room

For the guest, the quality of the facade is measured not by the cladding material but by whether the street can be heard in the room and whether there is a draught from the window. The external wall of a hotel is therefore designed as a single system: the load-bearing substrate, insulation, cladding and the window unit with its junctions.

The main path for noise is the window and its installation joint, not the solid wall. A ventilated system with mineral wool insulation adds to the sound insulation of the wall, but the effect is lost if the sashes and glazing units are selected without regard to the noise load. For rooms facing a promenade, a main road or a restaurant terrace, the glazing specification is determined by an acoustic calculation. For more detail, see the article on sound insulation of a building facade.

  • Ventilation valves and grilles in the external wall are selected with sound attenuation.
  • Outdoor units of air-conditioning systems are moved to the roof or to plant recesses rather than placed under room windows.
  • The reveals are insulated around the full perimeter of the opening to rule out condensation and draughts.
  • The fixings of the cladding and sills must not cause knocking or drumming in wind and rain.

Hotel balconies, loggias and terraces: what to look out for

The balcony is the most heavily loaded and most conspicuous part of a hotel facade: the guest sees it at close range and touches it. The requirements for the quality of surfaces, joints and balustrading are higher here than on a solid wall.

ElementPurposeSolution
GuardingSafety and the view from the roomBalustrade, solid parapet or glass; the height and the load on the handrail are taken in accordance with the regulations
Balcony slabWater drainage, no stainingFall to the outside, drip along the edge, waterproofing beneath the finish
Edge and soffit of the slabA neat appearance from belowCladding with a band or with GFRC panels with cast-in fixings
Partitions between roomsPrivacyScreens of GFRC, stone or frosted glass
Floor finishA slip-resistant surfaceStone with a flamed or bush-hammered finish, terrace systems

For hotels in the classical style the standard solution is GFRC balustrades: they are lighter than stone ones, place less load on the slab cantilever and are supplied in series of identical balusters, pedestals and handrails. The fixing is designed for horizontal load, and the embedded parts are provided in the slab before concreting.

How can facade works be tied to the hotel opening date?

The hotel facade must be finished before the fit-out of the rooms and the landscaping begin, and the opening is usually tied to the season. A month’s slippage for a resort hotel means losing part of the year, so the facade programme is built backwards from the opening date.

  1. Approval of the concept and material samples.
  2. Working documentation and detailing of the elements.
  3. Making of models and moulds, ordering of stone — the items with the longest lead time.
  4. Series production and delivery in sets, section by section.
  5. Installation of the subframe and insulation as the shell becomes ready.
  6. Cladding, decorative elements, lighting, striking of the scaffolding.

Some stages run in parallel: the moulds are made while the frame is being built, and the cladding is cast while the subframe is being installed. What the overall duration depends on is examined in the article on facade installation times. In remote and mountain areas delivery is built into the programme: large panels are transported in racks, and the delivery window is limited by weather and road conditions.

Refurbishing the facade of an operating hotel

The appearance of a working hotel can be renewed without stopping the reception of guests if the work is divided into sections and carried out in the low season. A ventilated system is better suited to this than rendered ones: the processes are dry, with no long technological breaks.

  • Before starting, the walls are surveyed, anchors are tested and the balcony slabs are checked for their ability to carry the new load.
  • The block is divided into vertical sections; rooms within a section are taken off sale for the duration of the work.
  • Noisy operations — drilling, cutting — are carried out at agreed hours.
  • Entrances, terraces and walkways are protected by canopies and netting, and the scaffolding is covered with a banner showing the future facade.
  • Materials are delivered in small batches so as not to occupy the grounds.

The procedure for such work is described in the article on how facade refurbishment is carried out. When the brand or category changes, facade decoration makes it possible to alter the style of the building without interfering with the load-bearing structures.

Logistics on the grounds are planned separately. Vehicle access, storage and hoisting of materials are kept apart from guest routes and the operation of the restaurants, and the zone beneath the scaffolding is fenced off over its full height. Before each section starts, a calendar is agreed with the hotel manager: which rooms are closed, for how long, and when the facade in that part must be fully uncovered. This procedure allows the work to proceed predictably and without complaints from guests.

Seismic conditions and mountain terrain: requirements of resort regions

A considerable share of hotel construction in the south of Russia takes place in seismic areas — on the Black Sea coast, in Dagestan, Chechnya and the Caucasian Mineral Waters. For the facade this means additional checks of the fixings and limits on the mass of suspended elements.

Light thin-walled cladding has an advantage here: the lower the mass of an element, the lower the inertial force during ground motion. Large parts — cornices, columns, pediments — are designed for horizontal actions and divided by joints so that racking of the frame is not transferred to the cladding. The subject is covered in detail in the article on facades in seismic regions.

On slopes there are also changes in level and retaining walls, which are clad in the same stone as the plinth so that the complex reads as a single whole.

What mistakes are made when choosing a facade for a hotel?

Mistakes in hotel projects most often arise where architecture, operation and programme meet. After opening they have to be corrected in the presence of guests.

  • The decoration is designed without standardisation: dozens of similar but different profiles instead of a few serial ones.
  • Light-coloured porous cladding is used near the ground, by the car park and kitchen exits, where it soon becomes soiled.
  • Horizontal surfaces have no fall or drip — after one season streaks appear on the walls.
  • No access to the facade is provided from the side of the pool, the slope or the neighbouring plot.
  • Lighting is added at the last stage, without embedded parts and cable routes in the subframe.
  • No reserve of cladding and decorative elements from the same batch is ordered, and during repair the new element differs in shade.

Most of these decisions do not increase the budget if they are taken at design stage. For this reason a representative of the future facilities team is included in the project team from the outset: they know where the building will be washed, what it will be cleaned with and where equipment will be brought in from.

What to prepare for a hotel facade estimate

An outline design is sufficient for a preliminary assessment; an accurate estimate requires the full set. The earlier the contractor receives the initial data, the more accurate the budget.

  • Architectural drawings of the facades, sections, plans of a typical floor and the ground floor.
  • Information on the construction of the walls and floors, the region and the site.
  • Visualisations or references showing the desired style.
  • A list of decorative elements and their approximate dimensions.
  • Preferences for the types of stone for the plinth, entrance and terraces.
  • The opening date and the desired sequence of blocks.

From these data the TECHNOFORMA Group prepares a layout, proposals for standardising the decoration and a cost estimate, and on order carries out turnkey ventilated facade installation using its own installation division of more than 100 specialists. If there is no design yet, the work begins with a sketch: the company’s architects propose options for the modelling and materials to suit the category of the hotel and the climate of the site, after which the solution is refined on samples of texture and colour.

FAQ

What is the best cladding for a hotel facade?

GFRC or porcelain stoneware on a rainscreen system for the main cladding and ornament, hard natural stone for the plinth and entrance. The choice is refined according to the climate.

Can hotel ornament be made to a custom design?

Yes. TECHNOFORMA designs the elements and makes models and moulds in its own workshops, so ornament is produced for the specific project.

How does a hotel facade cope with a seaside climate?

It needs a corrosion-protected substructure and cladding with low water absorption. Specific solutions are defined in the design.

Does a hotel have to close for facade repairs?

With a rainscreen system, usually not: damaged elements are replaced individually from cradles or lifts.

When should facade works start in order to open a hotel for the season?

The programme is built backwards from the opening date. Moulds for the decorative elements and stone are ordered while the frame is going up, and installation of the subframe begins as the walls become ready. A ventilated facade can be installed all year round, but the scaffolding should preferably be struck before landscaping starts.

What balustrading is suitable for hotel balconies?

The choice depends on the style: GFRC or stone balustrades are used for classical facades, glass or solid parapets for contemporary ones. In every case the guarding is designed for horizontal load, and the height is taken in accordance with the current regulations.

Can the style of a hotel facade be changed during rebranding?

Yes. Ventilated cladding and decorative elements are fixed to the existing walls after they have been surveyed and the anchors tested, so the appearance of the building is changed without interfering with the load-bearing frame. The work is usually carried out section by section in the low season.
