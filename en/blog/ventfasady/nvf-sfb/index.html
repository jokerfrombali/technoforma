A GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) ventilated facade is a rainscreen system in which the cladding consists of thin-walled panels and architectural elements made of GFRC, fixed to a metal substructure with an air gap over the insulation. The material is chosen when a facade needs plasticity — cornices, pilasters, window surrounds, rusticated surfaces, large relief panels — while the cladding must remain non-combustible and relatively light.

The key selection criterion is not the panel itself but the combination of panel, cast-in fixings and substructure, engineered for a specific building. The solution suits residential complexes, hotels, public and office buildings where the architect works with a classical or neoclassical order or with a sculptural contemporary form.

General principles of rainscreen systems are covered in the Ventilated rainscreen facades section; this article focuses on what sets GFRC apart.

GFRC in a ventilated facade

GFRC is a fine-grained concrete reinforced with alkali-resistant glass fibre. According to TECHNOFORMA production data, the mix has four components: glass fibre, sand, high-grade cement and water. The fibre is distributed throughout the cross-section, so the panel wall can be made thin.

  • Non-combustibility. The material is classified as non-combustible (NG group under the Russian classification): the mineral matrix and glass fibre do not support combustion. This matters for a rainscreen facade because the cladding is the first layer exposed to flames from a window opening.
  • Weight. Panels are thin-walled and usually have stiffening ribs or a steel frame on the back. The weight per square metre is lower than that of natural stone of comparable strength, but higher than that of metal cassettes — this is taken into account when designing the brackets.
  • Freedom of form. The product is cast or sprayed into a mould, so a single element can combine a flat surface, a cornice profile and ornament. Fewer joints mean fewer places where water can enter the system.
  • Frost resistance. High, and further improved by modifying admixtures. The specific grade depends on the mix design and is recorded in the product passport and test report.
  • Texture and colour. The surface can imitate stone or be smooth or textured; colour is set by pigments in the mix or by a finishing coat.

A separate characteristic is dependence on production discipline. The strength and durability of GFRC are determined by fibre dosage, water–cement ratio, compaction and curing conditions. That is why, for a facade, it matters who makes the moulds and how each batch is controlled. At TECHNOFORMA, models and moulds are made in the company's own mould shops, and quality control is carried out at every production stage.

Cast-in fixings and attachment

GFRC is attached to the facade via cast-in fixings moulded into the product at the factory. On site, the panel is neither drilled nor trimmed: everything the substructure holds it by is built into the element in advance. That is why the fixing scheme is designed together with the panel layout.

Several types of connection are used:

  • Steel stud frame on the back. For large flat and profiled panels: a light frame of steel sections connected to the GFRC skin with flexible anchors. The frame takes the load and transfers it to the brackets, while the skin can move freely with changes in temperature and humidity.
  • Cast-in sockets and plates. For small elements — window surrounds, keystones, rosettes. A threaded socket or plate of stainless or galvanised steel is cast into the body of the element.
  • Bearing and restraint connections. The weight of the element is transferred to bearing brackets and wind load to restraint brackets. A panel usually has fewer bearing points, to avoid rigid restraint.

Key requirements for connections: adjustment in three planes to align the facade plane, no rigid restraint of the skin, compatibility of metals (contact between dissimilar metals without isolating pads causes corrosion), and access to the connection for installation and possible replacement of an element. Cornices and strongly projecting profiles require separate calculation: their load is eccentric, so the bracket is also subjected to an overturning moment.

The practical takeaway for the client: the fixing layout is part of the manufacturer's working drawings. If panels are made by one contractor and the substructure is designed by another, the connections must be agreed before the moulds are made — a cast-in fixing cannot be altered in a finished element.

Joints

Joints in a GFRC facade are not a defect to be hidden but a working part of the system. Panels change size as they heat and cool, and the joint gives them room for this movement.

  • Open joints. The classic rainscreen option: the gap between panels is left unfilled; water that gets behind the cladding runs down the membrane and is drained out at the bottom. Joint width is set by the design, taking into account element size and thermal movement.
  • Sealed joints. Filled with elastic sealant over a backer rod. Used when the architect wants a visually continuous surface or when the joint runs along a projecting element where water must not get in. They need periodic inspection: sealant ages faster than concrete.
  • Architectural joints and rustication. Some joints can be aligned with rustication or a profile, so the working joint does not read on the facade.

In the panel layout, joints are tied to window axes, floor slabs and fire barriers. A horizontal joint above a cornice or sill element must be protected by a flashing or by the profile of the element itself so that water does not enter the system.

Substructure

The substructure for GFRC is a metal frame of brackets, rails and fixing connections that transfers loads from the cladding to the building's load-bearing structure. Two things distinguish it from substructures for light cassettes: a higher vertical load and the need to accommodate elements of complex shape.

  • Material. Galvanised or stainless steel or aluminium alloys — the choice depends on cladding weight, environmental aggressiveness and fire safety requirements. Heavy elements more often use steel.
  • Bracket spacing. Determined by calculation for the cladding's self-weight and wind load, and by the load-bearing capacity of anchors in the specific substrate.
  • Substrate. Cast-in-situ concrete, brick and aerated concrete behave differently. For weak substrates, brackets are fixed to the floor slabs, with intermediate supports on the wall between them.
  • Offset. The sum of insulation thickness, ventilation gap and panel fixing depth. For cornices and projecting decorative elements the offset varies, and connections are designed individually.

TECHNOFORMA installs the substructure, insulation and panels with its own installation teams — this removes the typical conflict of "panels don't fit someone else's frame". Design is done in the same loop: the company's design department (12 designers led by two architects) develops the layout and connections and coordinates them with the substructure calculation.

Codes and standards

A ventilated facade clad in GFRC is designed and accepted under the same rules as any rainscreen system: the applicable codes of practice on fire safety, on loads and actions and on thermal performance of buildings, plus the documents for the specific facade system.

  • Loads. The substructure is calculated for the cladding's self-weight, wind pressure and suction, taking into account the construction region, height and zones of increased load at corners and parapets — under the Russian code of practice SP 20.13330 "Loads and Actions".
  • Fire safety. Requirements depend on the building's structural fire hazard class and its use. The non-combustibility of GFRC cladding simplifies the task but does not remove the requirements for the insulation, membrane, fire barriers and the system as a whole, which is confirmed by fire tests on a facade mock-up.
  • Thermal protection. Insulation thickness is determined by thermal calculation, taking into account thermal bridges — the brackets.
  • System documents. The technical certificate for the facade system and the album of technical solutions on which the design is based; for the products — passports and test reports.

A practical approach for technical supervision is to compare the actual connections on site with the album of technical solutions: bracket type, spacing, anchors, fire barriers, insulation fixing. Deviations are acceptable only through a design decision.

Which GFRC products are installed on a ventilated facade: types and indicative sizes

Not only flat panels are mounted on a ventilated system but the entire architectural decoration: cornices, string courses, pilasters, window surrounds, rusticated and relief elements. The shell thickness, method of stiffening and support arrangement depend on the product type, so the product range is defined before the frame is calculated.

A large element reduces the number of joints and installation operations but requires a crane or winch and a more accurate frame. A fine subdivision is easier to lift but increases the number of connections and joints. The optimum size is found during layout by weighing the mass of the product, the lifting options on the particular site and the facade pattern.

How to combine GFRC with tile and slab claddings

On most large projects glass fibre reinforced concrete works together with other materials: decoration and accent planes are made of GFRC, while ordinary areas are covered with porcelain stoneware, fibre cement or stone. This combination reduces the budget and the mass of the facade without impoverishing the architecture, but three things need attention.

  • Different cladding thickness. The face planes are brought to one level or a step is made deliberately; in both cases the bracket stand-off is set separately for each zone.
  • Different mass. Under panels and cornices the bracket spacing and profile sections are greater than under tiles, so the zone boundaries are shown on the frame layout.
  • Junction line. It is run along a string course, pilaster or rustication groove so that the joint between materials reads as an architectural decision rather than an accidental boundary.

The colour and texture of the products are selected from samples placed next to the tile or stone, in daylight: shades that match in a catalogue may diverge on the facade.

From sketch to installation: how a GFRC ventilated facade project proceeds

A facade project with GFRC cladding runs in the reverse order compared with tile systems: first the products themselves are developed, then the frame is designed for them. The reason is that each element is made in a mould and cannot be altered after casting.

  1. Input data. Architectural elevations, sections, information about walls and floor slabs, insulation requirements, desired texture and colour.
  2. Survey and model. Geodetic survey of the erected shell and a three-dimensional model of the facade showing the real deviations of the walls.
  3. Layout. Division of the facade into products, setting of joints, standardisation of sizes so that one mould is used many times.
  4. Working drawings of products and frame. Position of embedded parts, mass of each element, fixing details, schedules. The content of such documentation is covered in the article facade shop drawings.
  5. Patterns, moulds and sample. Making of master patterns and moulds, approval of a texture and colour sample.
  6. Production and marking. Each product receives a mark corresponding to its place on the facade.
  7. Delivery and installation section by section. Products are dispatched in sets in the sequence in which they are needed on site.

At TECHNOFORMA Group all stages are carried out within one organisation: the design department, the mould shops and production in the Moscow region covering more than 2 hectares, and the company's own installation division. Production capacity exceeds 20,000 m² of GFRC per month, which allows supply to run in parallel with installation of the frame.

Delivery, storage and lifting of panels

A glass fibre reinforced concrete panel is designed for loads in its design position, not for arbitrary forces during transport, so logistics are thought through as carefully as the fixing details. Most chips and cracks appear not on the facade but during unloading and storage.

  • Transport. Flat panels are carried vertically or at a slight incline in racks and A-frames, three-dimensional elements in individual cradles. The face surface is protected with packing.
  • Slinging. The product is lifted by the lifting loops or embedded parts shown on the drawing. Gripping by the edge or at arbitrary points is not permitted.
  • Storage. A level area, timber bearers under the ribs or frame, storage by mark and section. Stacking face to face without packing is not allowed.
  • Lifting. Tower crane, mobile crane, winch or mini-crane on the floor slab; the choice depends on the mass of the element and the construction stage. In wind, lifting of large panels is stopped.
  • Completeness. Products for the next section are brought to site: excess stock increases the risk of damage.

For remote sites the dispatch schedule is tied to the frame installation programme. If the panels arrive before the brackets are ready, they lie on site for weeks; if later, the crews and scaffolding stand idle.

In what order are panels installed and which tolerances are checked?

Installation proceeds from corner and bearing elements to ordinary ones, bringing the facade plane into line step by step by adjusting the connections. Accuracy is set by the frame: if the brackets and rails are positioned to the geodetic setting-out, the panels fit without adjustment.

  1. Checking the frame: position of brackets and bearing cleats, levels, readiness of insulation and fire barriers in the section.
  2. Fitting of datum products at corners and openings, stringing of control lines.
  3. Hanging of ordinary panels on the bearing connections, securing of the wind restraints.
  4. Adjustment in three planes, checking of joint width with gauges.
  5. Tightening and locking of threaded connections, anti-corrosion treatment of welds where the design provides for welding.
  6. Installation of flashings and filling of joints where specified.

The alignment of adjacent panel planes, the straightness and constant width of joints, the plumb of the rows and the ventilated cavity gap are checked. The specific tolerance values are set by the design and the method statement; for rusticated and relief elements they are usually tighter, because a step at the joint is emphasised by shadow.

Typical mistakes when constructing a ventilated facade with GFRC cladding

Mistakes on glass fibre reinforced concrete facades are most often due to the panel being treated like an ordinary tile: trimmed, drilled, fixed rigidly. The material is strong in its design mode of working but does not forgive improvisation on site.

  • The frame is designed before the product layout. The brackets miss the embedded parts and the connections have to be reworked in situ.
  • Rigid restraint of the panel. All fixing points are tightened solid, thermal and moisture movement has nowhere to go, and cracks appear in the shell.
  • Drilling and trimming on site. These disturb the reinforcement at the edge and expose an end without a protective layer.
  • Joints narrower than designed or filled with rigid mortar. Adjacent panels bear against each other and the edges chip.
  • No drainage from horizontal surfaces. Water stands on cornices and string courses, and streaks and efflorescence appear on the panels below.
  • Unprotected mild steel in the connections. Rust stains come out onto the face through the joints.
  • Installation without marking. Elements of similar appearance but with different embedded parts are swapped.

Substitution of material during tendering deserves separate mention. Products made of polymer compounds or coated expanded polystyrene resemble GFRC externally but have different combustibility, mass and fixing arrangement. They cannot be dropped into a design calculated for glass fibre reinforced concrete without revising the fire section and the frame.

What does the cost depend on and how can the design be optimised?

The cost of a glass fibre reinforced concrete ventilated facade is determined above all by the number of different moulds and the complexity of the connections, not only by the cladding area. Two facades of the same area can differ markedly in price if one has ten sizes and the other a hundred.

What to prepare for a preliminary estimate

For a first estimate, sketch-stage materials or design documentation are sufficient. The more precisely the decoration and the substrate are described, the fewer assumptions the estimate contains.

  • elevations and sections with dimensions, in an editable format if available;
  • sections of cornices, string courses and surrounds, or at least their overall size and projection;
  • material of the walls and structural frame, number of storeys, location;
  • required insulation thickness;
  • preferences for texture and colour, reference images;
  • construction stage and desired start date for installation.

From this data the designers prepare a preliminary layout and assess the number of moulds and the mass of the products, after which an estimate is drawn up, broken down into products, frame, insulation and labour. Final quantities are refined after the building has been surveyed.

In use: inspections, cleaning and repair of the cladding

Glass fibre reinforced concrete cladding does not need regular renewal, but the facade requires planned supervision: inspection of joints and fixings, cleaning and, where necessary, restoration of the protective coating. The amount of maintenance depends on the finish and on air pollution.

  • Inspections. Usually once a year and after extreme weather events: condition of the sealant in closed joints, flashings, signs of leaks, cracks, displacement of elements.
  • Cleaning. Water at moderate pressure and neutral products. Aggressive acidic products damage the cement matrix and the coating.
  • Protective treatments. A water repellent or finish coating loses its properties over time; the renewal interval is stated by the product manufacturer.
  • Local repair. Chips are made good with repair mortars matched for colour and texture; a damaged element can be replaced by casting it in the retained mould.

Equipment not provided for in the design must not be fixed to the panels: loads from signs, air conditioners and light fittings are transferred to the wall or frame through separate connections. FAQ

How does a GFRC rainscreen facade differ from a porcelain stoneware one?

GFRC allows large and three-dimensional elements — cornices, pilasters, relief — whereas porcelain stoneware remains a flat tile. GFRC panels are heavier, so the substructure and anchors are designed for a higher load.

Does GFRC burn on a facade?

No, according to the manufacturer the material is classified as non-combustible (NG). However, the fire safety of a facade is confirmed for the whole system: insulation, membrane, fire barriers and substructure.

How is GFRC fixed to a facade?

Via cast-in fixings moulded into the element at the factory: a steel frame with flexible anchors for large panels, or sockets and plates for small elements. The connections are joined to the substructure brackets with adjustment in three planes.

Can GFRC panels be installed on another manufacturer's substructure?

Yes, if the fixing connections are agreed before the moulds are made. Cast-in fixings cannot be changed in a finished element, so it is better to design the layout and substructure in one loop.

How much does a GFRC ventilated facade cost?

The cost is determined after a site inspection and development of the panel layout: it depends on element complexity, area, number of storeys, substrate and scope of work.

How thick are GFRC panels for a ventilated facade?

The panel shell is usually 10–20 mm, with stiffness provided by ribs or a steel frame on the rear face. The overall depth of the product with the frame is greater and is set by the design; exact values are given in the working drawings for the specific element.

Can GFRC be combined with porcelain stoneware or stone on one facade?

Yes, this is a common solution: glass fibre reinforced concrete forms the cornices, surrounds and accent planes, while tile and slab materials cover the ordinary areas. The frame for each cladding is calculated separately, and the junction line is aligned with the architectural articulation.

Can glass fibre reinforced concrete panels be installed in winter?

Hanging panels on the frame is a dry process and is possible at sub-zero temperatures. The limitations concern sealing of closed joints, painting and repair compounds, which have a minimum application temperature, and crane operation in wind.
