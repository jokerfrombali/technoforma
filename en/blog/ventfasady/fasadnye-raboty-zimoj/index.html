Facade Work in Winter: What Can Be Done

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

Contents

  2. Dry and wet processes
  3. Heated enclosures
  4. Storing materials

Facade work in winter is possible, but not all of it. The dry processes of a ventilated rainscreen facade — installing brackets, insulation, membrane and rails and hanging cladding on mechanical fixings — can be carried out at sub-zero temperatures, subject to material and safety limits. Wet processes — render, adhesives, painting, some sealants and chemical anchors — require above-zero temperatures or special conditions: heated enclosures, heating, winter grades of materials.

The key criterion is the application temperature range of each material from its technical documentation, not a general rule like "it's fine down to minus ten". This article is for general contractors, clients and technical supervisors deciding whether to continue facade work in the cold season.

How rainscreen facades work is in the Ventilated rainscreen facades section.

Temperature limits

There is no single "winter" temperature for all facade work — limits are set by each material and each type of equipment.

OperationTypical limitWhere to check
Installing expansion anchors, brackets, railsPossible at sub-zero temperaturesDocumentation for the anchors and system
Chemical anchorsWinter grades work at lower temperatures; curing time increasesProduct data sheet
Insulation, membraneInstallation possible, but the material must be dry and free of iceManufacturer's instructions
Sealants, adhesive cladding fixing systemsUsually above-zero substrate and air temperatureProduct technical sheet
Render, paintingAbove-zero temperature or a heated enclosureProduct technical sheet
Operating cradles and hoistsLimits on temperature and wind speedEquipment passport, method statement

Workers at height are covered by occupational safety requirements for cold weather: warming breaks, restrictions in strong wind and icy conditions. These conditions are built into the method statement and reduce daily output. Air and substrate temperatures are recorded daily in the work log — this is the basis on which technical supervision accepts or stops an operation.

Dry and wet processes

The difference between dry and wet processes is the main reason rainscreen facades are easier to install in winter than render facades.

  • Dry processes — all mechanical connections: anchors, bolts, rivets, clips, runners. The strength of the connection does not depend on mortar curing, so such work is possible in winter. A rainscreen facade of porcelain stoneware, GFRC or stone with mechanical fixing consists mainly of dry operations.
  • Wet processes — everything involving curing or drying: external render insulation systems, adhesive tile cladding, painting, filling joints with mortar. At sub-zero temperatures the water in the mortar freezes and strength does not develop.

A real facade almost always has both: repairing the substrate with mortar before installation, sealing window junctions, painting trims. These operations are moved to the warm season or carried out in a heated enclosure. GFRC cladding itself is a finished factory product that has gained its strength in the plant; on site it is only fixed mechanically.

Heated enclosures

"Thermal envelopes" can mean two different solutions.

  • Heated enclosures on the facade — temporary shelters of film or tarpaulin on the scaffolding, with heating. They create above-zero temperatures inside for wet processes: substrate repair, rendering, sealing. They are expensive and labour-intensive, so they are used selectively.
  • Closing the building's thermal envelope — installing windows, insulating and cladding the walls so that heating can be switched on inside and finishing can proceed. This is one of the main arguments for continuing facade work in winter: a closed envelope means interior work does not have to stop.

When planning, it is worth deciding which facades and sections need to be closed before the cold sets in — usually those behind which interior finishing begins.

A heated enclosure is justified when the critical path of the schedule would otherwise stall: for example, when the substrate must be repaired before brackets are installed, or window junctions sealed on a section where finishing is already under way. In other cases it is cheaper to move wet operations to spring and carry on with dry processes on other sections in winter. The decision is made in the method statement, taking into account the costs of the enclosure, heating and fire safety of temporary structures.

Storing materials

In winter, correct storage of materials is a condition of quality.

  • Insulation is stored in factory packaging on pallets under cover. Wet or iced insulation must not be installed: moisture behind the cladding will take a long time to escape and will reduce thermal performance.
  • Membranes, sealants, chemical anchors — in a heated room until use, if the manufacturer requires it.
  • Cladding — on pallets, in packaging protected from snow and moisture. Ice between panels during lifting can cause chipping or a fall.
  • Substructure metal — under cover, not in contact with the ground or snow.

Before installation, the surfaces of cladding and connections are cleared of snow and ice. Elements with cast-in fixings — GFRC, kerfed stone — are checked for ice in grooves and holes.

Inspection

In winter, technical supervision checks the same things as in summer, plus some specific points:

  • air and substrate temperature for operations with limits — recorded in the work log;
  • condition of the insulation: dry, free of ice, fitting tightly;
  • suitability of chemical products for the temperature conditions, curing time;
  • no snow or ice behind the cladding;
  • serviceability of access equipment, wind restrictions;
  • postponement of wet processes to the warm season, recorded in the schedule.

In spring it is worth re-inspecting junctions and sealing done in cold weather and checking that no areas of wet insulation remain behind the cladding. It is useful to compile all postponed wet operations into a separate list linked to sections, so they are not lost at handover.

FAQ

What does facade work include?

Construction, cladding, insulation and repair of external walls: ventilated rainscreen facades, render systems, stone and tile cladding, glazing, painting, joint sealing.

Which Russian OKVED activity code covers facade work?

Codes from section 43 "Specialised construction activities" are usually used, for example 43.39 or 43.99; the final choice of code is best agreed with an accountant based on the actual scope of work.

At what temperature can a ventilated facade be installed?

Dry rainscreen operations are possible at sub-zero temperatures; limits are set by anchor compounds, sealants and equipment according to their documentation.

Can a facade be rendered in winter?

Only in a heated enclosure or with winter-grade products that allow lower temperatures. Otherwise the mortar does not gain strength.

Why is facade installation slower in winter?

Short daylight hours, warming breaks, restrictions due to wind and ice, and postponement of wet processes reduce output.
