Facade Work in Winter: What Can Be Done

Facade work in winter is possible, but not all of it. The dry processes of a ventilated rainscreen facade (installing brackets, insulation, membrane, rails and hanging cladding on mechanical fixings) can be carried out at sub-zero temperatures, observing the limits for materials and safety. Wet processes (render, adhesives, painting, some sealants and chemical anchors) require above-zero temperatures or special conditions: heated enclosures, heating, winter grades of materials.

The main criterion is the application temperature range of each material stated in its technical documentation, not a general rule like 'fine down to minus ten'. This article is for general contractors, clients and technical supervision deciding whether to continue facade work in the cold season.

For how rainscreen facades are built, see Ventilated rainscreen facades.

Temperature limits

There is no single winter temperature for all facade work: each material and each piece of equipment sets its own limits.

OperationTypical limitWhere to check
Installing expansion anchors, brackets, railsPossible at sub-zero temperaturesAnchor and system documentation
Chemical anchorsWinter grades work at lower temperatures; curing time increasesProduct data sheet
Insulation, membraneInstallation possible, but the material must be dry and free of iceManufacturer's instructions
Sealants, adhesive cladding fixing systemsUsually above-zero substrate and air temperatureProduct technical sheet
Render, paintingAbove-zero temperature or a heated enclosureProduct technical sheet
Cradles and liftsLimits on temperature and wind speedEquipment manual, method statement

Workers at height are subject to cold-weather health and safety requirements: warming breaks, restrictions in strong winds and icy conditions. These are built into the method statement and reduce daily output. Air and substrate temperatures are recorded in the site log every day; this is the basis on which technical supervision accepts or stops an operation.

Substrate temperature and air temperature are different values: in the morning the wall can be colder than the air, and on the sunny side warmer. For operations with adhesives and sealants, the reference is the surface temperature, measured with a contact or infrared thermometer, and the dew point: in humid air, moisture condenses on a cold substrate and adhesion fails.

Dry and wet processes

The difference between dry and wet processes is the main reason rainscreen facades are easier to install in winter than render systems.

  • Dry processes are all mechanical connections: anchors, bolts, rivets, clips, sub-frames. Connection strength does not depend on mortar curing, so this work is possible in winter. A rainscreen facade of porcelain stoneware, GFRC or stone on mechanical fixings consists mainly of dry operations.
  • Wet processes are everything that involves curing or drying: external insulation render systems, adhesive tiling, painting, filling joints with mortar. At sub-zero temperatures the water in the mortar freezes and strength does not develop.

A real facade almost always has both: repairing the substrate with mortar before installation, sealing junctions with windows, painting trims. These operations are moved to the warm season or carried out in a heated enclosure. GFRC cladding itself is a finished factory product that has gained its strength in the workshop; on site it is only fixed mechanically.

Should insulation be covered for the winter if the cladding has not been installed? Yes: mineral wool insulation on an open wall absorbs moisture from snow and rain, and a membrane, if used, is designed for limited UV exposure. Uncovered areas are protected with sheeting or tarpaulin fixed to the scaffolding and substructure, and the top edge of the insulation is protected from water ingress. In spring, before cladding, the insulation is inspected and wet boards are replaced.

Heated enclosures

Heated enclosures can mean two different things.

  • Enclosures on the facade: temporary sheeting or tarpaulin shelters on the scaffolding with heating. They create an above-zero temperature inside for wet processes: substrate repair, render, sealing. They are expensive and labour-intensive, so they are used selectively.
  • Closing the building envelope: installing windows, insulating and cladding the walls so that heating can be switched on inside and finishing can proceed. This is one of the main arguments for continuing facade work in winter: a closed envelope means interior work does not have to stop.

When planning, decide which facades and work zones must be closed before the cold sets in, usually those behind which interior finishing begins.

An enclosure is justified when the critical path of the programme stops without it: for example, the substrate must be repaired before brackets are installed, or window junctions sealed in a zone where finishing is already under way. Otherwise it is cheaper to move wet operations to spring and carry out dry processes in other zones during winter. The decision is made in the method statement, taking into account the cost of shelters, heating and fire safety of temporary structures.

Storing materials

In winter, correct storage of materials is a condition of quality.

  • Insulation is stored in factory packaging on pallets, under cover. Wet or iced insulation must not be installed: moisture behind the cladding takes a long time to escape and reduces thermal performance.
  • Membranes, sealants, chemical anchors are kept in a heated room until use, if the manufacturer requires it.
  • Cladding is kept on pallets in packaging protected from snow and moisture. Ice between slabs during lifting can cause chipping or a fall.
  • Substructure metal is kept under cover, out of contact with the ground and snow.

Before installation, snow and ice are cleared from the cladding surface and connections. Elements with cast-in fixings (GFRC, stone with kerfs) are checked for ice in slots and holes.

Chemical products are taken out of the heated room immediately before use.

Inspection

In winter, technical supervision checks the same things as in summer, plus some specific points:

  • air and substrate temperature for operations with limits, recorded in the site log;
  • condition of the insulation: dry, free of ice, fitted tightly;
  • suitability of chemical products for the temperature conditions, curing time;
  • no snow or ice behind the cladding;
  • serviceability of access equipment, wind limits;
  • wet processes moved to the warm season and recorded in the programme.

In spring it is worth re-inspecting junctions and sealing done in the cold season and checking that no wet insulation remains behind the cladding. All postponed wet operations should be collected in a separate list linked to work zones so they are not forgotten at handover.

Is winter installation worthwhile? For rainscreen facades it often is, if the work is planned: the building is handed over earlier, the closed envelope allows interior finishing, and installation crews are better utilised across the year. Costs rise due to lower output, heating, shelters and snow clearing. For render systems the economics are usually the reverse: enclosures and heating cost more than moving the work to spring.

How to plan facade works in winter: a programme by work sections

A winter facade is planned not in November but at the end of summer: by the first frosts all wet operations on the sections that will run into winter must be finished. The point of planning is to divide the works into those that must be completed in the warm season and those that can be moved to the cold period without loss of quality.

  1. August — September. Repair and levelling of the substrate, pointing of masonry joints, priming, sealing of window junctions on all facades where winter installation is planned.
  2. October. Anchor tests, installation of brackets over the largest possible area, delivery and storage of materials under cover, preparation of enclosures.
  3. November — February. Insulation, rails and hanging of cladding on mechanical fixings in sections that are closed completely — from bracket to panel.
  4. March — April. Postponed wet operations, sealing, painting of trim elements, remedying of defects.

The main rule of a winter section is not to leave the insulation exposed for more than a few days: only as much work front is opened as the team can close with cladding. A time allowance is built in beforehand: because of wind, snowfall and frost, the number of working days in a winter month is usually noticeably lower than the calendar figure. How to build the overall programme is explained in the article on facade installation times.

In the contract with the contractor it is useful to list explicitly which operations are carried out in winter, under what conditions work is suspended and who pays for enclosures, heating and snow clearance. Without this, disputed downtime becomes a matter of correspondence as early as January.

How does frost affect the subframe, fixings and cladding?

Metal, stone and glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) are not in themselves afraid of frost, but in the cold their dimensions, the brittleness of accompanying materials and the behaviour of tools all change. Most winter defects are related not to strength but to the fact that the structure was assembled at minus fifteen and will also have to work at plus thirty in the sun.

ElementWhat happens in the coldWhat to do
Aluminium and steel railsThe profile is shorter than in summer; it will lengthen when heatedLeave an expansion gap at the joints as designed, do not butt the rails tightly
Anchors in concrete and masonrySnow and ice accumulate in the holes, frozen masonry crumblesBlow out and clean the holes, do not drill frozen wet block, check the tightening torque
Membranes, tapes, sealsFilms and rubber harden, the adhesive layer of tapes does not bondStore in the warm, use materials with a confirmed installation temperature range
Cladding panelsMoisture in grooves and kerfs freezes and may cause chippingStore dry, clean the grooves before hanging
Cordless toolsBattery capacity drops, lubricant thickensKeep batteries warm, have a spare set

Expansion gaps are the most frequent omission. If long rails are installed in frost without a gap, the metal will lengthen in summer and the panels will be pushed out of plane or the fixings torn. The size of the gap is taken from the system's book of technical details; it depends on the profile material and the length of the element. The differences between aluminium and steel are discussed in the article on subframes for ventilated facades.

Insulation and membrane in the cold season: what must not be allowed

Mineral wool insulation can be installed at sub-zero temperatures, but only dry and onto a dry wall. Moisture trapped behind the cladding in December takes months to escape through the gap, and until then the wall works with reduced thermal resistance — precisely in the heating season.

  • do not fix slabs to a wall with ice or hoar frost: under the insulation the ice will melt from the heat of the room and wet the inner layer;
  • open the packaging immediately before installation and do not leave opened packs in the open air;
  • butt the slabs tightly: in frost the wool is stiffer and fits less closely around the brackets, so gaps around them are filled with offcuts;
  • drive disc fixings without excess blows: in the cold the plastic is brittle and the head cracks;
  • every evening cover the top edge of an unfinished area against snow.

If the system provides for a membrane, the sheets are fixed immediately after the insulation and the laps are sealed with a tape approved by the manufacturer for use in the cold. Why this layer is needed and when it can be omitted is described in the article on the wind barrier membrane. Thermal insulation works as a separate service are presented in the facade insulation section.

Winter installation of GFRC and natural stone

Heavy cladding on mechanical fixings is one of the most convenient options for winter: the products arrive from the factory finished, and only lifting, hanging and adjustment remain on site. The limitations relate not to the material but to lifting operations and to details where chemical products are used.

GFRC panels and decorative elements gain strength in the workshop at controlled temperature and humidity, so their quality does not depend on the weather on site. In frost something else matters: embedded fixings and the mating parts of the subframe must be cleared of ice, and a panel must not be wrenched from the pallet if it has frozen to the spacers. TECHNOFORMA Group products are manufactured at the plant in the Moscow region and dispatched with marking according to the layout — see the GFRC for facades section for the product range.

Stone slabs are installed in winter on concealed or visible mechanical fixings. If the design provides for fixing pins in kerfs with an adhesive compound, a compound with confirmed performance at low temperature is used, or the operation is moved into a heated room at the slab preparation stage. Wet stone is not taken out into frost: water in the pores of limestone, travertine or sandstone may cause the face to scale when it freezes. Which rock types are suitable for a severe climate is indicated in the natural stone for facades section.

  • crane lifting is stopped when the wind exceeds the value given in the method statement for elements with a large wind area;
  • slings and grips are checked for icing before each shift;
  • joints between panels, if they are to be sealed, are left open until warm weather and filled in spring.

Site organisation in winter: snow, ice and lighting

In winter the productivity of a facade team is determined not so much by temperature as by the state of the site: whether the walkways are cleared, the workplaces lit and there is somewhere to warm up. These matters are settled before the season begins, otherwise every morning starts with an hour of snow clearing.

  • Snow and ice on scaffolding. Decks are cleared before the shift and sanded; icicles and snow overhangs on the upper lifts and parapet are knocked down before work starts below.
  • Lighting. Daylight in central Russia in December lasts about seven to eight hours, so working areas, walkways and storage areas are fitted with floodlights. The shade and flatness of cladding are harder to judge under artificial light, so visual acceptance is carried out in the daytime.
  • Warming of workers. Welfare cabins are placed as close as possible to the work front; the schedule of breaks is set in the method statement according to temperature and wind.
  • Storage. Materials are kept on pallets under cover and the access routes to them are cleared; pallets of cladding are not placed under roof slopes from which snow slides.
  • Fire safety. Fan heaters and other heaters in enclosures and cabins are a frequent cause of winter fires on site; their placement and power supply are agreed separately.

Snow packed behind the rails and in the air gap is removed before the cladding is hung: in spring it will melt and wet the insulation from below.

Mothballing an unfinished facade for winter: the procedure

If the decision has been taken to stop work until spring, the facade is not abandoned in its current state but mothballed. The aim is that over three to four months the insulation does not get wet, unclosed details are not damaged and nothing falls.

  1. Bring the sections already started to a logical boundary: close with cladding everything where insulation is already in place, or remove the insulation from areas that cannot be closed in time.
  2. Cover the areas left open with film or tarpaulin securely fixed, and protect the top edges and window junctions.
  3. Check and secure everything the wind could tear off: edge panels, unfixed rails, sills, scaffold netting.
  4. Remove materials and debris from the scaffolding and check the scaffold ties to the wall.
  5. Draw up a record with photographs of the state of each section and a list of postponed operations.
  6. Arrange periodic inspections — after strong wind and snowfall.

In spring the mothballing record becomes the starting point: it is used to check what has changed over the winter and to delimit responsibility if the contractor has changed.

Continue the facade in winter or stop: a checklist for the decision

There is no universal answer: the decision depends on the type of system, the readiness of the substrate and what lies on the critical path of the programme. A quick point-by-point check helps to take it dispassionately.

  • A ventilated system with cladding on mechanical fixings — an argument for continuing.
  • Wet operations on the planned sections are complete or small in volume — for continuing.
  • Materials have been delivered and are stored under cover, winter-grade compounds have been purchased — for continuing.
  • Starting the heating and the fit-out depends on closing the envelope — a weighty argument for continuing.
  • The substrate needs a large amount of mortar repair, the facade is rendered or the cladding is adhesive-fixed — for stopping and mothballing.
  • The site is windy, access is by cradle only, the climate has sustained severe frosts — for reducing the winter scope to a minimum.

If the arguments for continuing prevail, the winter stage is set out in a separate section of the method statement with a list of operations, temperature limits and the procedure for suspending work.

The region should be assessed separately. In southern areas with mild winters sub-zero temperatures do not last long, and the limitations mainly concern wind and precipitation. In a continental climate with sustained severe frosts, work in cradles and on open scaffolding has to be stopped for days and weeks, and these breaks are built into the programme in advance.

FAQ

What is facade work and what does it include?

Work on building, cladding, insulating and repairing external walls: ventilated rainscreen facades, render systems, stone and tile cladding, glazing, painting and joint sealing.

Which Russian activity code (OKVED) covers facade work?

Codes from section 43 'Specialised construction activities' are usually used, for example 43.39 or 43.99; the final choice is best agreed with an accountant based on the actual scope of work.

At what temperature can a rainscreen facade be installed?

Dry rainscreen operations are possible at sub-zero temperatures; limits are set by anchor products, sealants and equipment according to their documentation.

Can a facade be rendered in winter?

Only in a heated enclosure or with winter products rated for low temperatures. Otherwise the mortar does not gain strength.

Why is facade installation slower in winter?

Short daylight hours, warming breaks, wind and ice restrictions and postponed wet processes reduce output.

Does insulation need covering if the cladding is not finished before winter?

Yes. Exposed mineral wool gets wet from snow and rain, so it is covered with sheeting or tarpaulin, its top edges are protected, and in spring it is inspected before cladding and wet boards are replaced.

Do expansion gaps need to be left in the subframe when installing in frost?

Yes, and in winter this is especially important. Metal installed in the cold will lengthen in summer; if the rails are butted without a gap, the cladding will be pushed out of plane. The size of the gap is taken from the documentation for the facade system.

What should be done if snow has got behind the cladding or into the air gap?

Remove it before the panels are hung in that area. Snow left behind the rails will melt in spring and wet the insulation; if the area is already closed, it is opened up selectively and the state of the insulation is checked.

How should the suspension of facade works for the winter be documented?

With a mothballing record with photographs: it states the condition of each section, the protective covers installed and the list of postponed operations. In spring this record is used to check what has changed and to resume the works.
