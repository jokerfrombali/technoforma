Business Centre Facades: Materials and Image

A business centre facade is both a technical envelope and the building's main marketing tool: tenants judge the class of the property before they even enter the lobby. As a rule it is a ventilated rainscreen facade or glazed curtain walling, complemented by cladding in porcelain stoneware, glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC), natural stone or metal.

The main selection criterion is the balance between an image that supports the building's stated class and the cost of operation: the cladding must keep its appearance for a long time, be easy to clean and be repairable element by element. Decisions at this level concern commercial property developers, architects and property management companies.

On how rainscreen systems are built, see Ventilated rainscreen facades.

What a business centre facade has to do

An office building facade does several jobs at once:

  • Positioning. Materials and details must match the class of the property. Natural stone, expressive modelling and well-made details are read as signs of a high class.
  • Recognisability. A business centre often becomes a local landmark; a distinctive silhouette and facade rhythm build the building's brand.
  • Energy efficiency. Opaque areas are insulated; glazing is selected with heat loss and overheating in mind.
  • Safety. Non-combustible materials and certified systems are mandatory for public buildings under the current Russian fire safety codes (SP).
  • Indoor comfort. Sun-shading fins, deep reveals and projecting decorative elements reduce overheating of offices on south-facing facades.
  • Predictable costs. The management company needs a facade that does not require constant repairs and can be accessed for cleaning.

The entrance

The entrance is the first point of contact between tenants, visitors and the building, and it usually concentrates the most expensive materials and details.

  • Natural stone on floors and pier cladding withstands heavy use; for external steps, hard frost-resistant stones such as granite or basalt with an anti-slip finish are chosen.
  • The canopy and portal are made of GFRC or metal; glass fibre reinforced concrete gives large elements a monolithic look without visible joints where the architecture requires it. Large elements are designed with an internal steel frame fixed to the load-bearing structure.
  • The entrance portal is often emphasised by scale: double height, columns or piers, a projecting canopy. Such elements are easier to make in GFRC than in solid stone: less weight, any shape.
  • Wayfinding and the logo are integrated into the cladding; embedded fixings and cable ducts are installed before the cladding.
  • The entrance must meet accessibility requirements: a ramp, adequate door width, no level differences.

Lighting

Lighting turns a business centre facade into a night-time landmark. It is especially effective on relief facades in GFRC and stone: grazing light brings out the shadows of cornices, fins and pilasters.

  • Outline lighting traces the building's silhouette with linear luminaires along cornices and edges.
  • Floodlighting illuminates a surface evenly; on a deeply modelled facade it is set at a steep angle to preserve the shadows.
  • Accent lighting highlights the entrance, logo, columns or piers.
  • Dynamic lighting with colour and scene control is used when the building has to act as a media surface, but it requires approval under city rules.

The practical side matters for a business centre too: lighting for the entrance, car park and wayfinding must work independently of architectural scenes.

Cable routes, fixings for luminaires and locations for drivers must be provided in the rainscreen facade design; the wiring is then hidden in the cavity behind the cladding and the luminaires are built into the ornament or recesses. The techniques are covered in detail in the article on architectural lighting of facades with ornament.

Operation and maintenance

The management company is responsible for the facade throughout its service life, so operational issues are settled at the design stage:

  • Cleaning. Smooth, dense surfaces soil more slowly; porous materials are protected with water repellents. Anchors for cradles or a rope access system are provided on the roof.
  • Repairability. In a rainscreen facade a damaged tile or panel is replaced without opening up adjacent areas; this requires spare cladding from the same batch.
  • Monitoring. Regular inspection of fixings and ornament, especially after storm-force winds.
  • Documentation. As-built drawings, layouts and material data sheets are handed over to the client; without them, a repair several years later becomes a search.
  • Tenants. Signs, air conditioners and antennas are best included in the facade design code from the start, with designated fixing points; otherwise within a year people will start drilling the cladding at random.

How the class of a business centre affects the choice of facade solutions

The higher the declared class, the greater the requirements for the durability of the cladding, the proportion of glazing and the quality of the details. There is no single standard prescribing materials by class: market classifications of office property and tenants’ expectations serve as the guide. The differences usually look like this.

ParameterClass AClass B
Cladding of opaque areasNatural stone, GFRC, large-format panels with concealed fixingPorcelain stoneware in standard formats, fibre cement, individual decorative elements as accents
GlazingHigh proportion, floor-to-ceiling glazing, stick or unitised curtain wall systemsWindow openings, ribbon glazing
EntranceBespoke design, double-height lobby, stone, illuminated canopyStandard solutions with accent finishes to the portal
Services equipment on the facadeConcealed behind fins and screens, signage under a single set of rulesVisible grilles and equipment cages permitted in designated zones

When a building is repositioned, replacing the cladding and the entrance is one way of raising the property in tenants’ eyes without altering the frame. In that case the work begins with an assessment of the condition of the walls and the existing subframe.

How is the cladding layout tied to the office planning grid?

The facade of an office building is governed by the planning module — the spacing at which partitions can be placed inside. It is usually about 1.35–1.5 m, and the axes of the mullions and piers and the cladding joints are tied to it. If the panel layout does not match the module, tenants’ partitions end against glass, and make-up pieces of arbitrary width appear on the facade.

The usual sequence is as follows:

  1. the module and storey height, slab levels and the spandrel zone are fixed;
  2. a typical facade bay is defined — window, pier, spandrel band;
  3. the cladding format is selected so that a whole number of elements fits the bay;
  4. non-typical zones are identified: corners, ground floor, plant floors, the crown of the building;
  5. the number of sizes is reduced to a minimum and the layout is handed over to production.

For GFRC facade panels standardisation is particularly important: every new mould costs money and time, while a repeating bay allows hundreds of identical elements to be cast. The outcome of this work is a set of fabrication detail drawings from which the elements are manufactured and marked.

Interface between cladding and glazed structures

On a business centre the ventilated cladding almost always adjoins curtain walling, and the junction of the two systems is the most critical place on the facade. They are installed by different contractors working to different tolerances, so the junction detail is designed jointly and before procurement begins.

  • Sequence. Curtain walling and windows are usually installed first, then the subframe and cladding; the reverse order is stipulated in the design.
  • Tolerances. The building frame, the curtain wall and the cladding have different accuracies, and the detail must absorb the cumulative deviation without cutting on site.
  • Weathertightness. Water from the cladding must not reach the frame, and the vapour control and waterproofing layers at the junction must not be interrupted.
  • Fire protection. Spandrel zones and the surrounds of openings are built to the requirements for the specific system, including protection of the cavity.
  • Movement. The curtain wall and the ventilated facade subframe move independently; a rigid connection between them is unacceptable.

Standard solutions for these joints are examined in the article on junction details of a ventilated facade.

Building services and signage: what to provide for in the facade design of a business centre

An office building is full of equipment that needs an outlet on the facade. If places for it are not provided in advance, grilles and brackets appear haphazardly and destroy the architectural image.

  • Air intake and exhaust grilles — in the plane of the cladding, on the same joint grid, or behind decorative fins.
  • Plant floors and rooftop equipment are enclosed by screens designed for wind load.
  • Outdoor units of tenants’ air conditioners are placed in designated recesses with organised condensate drainage.
  • Advertising and wayfinding signs are fixed to embedded parts provided in the subframe, not to the cladding.
  • For tall buildings the method of facade access is chosen in advance: cradles, anchor points for rope access technicians, permanent maintenance systems.

In the capital the external appearance of a building and the placement of signage require approval; the procedure is described in the article on facade approval in Moscow. For buildings beside main roads the design separately checks the protection of rooms from noise — see the article on facade sound insulation.

Mock-up and decision stages for the developer

Major decisions on the facade of a business centre are taken in three steps: concept, full-scale mock-up, working documentation. The earlier the facade contractor is brought into the project, the less rework there is at the final stages.

At concept stage the materials, the modelling of the facade and the budget limits are determined. A full-size fragment of the facade is then produced — one or two bays with a window, joints and lighting. It is used to approve colour and texture in daylight and evening light and to check the details and buildability. A change made on the mock-up costs incomparably less than the same change after series production has started.

Once the mock-up is approved, the detailed facade design is carried out, and moulds and long-lead materials, above all stone, are ordered. What makes up the facade budget of a business centre

The cost of an office building facade is determined by more than the square metres of cladding. The estimate accounts separately for several groups of costs, and the ratio between them depends on the architecture.

  • Design: layout, calculations, details, fabrication drawings of the elements and the full-scale mock-up.
  • Subframe and fixings: profile material, projection from the wall, bracket spacing for the weight of the cladding.
  • Insulation and fire protection elements.
  • Cladding of plain areas and, separately, non-typical elements: corners, fins, surrounds, cornices, the crown of the building.
  • Moulds and tooling for GFRC elements; their cost is spread over the production run.
  • The entrance and the stone plinth, including selection and delivery of the material.
  • Installation, access equipment, working in restricted urban conditions.

The main lever for savings is repetition. The fewer the sizes and the larger the run from each mould, the lower the price of an element for the same appearance. The second lever is the distribution of materials by zone: costly solutions are concentrated where they are seen at close range — on the lower floors and at the entrance — while the upper tiers are handled by simpler means in the same colour and texture. Prices are not published on the company’s website: the estimate is prepared from a design or a sketch.

Typical mistakes in designing the facade of an office building

Mistakes on the facade of a business centre are rarely connected with strength — more often with the fact that decisions were taken separately and at different times.

  • Materials are chosen from a render, without samples and without assessing how the surface will look after several years in an urban environment.
  • The budget is calculated on the area of plain cladding, without corners, surrounds, the crown and the entrance, which account for a noticeable share of the cost.
  • The layout is not coordinated with the glazing module, and make-up pieces appear on site.
  • Lighting and signage are added after installation, with cable run over the finished cladding.
  • Access for washing and repair has not been thought through: part of the facade cannot be reached without costly equipment.
  • Stone for the plinth is purchased without checking the frost resistance and water absorption of the batch.

The general way to avoid them is to run the facade as a separate section of the project with a single responsible contractor. When the architectural part, subframe, cladding and lighting are brought together in one model, contradictions are visible on the drawing rather than on the scaffolding. For the developer this means a clear budget and programme without supplementary agreements for corrections.

FAQ

What material should be used to clad a business centre?

Porcelain stoneware or GFRC for the main wall areas, GFRC for accents and complex modelling, natural stone for the plinth and entrance. All materials must be non-combustible within a certified system.

How does an office building facade differ from a residential one?

More attention to image and the class of the property, a larger share of glazing, higher demands on the entrance and on the maintenance regime.

Can lighting be built into business centre cladding?

Yes, if fixings and cable routes are provided in the rainscreen facade design: wiring runs in the cavity behind the cladding, and luminaires are built into the ornament or recesses.

How much does it cost to clad a business centre?

The cost depends on area, materials, number of storeys and complexity of the ornament. TECHNOFORMA determines it after inspecting the site or reviewing the design.

At what stage should a facade contractor be engaged for a business centre?

Ideally at concept or design documentation stage, before the budget is fixed. At that point it is still possible to tie the cladding layout to the glazing module, select a subframe for the weight of the materials and estimate the cost of non-typical zones.

Why is a full-scale mock-up of a facade fragment needed?

It makes it possible to approve colour, texture, joint width and lighting at real scale and in natural light, and to check the details for buildability. Correcting a solution on the mock-up is considerably cheaper than after series production has started.

Can the facade of an operating business centre be renewed without moving the tenants out?

As a rule, yes. The work is carried out in sections with protection of entrances and walkways, and noisy operations are done at agreed hours. The walls and existing fixings are surveyed beforehand to determine what can be retained.
