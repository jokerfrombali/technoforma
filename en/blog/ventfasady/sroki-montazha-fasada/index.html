Facade Installation Timescales: How to Plan

Facade installation timescales are determined not by how fast the cladding is hung but by the whole chain: design and approvals, manufacture and delivery of materials, substrate preparation, substructure and insulation installation, cladding, acceptance. For a multi-storey building this means months; for a large complex, one or two construction seasons or more.

The main planning principle is that facade preparation starts long before the installers arrive: the layout, calculation and cladding order are launched while the shell is still being built. This article is for clients, general contractors and project managers who draw up the construction schedule and want to understand what drives the duration of facade work.

How rainscreen systems work is in the Ventilated rainscreen facades section.

What affects the timescale

The duration of facade work is affected by:

  • Facade area and complexity. The number of openings, corners, projections and cornices. Complex facades are installed more slowly than blank planes.
  • Cladding type. Standard porcelain stoneware and sheet materials come from stock; GFRC and stone are made-to-design products that require layout, manufacture and marking.
  • Building readiness. Shell, windows, roof junctions, services penetrating the facade. Cladding cannot start before windows are installed — reveal details cannot be assembled.
  • Documentation. The rainscreen facade design, approval of the architectural appearance where required, approval of the layout and samples by the client.
  • Access equipment. How many work fronts can be opened at once.
  • Crew numbers and site organisation.
  • Season and weather.

The timescale for a specific project becomes clear after inspection and layout development — which is why TECHNOFORMA determines it, like the cost, based on a survey of the building.

Production and logistics

For made-to-design products, production and logistics are the critical path of the schedule.

  • Layout and working drawings. The facade is divided into elements, and each is given a mark. For GFRC and stone the layout must reflect the building's actual geometry — based on measurements and geodetic survey.
  • Tooling. Models and moulds are made for GFRC. At TECHNOFORMA they are made in the company's own mould shops, so this stage does not depend on third-party contractors.
  • Production. According to the company, TECHNOFORMA's GFRC production capacity exceeds 20,000 m² per month, with quality control at every stage. Elements are produced in installation sequence, not "all at once".
  • Stone. Stock items are delivered faster; stone ordered from abroad takes longer, which must be allowed for at the start.
  • Logistics. Delivery, receipt, on-site storage, lifting to level. The company's warehouses are located from Novorossiysk and Makhachkala to Novosibirsk and Yekaterinburg.

A proper delivery schedule is synchronised with installation sections: the site receives elements for the next sections, not the entire volume at once, otherwise the site turns into a warehouse and the products into a breakage risk.

Seasonality

Seasonality primarily affects wet processes and working conditions at height.

  • Dry processes — installing brackets, insulation and rails and hanging the cladding — are possible all year round on rainscreen facades, subject to safety measures and the temperature limits set for materials and equipment.
  • Chemical anchors, sealants and painting have temperature limits.
  • Wind and ice restrict cradle work and lifting of large elements.
  • Short daylight hours reduce output.

That is why facade work usually goes more slowly in winter than in summer. This must be taken into account in planning: moving the start of installation from September to December extends the overall duration more than the calendar suggests. A practical strategy is to cover the insulation and membrane with cladding on at least some facades before the cold sets in, and to continue dry processes on protected sections in winter.

Parallel work

The facade schedule is built around sections — areas of the facade where all operations are carried out in sequence. While brackets are being installed on one section, the next is being insulated and a third is being clad.

  • Each crew follows the previous one across the facade without getting in its way.
  • Insulation is covered by cladding quickly, without standing exposed to rain.
  • Concealed works are accepted section by section, not in one record for the whole facade.
  • Production supplies elements for the next section.

On large projects, work runs in parallel on different buildings and facades. Facade work is also combined with interior finishing: once the envelope is closed — windows and insulated walls — finishing can proceed inside in heated rooms. Parallel work is limited by safety: work must not be carried out on the same vertical at different levels without protection.

Risks of delay

The main causes of delays in facade work:

  • late start of design and layout — the cladding is ordered when the shell is already finished;
  • changes to the architectural design after production has started;
  • building not ready: windows not installed, roof junctions not completed;
  • low anchor test results discovered too late;
  • lack of coordination between the cladding manufacturer, substructure designer and installer;
  • shortage of access equipment;
  • delivery delays, especially of imported materials.

The "one contractor for the full cycle" model — design, production, logistics, installation — reduces the number of interfaces where time is lost. This is how TECHNOFORMA works: design department, mould shops, production, logistics and its own installation teams.

How to calculate the duration of facade installation

Installation time is derived from labour input rather than from the calendar: the quantity of each operation is divided by the output of one crew and by the number of crews that can actually fit on the work front. The resulting days are then combined, bearing in mind that operations follow one another with an offset rather than strictly in sequence.

Output is not a reference value; it is specific to the project. On a blank wall with large-format cladding a crew covers several times more area per shift than on a facade with frequent openings, pylons and cornices. The calculation is therefore made not for the building as a whole but by type of area: plain wall, zones around openings, corners, plinth, parapet and decorative elements.

OperationWhat determines output
Setting out and fixing bracketsWall material, number of anchors per square metre, deviations of the substrate
Insulation and membraneNumber of layers, amount of cutting around brackets and openings
RailsAccuracy of plane alignment, number of profile sizes
Hanging the claddingFormat and weight of the element, fixing method, need for lifting equipment
Reveals, sills, parapetsNumber of openings and length of junctions

Operations that produce no square metres but still take time are listed separately: erecting and moving scaffolding, hoisting materials, surveying, anchor pull-out tests and handover of concealed works.

It is convenient to present the result not as a single date but as a range with its conditions stated: the number of work fronts, shift pattern and delivery schedule under which it is achievable. The client can then see by what means the period can be shortened and what that would require in terms of site organisation.

Which milestones should a facade works programme include?

A working programme rests on milestones: events without which the next stage cannot begin. If a milestone is not tied to a date and a responsible party, slippage is discovered only when there is nothing left to offset it with.

  1. Approval of the architectural design and cladding samples; after this point any change is paid for in time.
  2. As-built survey of the walls, which is the basis for the cladding layout and the choice of bracket projections.
  3. Issue of working drawings and shop drawings for the elements, which starts production.
  4. Tooling and the first batch of elements ready; trial assembly or a reference area.
  5. Handover of the work front: closed envelope, windows installed, services brought out to the facade.
  6. Erection of access equipment and admission of the crews.
  7. Acceptance of concealed works on each section and final handover of the facade.

It is useful to show the dependencies between milestones: which delivery is tied to which section and which adjacent trade has to clear the area. The detailed sequence of operations is described in the article on the stages of ventilated facade installation.

Facade installation time for different types of cladding

The type of cladding changes not so much the speed of hanging as the part of the programme that becomes critical. For stock materials the bottleneck is installation; for project-specific elements it is production preparation.

CladdingWhat determines the timeWhat to watch
Porcelain stonewareInstallers' output and the amount of cuttingAvailability of the required batch and calibre for the whole quantity
Composite and metal cassettesCutting and fabrication of cassettes to site measurementsAccuracy of measurements, trim elements
Natural stoneSupply of blocks or slabs, sawing and finishingImport lead times, tone matching, spare stock for replacement
GFRC panels and decorative elementsModels, moulds, mould turnoverNumber of element types, order of output by section

For glass fibre reinforced concrete the key parameter is the number of unique moulds. The higher the repetition of elements, the faster the output, since one mould is used many times. Standardising the GFRC facade panels at the layout stage therefore often shortens the programme more than adding installation crews.

For stone the timing is largely set by logistics: material from Turkey, Iran, India or China takes considerably longer to reach the site than material from a warehouse in Russia.

How much time reserve should the programme contain?

A reserve is needed wherever an event does not depend on the contractor: weather, readiness of adjacent trades, customs clearance, approvals. There is no universal percentage; in practice the reserve is distributed across the stages rather than added as a single line at the end of the programme.

  • For design: time for one or two rounds of comments from the client and the architect.
  • For production: an allowance for remaking individual elements and for items damaged in transit.
  • For installation: days lost to wind, precipitation and low temperatures, which are unavoidable for work at height.
  • For handover: time to clear the supervisor's comments and compile the as-built documentation.

If the completion date is fixed, the reserve is created with resources rather than with the calendar: an additional work front, a second shift in production, scaffolding delivered in advance. The choice between scaffolding, cradles and mast climbers also affects the pace; this is covered in a separate article on facade access equipment.

How to tie the facade schedule to the overall construction programme?

The facade programme is part of the overall project programme, and its bottlenecks are found more often at the interfaces with other trades than within the facade works themselves. The first things to check are the intersections with windows, roofing, lifting equipment and external works.

  • Windows and glazed screens. Their installation sets the moment from which reveals can be closed; a delay by the window contractor stops cladding on all sections at once.
  • Roof and parapets. The top rows of cladding and the parapet cappings are completed after the roof junctions, otherwise the detail has to be dismantled.
  • Tower crane and hoists. Their ties to the building leave unclad strips on the facade, which are finished after the equipment is removed.
  • External works and utilities. Scaffolding occupies a strip along the wall, so the perimeter paving, entrance areas and external utility runs are planned around its removal.

On developments with several blocks the crews are moved from block to block as work fronts become available. This arrangement avoids idle time but requires deliveries of elements to follow the same order.

How to monitor progress during facade works

A delay is easier to recover in the first week than in the last month. For this the programme is kept in physical quantities for each operation, and plan is compared with actual at least once a week.

  • Records by section: how many square metres of brackets, insulation, rails and cladding have been completed on each.
  • Control of the gap between operations: if insulation runs far ahead of cladding, the insulation is left exposed and the money is tied up.
  • Reconciliation of deliveries with installation: what is on site, what is in transit, what is in production.
  • A register of comments and the deadline for clearing them.
  • A weekly meeting with adjacent contractors on handover of work fronts.

When design, production and ventilated facade installation are carried out by one organisation, these data are gathered in one place. For this TECHNOFORMA has its own production facility in the Moscow region, 12 design engineers and an installation division of more than 100 specialists.

A convenient tool is a programme in the form of a section-by-operation table in which each cell receives a planned and an actual completion date. It shows at once which operation is holding back the others: usually it is not the cladding but the preceding stage or a late delivery of trim elements.

What to prepare so that the contractor can state a realistic period

A period quoted without input data is an estimate by analogy. To obtain a programme that can be relied upon, the contractor is given:

  • architectural facade drawings, sections and details where they have been developed;
  • information on the material and condition of the walls, the number of storeys and the levels;
  • the programme for the structural frame, window installation and roofing;
  • cladding requirements: material, texture, colour, format;
  • site constraints: storage space, access, crane operation, working hours;
  • the desired completion date and the order of the blocks.

These data are used to draw up a preliminary programme, which is refined after the building survey and development of the cladding layout.

The earlier this information reaches the contractor, the more preparatory work can be taken out of the installation period: the elements are manufactured in parallel with construction of the structure, and the crews arrive on site with the first batch already available.

FAQ

How long does it take to install the facade of a multi-storey building?

It depends on area, cladding, complexity, number of crews and season. For a residential building it is usually months; the exact timescale is determined after a survey and layout.

When should facade cladding be ordered?

Layout and ordering of bespoke products — GFRC, stone — should start while the shell is being built, so that production does not become the critical path.

Can a facade be installed in winter?

Dry rainscreen processes are possible all year round within the temperature limits of the materials, but output is lower and wet processes are restricted.

How is a facade work schedule drawn up?

By sections: on each, brackets, insulation, rails and cladding follow in sequence, while crews work in parallel on different sections.

What most often delays facade installation?

Late design, architectural changes after production has started, the building not being ready and delivery delays.

Can facade installation be speeded up simply by adding people?

Only up to the limit set by the work front and by deliveries. A limited number of crews can work on one section, and project-specific elements arrive at the pace of production. It is usually more effective to open an additional work front and to increase the repetition of elements.

How long does preparation take before the installers arrive?

It depends on the cladding. For stock materials it is mainly design and procurement; for GFRC and stone, layout, tooling, manufacture and delivery are added. Preparation is therefore started while the structural frame is being built.

Is a reference area of the facade necessary?

On large projects it is useful: details, colour, texture and workmanship are checked on a small fragment before bulk works begin. The time spent on the reference area is repaid by the absence of rework.
