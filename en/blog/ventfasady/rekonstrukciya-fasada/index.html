Facade Refurbishment: Replacing Cladding and Adding New Ornament

Facade refurbishment is a package of works to replace cladding, add insulation and renew the architectural appearance of an existing building. Unlike cosmetic repairs, it involves the structure: the old finish is removed, the substrate is surveyed and strengthened where necessary, and then a new system is installed, most often a ventilated rainscreen facade with new cladding and ornament.

The key to a successful project is reliable data on the load-bearing capacity of the walls. It determines what cladding and ornament the building can carry and how many anchors the substructure will need.

This article is for owners of commercial buildings, management companies, clients of major repair works and architects. More on the systems in Ventilated rainscreen facades.

Survey

Refurbishment starts with an instrumental survey of the facade. It is carried out by a specialist organisation, and the result is a technical report on which the design is based.

  • Visual inspection: cracks, delaminating finishes, signs of leaks, corrosion of metal elements, condition of joints and reveals.
  • Substrate strength: by non-destructive methods and opening-up; for brick and concrete the grade and condition are assessed, for hollow blocks whether anchoring is possible.
  • Anchor pull-out tests: the key data for designing the rainscreen substructure. The anchor is chosen for the actual wall material, not the one in the original building design.
  • Surveying: deviations of wall planes from vertical and from each other, which determine bracket projection.
  • Thermal assessment: whether additional insulation is needed and of what thickness.

Skipping the survey is the most common cause of cost overruns: unexpectedly weak masonry or voids are discovered during installation and require redesign.

Removal of old cladding

Old cladding is removed according to a works organisation plan, bearing in mind that the building often stays in use.

  • Tiles, render and old panels are removed from the top down, with the danger zone fenced off and entrances protected by canopies.
  • Attached items such as air conditioners, signs, cables and downpipes are temporarily removed or relocated in agreement with their owners.
  • A decision is made on existing ornament: keep it, restore it, or remove and reproduce it. If historic elements need to be replicated, measurements or casts are taken and used to make copies.
  • Construction waste is sorted and removed; special disposal requirements may apply to certain materials (for example, old insulation).

Full removal is not always necessary: if the old cladding is firmly attached and does not interfere with the substructure, the new rainscreen facade is sometimes installed over it, with brackets fixed through it into the load-bearing substrate. The decision is based on the survey results.

If the building remains in operation during the works, removal and installation are done in sections: the facade is divided into zones, and entrances, office windows and escape routes are protected with canopies and nets. This schedule is agreed in advance with tenants and the facilities team.

Strengthening the substrate

If the survey shows that the walls cannot carry the load of the new system or the anchors do not achieve the required pull-out force, the substrate is strengthened:

  • Masonry repair: filling cracks, rebuilding damaged areas, injection.
  • Different anchors: chemical anchors for hollow and weak materials instead of expansion anchors.
  • Fixing into floor slabs: when the wall is weak but the edges of concrete floor slabs are sound, the substructure is supported on the floor bands.
  • Steel frame: for heavy cladding and large ornament, an additional frame is installed to transfer loads to the load-bearing structure.

All strengthening decisions are made in the design with calculations; increasing anchor spacing or substituting anchors on site without authorisation is not acceptable.

The choice of cladding and the strengthening method are linked: heavy natural stone may require a frame where lighter GFRC panels or porcelain stoneware can use a standard substructure. That is why it is useful to compare cladding options at the survey stage, together with an estimate of strengthening costs.

Approvals

Changing a building's external appearance almost always requires approval:

  • Architectural appearance: in large cities, facade changes are approved by the architecture authorities under local rules; in Moscow, many buildings are subject to the AGO (architectural and urban appearance) approval procedure.
  • Apartment buildings: the facade is part of the common property, so a decision of the general meeting of owners is required.
  • Listed heritage buildings: work is only carried out to a design approved by the heritage protection authority, with restrictions on materials and methods.
  • Expert review: if refurbishment affects structural safety, the design may require expert review.

If tenants' signs and equipment are placed on the facade, their new locations should be included in the design from the start to avoid a second approval.

When facade reconstruction is needed and when repair is enough

Reconstruction is justified when the defects are related to the finishing system itself or when the requirements for the building change in terms of thermal performance, fire safety or appearance. If the damage is local and the substrate is sound, repair with retention of the existing cladding is usually sufficient.

SituationUsually sufficientWhen reconstruction is chosen
Isolated chips, soiling, efflorescenceCleaning, local repair, protective treatmentDefects recur over the whole area and are linked to moisture in the wall
Detachment of tiles or renderReplacement of areas after soundingDetachment is widespread and there is a risk of falling fragments
Cracks in the finishMaking good once the cause is establishedCracks originate in the substrate or from movement of the building
High heat lossRepair of joints and junctionsNew thermal insulation is needed over the whole wall area
Change of use or class of the building—A new appearance and compliance with current codes are required

A facade survey helps to tell one from the other: on its basis the cost of recurring repairs over several years is compared with the cost of replacing the system completely. The causes of cracking should be established before a solution is chosen; they are examined in detail in the article on cracks in facades.

There is also an intermediate option, partial reconstruction. The new system is installed on the main elevation, the plinth or the entrance, while the courtyard walls are repaired with the finish retained. This approach reduces the budget, but it requires careful treatment of the junctions between old and new cladding at the corners and at the parapet, and a check that the different parts of the wall will be equally protected against moisture and frost penetration.

What load new cladding adds to an old building

A ventilated facade adds the weight of the subframe, insulation and cladding to the wall, and it is applied eccentrically, at the bracket projection. For the building as a whole this addition is usually small compared with the weight of the walls and floors, but for individual elements it may prove significant.

  • Piers and lintels. Narrow sections of masonry between windows take load from brackets on both sides; they are checked first.
  • Parapets and eaves overhangs. Old parapets are often not designed for the fixing of heavy elements or for the wind load from new cladding.
  • Balcony slabs and canopies. Lining and decor on them are specified only after the condition of the reinforcement and the slab bearing has been assessed.
  • External panels of framed and panel buildings. Curtain and sandwich panels have limited capacity in the outer leaf; brackets are fixed to the load-bearing leaf or to the floor slabs.
  • Foundations. A check is needed with heavy stone cladding over a great height and where there are signs of differential settlement.

In practice the question is answered at two levels: whether a particular area of wall can hold an anchor, and whether the structure can accept the total load. The first is answered by anchor pull-out testing, the second by a verification calculation based on the survey. Lightweight thin-walled GFRC panels widen the choice in this respect: relief and decor can be obtained without the load typical of solid stone.

What to do about windows, reveals and building services

Windows are replaced before the new cladding is installed or at the same time, but not afterwards: the reveals, sills and fire lining of the opening are tied to the position of the window frame. If window replacement is postponed, the detail has to be dismantled and rebuilt.

  • Window position. When insulation is added the opening becomes deeper; where possible the frame is moved towards the outer face of the wall to reduce heat loss through the reveal.
  • Sills and sill elements. Their projection is set from the new cladding plane, with a drip and a fall.
  • Air conditioning. Outdoor units are moved onto brackets fixed to the substrate or into baskets and recesses built into the panel layout; pipe runs are concealed before the cladding is hung.
  • Rainwater goods. Outlets, downpipes and fixings are checked; discharges must not wet the new plinth.
  • Lightning protection, low-voltage networks, lighting. Down conductors, cables and cast-in fixings for luminaires are included in the design before the cladding is ordered.

The most frequent problem is tenants' equipment that returns to the facade after handover and is fixed through the new cladding. The rules for placement, the fixing locations and the responsible parties are best set out in the lease agreements and in the facade passport.

What stages reconstruction consists of and what affects the programme

The duration is determined not so much by the area as by the number of work sections, access to the walls and the readiness of the input data. In general terms the sequence of works is as follows:

  1. Survey, anchor tests, measured survey and geodetic survey of the walls.
  2. Concept for the new appearance, choice of cladding and approvals.
  3. Working documentation: subframe, insulation, panel layout, details and shop drawings of the units.
  4. Production of models, moulds and units; supply of the subframe and insulation.
  5. Site preparation: scaffolding or cradles, protective canopies and temporary hoardings.
  6. Stripping out, repair and strengthening of the substrate section by section.
  7. Installation of brackets, insulation, fire barriers, rails and cladding.
  8. Junctions, reveals, sills, reinstatement of equipment and handover.

Design and production run in parallel with site preparation, so it pays to decide on the cladding early: bespoke decor needs time for models and moulds. Subframe installation and panel hanging are possible in cold weather, whereas wet operations such as masonry repair, injection and sealing depend on temperature. The thermal envelope is closed under a separate package; the sequence of works is described on the facade insulation page.

The programme is also noticeably affected by how access to the walls is organised. Scaffolding allows stripping out, substrate repair and installation to proceed on one section without repositioning, but it occupies the pavement and requires approval; cradles and lifts are quicker to deploy, yet they are inconvenient for strengthening masonry and installing heavy elements. For buildings standing on the street line, the access scheme and the delivery times for materials are determined as early as the construction method statement.

How the budget for facade reconstruction is formed

A reconstruction estimate differs from that for a new facade in that it contains items absent on a new build and carries a greater share of uncertainty. The accuracy of the estimate improves as survey data become available.

  • Survey, testing and measured survey work.
  • Design and approvals.
  • Stripping out, removal and disposal of the old finish.
  • Repair and strengthening of the substrate, the item with the widest spread.
  • Subframe, insulation, membrane and fire protection elements.
  • Cladding and decor, including tooling for bespoke units.
  • Access equipment, protection of entrances and work in confined conditions.
  • Relocation of equipment and reinstatement of landscaping next to the walls.

The total is influenced most by the condition of the walls, the type of cladding and whether the building remains in operation during the works. A contingency for concealed defects is included in advance and refined after opening-up. The cost structure of the system itself is examined in the article on what makes up the cost of a ventilated facade.

Contractors' offers are worth comparing only where the scope of works is identical: stripping out, strengthening of the substrate and access equipment are often left outside the quotation.

Common mistakes when replacing the cladding of an existing building

Most mistakes stem from an attempt to transfer to an old building solutions that have been proven on new builds. Before a project is launched it is useful to check it against this list.

  • The cladding is selected and ordered before the anchor test results are received.
  • The panel layout is based on archive drawings without a measured survey: the actual dimensions and levels of the openings differ.
  • Deviations of the walls are not allowed for, and the bracket projection is insufficient to true up the plane.
  • Old insulation or a combustible finish is left beneath the new system without assessing the fire requirements.
  • Drainage from the roof and parapet is not resolved, and the new cladding gets wet just as the old one did.
  • Historic decor is removed without measurement or casts, and it cannot be accurately reproduced from photographs.

If the building has historic value the approach changes: the task is not to replace but to preserve and make good. The differences in method are described in the article on restoration of a historic facade.

FAQ

Where do you start when upgrading a building facade?

With an instrumental survey: wall strength, anchor pull-out tests, surveying. Based on the results, the system and cladding are chosen and the design is developed.

What can replace travertine on a facade?

Limestone or another dense sedimentary stone of a similar shade, travertine-look porcelain stoneware, or glass fibre reinforced concrete with a travertine texture. The choice depends on the goal: keeping natural stone or reducing weight and cost.

Can a ventilated facade be installed over old cladding?

Sometimes, yes, if the old finish is firmly attached and the brackets can be fixed through it into the load-bearing substrate. The decision is based on the survey results.

Does replacing facade cladding need approval?

As a rule, yes: changes to the external appearance are approved by the architecture authorities, apartment buildings need an owners' decision, and listed buildings need approval from the heritage protection authority.

Can lost ornament be restored?

Yes. Moulds are made from measurements or casts, and copies of the elements are cast in glass fibre reinforced concrete.

Can a facade be reconstructed without interrupting the operation of the building?

Yes, this is common practice for office and retail buildings. The works are carried out section by section, entrances and escape routes are protected with canopies, and noisy operations are scheduled for agreed hours. This lengthens the programme and increases the cost of organising the works, which is allowed for in the estimate.

Do windows have to be replaced during facade reconstruction?

Not necessarily, but the decision is taken before the facade works begin. If the windows are near the end of their service life they are replaced before the cladding is installed or together with it, because the reveals, sills and linings of the openings are tied to the position of the window frame.

Will an old building carry natural stone cladding?

This is shown by the survey, the anchor tests and a verification calculation. Stone is considerably heavier than other claddings, so for weak walls the options considered are bearing on the floor slabs, an additional frame or lighter materials with a stone texture.
