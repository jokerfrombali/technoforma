Facade Inspection: When and How It Is Done

A facade inspection (condition survey) is an assessment of the technical condition of the external walls, cladding, fixings and insulation, concluding whether the facade is fit for continued use and listing the work required. It is carried out before repair or reconstruction, when buying a building, after emergencies and when defects appear.

The main criterion of survey quality is completeness of access: a facade inspected only from the ground gives no information on the condition of anchors, brackets and insulation. A reliable conclusion requires selective opening-up and instrumental testing.

The article is aimed at owners, property managers, developers and technical supervision. Related articles: Facade maintenance, standards and repair.

Reasons for a survey

The main reasons for a survey:

  • Defects — cracks, delamination, falling cladding elements, rust streaks, freezing and damp walls on the inside.
  • Preparing for major repair or reconstruction — to decide whether to repair the existing system or replace it, and to obtain input data for the design.
  • Change of ownership or acceptance — assessing the condition when buying a building or handing it over for operation.
  • Adding storeys, cutting openings, mounting heavy equipment on walls — the load-bearing capacity of the substrate must be confirmed.
  • Emergencies — fire, earthquake, hurricane, flooding, construction nearby.
  • Routine monitoring — periodic inspection as part of operation, especially for high-rise buildings and rainscreen systems above pedestrian areas.
  • Dispute with a contractor — recording defects during the warranty period.

A special case is rainscreen facades installed without a complete set of as-built documentation. If it is unknown which anchors and brackets were used, at what spacing and in what substrate, it is impossible to judge the safety margin. Here the survey fills the gaps: opening-up and testing provide actual data from which the system can be assessed by calculation.

The earlier the survey is done, the more options the owner has: at an early stage defects can usually be fixed by local repair, while a neglected condition may require complete replacement of the system.

Method

A survey usually proceeds in three stages.

  1. Preparation. Collecting documents: design, as-built drawings, material certificates and data sheets, records of concealed work, inspection logs. Studying the history of the building — repairs, incidents.
  2. Preliminary (visual) inspection. A full inspection of the facade, mapping defects on elevation drawings, photo records, tapping the cladding, identifying zones that need detailed study.
  3. Detailed instrumental survey. Selective opening-up of the rainscreen cladding to inspect the substructure, anchors, insulation and wind barrier; measurements of strength, moisture and deviations; anchor pull-out tests; if necessary, sampling for laboratory analysis.

The procedure and scope of building surveys are governed by the Russian standard GOST 31937 "Buildings and structures. Rules of inspection and monitoring of technical condition". Access to the facade is provided by an aerial platform, a gondola, scaffolding or rope-access technicians; opening-up locations are chosen to cover different orientations, floors and typical details.

Instruments

The set of instruments is chosen for the task; a typical kit:

Thermal imaging is carried out when there is a marked difference between indoor and outdoor temperatures — during the heating season, without direct sun on the facade.

Report

The result of the survey is a technical report (conclusion). It usually contains:

  • a description of the building and the facade construction, a list of documents reviewed;
  • the method and list of instruments used;
  • a schedule of defects referenced to gridlines and levels, facade elevations with a defect map, photographs;
  • results of instrumental measurements and laboratory tests;
  • an assessment of the technical condition category;
  • conclusions on the causes of defects;
  • recommendations: urgent safety measures, repair, strengthening, replacement of the system, further monitoring.

A good report makes it possible to move straight on to design and cost estimating: it shows which elements to replace, in what quantity and why.

Decisions

Based on the survey, one of the following decisions is made:

  • Continued use without changes with scheduled inspections — if the defects are minor.
  • Routine repair — filling cracks, replacing individual cladding elements, restoring sealing, cleaning and water-repellent treatment.
  • Substructure repair — replacing corroded brackets and anchors, adding fixing points, restoring insulation.
  • Replacing the facade system — with widespread defects or if the wall needs insulating: removing the old cladding and installing a ventilated rainscreen facade.
  • Urgent measures — fencing off the danger zone, protective nets, removing unsafe elements.

When the system is replaced, the survey data — substrate strength, anchor pull-out results, wall geometry — are used in the substructure design.

It is convenient to split the report's recommendations by urgency: what to do immediately, what before next winter, what within the major repair plan. This order helps to allocate the budget without postponing work that affects the safety of people below the facade.

Technical condition categories of a facade: what does the survey conclusion mean?

The outcome of a survey is the assignment of the structure to one of the technical condition categories under GOST 31937. The category determines whether the facade can remain in use and how urgent the works are.

CategoryWhat it meansFacade exampleActions
NormativeParameters comply with the design and standardsCladding and fixings free of defectsScheduled inspections
ServiceableThere are deviations that do not reduce load-bearing capacityIsolated chips, soiling, local wear of sealantRoutine repair
Limited serviceabilityDefects reduce reliability; use is possible under monitoringCorrosion of some brackets, displaced slabs, wet insulationRepair or strengthening, monitoring
EmergencyThere is a risk of collapseWidespread failure of fixings, detachment of claddingFencing off the zone, dismantling, system replacement

The category is assigned not to the building as a whole but to a specific structure, so different areas of one facade may receive different ratings. This makes it possible to plan repairs section by section, starting with the most hazardous zones.

The assigned category must be accompanied by a justification: which defects were found, in what quantity, and why they do or do not affect load-bearing capacity. A conclusion without a calculation or a reference to test results is hard to use in a dispute with a contractor or when defending a repair budget.

Where and how many openings are made when surveying a ventilated facade?

The number of openings is set by the survey programme: it depends on the facade area, the number of construction types and the defects found during inspection. There is no single figure per thousand square metres, but the principle is common — every characteristic zone and all areas showing signs of trouble are opened up.

  • the typical plane at different heights — to assess the general condition;
  • building corners and upper floors — zones of maximum wind load;
  • the parapet and roof junction — where wetting is most common;
  • the plinth and bottom row of cladding — splashes, de-icing agents, mechanical damage;
  • window reveals and details at balconies — complex junctions;
  • places with rust streaks, displaced slabs, or damp patches inside.

At each opening the type and spacing of brackets, the presence of thermal break pads, the condition of anchors, insulation and membrane, and the size of the ventilated cavity are recorded. Control pull-out tests are performed on some of the anchors. After inspection the cladding is reinstated, so thought is given in advance to how to replace elements that cannot be removed without damage.

Opening locations are marked on the facade elevations and numbered so that results can be unambiguously tied to an area. Each opening is photographed before and after, with a scale rule.

The number of anchors tested and the control load are set by the programme; the results are compared with the design values or, where there is no design, with loads obtained by a verification calculation.

How does the survey of stone, GFRC and render facades differ?

The methodology is common, but the list of characteristic defects and the inspection methods depend on the cladding material.

  • Natural stone. The condition of slabs at the fixing zone is checked — cracks from kerfs and dowel holes, corrosion of cast-in parts, efflorescence and biological growth. For slabs bedded in mortar, tapping reveals delamination.
  • Glass fibre reinforced concrete. Cast-in parts and the hanging details of panels and decoration, the joints between elements and the surface condition are examined: networks of hairline cracks, chipped edges, colour change. Repair methods are described in the article on repairing chips and cracks in GFRC.
  • Porcelain stoneware and cassettes. The main focus is on clips, rivets and rails; slab play and corrosion of fixings are checked.
  • Rendered insulation systems. Tapping locates delamination; cracks and the condition of the reinforcing layer and junctions are assessed.

On facades with architectural decoration the projecting elements — cornices, string courses, balusters, corbels — are inspected separately. They get wetter, snow and ice accumulate on them, and the consequences of a fall are more serious than with a flat slab. The integrity of the product itself, the condition of cast-in parts and anchors, the tightness of the top joints and the presence of flashings are checked.

Cracks in any material are classified by cause: shrinkage, thermal, structural, or related to settlement of the building. This determines whether cosmetic repair is sufficient; see the article on facade cracks for more.

Whatever the cladding type, the condition of the joints is also assessed: open, sealant-filled or mortar-filled, they show how the facade copes with thermal movement.

What should the client prepare before a facade survey?

The more complete the input data, the more accurate the work programme and the shorter the timescale. Before approaching a surveyor it is worth gathering the documents and stating the purpose: the depth of the survey depends on it.

  1. Purpose: assessment before repair, a dispute with a contractor, purchase of a building, complaints about cold walls, scheduled control.
  2. Documents: the facade design, as-built drawings, concealed works certificates, material certificates — whatever has survived.
  3. Operating history: year of installation, repairs carried out, incidents of falling elements, leaks.
  4. Access conditions: whether a platform or cradle can be set up, anchorage points on the roof, restrictions on working hours.
  5. Access to rooms — for thermal imaging from inside and inspection of the walls.
  6. Spare cladding to close the openings, if available.

It is also useful to settle organisational matters in advance: who accompanies the specialists on the client's behalf, how fencing of pavements is agreed, and who reinstates the cladding after opening-up — the surveyor or the facilities team.

The brief states whether only a report is required or also design solutions for repair. In the latter case it makes sense to entrust the survey to an organisation that carries out construction, installation and facade works and can assess the quantities straight away.

What do the timescale and cost of a facade survey depend on?

Cost is determined by labour input, not area alone: two facades of the same size may require different amounts of work. An accurate estimate is given after reviewing the building and its documentation.

  • building height and the access method — which often accounts for a significant share of the cost;
  • the number of facade construction types and materials;
  • the number of openings and anchor tests;
  • the need for laboratory testing of samples;
  • whether as-built documentation exists;
  • the content of the report: measured drawings, verification calculations, recommendations with quantities.

The timescale consists of three parts: preparation and document review, fieldwork, and desk analysis with issue of the report. The field stage depends on the weather and access arrangements, the desk stage on the volume of verification calculations. Documentation gathered in advance and prompt agreement of opening locations help shorten the overall period.

The season matters too. The thermal imaging part is done in the cold season, whereas opening-up and inspection from access equipment are easier in the warm period, so a comprehensive survey is sometimes split into two visits. If the system needs replacing as a result, the findings become the input data for a facade refurbishment.

When is monitoring prescribed after a facade survey?

Monitoring is prescribed when a defect has been identified but its progression is unclear: a single measurement cannot show whether a crack has stabilised or is still opening. Observations are then carried out to a programme over several months or seasons.

  • tell-tales and crack gauges on cracks — record changes in crack width;
  • survey markers — track deviation of the cladding plane and settlement;
  • repeat thermal imaging — shows whether the zone of wet insulation is changing;
  • periodic inspection of control openings — the progress of fixing corrosion.

At the end of the observation cycle either continued use is confirmed or the scope of repair is refined. For structures of limited serviceability, condition monitoring is a condition of use until the repair is carried out. If the defects are linked to weakened fixing of elements, the article on the causes of falling cladding is useful.

FAQ

What does a technical facade survey include?

Review of documents, a full visual inspection with defect mapping, selective opening-up of the cladding, instrumental measurements, anchor tests and a technical report with conclusions and recommendations.

How does a facade expert assessment differ from a survey?

A survey collects data on the technical condition. An expert assessment answers specific questions, for example in a dispute with a contractor or insurer, and often relies on survey results.

When is the best time for a thermal imaging survey?

During the heating season, with a marked difference between indoor and outdoor temperatures, no direct sun on the facade and no precipitation.

Can a rainscreen facade be surveyed without removing the cladding?

Partly — by endoscope and thermal imaging. A reliable conclusion on fixings and insulation requires selective opening-up.

What does the client receive after the survey?

A technical report with a schedule of defects, facade maps, measurement results, a condition assessment and recommendations for repair or replacement.

Is a survey needed for a new facade under warranty?

Scheduled inspections are needed during the warranty period too: installation defects often appear in the first years. If deviations are found, a survey that records the defects serves as the basis for a claim to the contractor.

How often is a facade survey carried out?

Scheduled visual inspections are usually done twice a year, while a detailed instrumental survey is carried out according to the building's schedule, before repair and when defects appear. For buildings where people are constantly present beneath the facade, shorter intervals are advisable.

Can the building remain in use during the survey?

Yes, the work is carried out without interrupting use. The zones beneath opening locations are temporarily fenced off, and the schedule is agreed with the owner and tenants.
