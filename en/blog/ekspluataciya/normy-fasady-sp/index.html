Facade regulations in Russia are a set of federal laws, codes of practice (SP) and standards (GOST) that set requirements for safety, fire protection, strength and thermal protection of facades, and for how work is carried out and accepted. There is no single 'facade law': the requirements are spread across documents in different fields.

For the client and designer, the key is to understand which document covers which issue: fire safety determines the permitted materials and systems, load codes govern fixing calculations, thermal protection determines insulation thickness, and rules for execution of works govern installation and acceptance. The current editions of documents must be checked as of the design date.

This article is for architects, designers, technical supervision and general contractors. Related material is in Facade maintenance, codes and repair.

The list of documents for a specific project is fixed in the design.

Fire safety requirements

Fire safety is the main constraint when choosing a facade system, especially for multi-storey and public buildings.

  • Structural fire hazard class (K0–K3). For the outer side of external walls, the codes set the required class depending on the fire resistance degree and the structural fire hazard class of the building. High-rise and many public buildings require K0: the construction must not spread fire.
  • Confirming the class. The class of a facade system is determined by fire testing a mock-up to Russian standard GOST 31251; the whole system is tested: cladding, substructure, insulation, wind barrier.
  • Combustibility of materials. The combustibility group of the cladding and insulation (NG, G1–G4) is determined by combustibility tests. Rainscreens on taller buildings use non-combustible insulation and cladding.
  • Details. Fire barriers in the ventilated cavity and non-combustible opening surrounds follow the system's technical details.

GFRC is a mineral material; according to manufacturers, including TECHNOFORMA Group, it belongs to combustibility class NG (non-combustible). The class of a specific system is confirmed by documents for the assembled system.

How often-confused terms relate to each other:

CharacteristicApplies toDesignationHow it is confirmed
Combustibility groupMaterialNG, G1–G4Tests to GOST 30244
Fire hazard classConstruction, facade systemK0–K3Fire tests of a mock-up to GOST 31251
Fire resistance degreeBuildingI–VDesign of load-bearing and enclosing structures

Hence a practical rule: non-combustible cladding does not automatically make a system K0. If there is combustible insulation, a combustible membrane or incorrectly installed fire barriers behind it, the system may fail the test. So technical supervision checks not only the certificate for the slab but also that the installed system matches the tested build-up, including the insulation brand, membrane, fire barrier spacing and opening surrounds.

Loads

The substructure and cladding fixings are calculated for a combination of loads according to Russian standard SP 20.13330:

  • Dead load of cladding, rails and insulation; decisive for heavy materials (stone, GFRC, concrete panels).
  • Wind load: pressure and suction; depends on the wind region, terrain type, height and facade zone. At corners and parapets peak suction is higher, and fixing spacing is reduced there.
  • Snow and ice loads for canopies, cornices and horizontal elements.
  • Thermal actions: expansion of rails, accommodated by movable connections.
  • Seismic loads in seismic areas, according to SP 14.13330.

The capacity of anchors in the actual substrate is confirmed by on-site pull-out tests; the design value is taken from their results with safety factors. Test results are included in the as-built documentation.

The calculation procedure for a rainscreen facade in outline: determine the wind region and terrain type according to SP 20.13330, find the characteristic wind pressure for each facade zone taking into account height and aerodynamic coefficients (higher in corner zones and at parapets), add the dead load of cladding and substructure and, where necessary, snow, ice and seismic loads. Then check the strength and deflection of the rails and the capacity of brackets, clips, rivets and anchors. The result is bracket and rail spacing for each zone, the type and number of anchors, and testing requirements.

Facade zoneLoad featureTypical solution
General zoneBasic wind pressureStandard bracket spacing by calculation
Building cornersIncreased suctionReduced spacing, additional clips
Parapet, upper floorsMaximum wind actionsReinforced fixing, check of slab format
Cantilevered decor, canopiesSnow, ice, weightSeparate calculation of the detail, safety fixing

Thermal protection

Thermal protection of external walls is governed by Russian standard SP 50.13330 'Thermal performance of buildings'. It sets:

  • the required thermal resistance of walls depending on the heating degree-days of the construction area and the building's use;
  • accounting for non-uniformities (brackets, anchors, reveals, junctions) through the reduced thermal resistance;
  • requirements for protecting the envelope from excessive moisture;
  • sanitary and hygienic requirements for internal surface temperature.

For a rainscreen this means the insulation thickness is chosen not by a 'standard 100 mm' but by calculation for the specific region, taking into account the thermally conductive inclusions of the substructure. Brackets are installed with thermal break pads, and the insulation is laid without gaps. More on this in the article on facade energy efficiency.

Besides thermal resistance, moisture requirements matter for rainscreens: the insulation must release moisture into the ventilated cavity, and the cavity must work as a channel with an inlet at the plinth and an outlet below the parapet. Cavity dimensions and the area of ventilation openings are set by the system's technical details. A breather membrane, if used, must be vapour-permeable and permitted by fire requirements for a building of that height.

A thermal calculation that accounts for brackets usually shows that the conductive inclusions of the substructure reduce the reduced thermal resistance noticeably, and the insulation thickness has to be increased. So brackets with a smaller cross-section, thermal break pads and optimal spacing are not only a way to save metal but also a way to meet thermal requirements without excess insulation thickness.

Quality control

Quality control of facade work has several levels:

  1. Incoming inspection of materials: checking data sheets, certificates, compliance with the design, appearance and geometry.
  2. Operational control: setting out, installation of anchors, brackets, rails, insulation and cladding with tolerance checks.
  3. Inspection of concealed work: anchoring, insulation, wind barrier and substructure before they are covered by cladding, with formal records.
  4. Testing: anchor pull-out and, where necessary, checking tightening and strength.
  5. Acceptance: checking facade geometry, joints, junctions and completeness of as-built documentation.

On capital construction projects, control is carried out by the contractor and the technical client (construction control), and on projects subject to state construction supervision, also by the supervisory authorities. Geometric tolerances and the set of documentation are determined by the design and the codes for execution of works.

Typical comments at acceptance: no concealed work records for a substructure already covered by cladding; insulation or membrane replaced with an 'equivalent' without confirmation that the system was tested with it; anchors installed without tests in the actual substrate; incorrect bracket spacing in corner zones; ventilation gaps sealed with sealant. All of these are easier to prevent than to correct: checking the substructure means removing the cladding.

After acceptance, the regulations continue to apply in service: the owner must keep the facade in good condition and carry out inspections and repairs. For this, the facility manager is given the as-built documentation, cladding layout drawings and fixing details.

Which facade standards apply at each stage of a project?

Regulatory documents are distributed across stages: some define design solutions, others the sequence of site works, others operation. It is useful for the client to see this link in order to request the right evidence from the contractor at each stage.

StageKey questionsWhat it relies onWhat the client receives
Concept and system selectionWhether the system is permitted for a building of this height and useFire regulations, the system's suitability documentJustification for the choice of cladding and sub-frame
DesignFixing, thermal performance, detailsStandards for loads, thermal protection and corrosion protectionCalculations, detail drawings, specifications
Manufacture of productsConformity of the cladding to declared propertiesMaterial standards and test methods, the manufacturer's technical specificationsProduct certificates, test reports
InstallationSequence of works, tolerances, concealed worksRules for execution and acceptance, the method statement, occupational safety requirementsAs-built documentation
OperationInspections, repair, surveysRules for surveying and monitoring, the operating manualInspection log, reports

In practice, errors most often arise at the boundaries between stages: the design is based on one system while another is purchased; the calculation is made for one insulation product while a different one is installed. Changes of materials after the design is issued are therefore formalised as a design change with the calculations re-checked. It is convenient to assign responsibility for that check in advance — in the contract and in the procedure governing interaction between the designer, the supplier and the installer.

Standards are revised periodically: amendments to codes of practice are issued, and standards are withdrawn and introduced. The design is carried out to the editions in force on the date determined by the contract terms and the rules of design review. If construction is delayed, the designer assesses whether the amendments issued affect the decisions taken. For the client this leads to a simple documentation requirement: calculations and drawings must state the specific editions of the documents they follow.

Regional requirements supplement the federal ones. Large cities have rules for approving the external appearance and colour schemes of buildings; for cultural heritage sites there is a separate procedure defined by the heritage protection authorities. These requirements do not cancel technical standards but may restrict the choice of materials and colour.

A convenient working tool is a project requirements register: a table that lists, for each solution, the applicable document, the responsible party and the supporting evidence (calculation, test report, certificate). The register is kept from the start of design and handed over to the facilities team together with the as-built documentation.

Mandatory and voluntary requirements: what must not be breached?

The requirements of the technical regulations — the federal laws on the safety of buildings and on fire safety — are mandatory. Codes of practice and standards fall into two groups: those included in the mandatory list and those applied on a voluntary basis to demonstrate compliance with the regulations.

Voluntary does not mean a document can be ignored. If the designer does not apply a code of practice from the voluntary list, compliance with the regulation has to be demonstrated in another way — by calculation, testing or risk assessment. For a typical building it is simpler and safer to follow the codes. In addition, a document becomes binding on the parties if a reference to it is included in the contract or the design brief.

For the facade contractor and product supplier there is a further level — company standards and technical specifications. They describe the product composition, tolerances, inspection methods and acceptance rules where a national standard is absent or gives only general requirements. Such documents are binding on the manufacturer itself, and for the buyer they take effect through the supply contract. When ordering bespoke products it is therefore worth requesting the text of the technical specifications and making sure it contains measurable values rather than general wording alone.

Operating requirements are also mandatory: the technical regulation on the safety of buildings covers the entire life cycle, including maintenance and repair. An owner who does not inspect the facade is breaching not the manufacturer's recommendations but the legal requirement for safe operation.

Where the standards contain no requirements for a particular solution or a departure from them is necessary, special technical conditions are developed. This is a separate document for a specific project, which goes through approval under the established procedure. For facades, special technical conditions occur on high-rise and unique buildings and with non-standard fixing systems.

  • Mandatory: the requirements of the technical regulations and of the clauses of codes of practice on the mandatory list.
  • Conditionally voluntary: the remaining codes and standards, unless safety is demonstrated otherwise.
  • Contractual: company standards, the manufacturer's technical specifications, the requirements of the design brief.

In practice, disputes arise less often from the status of a document than from the parties understanding its requirements differently. To avoid this, the construction contract lists the regulatory documents against which the works are accepted, the tolerances, and the procedure to follow where the design, the system album and the code of practice diverge.

Which sections of the design documentation relate to the facade?

Facade solutions are spread across several sections of the design, and none of them alone gives the full picture. The composition of design documentation sections is established by Russian Government Decree No. 87; the working documentation details them down to the level of junctions and specifications.

DocumentWhat it contains on the facade
Architectural solutionsExternal appearance, cladding layout, colour scheme, finishing materials
Structural solutionsExternal wall construction, substrates for anchors, supporting calculations
Fire safety measuresRequired fire hazard class of the walls, justification of the system used
Energy efficiencyThermal calculation of the building envelope
Construction organisation planAccess equipment, sequencing, safety measures
Facade working documentationSub-frame layouts, details, specifications, fixing calculation
Shop drawingsDetailing of sub-frame and cladding elements for manufacture

The minimum set of calculations that should be present in a ventilated facade package:

  • load take-down by facade zone;
  • calculation of brackets, rails and their connections;
  • calculation of the anchor fixing, taking on-site test results into account;
  • verification of the cladding elements and their fixings;
  • thermal calculation with non-uniformities taken into account;
  • for seismic regions — calculation for the special load combination.

The design review checks that the principal solutions comply with the technical regulations, but it does not replace a check of the working drawings. Fixing details, layout and specifications are assessed by the client's technical representative and construction supervision. On projects with complex cladding the design author is often engaged for author's supervision, so that departures from the drawings are identified during installation rather than at handover.

For complex facades with decoration and bespoke panels the set of shop drawings becomes key: moulds and cast-in parts are made from it. What such a set includes is described in the article on facade shop drawings. At TECHNOFORMA the working documentation and detailing are produced by the in-house department; see the facade design page for more.

What should be checked in a facade system's suitability document?

The suitability document confirms that a specific system in the described composition may be used in construction. What needs checking is not the mere fact that it exists but its match with the design in terms of composition, scope of application and validity period.

  1. Validity on the date of installation.
  2. Scope of application: building height, wind regions, seismicity, substrate types.
  3. System composition: grades of brackets and rails, types of anchors, insulation, membrane, cladding and its fixing.
  4. Fire-technical part: the fire hazard class and the conditions under which it was confirmed — thicknesses, fire barriers, opening surrounds.
  5. Album of technical solutions: the presence of the details used on the project, including corners, reveals, parapet and plinth.

The system document does not replace a calculation for the specific building. It gives the permitted scope of application and typical details, whereas the bracket spacing, anchor type and insulation thickness are determined by the design. The reverse is also true: a careful calculation does not make acceptable a system that has no confirmation of fire performance for a building of the given height.

At tender stage it is useful to request the full set from bidders: the suitability document with annexes, fire test reports, the album of details and a letter from the system developer on the applicability of the chosen cladding. Comparing bids on price per square metre alone, without these documents, often leads to substitutions during construction.

Where a project combines several cladding types — for example panels, stone at the plinth and porcelain stoneware on courtyard elevations — confirmation is needed for each combination of sub-frame and material, and for the details where they meet.

A common situation is cladding that is absent from the sub-frame document. Additional confirmation is then required: tests, an opinion from the system developer or a separate document for the combination of materials. The topic is covered in detail in the article on the technical certificate for ventilated facades.

Standards for GFRC and stone facades: how are properties confirmed?

Cladding properties are confirmed by the product certificate and test reports produced to standard methods. Several values matter to the client, as durability and the fixing calculation depend on them.

PropertyWhy it mattersWhat it affects in the design
Density and product weightDetermines the load on the sub-frameBracket spacing and type, anchor selection
Flexural and compressive strengthBehaviour of the panel under wind and at the fixing zoneProduct thickness, distance between fixing points
Frost resistanceNumber of freeze–thaw cycles without deteriorationSuitability for the regional climate, the plinth and the parapet
Water absorptionLinked to frost resistance and soilingNeed for water-repellent treatment
Combustibility groupThe material's contribution to the fire hazard of the systemAdmissibility for a building of the given class
Geometric tolerancesDimensional accuracy and flatnessJoint width, quality of the layout

For glass fibre reinforced concrete the requirements are, as a rule, fixed in the manufacturer's technical specifications and in the supply contract; this is discussed in the article on GOST and technical specifications for GFRC. For natural stone the values depend on the rock type and quarry, so tests are requested for the specific batch. What frost resistance grades mean and how to compare them is explained in the article on the frost resistance of facade materials.

The scope of testing and the frequency of inspection at the manufacturer vary: some values are checked on every batch, others periodically or when the mix or process changes. The supply contract may provide for reference samples of colour and texture, which are agreed before production starts and kept until acceptance is complete. For natural stone the same role is played by agreed samples with a permitted range of shade and pattern.

The values are best read together rather than separately. High strength combined with high water absorption does not guarantee durability in a climate with frequent passes through zero, and low water absorption does not compensate for insufficient slab thickness at the fixing zone. The supply brief therefore states the full set of requirements and the method by which each of them is checked.

A separate matter is the fixings and cast-in parts of the products themselves. What matters for them is the material (as a rule stainless or galvanised steel), the load-bearing capacity of the connection confirmed by calculation or testing, and protection against contact corrosion where they join an aluminium sub-frame.

Safety requirements for facade work at height

Facade installation is work at height, and it is subject to the occupational safety rules approved by the Ministry of Labour as well as the requirements for access equipment. Compliance is the contractor's responsibility, but the consequences of breaches also affect the client: work stoppages, missed deadlines, claims from the supervisory authorities.

  • work is carried out to a method statement that defines the layout of scaffolding or suspended cradles, ties to the building and storage areas;
  • scaffolding is accepted under a certificate and inspected during use;
  • installers are trained and authorised to work at height;
  • the danger zone beneath the facade is fenced off and protective canopies are provided over entrances;
  • heavy items — stone slabs, panels, cornices — are lifted by machinery to slinging diagrams.

The client does not need to supervise occupational safety in the contractor's place, but several signs of well-organised work are visible at once: an approved method statement on site, scaffold acceptance certificates, fencing and netting, and order in the cladding storage areas. Their absence usually says something about installation quality too: where scaffolding is not documented, concealed works certificates are rarely issued on time.

In confined urban conditions and on occupied buildings there are added restrictions on working hours, noise, pedestrian access and vehicle movement. They are taken into account in the method statement and the programme: installation in sections, temporary canopies over pavements, and delivery of oversized items at agreed times.

For large-format panels and three-dimensional decoration the lifting method is decided at design stage: the position of cast-in parts and the weight of an individual element depend on it. A comparison of access methods is given in the article on scaffolding and cradles.

Typical breaches of facade standards and how to prevent them

Most breaches stem not from ignorance of the standards but from substitutions and simplifications during construction. They can be prevented if the requirements are fixed in the contract and checked before the construction is closed up.

  • System components replaced with cheaper ones. The contract names the specific system and prohibits substitutions without the designer's approval.
  • Fixing calculation made for the typical zone only. A calculation is required for corner zones, the parapet and decorative elements.
  • Insulation thickness adopted without calculation. A thermal calculation for the construction region, with non-uniformities taken into account, is needed.
  • Fire barriers and opening surrounds not built to the album. Staged acceptance with photographic records helps.
  • Anchors not tested on site. The test programme is approved before mass installation begins.
  • As-built documentation produced retrospectively. Concealed works certificates are signed section by section, before the cladding is installed.

The consequences of breaches vary in severity. Deviations in geometry and joints impair appearance and are corrected by re-installing sections. Errors in thermal protection show up in the first winter and require the cladding to be opened up. Breaches in fixing and fire details are the most dangerous: they may not show for years and are discovered during a survey, a strong wind or a fire. That is why concealed works deserve the closest control.

The same points are convenient to use as criteria when selecting a contractor: one that works to the standards has no difficulty showing calculations, system documents and samples of as-built documentation from past projects.

For their own check, clients need only three questions about any solution on the facade: which document justifies it, who is responsible for it, and what confirms the actual execution. If one of them has no answer, the solution should be put on hold until it is clarified.

FAQ

Which SP codes apply to facades?

The main ones are SP 2.13130 (fire safety), SP 20.13330 (loads and actions), SP 50.13330 (thermal protection), SP 70.13330 (execution and acceptance of works) and, in seismic areas, SP 14.13330. The current edition is checked as of the design date.

Which GOST standard governs facade systems?

The fire hazard of facade systems is determined by tests to Russian standard GOST 31251. For facing stone, GOST 9480. The suitability of a rainscreen system is confirmed by a technical assessment.

What is K0 class for a facade?

The fire hazard class of a construction that does not spread fire. It is confirmed by fire testing a mock-up of the assembled facade system.

Does a rainscreen substructure need calculation?

Yes: the substructure is calculated for the dead load of the cladding, wind and other loads according to SP 20.13330, and anchors are checked by on-site pull-out tests.

Which documents are checked at facade acceptance?

The design, as-built drawings, concealed work records, anchor test results, data sheets and certificates for materials, and the facade system suitability document.

How does combustibility group differ from a facade's fire hazard class?

The combustibility group (NG, G1–G4) describes an individual material and is determined according to GOST 30244. The fire hazard class (K0–K3) applies to the facade system as a whole and is confirmed by fire testing a mock-up according to GOST 31251. Non-combustible cladding alone does not guarantee K0 class.

Is it mandatory to follow codes of practice applied on a voluntary basis?

Applying them is the simplest way to demonstrate compliance with the technical regulations. It is possible not to, but then the safety of the solution has to be demonstrated otherwise: by calculations, tests or special technical conditions.

What should be done if the cladding is not listed in the facade system document?

Additional confirmation of applicability is needed: tests of the fixing detail, an opinion from the system developer or a separate document for the combination of sub-frame and cladding. The solution is agreed with the designer before materials are purchased.

Who is responsible for compliance with standards when a facade is built?

The designer is responsible for the design solutions and calculations, the contractor for the conformity of the works to the design and the rules of execution, and the client's construction supervision for checking and acceptance. Once the building is commissioned, responsibility for keeping the facade in sound condition lies with the owner.
