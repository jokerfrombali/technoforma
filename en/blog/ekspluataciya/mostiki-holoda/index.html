Thermal Bridges in Ventilated Facades

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

Thermal bridges (cold bridges) in a facade are areas of the building envelope through which heat escapes faster than through the rest of the wall. In a ventilated rainscreen facade the main bridges are the metal substructure brackets, which pass through the insulation layer and connect the cold cladding to the warm wall.

The main criterion of a correct solution is to reduce conductivity at the fixing points and keep the insulation continuous. To do this, thermal break pads are placed under the brackets, the number of fixing points is optimised, and reveals and junctions are carefully insulated.

The article is written for designers, technical supervision, installation companies and clients of ventilated facades. Related topics: Facade maintenance, standards and repair.

Where they come from

Thermal bridges occur where the continuous insulation layer is interrupted or crossed by a more conductive material. Sources in a rainscreen facade:

  • Brackets and anchors — metal conducts heat hundreds of times better than mineral wool; each bracket is a point thermal bridge.
  • Floor slab edges and concrete ring beams — if the wall is masonry and the slab reaches the facade without insulation, the result is a linear bridge along the whole perimeter of the floor.
  • Window and door reveals — difficult to insulate, especially with a narrow reveal and the frame set towards the inner face of the wall.
  • Balcony slabs and cantilevers — reinforced concrete elements passing through the wall.
  • Joints and gaps in the insulation — boards laid without tight contact with each other and with the brackets.
  • Parapets, corners, plinth — geometric bridges, where the outer surface area is larger than the inner one.

The effect of each element is taken into account when calculating the reduced thermal resistance: the more bridges there are, the lower the actual thermal protection of the wall compared with the value calculated for the plain wall.

Brackets

The bracket is the main point thermal bridge in a ventilated facade. Its conductivity is affected by:

FactorEffect
MaterialAluminium conducts heat much better than steel, and stainless steel worse than galvanised. That is why aluminium systems particularly need thermal breaks
Cross-section and base plate areaThe larger the contact area with the wall, the greater the heat flow
Number per 1 m²Depends on cladding weight and wind load; heavy cladding needs more fixing points
ProjectionA long bracket for thick insulation transfers heat over a longer section
Thermal breakA pad under the base plate reduces contact heat transfer to the wall

A bracket thermal break is a pad between the bracket base plate and the substrate, sometimes with an insulating sleeve for the anchor. It interrupts direct metal contact and reduces losses at each fixing point. The spacing and type of brackets are determined by the substructure load calculation, so reducing their number for thermal reasons is permissible only within limits confirmed by calculation.

Thermal breaks

Thermal insulating pads for ventilated facades are made from materials with low thermal conductivity and sufficient compressive strength so as not to be crushed under the anchor load. Used materials:

  • Paronite (compressed fibre gasket material) — a traditional pad material, resistant to loads and ageing.
  • Polymer pads — polyamide, polypropylene, foamed polymers, sometimes reinforced.
  • Combined solutions — a pad under the base plate plus a thermal sleeve on the anchor, preventing contact between the anchor metal and the bracket.

Requirements for the pad: no smaller than the bracket base plate, keeps its thickness under load, resistant to moisture and temperature fluctuations, compatible with the system's fire requirements. The pad should be part of the tested facade system kit, not chosen by installers on site. Installation quality also matters: a pad that is shifted or missing under some of the brackets cancels out the effect.

Thermal imaging

A thermal imaging camera is the main tool for finding thermal bridges in a completed building. It shows the temperature distribution on the surface: above thermal bridges the outer surface is warmer and the inner surface colder than the surrounding wall.

  • When to survey. During the heating season with a stable indoor–outdoor temperature difference; in overcast weather or at night, without direct sun on the facade, precipitation or strong wind.
  • Outside or inside. On a ventilated facade the cladding screens the wall, so bridges are less visible from outside; an internal survey is often more informative — cold corners, reveals and slab edges show up.
  • Method. The procedure for thermal imaging inspection of building envelopes is set by the Russian standard GOST 26629 "Buildings and structures. Method of thermovision control of enclosing structures thermal insulation quality".
  • Interpretation. A thermogram shows anomalies; the cause is confirmed by opening up or by comparison with the design.

A thermal imaging survey is useful at acceptance of a new facade and when there are complaints about cold walls in an occupied building.

Remedies

How thermal bridges are eliminated depends on their type and the stage of the project.

  1. At design stage — choosing a system with thermal breaks, calculating the optimum bracket spacing, details for insulating reveals, slab edges and balconies. This is the cheapest stage.
  2. During installation — checking that a pad is installed under every bracket, cutting insulation tightly around brackets, laying boards staggered, insulating reveals before the cladding is installed. Recorded in concealed work records.
  3. On a facade in service — partial removal of cladding in problem areas, additional insulation of reveals and gaps, fitting pads when replacing brackets, restoring the wind barrier.
  4. From inside — as a temporary measure for individual cold corners; internal insulation moves the dew point into the wall and requires a vapour barrier.

After the remedy, the effect is confirmed by a repeat thermal imaging survey in the heating season.

If cold walls are accompanied by damp and mould, the insulation is checked at the same time: a layer wetted by leaks or condensation loses its thermal properties, and the thermal bridge is then formed not in the metal but across a whole area of the wall. Such insulation is replaced and the source of moisture is eliminated.

FAQ

Why is a paronite pad used in a rainscreen facade?

It is placed under the bracket base plate as a thermal break: it interrupts direct metal contact between the bracket and the wall and reduces heat loss at the fixing point.

What is a bracket thermal break?

A pad of low-conductivity material between the bracket and the substrate, sometimes with a thermal sleeve on the anchor. It is part of the facade system kit.

How are thermal bridges found on a facade?

By thermal imaging in the heating season with an indoor–outdoor temperature difference; for a ventilated facade an internal survey is also informative. The method is set by the Russian standard GOST 26629.

Can the number of brackets be reduced for better thermal performance?

Only within limits confirmed by the substructure calculation for the dead weight of the cladding and wind loads.

Which thermal bridges matter most in a ventilated facade?

Brackets, floor slab edges, window reveals and balcony slabs; their effect is accounted for through the reduced thermal resistance.
