Caring for glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) comes down to three regular tasks: inspection, gentle cleaning and periodic renewal of the protective impregnation. GFRC is a fine-grained concrete reinforced with alkali-resistant glass fibre; it does not rot or corrode and is classed as non-combustible, so it needs no special care of the kind timber or metal require.

The durability of GFRC decoration and panels depends above all on the condition of the surface layer and the fixing points. If the surface is protected from water saturation and the joints and cast-in anchors are sound, the elements keep their shape and colour for years. The material is chosen by developers and managers of apartment buildings, owners of private houses with facade decoration, and maintenance teams of public buildings.

Below is the maintenance procedure from a GFRC manufacturer. More articles in this section: Facade maintenance, standards and repair.

Regular inspection

Inspection is the basis of maintenance: most problems on a GFRC facade are cheaper to fix at an early stage than after a winter of freeze–thaw cycles. A practical routine is a scheduled inspection in spring and autumn, plus an unscheduled one after strong wind, hail, an earthquake, a fire nearby or repair work on adjacent structures.

What to check during an inspection:

  • Element surfaces — hairline and through cracks, chipped edges, delamination of the decorative layer, efflorescence, leak stains.
  • Joints — integrity of the sealant, separation from the edges, cracks, plants growing in the joints.
  • Fixing points — visible cast-in anchors and brackets, traces of rust, streaks below anchors, displacement of elements relative to their neighbours.
  • Drainage — sills, parapet cappings, cornices: water should not run down the face in a constant stream.
  • Junctions — windows, balconies, roof, where wetting most often occurs.

It is convenient to record the results with photos referenced to the building's gridlines and levels. Repeated photos from the same points show whether a defect is developing. Lower tiers are inspected from the ground and ladders, upper tiers from an aerial platform, a gondola or by rope-access technicians; binoculars help to find suspicious areas but do not replace a close inspection of the fixings.

Cleaning

GFRC needs cleaning when dirt spoils its appearance or retains moisture: urban dust and soot, streaks from metal elements, biological growth on north-facing sides, efflorescence. The rule is to start with the gentlest method and move to a more aggressive one only if the first does not work.

  1. Low-pressure water and a soft brush — for dust and fresh dirt. Suitable for any element, including decoration with fine relief.
  2. Neutral detergents (pH around 7) — for greasy soot and deposits; after treatment the surface is rinsed with clean water.
  3. Special products — for efflorescence, rust, moss and lichen. They are chosen for the type of contamination and tested on an inconspicuous area.

What to avoid: concentrated acids (they dissolve the cement matrix and open the pores), stiff wire brushes, abrasive blasting and high-pressure washing at close range — all of these destroy the face layer, after which the element absorbs water and dirt faster. Painted and textured surfaces are cleaned with particular care. Work is carried out at above-zero temperatures so that the water can evaporate before night frosts.

Water-repellent treatment

Water-repellent (hydrophobic) treatment is an impregnation that reduces the capillary water absorption of the surface while keeping it vapour-permeable. For GFRC this is an important measure: less water in the pores means less frost damage, less efflorescence and biological growth, and dirt sticks less and is washed off more easily by rain.

Silane and siloxane impregnations are normally used; they penetrate the surface layer and do not form a film. The procedure:

  • clean and dry the surface — impregnation works worse on wet concrete;
  • repair cracks and chips before impregnating, otherwise water will get in around the protection;
  • apply the product according to the manufacturer's method — as a rule, "wet on wet" until saturation;
  • check the effect: water on the treated surface should bead up.

How long the impregnation lasts depends on the product, the facade orientation and the climate; the reference is the impregnation manufacturer's instructions and a simple water test. When droplets no longer bead and the surface darkens in rain, the treatment is repeated. If the elements are painted with facade paint, check in advance that the impregnation is compatible with the paint.

Painting

GFRC elements are often left in their through-body colour — the pigment is added to the concrete during manufacture, and such colour does not peel. Painting is needed when the design calls for an exact colour, to even out shades after repair, or when refreshing the facade.

Painting requirements:

  • Vapour permeability. Use facade paints for mineral substrates — silicate, silicone or vapour-permeable acrylic. Dense film-forming coatings trap moisture, and the coating peels off in blisters.
  • Preparation. The surface is cleaned, dedusted, efflorescence is removed, and if necessary it is primed with a primer from the same system as the paint.
  • Conditions. Dry weather, substrate and air temperature within the limits specified by the paint manufacturer; do not paint over fresh repair mortar before it has gained strength.
  • Compatibility. Repainting is done with the same system, or the adhesion of the new coat to the old one is checked on a trial area.

How often to repaint depends on the condition of the coating: fading, chalking and peeling are signs that the coat needs renewing.

Minor repairs

Minor repair means eliminating local defects that do not affect the load-bearing capacity of the element: chipped edges, blowholes, hairline cracks, a failed section of sealant in a joint. The sooner a defect is closed, the less water gets inside and the lower the risk that it grows over the winter.

  • Chips and blowholes are filled with concrete repair mortars compatible with GFRC in strength and colour: the surface is cleaned, dampened, primed if necessary, filled and the relief is shaped.
  • Hairline cracks are usually not dangerous; they are treated with impregnation or elastic compounds to prevent wetting.
  • Joints — the failed sealant is removed and restored using the same system.

If a crack is open, runs through the element, the element has shifted or there are signs of corrosion of the fixing, this is no longer a minor repair. Such an element is inspected and a decision is made whether to repair or replace it. The technique is covered in detail in Repairing chips and cracks in GFRC.

Maintenance schedule for a GFRC facade

The care of GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) elements is best managed by calendar rather than as problems arise: the work is inexpensive but loses its point if it is carried out a season late. An indicative frequency is given below; exact intervals depend on the climate, the orientation of the facade and the materials used.

TaskFrequency (indicative)When to carry it out
Scheduled inspection with a photographic recordTwice a yearIn spring after the snow has gone and in autumn before the frosts
Clearing of gutters, sill flashings and parapet cappingsAt least once a year, more often in green areasIn autumn after leaf fall
Washing of soiled areasAs required, usually once every few yearsA warm, dry period
Checking and renewal of the water-repellent impregnationAccording to the result of a water testAfter washing and complete drying
Review of the sealant in the jointsAnnual inspection, replacement in sectionsAt above-zero temperatures
Detailed close-up inspection of fixing pointsOnce every few years and after exceptional eventsCombined with washing or repairs, when access is available

It pays to combine work at height: one visit by an aerial platform or one set-up of cradles covers inspection, washing, joint repairs and impregnation. For a residential complex, the schedule is usually included in the managing company's procedures; for a private house, a short checklist with dates is enough.

How does the care of cornices, balustrades, columns and panels differ?

The material is the same, but the elements work in different conditions, so attention during maintenance is distributed unevenly. Elements with horizontal surfaces, and those within the splash zone and in contact with people, suffer most.

  • Cornices and string courses. The top ledge collects water, snow and dust. The fall and the condition of the covering or flashing, the drip on the lower edge and the joints between sections are checked. More on these products is given on the page GFRC cornices.
  • Balustrades and railings. They are exposed to precipitation on all sides and carry loads from people. The seating of the balusters, the handrail joints and the fixings to the slab are inspected; see GFRC balustrades.
  • Columns and pilasters. The lower part is vulnerable: splashes from the paving, de-icing agents, impacts from trolleys and machinery. The base and the first metre of the shaft are washed and impregnated more often than the rest of the surface.
  • Architraves and surrounds of openings. The junction with the window unit and the sill flashing is monitored, as this is where streaks on the facade most often originate.
  • Flat and three-dimensional panels. The main points are the joints and the uniformity of the gaps: a change in joint width indicates displacement. The design of such systems is described in the section GFRC facade panels.

Small architectural forms at ground level are maintained by the same rules but more often: they are in contact with the soil, irrigation and de-icing agents.

How to remove efflorescence, rust, graffiti and biological growth

Persistent soiling is removed once its cause has been found and eliminated, otherwise the stain will return within a season. The cleaning method is chosen according to the nature of the soiling and is always tested on an inconspicuous area.

SoilingWhere it comes fromWhat to do
Efflorescence (white deposit)Water passes through the concrete or a joint and carries salts to the surfaceEliminate the source of wetting, dry brushing, a special efflorescence remover if necessary, then impregnation
Rust streaksUnprotected metal higher up the facade: railing fixings, brackets, grillesProtect or replace the source, clean the stain with a rust remover for mineral substrates
Moss, algae, dark depositConstant damp, shade, nearby vegetationA biocidal treatment, gentle mechanical cleaning, restoration of water drainage
GraffitiVandalism at the lower levelsProfessional removers matched to the type of paint; for areas at risk, an anti-graffiti coating compatible with the substrate
Soot, oil stainsTraffic, kitchen extracts, car parksDetergents with a degreasing action, rinsing with water

Before large areas are treated, the result on a trial area is compared after drying: a wet surface looks darker and cleaner than it will a day later. General rules and methods for different claddings are brought together in the article on facade cleaning.

Winter use: snow, ice and de-icing agents

In winter, the elements are harmed not by frost itself but by the combination of water, salt and mechanical action. The frost resistance of the material is designed for freeze-thaw cycles, but knocking ice off with a crowbar or constant contact with brine are not loads it is designed for.

  • Ice and icicles are removed from cornices without striking the element; it is more reliable to eliminate the cause, namely a leak or a faulty gutter.
  • Snow is cleared from parapets, balustrades and steps with plastic or wooden shovels, without metal scrapers.
  • De-icing agents are not spread right up against the plinth, column bases and planters; in spring these areas are rinsed with water.
  • Snow banks from clearing machinery are not piled against clad walls: melting snow with salt keeps the bottom course damp for a long time.
  • Washing and impregnation are postponed until the warm season.

It is sensible to make the lower levels, which come into contact with de-icing agents every year, a separate inspection item: the first signs of surface scaling appear precisely here.

Which actions by tenants and contractors damage decorative elements?

A considerable share of damage arises not from the weather but from work carried out on the facade by other people. A thin-walled element is designed for its own weight and wind load, but not for equipment hung on it.

  • Fixing signs, light fittings, cameras and air-conditioning units directly to GFRC elements. The load is transferred to the load-bearing wall or to separate brackets, and penetrations through the cladding are sealed.
  • Drilling close to embedded parts and edges without reference to the design.
  • Resting scaffolding, ladders and cradles on cornices, string courses and sill elements.
  • Filling joints with cement mortar instead of elastic sealant: the joint loses its mobility and a crack appears alongside.
  • Discharging condensate from air conditioners onto the face surface, which means constant wetting and dark streaks.

A practical solution is a short set of rules for work on the facade for tenants and contractors, with a list of permitted fixing locations. For retail ground floors, such locations are best provided for at the design stage.

What makes up the maintenance budget, and when are specialists needed?

The cost of maintaining a GFRC facade is determined mainly not by materials but by access to the surface. Detergents, impregnation and sealant make up the smaller part of the estimate; the main share goes on lifting equipment, work at height and setting up a safe zone beneath the facade.

Several factors affect the budget:

  • the height of the building and the means of access: from the ground, from an aerial platform, from facade cradles or by rope access;
  • the complexity of the modelling: deep relief, projecting cornices and balustrades take more time than flat panels;
  • the degree and type of soiling, the need for special products and trial areas;
  • the amount of accompanying repair to joints and local defects;
  • site restrictions: night-time working only, entrances in use, car parks and shop windows beneath the facade.

Scheduled washing of the lower levels and visual inspections are usually carried out by the in-house maintenance team. A specialist organisation is brought in in three cases: for work at height, for removing stubborn soiling with a selection of chemicals, and at any signs that go beyond routine care, namely displacement of elements, open cracks or traces of corrosion on fixings. In the last case a survey is carried out first, and the scope of work is determined from its results.

It is more sensible to save not on frequency but on organisation: combine operations in a single lift and keep a log so that the next contractor does not start from scratch.

What to hand over to the maintenance team together with the facade

The quality of maintenance depends on whether the operating organisation knows what the facade is made of and how it is assembled. A set of documents received at handover removes the guesswork at the very first repair.

  1. As-built layout drawings of the elements and fixing details, with the elements marked.
  2. Information on materials: type of surface finish, integral colour or paint, brand of paint and impregnation, type of sealant.
  3. Care instructions with permitted cleaning methods and a list of prohibited products.
  4. Spare elements or retained moulds for frequently damaged parts at the lower levels.
  5. An inspection log in which dates, photographs and completed work are entered.

The contents of such a set are described in the article on the facade passport. The service life of the cladding depends directly on the regularity of care; this question is examined in the article on the service life of GFRC.

FAQ

How often should a GFRC facade be inspected?

A practical routine is a scheduled inspection in spring and autumn, plus an unscheduled one after strong wind, hail or work on adjacent structures. Results are recorded in photos referenced to the building gridlines.

What should GFRC be washed with?

First low-pressure water and a soft brush, then neutral detergents. Concentrated acids, wire brushes and abrasive blasting destroy the face layer.

Do GFRC elements need water-repellent treatment?

It is advisable: a silane-siloxane impregnation reduces surface water absorption, cuts efflorescence and deposits and keeps the surface vapour-permeable. It is repeated when water no longer beads up.

Can GFRC decoration be repainted?

Yes, with vapour-permeable facade paints for mineral substrates after cleaning and priming. Dense film-forming coatings should not be used.

What should be done about a chip in GFRC decoration?

A small chip is filled with a compatible repair mortar. If the crack runs through, the element has shifted or the fixing shows rust, the element must be inspected and replacement considered.

Can a GFRC facade be cleaned with a pressure washer?

It is acceptable at moderate pressure, with a wide fan and from a distance at which the jet does not damage the face layer or the sealant in the joints. The setting is selected on a trial area. For painted surfaces and fine relief, low-pressure water and a soft brush are preferable.

Does the facade need maintenance in the first years after handover?

Yes. The first seasons show how the joints, water drainage and junctions perform, and minor shortcomings are easier to remedy during this period. In addition, documented inspections are usually required for the warranty obligations to remain in force.

Who should carry out inspection and care: the contractor or the managing company?

Routine care and scheduled inspections are carried out by the operating organisation or the owner. The manufacturer or the installation company is brought in for detailed inspection of fixing points, repairs and replacement of elements.
