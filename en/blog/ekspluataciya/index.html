Facade maintenance, standards and repair

A facade works for decades, and its condition depends not only on materials and installation but also on maintenance. This section covers how to care for GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) elements and repair chips, where cracks come from and why cladding falls off, when a facade inspection is needed and how to clean stone and concrete.

Separate articles explain what the frost resistance grade F and water absorption mean, which Russian building codes (SP) and Russian standards (GOST) govern facades, how warranties work, how a facade affects energy efficiency, thermal bridges and sound insulation, and how restoring a historic facade differs from repairing a modern one.

This knowledge is also useful when choosing materials: how a facade will age is decided at the design stage.

How often to inspect a facade: an indicative schedule

A facade is inspected on a planned basis — as a rule twice a year, in spring and autumn — and on an unplanned basis after strong wind, heavy rain, hail or a roof failure. The exact frequency is set by the design documentation, the operating instructions for the system and the regulations of the managing organisation; the table below serves as a guide.

Visual inspection from the ground and from the roofIn spring and autumnCracks, chips, streaks, displacement of elements

For rainscreen systems the fasteners and the ventilation cavity are checked in addition — see the article on maintenance of a ventilated facade.

Which signs call for an urgent facade inspection?

Anything that points to a loss of bond between the cladding and the wall requires an immediate response. A cosmetic defect can wait for the season, but movement of an element cannot: the area beneath it is cordoned off until a specialist has given an opinion.

  • a decorative element or a slab has shifted, a new gap or step has appeared;
  • a crack is growing or runs through several elements in succession;
  • traces of rust around the fixings and along the joints;
  • constant wetting of a section of the wall, ice and icicles in one and the same place;
  • a hollow sound when cladding bedded in mortar is tapped.

How to tell a shrinkage crack from a structural one, and when monitoring with tell-tales is sufficient, is covered in the article on cracks in a facade.

Documents for facade operation and repair

Repairs and warranty claims proceed faster when the owner holds a complete set of facade documents. Without as-built drawings it is hard to establish where the brackets and embedded parts run, and without material data sheets it is hard to select a repair compound and colour.

The set usually kept comprises the design with details, as-built documentation, data sheets and certificates for materials, operating instructions and an inspection log. Separate articles explain what information goes into a facade passport and how inspection records affect warranty obligations.

If a defect goes beyond routine care, a contractor is brought in. Facade works on an occupied building begin with a survey and a schedule of defects, and replacement elements — for example, GFRC products — are manufactured from measurements of the existing mouldings, with the colour and texture matched.

Which aspects of facade operation are determined at the design stage?

The service life of a facade is largely determined by decisions made before installation. If water run-off, access for inspection and repairable details are provided for at the design stage, upkeep comes down to planned inspections and cleaning.

  • falls and drips on all horizontal elements, drainage of water away from joints;
  • materials with frost resistance and water absorption appropriate to the climate of the region;
  • corrosion-resistant fasteners and mutually compatible metals;
  • the possibility of removing and replacing an individual slab or element without dismantling the adjacent ones;
  • anchor points for cradles or other access to the upper levels.

These points are worth checking in the documentation before work begins: correcting them on a finished facade is considerably more difficult.

FAQ

Who is responsible for the condition of the facade after the building is handed over?

Routine upkeep is the responsibility of the owner or the managing organisation: inspections, cleaning and keeping the water run-off in working order. The contractor and the manufacturer are liable for defects in workmanship and materials within the warranty period, provided the operating rules have been observed.

Can a facade be cleaned with a pressure washer?

It depends on the material and the condition of the surface. Dense stone and concrete usually withstand such washing at moderate pressure, whereas soft stones, painted surfaces and joints can be damaged. Before the whole surface is cleaned, a trial area is done in an inconspicuous place.

When is it best to plan a facade repair?

Work with mortars, paints and sealants is usually carried out in the warm dry season at above-zero temperatures. It is sensible to carry out the survey and order the products in advance, in winter or early spring, so that the materials are already on site by the start of the season.
