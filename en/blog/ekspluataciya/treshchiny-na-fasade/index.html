Facade Cracks: Causes and What to Do

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

  4. Survey
  5. Repair

Cracks in a facade are a sign that the stresses in the wall or cladding have exceeded the strength of the material. The causes vary: shrinkage of render, thermal movement, corrosion of fixings, foundation settlement. Some cracks are cosmetic, others signal a structural problem, and they can only be told apart by their pattern, location and development over time.

The main rule: first find the cause, then repair. A crack from settlement or a misaligned substructure that is filled without investigation will open again, and sometimes it hides a dangerous condition of the cladding above a pavement.

The article is useful for owners and property managers, technical supervision and maintenance teams. More articles: Facade maintenance, standards and repair.

Types of cracks

Facade cracks are classified by several characteristics — this helps to understand their nature even before a survey.

CharacteristicTypeTypical meaning
WidthHairline (fractions of a millimetre), small, largeHairline cracks are more often superficial; large ones require a survey
DepthSurface, in the cladding, through the wallThrough cracks affect the structure
PatternNetwork, single, stepped along masonry jointsNetwork — shrinkage; stepped — wall movement
DirectionVertical, horizontal, diagonalDiagonal cracks at openings and corners are often settlement
DevelopmentStable, growingGrowing cracks are dangerous regardless of width

Cracks in cladding behave differently depending on the system: in a wet facade (render, tiles on adhesive) they often follow cracks in the substrate; in a ventilated rainscreen facade the cladding is separated from the wall, and its cracks are usually related to the fixings, impacts or the element itself.

Causes

The main causes of cracks:

  • Shrinkage — of render, concrete or masonry mortar as it dries. Produces a network or single fine cracks, which usually stabilise.
  • Thermal movement — long stretches of wall and cladding without movement joints, dark facades on the sunny side.
  • Settlement and uneven movement of the foundations — diagonal cracks from the corners of openings, cracks where parts of the building of different heights meet.
  • Corrosion — of reinforcement in concrete, anchors and cast-in parts in cladding; corrosion products split the material.
  • Frost damage — water in pores and cracks widens defects through repeated freezing.
  • Cladding installation errors — rigid fixing with no allowance for movement, too few fixing points, misaligned substructure.
  • External actions — impacts, vibration from traffic and nearby construction, seismic activity.

Several causes often act at once: a small shrinkage crack lets water in, and freezing water widens it.

Warning signs

There are signs that mean a survey must not be postponed and access beneath the facade should be restricted:

  • the crack is growing — a mark at its end ends up inside the crack a few days later;
  • the width is noticeably greater than hairline and uneven along its length;
  • cladding elements have shifted relative to their neighbours, the plane or joints are disrupted;
  • a hollow sound when tapped — the cladding has come away from the substrate;
  • rust streaks from under panels, at anchors and brackets;
  • cracks at the supports of balconies, canopies, cornices;
  • small fragments falling, debris on the pavement around the building;
  • windows and doors jamming at the same time, cracks appearing inside the rooms.

The last sign indicates movement of the building structure, not just the cladding — this calls for an assessment by specialists in load-bearing structure surveys.

If delamination or displacement of cladding is noticed above a pavement, entrance or playground, the first measure is not repair but safety: fencing off the danger zone, a protective net or canopy, removal of any element that might fall. The owner or property manager is responsible for the safe operation of the facade, so such measures are taken immediately, without waiting for a repair design.

Survey

The survey begins with a visual inspection and recording: a facade drawing with the cracks marked, photos referenced to the gridlines, crack width, length and direction. Tell-tales — gypsum or plate markers with the date — are placed across the cracks to find out within a few weeks whether the defect is growing.

Then, depending on the situation:

  • tapping the cladding to find delamination;
  • opening up individual panels of the rainscreen facade to inspect the substructure and anchors;
  • non-destructive testing of concrete strength, rebar location, thermal imaging;
  • pull-out testing of anchor capacity.

If deformation of load-bearing structures is suspected, the survey is carried out by specialist organisations under the rules for surveying the technical condition of buildings. The result of the survey is a schedule of defects, an assessment of their causes and recommendations: where a cosmetic repair is enough, where fixings must be corrected, and where the cladding must be replaced or the structure strengthened. Without such a document it is hard to prepare an accurate cost estimate and plan the work in sections. More on the procedure in Facade inspection: when and how it is done.

Repair

The repair method follows from the cause:

  • Shrinkage and hairline cracks — chasing out, filling with repair mortar or elastic filler, painting with vapour-permeable paint, water-repellent treatment.
  • Cracks in cladding from impacts — local repair of the element or its replacement.
  • Cracks from fixings — correcting the fixing (loosening over-rigid fasteners, adding fixing points, realigning rails), then repairing or replacing the element.
  • Corrosion cracks — opening up, cleaning or replacing the cast-in parts, restoring the concrete cover.
  • Cracks from structural movement — first strengthening or stabilisation according to a design, then finishing repairs.

If there are many cracks and the cladding is worn, it is sometimes more rational to replace the whole system — for example, to install a ventilated rainscreen facade, which separates the cladding from wall movement and allows the building to be insulated. The decision is made based on the survey and a cost comparison of the options.

FAQ

Are cracks in a building facade dangerous?

Hairline shrinkage cracks are usually not dangerous. Growing cracks, cracks with displaced cladding, cracks at anchors and balcony supports, and cracks accompanied by jamming windows and doors are dangerous.

How can I tell whether a crack is growing?

Place a tell-tale or a dated mark at the ends of the crack and check after a few weeks: if the crack has passed the mark or the tell-tale has broken, it is developing.

Why do cracks appear in cladding?

Because of impacts, restrained thermal movement, fixing errors, anchor corrosion, wetting and frost damage, and in wet systems — cracks in the substrate.

Can a facade crack simply be filled over?

Only if it is superficial and stable. Otherwise, without removing the cause, the crack will open again, and the filling will hide a dangerous condition of the cladding.

Who should investigate the cracks?

Cladding and substructure — facade specialists; if there are signs of movement in load-bearing structures — organisations that survey the technical condition of buildings.
