Facade Cracks: Causes and What to Do

Cracks on a facade are a sign that stresses in the wall or cladding have exceeded the strength of the material. The causes vary: render shrinkage, thermal movement, corrosion of fixings, foundation settlement. Some cracks are cosmetic, others signal a structural problem, and they can only be told apart by their pattern, location and development over time.

The main rule is to find the cause first and repair second. A crack from settlement or a distorted substructure that is filled without a survey will open again, and it can sometimes mask a dangerous condition of cladding above a pavement.

This guide is useful for building owners and management companies, technical supervisors and maintenance services. Other articles are in the Facade maintenance, regulations and repair section.

Types of cracks

Cracks in building facades are classified by several features, which helps understand their nature even before a survey.

FeatureTypeTypical meaning
WidthHairline (fractions of a millimetre), fine, wideHairline cracks are usually superficial; wide ones need a survey
DepthSurface, within the cladding, through the wallThrough cracks affect the structure
PatternMap cracking, single crack, stepped along masonry jointsMap cracking indicates shrinkage; stepped cracks indicate wall movement
DirectionVertical, horizontal, diagonalDiagonal cracks at openings and corners often indicate settlement
DevelopmentStable, activeActive cracks are dangerous regardless of width

Cracks in cladding behave differently depending on the system: on a bonded facade (render, tiles on adhesive) they often follow cracks in the substrate; on a ventilated rainscreen facade the cladding is separated from the wall, and its cracks are usually related to the fixings, impacts or the element itself.

Causes

The main causes of cracks:

  • Shrinkage of render, concrete or masonry mortar as it dries. Produces map cracking or single fine cracks, usually stabilises.
  • Thermal movement: long stretches of wall and cladding without movement joints, dark facades on the sunny side.
  • Settlement and differential movement of the foundation: diagonal cracks from the corners of openings, cracks at the junction of parts of the building of different heights.
  • Corrosion of reinforcement in concrete, or of anchors and embeds in cladding; corrosion products split the material.
  • Frost damage: water in pores and cracks widens defects through repeated freezing.
  • Cladding installation errors: rigid fixing without allowance for movement, too few fixing points, a distorted substructure.
  • External actions: impacts, vibration from traffic and nearby construction, seismic activity.

Several causes often act at once: a small shrinkage crack lets water in, and freezing water widens it.

Warning signs

Some signs mean a survey cannot be postponed and access below the facade should be restricted:

  • the crack is growing: a mark at its end is inside the crack a few days later;
  • the width is noticeably greater than hairline and uneven along the length;
  • cladding elements have shifted relative to their neighbours, and the plane or joints are disturbed;
  • a hollow sound when tapped, indicating cladding detached from the substrate;
  • rust streaks from under slabs, at anchors and brackets;
  • cracks at the supports of balconies, canopies and cornices;
  • small fragments falling off, debris on the perimeter apron;
  • windows and doors sticking at the same time, and cracks appearing indoors.

The last sign points to deformation of the building structure, not just the cladding; this requires assessment by specialists in load-bearing structure surveys.

If detached or shifted cladding is noticed above a pavement, entrance or playground, the first step is not repair but safety: fencing off the danger zone, a protective net or canopy, removing any element that could fall. The owner or management company is responsible for the safe operation of the facade, so these measures are taken immediately, without waiting for a repair design.

Survey

The survey begins with a visual inspection and recording: a facade drawing with the cracks marked, photos referenced to the grid axes, crack width, length and direction. Tell-tales (plaster or plate markers with a date) are fixed across the cracks to see, after a few weeks, whether the defect is growing.

Then, depending on the situation:

  • tapping the cladding to find detachment;
  • opening up individual rainscreen panels to inspect the substructure and anchors;
  • non-destructive testing of concrete strength, rebar location, thermal imaging;
  • pull-out testing of anchor capacity.

If deformation of load-bearing structures is suspected, the survey is carried out by specialised organisations in accordance with the Russian rules for surveying the technical condition of buildings. The survey results in a schedule of defects, an assessment of their causes and recommendations: where cosmetic repair is enough, where the fixing details must be corrected, and where the cladding must be replaced or the structure strengthened. Without such a document it is difficult to prepare an accurate estimate and plan the work by sections. More on the procedure in Facade survey: when and how it is carried out.

Tell-tales are usually monitored for several weeks, with their condition recorded in a log with dates and photos. If a tell-tale has broken, the crack is active, and repair without removing the cause is pointless.

Repair

The repair method follows from the cause:

  • Shrinkage and hairline cracks: raking out, filling with a repair compound or flexible filler, painting with vapour-permeable paint, water-repellent treatment.
  • Cracks in cladding from impacts: local repair of the element or its replacement.
  • Cracks from fixings: correcting the detail (loosening rigid fasteners, adding fixing points, realigning rails), then repairing or replacing the element.
  • Corrosion cracks: opening up, cleaning or replacing embeds, restoring the concrete cover.
  • Cracks from structural deformation: first strengthening or stabilisation according to a design, then finishing repair.

If there are many cracks and the cladding is worn, it is sometimes more rational to replace the whole system, for example with a ventilated rainscreen facade, which separates the cladding from wall movement and allows the building to be insulated. The decision is made based on the survey and a comparison of the costs of the options.

A common mistake is filling a moving crack with rigid cement mortar: it quickly detaches, and the crack reappears next to it.

Cracks in the facade: causes by type of facade system

The same crack means different things depending on how the facade is built. In a rendered system it most often comes from the substrate or the insulation, in a ventilated system from the fixing point, and in masonry from movement of the wall itself. Diagnosis therefore begins with the question of what exactly has cracked and what it is supported on.

Facade systemTypical patternMost likely causes
Render over insulationCracks along the joints of insulation boards, diagonal cracks from window cornersGaps between boards, no mesh reinforcement at openings, a dark colour on the sunny side
Render over masonry or concreteA network of fine cracks, lines along the boundary between different materialsShrinkage of the mortar, different movements of masonry and concrete, over-drying during application
Facing brickworkVertical cracks at corners, stepped cracks along the jointsNo movement joints, corrosion of wall ties, deflection of supporting elements
Ventilated facade with stone or porcelain stoneware slabsA crack from the fixing point, a broken-off cornerThe slab is clamped without a gap, the rails are out of alignment, the kerf is too close to the edge
GFRC panels and decorative elementsHairline cracks in the face layer, a crack at the embedded partRigid fixing at all points, an impact during installation, wetting of the connection
In-situ reinforced concrete without claddingCracks along the reinforcement, rust marksInsufficient concrete cover, corrosion of the reinforcement

The general pattern is this: the more rigidly the cladding is bonded to the substrate, the more exactly it repeats its movements. A ventilated system with sliding connections forgives the wall more, but places high demands on the subframe itself; its design is discussed in the article on the ventilated facade subframe.

What does the time at which a crack appeared tell us?

The period after which a defect showed itself following completion of the works narrows the range of possible causes considerably. The exact date is rarely known, but photographs from handover and from previous inspections usually show whether the crack is old or new.

  • The first weeks and months. Shrinkage of renders and repair mortars, breaches of application procedure, loading too early. Such cracks are, as a rule, fine and stabilise quickly.
  • The first one to three years. Settlement of a new building, shrinkage and creep of the concrete frame, the first full cycles of heating and cooling. Mistakes with movement joints and cladding fixings become apparent.
  • After a particular winter. Frost damage to damp areas, usually where water drainage has failed: under the parapet, at sill flashings, on the plinth.
  • After many years. Corrosion of fixings and reinforcement, ageing of sealants, accumulated damage from leaks.
  • After an event. Construction on a neighbouring plot, the laying of utilities, an earthquake, an impact from machinery, alterations that weakened the walls.

If cracks appeared simultaneously on different elevations and inside the rooms, the cause is sought in the structure and the foundations. If they are concentrated on one side or in one zone over the height, climatic effects or a local installation error are more likely.

Why crack width changes with the seasons

Many cracks 'breathe': they open in the cold and narrow in the heat because materials change size with temperature and humidity. This behaviour does not in itself mean that the defect is developing, but it shows that the crack is live and that a rigid filling will not hold in it.

Several practical monitoring rules follow from this. Measurements are compared at a similar temperature and at the same time of day, otherwise daily fluctuations are easily mistaken for growth. For each control point, the date, the air temperature and the crack width are recorded; a crack width gauge or a measuring magnifier is convenient for this. Monitoring that covers both a warm and a cold period gives a more reliable conclusion than several weeks within one season.

Live cracks are repaired with elastic materials or turned into a properly formed movement joint. Stable ones allow rigid filling. Developing cracks, whose width grows from season to season, require the cause to be eliminated before any finishing work.

Which cracks in the facade can be left under observation?

Immediate repair is not always necessary: it is more sensible to monitor some defects until the warm season or until scheduled repairs. The decision depends on three conditions, which must all be met at the same time.

  • The crack is stable: control measurements over several months show no growth, and seasonal fluctuations return to the initial values.
  • No water enters through it: there is no darkening after rain, no efflorescence, no traces of leaks inside and no rust streaks.
  • The element is securely fixed: there is no displacement, no hollow sound when tapped and no damage at the fixing points.

If even one condition is not met, the crack is moved from the monitored category to the repair category. For monitored defects, an inspection frequency and a responsible person are assigned, and the records are kept together with the facade documentation; this history is especially valuable when the managing company changes.

How to prevent cracks at the design and installation stage

Most cracks in cladding can be ruled out at the design stage: they arise where the material has been left no room to move. The main measures are well known and inexpensive compared with repairs at height.

  1. Movement joints. The joints of the building must be repeated in the cladding and the subframe; long stretches of facade are divided by additional thermal joints according to calculation.
  2. Fixed and sliding points. Each panel or rail is given a fixed support and sliding fixings that allow thermal expansion.
  3. Gaps between elements. Slabs are not set tight against one another or against window units; the joint width is specified with regard to the size of the element and the temperature range.
  4. Reinforcement of stress concentration zones. Corners of openings in rendered systems are reinforced with additional mesh, and heavy decorative elements are fixed to the load-bearing substrate rather than to the insulation.
  5. Water. Sill flashings, parapet cappings and drips carry water away from joints and fixing points.
  6. Control of workmanship. Anchor testing, survey control of the plane, acceptance of concealed work before it is covered by the cladding.

These solutions are set out in the working documentation; how this work is organised is described in the section on facade design. In refurbishment where render is replaced by a ventilated facade, the load-bearing capacity of the wall for the anchors is checked afresh.

Who is responsible for cracks: warranty and maintenance

Responsibility is determined by the cause of the defect and the time it appeared. Within the warranty period, the contractor rectifies defects related to the quality of work and materials; damage from external effects, alterations and lack of maintenance remains with the owner.

To make the claim well founded, before any repair action it is worth doing the following:

  • record the defect in photographs with a scale rule and a general view;
  • state the location by grid lines and levels, the date of discovery and the weather conditions;
  • notify the contractor in writing and invite them to a joint inspection;
  • do not fill the crack yourself: afterwards the cause is harder to establish, and the warranty obligations may be disputed.

The terms and typical exclusions are discussed in the article on the facade warranty. If the crack is accompanied by displacement of slabs or decorative elements, the question of responsibility becomes secondary: first the risk described in the article on falling cladding is removed.

What to prepare for a repair estimate

The accuracy of a repair estimate depends on the input data. The fuller the picture of the defects, the fewer assumptions the contractor builds in and the less often the scope of work changes after opening up.

  • elevation drawings with the cracks marked, together with their length and width;
  • information on the wall construction and the facade system, and as-built documentation where possible;
  • the history: year of construction, previous repairs, leaks, works nearby;
  • the results of tell-tale monitoring and the survey report, if a survey was carried out;
  • access conditions: height, the possibility of erecting scaffolding or using lifts, restrictions on working hours.

For GFRC cladding, local defects are often remedied without replacing the element; the method is described in the article on repairing chips and cracks in GFRC. Replacement is specified when the zone of the embedded part is damaged or the crack passes through the full section of the element.

FAQ

Are cracks on a building facade dangerous?

Hairline shrinkage cracks are usually not dangerous. Dangerous ones are growing cracks, cracks with displaced cladding, cracks at anchors and balcony supports, and cracks accompanied by distorted windows and doors.

How can I tell if a crack is growing?

Fix a tell-tale or a dated mark at the ends of the crack and check after a few weeks: if the crack has passed the mark or the tell-tale has broken, it is active.

Why do cracks appear in cladding?

Because of impacts, restrained thermal movement, fixing errors, anchor corrosion, saturation and frost damage, and on bonded systems because of cracks in the substrate.

Can I just fill a crack on the facade?

Only if it is superficial and stable. Otherwise, without removing the cause, the crack will open again, and the filling will hide a dangerous condition of the cladding.

Who should survey the cracks?

Facade specialists for the cladding and substructure; if there are signs of deformation of load-bearing structures, organisations that survey the technical condition of buildings.

Should hairline cracks in the facade be sealed before winter?

Preferably, if the surface at that point is regularly wetted. Water that has entered a crack widens it on freezing, and by spring the defect is more noticeable. An elastic compound or a water-repellent impregnation is sufficient; rigid mortar is not suitable for live cracks.

Why do cracks appear more often on the south and west sides of a building?

These elevations are heated more strongly by the sun and cool more quickly, so the daily swings in surface temperature are greatest here. This is especially noticeable on dark finishes and on long stretches without movement joints.

Can a ventilated facade conceal cracks in the wall?

The cladding will hide them visually but will not remove the cause. Before the subframe is installed, the wall is surveyed: anchors must not be set in weakened areas, and developing cracks caused by structural movement are stabilised first.
