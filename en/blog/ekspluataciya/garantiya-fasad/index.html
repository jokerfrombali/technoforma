Facade Warranty: What It Covers and How It Is Documented

A warranty on facade works is the contractor's obligation to remedy, at its own expense, defects discovered during the warranty period, provided they did not arise through the client's fault. It covers both the work performed and the materials used, and its duration, conditions and claims procedure are set out in the construction contract.

The main things to check when signing a contract: what period is stated, what exactly is covered (installation, materials, substructure, cladding), what operating conditions the client must observe and how a claim is made. If the contract does not state a period, the period set by law applies.

The article is written for developers, clients, property managers and technical supervision. Related topics: Facade maintenance, standards and repair.

Statutory period

The Russian Civil Code governs warranties under a works contract in Articles 722–724, and for construction contracts in Article 756.

  • Contractual warranty period. The parties may set it in the contract. During this period the contractor is liable for defects unless it proves they arose from normal wear, improper operation, or improper repair by the client or third parties.
  • If no period is set. Under a construction contract, the maximum period for discovering defective results of work on buildings and structures is five years (Art. 756(2) of the Russian Civil Code), unless otherwise provided by law. In this case the client has to prove that the defect arose before the results were handed over or for reasons that arose before then.
  • Warranty on materials. The manufacturer of a material may set its own warranty period; it applies in the relationship with the purchaser of the material.

For housing built under shared-construction agreements, the developer is liable to the buyers under Federal Law No. 214-FZ; its relationship with the facade contractor is governed by the construction contract.

The facade warranty period usually starts from the moment the results of the work are accepted under a signed acceptance certificate.

Materials and works

A facade warranty consists of several layers, and each may have its own period and its own responsible party.

Subject of warrantyWho is responsibleTypical warranty cases
Installation workContractorLoosened fixings, distorted geometry, leaks through junctions, falling elements
Rainscreen substructureContractor and system manufacturerCorrosion, failure of brackets and rails
Cladding materialsManufacturer / supplierLatent material defects, non-conformity with stated properties
Insulation and wind barrierManufacturer and contractorSlumping, deterioration, incorrect installation
Sealants, coatingsContractor and manufacturerDetachment, cracking earlier than the stated life

When the contractor both manufactures the cladding and installs it, the question "is it the material or the installation" is settled within one company, which is simpler for the client: the claim goes to a single party. If materials and work come from different suppliers, liability has to be divided based on an expert assessment.

Conditions

The warranty applies provided that conditions are met which the contractor may specify in the contract and the operating instructions. Typical conditions:

  • use for the intended purpose and in accordance with the instructions;
  • scheduled inspections and keeping an operating log;
  • no interference with the system without approval — fixing air conditioners, signage or antennas through the cladding;
  • repairs only by the contractor or approved organisations during the warranty period;
  • timely notification of defects discovered.

The warranty usually does not cover normal wear, mechanical damage by third parties, consequences of natural disasters beyond design loads, vandalism, or defects caused by movement of the building's load-bearing structure that was outside the contractor's scope. It is better to read the list of exclusions before signing the contract rather than at the first claim.

Documents

Warranty obligations and the ability to use them are supported by documents:

  • Construction contract — warranty period, scope of warranty, conditions, notification procedure and deadlines for remedying defects.
  • Acceptance certificate for the results of the work — the start date of the warranty.
  • As-built documentation — as-built drawings, records of inspection of concealed work, anchor test results.
  • Data sheets, certificates and declarations for materials — cladding, substructure, insulation, fasteners.
  • Warranty certificate or warranty letter, if the contractor issues them.
  • Facade operating instructions and the inspection log.

Without as-built documentation and an inspection log, a dispute over the cause of a defect becomes harder for both sides. Documents should be kept for the entire service life of the facade, not just the warranty period.

Making a claim

The warranty claim procedure:

  1. Record the defect — photos referenced to gridlines and floors, date of discovery, description. If elements are at risk of falling, fence off the danger zone immediately.
  2. Send a written claim to the contractor at the address stated in the contract, within the deadlines set by the contract. Attach photos and copies of certificates.
  3. Draw up a joint inspection report — with representatives of the client and the contractor, recording the defects and preliminary causes.
  4. If there is disagreement — an expert assessment. If the parties do not agree on the causes, an independent expert assessment is commissioned; under Art. 720 of the Russian Civil Code it is paid for by the party that requested it, but if the contractor's breaches are confirmed, the costs are borne by the contractor.
  5. Remedy — within the agreed period, with a completion certificate.

If the contractor evades its obligations, the client may demand free remedy, a price reduction or reimbursement of the cost of remedying the defects itself — depending on the contract terms and Article 723 of the Russian Civil Code.

Warranty period, service life and limitation period: what is the difference

These three periods are often confused, although they answer different questions. The warranty period shows how long the contractor is liable for defects without his fault having to be proved. The service life indicates how long the facade should last under normal use. The limitation period determines how long a claim can be brought before a court.

PeriodWhat it meansWhat determines it
Warranty periodThe period in which the contractor remedies defects unless he proves the fault of the client or third partiesThe construction contract
Defect discovery periodThe maximum period in which the client may bring claims by proving that the cause arose before acceptanceThe Civil Code of the Russian Federation; five years for buildings and structures
Service lifeThe design durability of the system and materials when the rules of use are observedThe design, system technical documentation, material data sheets
Limitation periodThe time for going to court after a defect is discoveredArticle 725 of the Civil Code; the general period applies to buildings and structures

A facade warranty is always shorter than the service life, and that is normal: after the warranty expires the facade continues to perform, but repairs are paid for by the owner. When comparing proposals, both indicators are therefore considered, as well as whether the service life is confirmed by documents for the system and materials.

Manufacturers' warranties on materials should be considered separately. They run in parallel with the contractor's warranty and often contain their own conditions for storage, installation and care; failure to observe these conditions on site may remove the protection even if the construction contract has been performed.

How to set out the facade works warranty in the contract

The warranty section of the contract must answer the questions: for what, for how long, from what moment and by what procedure. The wording 'warranty in accordance with legislation' leaves the client with general rules only and shifts the burden of proof onto him.

  • Scope. List what is covered: installation, subframe, insulation, cladding, decor, joint sealing, junctions. If the periods differ by element, present them in a table.
  • Start date. Specify the document and date: the acceptance certificate for the whole scope or certificates for each stage. This is particularly important when buildings are handed over in phases.
  • Response time. How many days are allowed for a representative to attend and for the inspection report to be drawn up.
  • Remedy period. Separately for ordinary defects and for emergencies where there is a risk of elements falling.
  • Extension. Record that the warranty period is extended by the time during which the result of the works could not be used because of defects, and how the warranty on replaced elements is calculated.
  • Exclusions. A closed list of cases not covered by the warranty.

The operating instructions are appended to the contract: it is this document the contractor will refer to when assessing whether the client complied with the conditions. Questions worth asking at the selection stage are collected in the article on how to choose a facade contractor.

What is a warranty retention and what can replace it?

A warranty retention is a part of the contract price that the client pays not immediately after acceptance but at the end of an agreed period. It serves as security: if the contractor does not remedy defects, the client covers the costs from the retained amount.

The amount and term of the retention are set by the contract; as a rule it is a few per cent of the cost of the works for a term ranging from several months to the entire warranty period. For the contractor a retention means frozen working capital, so the parties often agree on alternatives.

  • Bank (independent) guarantee. The bank undertakes to pay the client a sum if the warranty obligations are not performed; the retention is then released at once.
  • Staged release. Part of the amount is released after the first winter in service, the remainder at the end of the term.
  • Security deposit. The contractor pays an agreed sum, which is returned if there are no claims.

The choice of method takes into account the scale of the project and the extent to which the chain of works rests with a single contractor. When GFRC production, design and installation are carried out by one company, the subject of the security is easier to describe and there are fewer disputed areas of liability.

Warranty case or in-service defect: how to tell them apart

A warranty defect is distinguished from an in-service one by answering the question of when and why it arose. A cause introduced during design, manufacture or installation is the responsibility of the contractor; a cause that appeared after acceptance through actions on site is the responsibility of the owner.

SignProbable causeWhose responsibility
A panel has shifted or sagged without external impactFixing error, insufficient anchorsContractor
Leak at a junction with a window or parapetDefect in the detail, sealing or flashingsContractor; disputable if damaged by roofers
Crack running through both cladding and wallDeformation of load-bearing structures, settlementUsually outside the facade works warranty
Chip at ground-floor levelImpact from a vehicle, trolley or cleaning equipmentOwner
Streaks and stains beneath an air conditionerUnauthorised installation of equipment, condensateOwner or tenant
Colour change of individual panelsDefective material batch or aggressive washingEstablished by expert examination

In disputed situations a joint inspection with opening-up of the detail is decisive. Cracks require particularly careful analysis, since different mechanisms lie behind the same pattern; they are described in the article on cracks in the facade.

Which client actions most often void the warranty?

As a rule, a warranty is lost not through one major breach but through minor interventions that nobody approved. Typical situations are given below.

  1. Fixing through the cladding. Air conditioners, signs, cameras and cable routes are mounted through the panels, compromising both the fixings and the protection of the insulation.
  2. Aggressive washing. High-pressure washers at close range and acidic products damage the coating and the joints.
  3. Repair by an outside firm. After third-party intervention it is impossible to separate whose defect has appeared.
  4. No scheduled inspections. If no log is kept, the contractor is entitled to point to late detection of the defect.
  5. Alteration of openings and attached structures. Balcony glazing, new canopies and extensions change the loads and the water run-off paths.
  6. Late notification. The defect was noticed but reported a season later, by which time the damage had developed.

A set of rules for tenants and residents and a clear care schedule help to avoid this. Its basis is set out in the article on ventilated facade maintenance.

The client's action plan for the facade warranty period

For the warranty to work, the client needs only a few actions spread over time. Their main purpose is to identify defects while the contractor's obligations are still in force.

  1. At acceptance. Check the set of as-built documentation and record comments in the certificate; the procedure is described in the article on acceptance of facade works.
  2. After the first winter. Carry out an unscheduled inspection: the first freeze-thaw cycle reveals most installation defects.
  3. Annually. Inspect joints, junctions, the plinth and horizontal elements, and record the results in the log with photographs.
  4. After extreme events. Gale-force wind, hail and roof leaks call for an inspection outside the schedule.
  5. Two to three months before the period ends. Arrange a joint inspection with the contractor and draw up a list of comments while the warranty is still valid.

If there is no in-house maintenance team, inspections and routine repairs can be entrusted to an organisation carrying out construction, installation and facade works, provided this is agreed with the contractor who gave the warranty.

It is useful to send the results of each inspection to the contractor even when there are no comments. Such correspondence confirms that the facade was operated in accordance with the instructions and simplifies the discussion if a defect appears towards the end of the warranty period. Photographs are taken from the same points so that changes can be compared year by year.

FAQ

What is the statutory warranty period for a facade?

If the contract does not set one, a five-year period for discovering defects in buildings and structures applies under Art. 756(2) of the Russian Civil Code. The contract may set its own warranty period.

When does the warranty on facade works start?

As a rule, from the date the acceptance certificate for the results of the work is signed, unless the contract provides otherwise.

What is not covered by a facade warranty?

Usually normal wear, damage by third parties, vandalism, consequences of loads beyond design values and unauthorised interference with the system. The exact list is in the contract.

How do I make a warranty claim if cracks appear on the facade?

Record the defect in photos, send the contractor a written claim, draw up a joint inspection report, and commission an expert assessment if there is disagreement.

What documents are needed for a warranty claim?

The contract, acceptance certificate, as-built documentation, material data sheets, operating instructions and inspection log.

Is the warranty extended after a defect has been remedied?

As a rule, the warranty period is extended by the time during which the result of the works could not be used because of defects. It is better to state expressly in the contract how the warranty on replaced elements is calculated.

What is a warranty retention for facade works?

It is a part of the contract price that the client pays at the end of an agreed period after acceptance. It secures the remedying of defects; the amount and term are set by the contract, and a bank guarantee may be used instead of a retention.

Does the warranty remain valid when the building changes owner?

Warranty obligations arise from the construction contract. A new owner may bring claims if the relevant rights have passed to him by law or by agreement of the parties, so this point should be provided for in the contract and in the transaction for the building.
