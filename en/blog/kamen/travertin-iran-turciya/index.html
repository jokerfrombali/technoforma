Iranian vs Turkish Travertine: The Differences

Iranian and Turkish travertine are the two largest segments of this stone on the Russian market. Both are the same rock with a similar porous layered structure, but they differ in palette, pattern and the properties of individual quarries. Iranian travertine is often chosen for its rich warm shades, from beige to red and gold; Turkish for its wide range of light cream and walnut tones and large production volumes.

The main selection criterion is not the country but the specific quarry and batch: the variation in properties within one country is greater than between countries. For a facade, test reports decide; for an interior, colour and pattern come first.

This article is for architects, designers and clients. Other stones are covered in Natural stone for facades and interiors.

Quarries

Travertine forms near mineral springs, and both Iran and Turkey have many such areas.

  • Turkey is one of the world's largest exporters of travertine. Well-known quarries are in the Denizli area (Western Anatolia), near the travertine terraces of Pamukkale. Turkish stone is quarried in large volumes and widely available on the market.
  • Iran has several major quarrying areas; Iranian travertines are known above all for their colour variety, including red, yellow and gold shades that are rare elsewhere.
  • Italy is the home of "Roman" travertine from the Tivoli area, the historic benchmark against which others are compared.

For a buyer in Russia there are practical differences too. Turkish stone is generally easier to find in large volumes from different suppliers and to reorder several years later. Iranian stone is valued for its distinctive colours, but a specific grade may be available only in limited batches, so it is best to reserve the full volume for the project at once.

Colour and structure

Colour and pattern are how travertines from the two countries are told apart by eye. Below are typical tendencies; a specific stone may fall outside them.

ParameterIranian travertineTurkish travertine
PaletteBeige, cream, as well as rich yellows, golds, reds and walnutsMostly light: ivory, cream, beige, light walnut; silver and dark grades also exist
PatternOften pronounced layering and contrastFrom uniform to striped, depending on quarry and cut
PorosityVaries by quarryVaries by quarry
RangeUnique colours, fewer uniform volumesLarge choice of standard light grades

The pattern also depends strongly on the cut: along the layers (vein cut) gives a striped texture, across them (cross cut) a uniform "cloudy" one. So when comparing samples it is important to look at the same cut and the same surface finish.

Quality

The quality of travertine is determined not by the country but by a number of measurable indicators:

  • Density and porosity: the denser the stone, the better it withstands outdoor conditions and the easier it is to maintain.
  • Frost resistance and water absorption: critical for facades; values are taken from the batch test reports.
  • Homogeneity: no large cavities, cracks or soft layers.
  • Grade: suppliers grade stone by the number of pores, colour uniformity and defects; grade designations differ between suppliers.
  • Quality of pore filling: evenness, colour and UV resistance of the filler.
  • Geometry: accuracy of thickness calibration and dimensions, square corners.

Practical advice: for one project, order the whole volume from one quarry and, where possible, one batch. Even within one country, the shade from different quarries varies noticeably.

Prices

The price of travertine depends on grade, quarry, format, thickness, surface finish and logistics. Rare colours (for example, rich red Iranian grades) are usually more expensive than mainstream light ones. Large volumes of standard Turkish stone are easier to source and to match when reordering.

The total cost of cladding depends not only on the price per square metre of slab but also on:

  • the wastage rate in the layout, which is higher for large formats and complex geometry;
  • manufacturing profiled elements such as cornices, sills and window surrounds;
  • the installation method and, for a facade, the substructure;
  • pore filling and protective treatment.

How to choose

How to choose between Iranian and Turkish travertine:

  1. Start with colour. If you need a rich warm or red tone, look at Iranian grades; for light cream and beige, the Turkish range is wider.
  2. Define the application. For a facade, request frost resistance and water absorption test reports; for floors, abrasion resistance.
  3. Compare samples of the same cut and finish, under different lighting, preferably large ones: travertine's pattern cannot be read on a small sample.
  4. Check the available volume in one batch, with a margin for breakage and future repairs.
  5. Get a layout: it shows the actual quantity required and allows slabs to be matched by pattern.

For facades in a cold climate, density and frost resistance are decisive, and there is no rule that Iranian or Turkish is better: both countries have dense grades suitable for exterior cladding and soft ones best kept for interiors. If several stones suit in colour, choose the one with better test results.

For interiors (floors, walls, bathrooms), pattern, shade and the pore-count grade come first. For high-traffic floors, dense grades with filled pores and a honed finish are chosen: it is less slippery and does not show scratches as quickly as a polished finish.

Filled or unfilled travertine: how does it affect a comparison of Iranian and Turkish stone?

The way the pores are treated affects the appearance and behaviour of a slab more than the country of origin does. The same Iranian or Turkish travertine with filled and with open pores looks like two different materials, so samples are only worth comparing in the same finish.

Filling is carried out at the factory: the cavities are closed with a cement-based or polymer compound matched to the stone, or with clear resin, and the surface is then honed. Open (unfilled) stone keeps its natural pores and gives a livelier, matt texture.

FinishWhere it is appropriatePoints to watch
Filled, honedFloors, interior walls, facades with a calm, even planeThe filler colour must match the stone; outdoors a polymer filler may change shade over time
Filled, polishedInteriors, entrance areas under a canopyPolish lasts better on dense grades; slippery for external paving
Open pores, honed or sawnFacades, plinths, boundary walls, landscapingDust collects in the pores; a protective impregnation is advisable in splash zones and near the ground
Brushed, antiquedPaving, terraces, classical interiorsEdges and relief hide minor chips but make cleaning harder

For external cladding, open pores or cement filling are chosen more often: such a surface ages evenly. The requirements for stone used outdoors are covered in more detail in the article on travertine on the facade.

Slab formats and thickness: is there a difference between the countries

There is no fundamental difference in sizes: both Iranian and Turkish factories produce slabs, cut-to-size panels and modular tiles. What differs is the customary product range: Turkish producers traditionally offer many ready-made formats and paving sets, while Iranian stone more often arrives as slabs and large panels for cutting.

  • Slabs. Thickness is usually 20 and 30 mm; facade panels, window sills, steps and worktops are cut from them. Slab size depends on the block, and in layered stone it is limited by fissuring.
  • Project-specific panels. Cut to a particular facade or floor layout. For mechanical fixing to a subframe the thickness is determined by calculation, as a rule from 30 mm.
  • Modular tiles. Standard formats for floors and walls, convenient for small quantities and repeat orders.
  • Profiled elements. Cornices, window surrounds, string courses and balusters are made from blocks or thick slabs and require dense stone without large cavities.

The larger the format, the higher the demands on the block and the greater the waste, so on large-format cladding the choice of grade has a more noticeable effect on the budget. How the section is selected for the loads and fixing method is explained in the article on stone slab thickness.

Calibration is worth clarifying separately. Panels calibrated for thickness are easier to fix with adhesive and to a subframe; uncalibrated material is cheaper but takes more time to level. For mechanical fixing the quality of the kerfs and fixing holes is also checked: in porous stone they are made at the factory, not on site.

How to compare samples and batches: a step-by-step procedure

A sound comparison sets two specific batches side by side, not two countries. To keep the choice between Iranian and Turkish travertine from resting on the impression made by a couple of small tiles, it helps to proceed in steps.

  1. Define the use: facade, plinth, floor, wall, paving. This determines the set of mandatory properties.
  2. Shortlist 3–5 grades by colour and request samples in the same finish and the same cutting direction.
  3. View the samples in daylight, both dry and wetted: wet stone shows how it will look after rain and after impregnation.
  4. Request a test report for each grade: density, water absorption, strength, frost resistance. The data must relate to the same quarry.
  5. Assess the variation within the batch — from slab photographs or at the warehouse, across several slabs rather than one.
  6. Clarify the available quantity, the option of reserving stock and the terms for reordering the same grade.
  7. Assemble a control mock-up from several panels and approve it as the reference for acceptance.

A reference mock-up removes most disputes: it records the permitted range of shade, the density of the pattern and the number of pores.

How is travertine supplied from Iran and Turkey?

The route and lead time depend on whether the required grade is in stock. A stock item is dispatched immediately; a made-to-order item goes the whole way from the quarry: block extraction, sawing, finishing, packing, transport and customs clearance.

Turkish stone generally travels by sea through the Black Sea ports, Iranian stone across the Caspian or by road. Transshipment warehouses in the south therefore matter to a supplier: TECHNOFORMA has them from Novorossiysk and Makhachkala to Yekaterinburg and Novosibirsk, which allows stone to reach the site without unnecessary handling.

What is worth agreeing in the contract in advance:

  • the quarry and commercial grade, not just the name of the stone;
  • slab selection from photographs or at the factory for stones with a pronounced pattern;
  • packing: wooden crates or pallets with spacers, and panel marking according to the layout;
  • an allowance for breakage and trimming that arrives together with the main batch;
  • a dispatch schedule by work section if the facade is installed in stages.

The supply chain is described in more detail in the article on importing natural stone.

The season also affects the lead time: in winter extraction and transport slow down in a number of areas, and demand for popular grades rises before the start of the construction season. An order for summer installation is therefore sensibly placed in advance.

Installation and protection: general rules for travertine of any origin

The installation method does not depend on the country of extraction: it is determined by the density of the particular stone, the panel format and the building type. On high-rise and public buildings travertine is usually fixed to a subframe with an air gap, so the cladding is not in contact with mortar and moisture leaves the wall freely. Clip options, concealed fixing and subframe requirements are described in the article on the ventilated stone facade.

For adhesive fixing on plinths and in interiors, white adhesives for natural stone are used with light grades: grey cement can cause staining and alter the tone of the slab. Horizontal surfaces — parapets, window sills, string courses — are formed with a fall and a drip, otherwise water will stand in the pores.

After installation, porous stone in wetted zones is treated with a vapour-permeable water repellent; the product is checked on a trial area, since some impregnations deepen the colour. The choice of products is covered in the article on protecting natural stone.

Joints between panels on a facade are not packed tight with rigid cement mortar: stone and substrate respond differently to temperature changes, and the cladding needs an allowance for movement.

Typical mistakes when choosing between Iranian and Turkish travertine

  • Choosing by name. Trade names are not standardised: different suppliers sell stone from different quarries and grades under one name.
  • Comparing different finishes. A polished filled sample and a sawn one with open pores cannot be compared for colour.
  • One sample for the whole project. A small tile does not show the variation of shade and pattern within a batch.
  • An interior grade on the facade. Stone with large cavities and no frost-resistance report is acceptable indoors but risky outside.
  • Buying in parts. A second batch almost always differs in tone, even from the same quarry.
  • Saving on thickness. A thin panel of layered stone for mechanical fixing calls for a calculation, not a decision based on what the neighbours did.

FAQ

What is Roman travertine?

Travertine from the Tivoli area near Rome, the material of many historic buildings in Rome. Today the name is also often used for similar-looking light travertines from other countries.

How does Turkish travertine differ from Iranian?

Turkish travertine is more often available in light cream and beige tones in large volumes; Iranian in rich warm shades, including reds and golds. Technical properties depend on the specific quarry.

Which travertine is best for a facade?

The one with confirmed frost resistance and low water absorption for your climate, regardless of country. For facades, grades with filled pores are usually chosen.

Is travertine connected with Gaudí?

Gaudí's works make wide use of local Catalan stone; there is no direct connection with Roman travertine. The search query probably combines an interest in travertine with an interest in Art Nouveau architecture.

Can Iranian and Turkish travertine be combined on one project?

Yes, if the concept provides for it: for example, light Turkish stone on the main plane and rich Iranian stone in string courses, portals or the entrance lobby interior. Stone from different quarries should not be mixed within a single plane — the difference in tone and pattern will read as a defect.

Which travertine is easier to reorder after several years?

Common light grades from high-volume extraction are usually easier to find. However, no quarry guarantees an exact match with the first batch, so for repairs and possible later works it is better to include spare panels in the main delivery and keep them in storage.

Do the pores of travertine need to be filled on a facade?

Not necessarily. On vertical planes open pores do not usually cause problems if the stone is dense and has confirmed frost resistance. Filling is justified on horizontal and sloping surfaces where water may stand in the pores, and where a smooth plane is required.
