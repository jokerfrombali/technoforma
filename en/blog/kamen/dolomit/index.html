Dolomite: Properties and Use in Cladding

Dolomite is a sedimentary carbonate rock whose main mineral is dolomite, a double carbonate of calcium and magnesium. In cladding it is a dense, light stone in white, grey, beige and yellowish tones that is harder and less porous than most limestones.

The key selection criterion is the density and water absorption of the specific batch: they determine whether the stone can be used on facades, plinths and paving or only indoors.

Dolomite is chosen for cladding facades, plinths, fences, paths and interiors in both private and multi-apartment construction. You can compare it with other rocks in the section Natural stone for facades and interiors.

Composition

In terms of composition, dolomite is a rock made up of more than half the mineral dolomite, CaMg(CO₃)₂. Besides this, the stone contains calcite, clay particles, quartz and iron oxides. Colour and properties depend on their amount: pure dolomite is light, almost white; iron impurities give yellowish and pinkish shades, while organic matter and clay give grey ones.

Dolomite forms in marine and lagoon conditions when magnesium replaces part of the calcium in calcareous sediments. That is why dolomites and limestones often occur side by side in nature, and transitional rocks are called dolomitic limestone or calcareous dolomite.

In construction, cladding dolomite means dense varieties suitable for sawing into slabs. Large deposits exist in various regions of Russia, including Dagestan: Dagestan dolomite is one of the best-known rocks on the facade stone market.

Properties

The properties of dolomite depend on the deposit. Typical ranges are given below; values for a specific batch are in the product data sheet or test report.

Dolomite is non-combustible, environmentally friendly and saws and hones well. It reacts with weak acids more slowly than limestone but is not fully resistant to them: aggressive chemicals and acidic cleaners leave marks on the surface.

Difference from Limestone

Dolomite and limestone are often confused: both are light, carbonate rocks and are frequently quarried in the same places. The difference lies in their mineral composition and, as a result, in how the stone behaves.

ParameterDolomiteLimestone
Main mineraldolomite (calcium magnesium carbonate)calcite (calcium carbonate)
Hardnesshigherlower
Reaction with hydrochloric acidweak, noticeable in powdervigorous fizzing
Porosityusually lowerfrom dense to very porous
Applicationsfacades, plinths, paving, stepsfacades, decoration, interiors

The practical conclusion: if you need cladding for an area with higher moisture and mechanical loads, dolomite is usually chosen out of the two similar-looking stones. Soft limestone is more convenient for carved details.

It is hard to tell the rocks apart by eye. The simplest field test is a drop of dilute hydrochloric acid: limestone fizzes actively, while dolomite reacts weakly or only as powder. For design decisions, however, what you need is not a test but a batch test report stating density, water absorption and strength.

Cladding

Dolomite for cladding is produced as sawn tiles and slabs of various thicknesses and finishes: sawn, honed, rock-faced (split), antiqued and bush-hammered.

Facade cladding with dolomite is done in two ways:

  • Wet method: tiles about 20–40 mm thick on frost-resistant adhesive over a prepared wall. Suitable for low-rise houses and plinths.
  • Ventilated rainscreen facade: slabs 30 mm thick or more on a substructure with clips or anchors. Required for multi-storey buildings and large formats; slab sizes, fixings and substructure spacing are determined by calculation.

Cladding dolomite is well suited to plinths: it is denser than most carbonate rocks and copes better with splashes and snow. For this zone the densest grades are used, always with waterproofing and impregnation.

TECHNOFORMA provides a full facade cycle: design, insulation, installation of ventilated rainscreen facade substructures and natural stone cladding. The cost of works is determined after a site inspection.

A few practical rules for dolomite cladding:

  • sort tiles by tone and lay them out on site before installation to avoid patchiness;
  • leave movement joints as per design, especially on large surfaces and at junctions with other materials;
  • use fixings and adhesives designed for natural stone and exterior work;
  • do not install stone on a wet or frozen substrate.

Care

Caring for dolomite is simple, but there are a few rules.

  • Impregnation. After installation the surface is treated with a water repellent for natural stone, which reduces soiling and efflorescence. The impregnation is renewed once it loses effect, when water no longer beads on the surface.
  • Washing. Use water with pH-neutral stone cleaners. Acidic products for removing cement residue must not be used: they etch the carbonate surface.
  • Efflorescence. First eliminate the source of moisture, then remove the deposit with a dry brush and special stone cleaners.
  • In winter, no salt-based de-icing agents on dolomite paths and steps; sand or marble chippings are safer.

Polished dolomite indoors is wiped with a soft damp cloth; marks from acids (wine, lemon juice, vinegar) should be removed promptly.

Dolomite finishes and formats: what to choose for each zone

The finish of dolomite is chosen by the zone of use: sawn or honed surfaces are usual for facade walls, rough finishes for plinths and steps, polished or satin finishes for interiors. Dense grades take a polish, while more porous ones stay matt whatever the treatment, so the ability to achieve a gloss is checked on a sample from the same batch rather than from a catalogue.

FinishAppearanceWhere it suits
Sawneven matt surface with faint saw marksfacade walls, fences, reveals

Bush-hammeredevenly rough, slip-resistantsteps, landings, plinth
Split face (rock face)textured natural cleftplinth, retaining walls, rusticated corners
Antiquedrounded edges, soft reliefpaving, landscape, interior

The methods of stone surface treatment are covered in detail in a separate article. In terms of format, tiles and slabs of modular sizes are most common, with thicker slabs used for ventilated systems. The thickness of cladding slabs depends on the fixing method, the height of the building and the size of the slab itself, and is determined by calculation.

Where should dolomite not be used?

Dolomite is not suitable for zones with constant acid and abrasive exposure, and its porous grades are not suitable for places where the stone stays wet for long periods and freezes. This is a general limitation of carbonate rocks: in dolomite it is less pronounced than in limestone, but it does not disappear entirely.

  • Kitchen worktops and bar counters: fruit acids, vinegar and wine leave dull marks on a polished surface.
  • Driveways and areas treated with de-icing agents in winter: salts accelerate surface deterioration, and granite or basalt is normally chosen for such places.
  • Horizontal surfaces without a fall: standing water and repeated freezing cause the top layer to flake.
  • Pool and fountain basins with chemically treated water, unless the stone has been tested for such conditions.
  • High-temperature steam rooms: the stone may crack under rapid heating and cooling.

In borderline cases the decision is made on the test report for the specific batch, not on the name of the rock. Dense dolomite with low water absorption often performs where loose limestone would not last long, but this has to be confirmed by figures.

How to check a batch of dolomite before purchase

A batch is assessed by its documents and by samples: the name "dolomite" in itself says nothing about whether the stone is fit for a facade. Properties vary widely between deposits and even between beds of one quarry, so it is convenient to carry out the check step by step.

  1. Request a certificate or test report stating density, water absorption, compressive strength and frost resistance grade.
  2. Obtain not one sample but several tiles showing the limits of acceptable variation in tone and pattern.
  3. Inspect the samples for cracks, cavities, clay seams and rusty inclusions: the latter eventually cause streaks on a light surface.
  4. Wet the stone: damp dolomite shows how the cladding will look after rain and after impregnation.
  5. Agree the tolerances for dimensions and thickness, especially for a ventilated system, where slab calibration matters.
  6. Record in the contract that the whole delivery comes from one section of the quarry and is sorted by tone before dispatch.

For a large project it is sensible to approve reference samples and keep them until the works are complete. They are used to settle disputed acceptance issues as successive lorries of stone arrive on site.

Dolomite or another stone: comparison with sandstone, travertine and granite

Dolomite occupies an intermediate position: it is denser and more durable than most limestones and sandstones, yet easier to work and calmer in pattern than granite. The choice depends on what matters most in the project — a warm light tone, carved decoration or maximum wear resistance.

Dolomite on different building types: residential complexes, hotels, private houses

Cladding tasks differ by building type, and dolomite performs differently in each case. In apartment buildings it is usually used to finish the lower storeys, entrance areas and plinth — the zones that are at eye level and bear the greatest loads. The upper storeys are often finished in lighter materials matched to the tone of the stone.

  • Residential complexes: consistency of tone over large areas matters, so the stone is purchased as a single batch with an allowance for cutting and replacement.
  • Hotels and public buildings: large formats and shaped details are in demand — portals, surrounds, columns; here a layout with joints aligned to the axes of openings is particularly important.
  • Business centres: a ventilated system with insulation is used more often, where the stone acts as a screen and does not depend on the condition of the wall.
  • Private houses: adhesive fixing on a sound substrate is common, and the overall volume allows slabs to be selected from stock.

In all cases it is useful to decide in advance who is responsible for design, supply and installation. When these stages are split between different contractors, discrepancies in dimensions and shades come to light on the wall itself, where correcting them is most expensive.

What determines the durability of dolomite cladding

The service life of a dolomite facade is determined not only by the rock but also by the construction details. With a competent design, dense stone lasts for decades without visible loss of appearance, and premature defects are almost always related to water.

Typical causes are missing flashings and drips on parapets and sills, stone abutting the perimeter paving without a gap, rigid adhesive without movement joints, and installation on a damp substrate. Water that gets behind the cladding carries salts to the surface: this is how efflorescence on stone appears. When it freezes, the same water detaches the tile from the substrate.

The second group of factors is the fixings. Stainless steel components are used in a ventilated system: mild steel and damaged galvanising eventually produce rust streaks, which are difficult to remove from light stone. The third is the compatibility of materials: adhesive and grout for light dolomite are based on white cement, otherwise dark stains may show through along the edges of the tiles.

What to prepare for a dolomite cladding estimate

For a preliminary estimate, facade drawings and an understanding of the fixing method are sufficient; the precise cost is established once the stone layout has been prepared. The more complete the input data, the fewer assumptions remain in the proposal.

  • Facade elevations with dimensions and levels, areas by zone: walls, plinth, reveals.
  • Wall material and condition, presence and thickness of insulation.
  • Desired finish, format and approximate slab thickness.
  • List of shaped elements: sills, parapet copings, corner pieces, surrounds.
  • Region of construction and number of storeys.
  • Deadlines and the preferred delivery schedule.

Using these data, the stone is selected from stock or to order, and the layout and schedule of items are prepared. If there is no cladding design, it is developed before the stone is ordered: this rules out a shortage of slabs of uncommon sizes at the final stage as well as unnecessary surplus.

FAQ

What kind of stone is dolomite?

A sedimentary rock consisting mainly of the mineral dolomite, a calcium magnesium carbonate. It is a dense, light stone used for cladding facades, plinths, paving and interiors.

Are dolomite and Dagestan stone the same thing?

No. “Dagestan stone” is a trade name for several rocks from Dagestan, including dolomite. Dagestan dolomite is one of them.

Is dolomite suitable for facades?

Yes, dense grades of dolomite are well suited to facades and plinths. The water absorption and frost resistance of the batch should be checked and a water-repellent treatment applied.

Can dolomite be used in a sauna?

In the changing room and relaxation area, yes, for wall cladding. In the steam room the stone is exposed to temperature and moisture swings; only dense grades are suitable for cladding there, and stones for the sauna heater are selected separately.

What are the characteristics of dolomite stone?

Typically: density about 2300–2800 kg/m³, compressive strength of tens of MPa, water absorption of a few percent, Mohs hardness 3.5–4. Exact values are in the batch test report.

Can dolomite be white?

Yes, pure dolomites are light, from white to milky. Impurities give grey, beige and yellowish shades.

Does dolomite darken on a facade over time?

Light dolomite gradually acquires a patina: the tone becomes slightly warmer and more even. Noticeable darkening is usually related not to the stone itself but to soiling and wetting — in areas without flashings and beneath parapets. Water-repellent impregnation and correct water-shedding details slow this process down.

Can dolomite be combined with GFRC decorative elements?

Yes, this is a common solution. The wall planes are clad in stone, while cornices, window surrounds and other three-dimensional details are made of GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete), with colour and texture matched to the stone. This reduces the load on the walls and simplifies the production of complex profiles.

What slab thickness is needed for a dolomite ventilated facade?

The thickness is set by the design: it depends on the strength of the stone, the slab size, the type of fixing and the wind load. Slabs for ventilated systems are, as a rule, thicker than those for adhesive fixing. The exact value is assigned by calculation and according to the recommendations of the subframe manufacturer.
