Natural stone from India is above all granite and quartzitic sandstone in a wide range of colours, from black and green to warm yellow-brown. Together with Turkish marble and travertine, Chinese granite and Iranian onyx and travertine, it forms the core of the imported range for facades and interiors in Russia.

The main criterion when choosing imported stone is not the country but the specific quarry and batch: they determine colour, uniformity, porosity, strength and frost resistance. So the decision is made on samples and batch documents, and the project schedule is checked against whether the required item is in stock or has to be shipped to order.

TECHNOFORMA imports stone from India, Turkey, China and Iran, keeps stock from these countries in a warehouse in the Moscow region, and brings in items to order from other quarries. An overview of stones and applications is in the section Natural stone for facades and interiors.

Each of the four countries is known for its own stones. The summary below is industry guidance; the specific range in stock is confirmed for each project.

Besides imports, TECHNOFORMA has extensive experience with stones from Dagestan, which are in demand on projects in southern Russia. If the required stone is not available from these sources, it can be brought to order from quarries in other countries.

Quality

The quality of imported stone is determined by the quarry, the bed being worked and the care taken in processing at the factory. Stone sold under the same trade name can differ noticeably in colour and pattern from batch to batch. What is checked:

  • Physical and mechanical properties: density, water absorption, compressive and flexural strength, frost resistance. Values depend on the quarry; they are taken from test reports for the specific batch, not from general reference books.
  • Batch uniformity: colour, pattern, cavities, cracks, inclusions, glassy veins and rust spots from iron-bearing minerals.
  • Geometry: tolerances on thickness, length and width, squareness of edges, flatness.
  • Surface quality: uniformity of polishing, flaming, brushing; resin repairs and mesh on the back.

For a facade in the Russian climate, the key questions are frost resistance and water absorption: a soft marble or travertine that performs perfectly in southern Turkey will deteriorate in central Russia without protection and a proper construction. The stone is matched to the climate at the design stage.

Logistics

Stone from India, China, Turkey and Iran is shipped by sea in containers or by combined sea and land routes, with transhipment in ports. The main links in the chain:

  1. selection of blocks or finished slabs at the factory, photo record of the batch;
  2. cutting and processing to specification, if the stone is ordered cut to size;
  3. packing: wooden crates or bundles of slabs on A-frames, spacers between polished faces;
  4. sea freight to port, customs clearance;
  5. delivery to the warehouse in Russia and on to the site.

Stone is heavy and fragile, so packing and the number of transhipments strongly affect cost and breakage. TECHNOFORMA has warehouses from Novorossiysk and Makhachkala to Novosibirsk and Yekaterinburg, and its production base and stone warehouses are in the Moscow region. This makes it possible to plan delivery according to the region of the project.

Lead times

The lead time for imported stone is made up of several stages, each depending on the specific order: availability of blocks of the required stone at the quarry, workload of the processing plant, volume and complexity of cutting, the transport route, customs and domestic delivery. There is no universal figure; it is given once the item and volume have been chosen.

How to shorten lead times in practice:

  • at the design stage, check the selected items against warehouse stock: stock stone ships without waiting for sea freight;
  • approve the layout and specification before ordering, so the volume does not have to be recalculated along the way;
  • include an allowance for trimming and breakage in a single order: a top-up order from another batch takes longer and risks a colour mismatch;
  • for large projects, split delivery into phases following the installation sequence.

Seasonality is also taken into account: quarries in some regions reduce extraction in the rainy season or in winter months, and in periods of high demand factories and container lines are more heavily loaded. The actual lead time for a specific item is fixed in the supply contract after the availability of blocks and the cutting schedule have been confirmed. For projects with a tight installation schedule, it makes sense to start purchasing stone in parallel with developing the working documentation, not after it is finished.

Warehouse in Russia

A warehouse in Russia serves three purposes: speed, the chance to see the stone in person and batch consistency. TECHNOFORMA keeps stone stock from all four import sources (India, Turkey, China, Iran) with a production base in the Moscow region. The stone is processed there too: the company's equipment handles both soft stones (sandstone, shell limestone, dolomite, limestone) and hard ones.

What this gives the client:

  • selection from real slabs and samples rather than catalogue photos;
  • cutting and edge and surface finishing to size for the project without another shipment abroad;
  • layout, measurements by the company's own surveyors, 3D visualisation and installation in one workflow;
  • supply to order from anywhere in the world if the required item is not in stock.

The range includes shell limestone, granite, basalt, dolomite, limestone, marble, onyx, sandstone and travertine.

How an order for imported stone proceeds: stages from sample to warehouse

Supplying stone from abroad for a project is a sequence of eight steps, and most problems with tone, dimensions and timing originate in the first three. The more precisely the brief is formulated before the order is placed, the fewer surprises there are on unloading.

  1. Stone selection. Based on samples and the technical brief: purpose, climate, finish, slab format, required quantity.
  2. Enquiry to the quarry or factory. The availability of blocks and the slab sizes on offer are clarified, and photographs and video of current extraction are requested.
  3. Agreeing the specification. Dimensions, thickness, tolerances, finish of face and edges, grade, packaging, marking.
  4. Material selection. Numbered photographs of slabs, approval of control samples; for large quantities an inspector's visit to the factory is justified.
  5. Production. Sawing, finishing, calibration and, for pattern-matched zones, a dry layout at the factory with photographic records.
  6. Packaging and dispatch. Crates or pallets, securing in the container or lorry.
  7. Transport and customs clearance.
  8. Acceptance at the warehouse. Inspection, sorting and make-up of consignments by work section for dispatch to site.

For facades a design component is added to this chain: the subframe calculation, the details and the slab schedule are prepared in parallel with material selection, so that the specification is final by the time sawing begins. If the design is still changing, it is sensible to cut the stone in stages from a reserved quantity of one batch: first the standard slabs of the main field, then the make-up and special pieces based on the actual survey of the building.

Each stage has a point after which changes are expensive: after sawing the format cannot be changed, and after dispatch no quantity can be added from the same block. The client's overall sequence of actions is covered in the article on how to order stone for a project.

Blocks, slabs or cut-to-size: in what form to ship stone

For facades with a completed design it is usually more economical to ship slabs cut to size at the producer's factory; for interiors and non-standard pieces, raw slabs that are cut in Russia. The form of supply affects the cost of transport, waste and flexibility when the design changes.

Form of supplyWhen appropriateAdvantagesLimitations
BlocksMassive pieces, very large quantities of one stoneFull control of sawing and patternA sawing facility is needed; hidden defects inside the block are possible
SlabsInteriors, worktops, book-matched layouts, non-standard formatsCutting to the task locally, quick changesWaste stays with the buyer; large slabs are fragile in transit
Cut-to-sizeFacades and floors with an approved layoutReady for installation; no surplus stone is transportedThe specification is needed in advance; changes after sawing are impossible
Modular tilesStandard floors and wallsLow price, stable geometryLimited range of sizes and thicknesses

Cut-to-size supply requires that a layout with slab marking is ready before the order: the factory cuts strictly to the schedule, and an error in it will be discovered only on site. For stones with a pronounced pattern, such as travertine, the specification states the cutting direction separately, along or across the bedding.

What is checked when an imported batch is accepted?

Acceptance is the last moment at which a quality claim can be made with good grounds, so it is carried out before the stone is sorted and sent to site. The check is made against control samples and the specification, and the results are recorded in a report with photographs.

  • Packaging. Integrity of crates, absence of impact marks and load shift, condition of the spacers between slabs.
  • Marking. Agreement of crate and slab numbers with the packing list and the layout.
  • Geometry. Length, width, thickness, difference of diagonals and flatness, sampled from each crate.
  • Tone and pattern. Comparison with approved samples in daylight, dry and wet.
  • Defects. Cracks, chipped edges and corners, unfilled voids, traces of repair on the face.
  • Stains. Light stones can be discoloured by damp packaging timber and metal strapping; such slabs are set aside immediately.
  • Quantity. By size type, not only by total area.

Slabs with deviations are not necessarily rejected: they are often used for trimming, on reveals or in inconspicuous zones. What matters is that this decision is taken at the warehouse, not by installers on the scaffolding.

Risks of importing natural stone and ways to reduce them

The main risks of supply from abroad are tone mismatch, breakage in transit, delays and changes in cost. None of them can be eliminated completely, but each is managed by organisational measures.

RiskCauseHow it is reduced
Tone differs from the sampleStone from different blocks or quarry benchesControl samples with limits of acceptable variation, slab selection from photographs, one order for the whole quantity
Breakage and chipsTranshipment, weak packagingPackaging requirements in the specification, spare slabs, cargo insurance
Late deliveryProduction queue, weather, port congestionOrdering ahead of the installation programme, warehouse storage
Thickness deviationsVarying standard of equipment at factoriesTolerances in the specification, calibration, sample checks
Hidden cracksNatural features of the stoneMesh reinforcement on the back, inspection before dispatch
Rising costExchange rates, freightPurchasing the whole quantity as one batch

A warehouse stock of popular items removes most of these risks: the stone is already in Russia, it can be inspected and received without waiting.

What documents and tests are needed for imported stone

A batch is accompanied by commercial and transport documents, while the project needs technical data on the stone. The former confirm the legality of import and the contents of the cargo; the latter confirm the suitability of the stone for a particular use.

The supplier is asked for a description of the stone and for test results: density, water absorption, compressive and flexural strength, frost resistance, and abrasion resistance for floors and steps. For granites and other igneous rocks, radiation indicators are checked in addition. Foreign laboratories work to their own methods, so for critical facades samples from the delivered batch are, as a rule, tested again in a Russian laboratory.

This is especially important for porous stones in a climate with frequent freeze-thaw transitions: the behaviour of stone on a facade in a warm country does not guarantee the same durability in central Russia. Requirements for facade stones, using the most durable material as an example, are described on the page about granite for facades and plinths.

When import is justified and when Russian stone is the better choice

Import is justified when the required stone has no domestic equivalent in colour and pattern, or when a large quantity of uniform material is needed. If suitable stone is quarried closer, short logistics almost always bring gains in lead time and in the ability to buy more.

  • Import. Marbles and onyxes with a pronounced pattern, travertine, coloured granites, exotic quartzites. The differences between the two main sources of travertine are examined in the article on Iranian and Turkish travertine.
  • Russian stone. Limestones, dolomites, sandstones and shell limestone from the North Caucasus, granites from northern and Urals deposits. Their behaviour in the local climate is well known, and buying additional material is easier.

For projects in the south of Russia, Dagestan stone is often the logical choice: the quarries are nearby and the stone is familiar to regional architecture. Both sources are frequently combined on one facade: imported stone on accent zones and local stone on the main field.

How to tie delivery to the construction programme

Stone to order must be put into production before installation of the subframe begins, otherwise facade work will stop while waiting for material. Exact lead times depend on the country, the stone and the route, so a reserve is built into the programme.

Duration is affected by factors unrelated to the order itself: the rainy season and holiday periods in producing countries, port congestion, weather restrictions at transhipment. Purchasing is therefore planned backwards from the date when the stone is needed on site, not forwards from the date the contract is signed. Delivery to site is best made by work section, by elevation or by storey in the order of installation, so that the stone does not lie in the open and is not re-stacked several times.

Storage on site also affects the result. Crates are placed on a level base under cover, and slabs are kept upright on edge with spacers; light stones are protected from contact with rusting metal, cement dust and wet timber. With winter deliveries the stone is allowed to warm up and dry before adhesive fixing, whereas ventilated cladding on mechanical fixings can also be installed at sub-zero temperatures if the method statement permits it.

FAQ

What is good about stone from Turkey?

Turkey is one of the largest sources of marble, travertine, limestone and onyx. These stones are valued for their warm shades and pattern; for facades in a cold climate they are chosen according to the frost resistance of the batch and protected with an impregnator.

Is stone from China lower in quality?

No, quality is determined by the quarry and processing, not the country. China supplies large volumes of granite and basalt; as with any import, the batch is checked against samples and test reports.

Which Indian natural stone is suitable for a facade?

Most often granites and dense sandstones and quartzites. Suitability of a specific item for the project's climate is confirmed by frost resistance and water absorption data for the batch.

Can you bring in stone that is not in stock?

Yes, TECHNOFORMA supplies stone to order from anywhere in the world. Lead times depend on the quarry, volume and route and are determined for each order.

Where can I see the stone in person?

At the warehouse in the Moscow region, which holds stone from India, Turkey, China and Iran. You can also request samples for your project.

What quantity is worth shipping to order?

The economics of supply to order are tied to the loading of a transport unit, a container or a lorry. Small quantities are more sensibly covered by stock items or shipped as part of a consolidated cargo if the stone is not available in Russia. For large facades, by contrast, an order for the project is more economical: the stone is cut straight to the required sizes from one batch.

Can more imported stone be bought from the same batch?

Only if the supplier has reserved blocks in advance, which is not always possible. In the usual situation a new delivery will come from a different block and will differ in shade. Spare stock for trimming, breakage and repairs is therefore included in the first order, and what remains after installation is kept for the operating period.

Is it necessary to visit the quarry to select stone?

For ordinary items with even colour, photographs of slabs and control samples are sufficient. For large projects, stones with an active pattern and book-matched layouts, an inspection at the factory is justified: the real spread of tone is visible on the spot and unsuitable slabs can be rejected before sawing.
