Facade Architectural Concept: Where a Project Begins

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

A facade architectural concept is the first stage of a project, where the image of the building is defined: composition, articulation, proportions, materials, colour and key details. The concept is presented as sketches, elevations and 3D visualisations; the client makes a decision based on it, and designers then develop the working documentation.

The main criterion of a good concept is that it can be built. A beautiful image that cannot be manufactured in the required quantities, installed on the given wall or approved in the given city will have to be reworked, and the client will lose time. That is why a concept should take materials, production technology and budget into account from the very start.

A concept is needed by developers of residential complexes and business centres, building owners refurbishing a facade, and architects who need the cladding worked out. At TECHNOFORMA concepts are prepared by the design department: 12 designers led by two architects, and 3D visualisers. More on selection and design is in the section Facade design and selection.

What it includes

The content of a concept depends on the project, but it usually includes:

  • Analysis of input data: site, surroundings, planning constraints, function, client wishes.
  • Composition: base, body and top of the building, vertical and horizontal articulation, rhythm of openings, emphasis at entrances and corners.
  • Materials and colour scheme: main cladding material, secondary materials, colours, textures; a concept-level finishes schedule.
  • Architectural details: cornices, pilasters, window surrounds, portals, reliefs, their scale and profile.
  • Visualisations: views from key viewpoints, daytime and evening lighting, and if needed a photomontage in context.
  • Facade elevations: with the main articulation and materials.
  • Preliminary assessment: feasibility of the solutions, approximate material quantities.

A concept is not a working design: it has no shop drawings, details or specifications. But it sets the framework within which the working design will be developed.

For multi-building residential complexes, the concept also includes principles of variation: how the buildings differ from each other within a common language (colour, accents, entrance ornament) and which elements remain standard. This directly affects production: the more repeated elements, the fewer unique moulds for GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) products and the lower the unit cost. A good concept finds the balance between variety and repetition before the working design begins.

Context analysis

A facade is perceived not on its own but in its surroundings. That is why a concept starts with an analysis of the site:

  • Surrounding buildings: height, materials and colours of neighbouring buildings, historical context. In a city centre a facade must fit in; in a new area it can set the tone.
  • Viewpoints: where the building is seen most often: from the main street, the embankment, the courtyard. These facades get more attention and budget.
  • Orientation: light and shadow affect how relief reads; on north-facing facades dark materials look gloomier.
  • Climate: precipitation, wind, frost and sun determine the acceptable materials and details. Horizontal projections must be protected from standing water.
  • Regulations: land use and development rules, appearance requirements, conservation zones.
  • Function: housing, office, hotel or educational building determines the character of the facade and the organisation of entrances and ground floors.

The result of the analysis is diagrams, photographs and conclusions that define the constraints and opportunities for the concept.

GFRC is especially important for richly ornamented styles: it allows cornices, columns and ornaments of any complexity to be produced from models and moulds and repeated in the required quantity. At TECHNOFORMA models and moulds are made in the company's own mould shops, so a concept with three-dimensional ornament can be checked straight away for production feasibility.

Visualisations

Visualisations are the main tool of a concept. Through them the client sees the future building and chooses an option. What matters:

  • Several options: comparing materials, colours and degree of ornament on one model.
  • Real materials: textures of specific stones, porcelain stoneware ranges and GFRC finishes rather than generic library ones.
  • Correct scale: storey heights, opening sizes and cornice depths match the design.
  • Human viewpoints: from pavement level, not only a bird's-eye view.
  • Context: a photomontage in the surroundings for approvals and evaluation.
  • Evening lighting: if architectural lighting is planned.

TECHNOFORMA has 3D visualisers on staff, so models are built on the same data that will later go into the working design.

Approval

Approval of a concept goes through several rounds:

  1. Within the client's organisation: the investor, sales team and facility management assess the image, budget and maintenance.
  2. With the lead designer: the facade concept is coordinated with the floor plans, structure and windows.
  3. With the city: for projects whose appearance must be approved (in Moscow this is the architectural and urban planning appearance approval, AGO), the concept becomes the basis of the document package.
  4. Recording: approved visualisations, elevations, the materials schedule and samples become the benchmark for the working design and acceptance.

Changing the concept after city approval often requires repeating the procedure, so the choice of materials and colours should be settled at this stage and backed by physical samples.

FAQ

What does a residential complex facade concept include?

Context analysis, composition, materials and colour scheme, architectural details, elevations and visualisations from key viewpoints. For multi-building complexes, also the principles of facade variation between buildings.

How does a concept differ from a working design?

The concept defines the image and materials; the working design defines how everything is manufactured and installed: substructure shop drawings, details, layout, specifications.

Is a concept needed when refurbishing a facade?

Yes, especially if the cladding or appearance changes. It starts with a survey of the existing building and an analysis of the surroundings.

Can the concept be changed after approval?

It can, but changes to an appearance approved by city authorities usually require re-approval. So it is better to approve materials on samples in advance.
