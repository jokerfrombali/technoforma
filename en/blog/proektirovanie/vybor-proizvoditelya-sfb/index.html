How to Choose a GFRC Manufacturer

A glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) manufacturer is a plant that makes its own models and moulds, prepares the mix of glass fibre, sand, cement and water, casts, cures and inspects the products. It should be chosen on three criteria: in-house full-cycle production, product properties confirmed by tests, and completed projects of comparable scale.

GFRC is a material for facade decoration, cladding panels and small architectural forms: cornices, columns, window surrounds, balustrades. Product quality is determined almost entirely by the technology of the specific plant, so the same material behaves differently at different manufacturers.

This article is for architects, developers and technical supervisors choosing a GFRC supplier for a project. Other cladding selection questions are covered in the section Facade Design and Selection.

In-House Production

In-house production is more than a workshop where the mix is poured. The full cycle of making GFRC decoration includes:

  1. Element design: dividing the facade into products, developing fixing details and embedded parts.
  2. Models and moulds: a master model, and from it a mould (usually silicone, polyurethane, fibreglass or combined). If moulds are ordered externally, lead times and accuracy depend on a third company.
  3. Mixing and casting to a proven mix design — by spraying or vibration casting.
  4. Curing and demoulding with control of humidity conditions.
  5. Finishing and inspection: texture, colour, geometry, embedded parts.
  6. Packaging and marking according to the installation layout.

Ask the manufacturer which of these stages are carried out on its site. Laboratory and Testing

The properties of GFRC depend on the mix design, the proportion and type of fibre, the casting method and curing. So the manufacturer should carry out regular tests — in its own laboratory or an accredited third-party one. What to ask for:

PropertyWhy it mattersWhere it is confirmed
Flexural strengthPerformance of a thin-walled element under wind and its own weightSample test report
Compressive strengthOverall strength class of the matrix concreteTest report
Frost resistanceDurability in the project's climateTest report, product passport
Water absorptionResistance to wetting and soilingTest report
CombustibilityFire requirements for the facadeFire safety documents
Alkali resistance of the fibreRetention of strength over timeGlass fibre documentation

Specific values depend on the mix design; exact figures should be in the product passport and test report. If the manufacturer cannot provide test reports for your mix design, the properties in a sales brochure mean little. According to TECHNOFORMA, its GFRC is in the non-combustible class (NG), and the material's frost resistance is high and further improved with admixtures.

Completed Projects

Completed projects show how a manufacturer copes with volume, shape complexity and installation. Judge them not by number but by relevance to your task:

  • Scale: is the GFRC area on the projects comparable to yours? A 5,000 m² facade and a 50,000 m² facade require different production organisation.
  • Complexity: does the portfolio include large profiled elements, columns, curved cornices — not just flat panels?
  • The manufacturer's role: supply only, or design and installation as well.
  • Age of projects: a facade that has survived several winters says more about durability than a new one.

TECHNOFORMA has 15+ large completed projects, including residential complexes built in 2016–2017 — they show how the products behave after many seasons.

When inspecting a building, look for signs of production quality: uniform colour between neighbouring elements, no hairline cracks or chipped corners, crisp cornice profiles, width and evenness of joints, condition of fixing points. Colour variation between batches indicates an unstable mix design, while cracks on thin flanges point to insufficient reinforcement or a breach of the curing regime.

Lead Times

The lead time for GFRC decoration is made up of several stages, and the client needs to understand what it depends on:

  • Documentation for elements and fixings — depends on facade complexity and completeness of input data.
  • Making models and moulds — the longest stage for unique decoration; shorter for standard elements.
  • Series production — determined by plant capacity and the number of moulds working in parallel.
  • Delivery — distance and availability of warehouses in the region.

A manufacturer can give specific lead times only after studying the design. It is more reliable when the delivery schedule is tied to installation zones: the first batches arrive for the start of installation, the rest as it progresses. TECHNOFORMA has warehouses from Novorossiysk and Makhachkala to Novosibirsk and Yekaterinburg, which simplifies logistics for projects in different regions.

Factory Audit

A factory audit before signing a contract is normal practice for large projects. What to look at on site:

  • mould-making workshop: master models, moulds, their condition and marking;
  • mixing area: batching of components, storage of fibre and cement;
  • curing area: how humidity conditions are maintained;
  • finished goods warehouse: storage, packaging, marking according to the layout;
  • technical control department: logs, control samples, test reports;
  • products currently in production for other projects.

Ask for a trial element or fragment in the required texture and colour. The approved sample becomes the reference against which the series is compared on delivery. TECHNOFORMA's production base is located in the Moscow Region.

Spraying or casting: which technology the GFRC manufacturer uses

GFRC units are made in two main ways: by pneumatic spraying of the mix with chopped fibre, and by casting a premix into which the fibre has been introduced beforehand. Each method has its own field of application, and a strong factory commands both, choosing the process to suit the unit.

ParameterSprayingPremix casting
Fibre contentHigher, usually about 4–5% by weightLower, usually about 2–3.5%
Fibre orientationPredominantly in the plane of the shellRandom throughout the volume
Flexural strengthHigher for the same thicknessLower, compensated by thickness or ribs
Typical unitsLarge panels, deeply projecting cornices, column shellsSmall ornament, balusters, units with fine relief
Demands on personnelHigh: quality depends on the operatorModerate, the process is easier to standardise

Ask which method will be used for each group of your units, and why. If large thin-walled panels are proposed as premix castings with no explanation of how strength is ensured, that is a reason to request the calculation and test reports. The methods are compared in detail in the article on GFRC production technology.

There is also a combined approach: a face coat with no fibre or a low fibre content is applied first to obtain a clean texture, and the reinforced layer is then sprayed on. Clarify whether it is used and how the thickness of each layer is controlled: both the appearance and the load-bearing capacity of the shell depend on it.

Raw materials: which questions to ask the factory about the mix?

The durability of GFRC is determined above all by the fibre: it must be alkali-resistant, otherwise the cement environment will destroy the reinforcement over time. This is the first question put to a manufacturer, and the answer should be backed by the fibre supplier's documents.

  • Glass fibre. Alkali-resistant, containing zirconium dioxide; ordinary E-glass fibre is unsuitable for a cement matrix.
  • Cement. Grade and producer, consistency of supply; white cement is usually used for light and coloured units.
  • Sand. Graded silica sand, washed and dry; moisture and impurities affect strength and colour.
  • Pigments. Inorganic, resistant to ultraviolet and alkalis; what matters is how tone is kept consistent from batch to batch.
  • Admixtures. Plasticisers, polymer dispersions or other modifiers, according to the factory's mix design.

Ask separately whether a batching log is kept and whether the date of manufacture and the raw material batch can be traced from the marking on a unit. This traceability is needed if a deviation in colour or strength is found on site. The role of each component is explained in the piece on the composition of GFRC.

Tolerances, marking and packaging: what to fix in the specification

The specification attached to the supply contract should describe not only the list of units but also the requirements for their geometry, appearance and condition on receipt. Without this, acceptance on site turns into an argument about what counts as a defect.

Section of the specificationWhat to state
GeometryPermitted deviations in length, width, thickness, straightness of edges and diagonals
Face surfaceReference sample, acceptable pores and tonal variation, procedure for repairing minor defects
Cast-in fixingsType, material, corrosion protection, positional accuracy
MarkingUnit number from the installation layout, date of manufacture, inspection stamp
PackagingPallets or stillages, spacers, protection of corners and the face, slinging diagram
DispatchCompleteness of the consignment by work section, accompanying documents

The actual tolerance values depend on the size and type of unit; they are agreed on the basis of the working documentation. For long profiles, such as GFRC cornices, straightness and the matching of the profile at the joints between adjacent units are specified separately.

It is also useful to agree in advance on the procedure in the event of non-conformity: the time within which a defect is recorded, who attends to inspect it, what is repaired in place and what is replaced. Minor chipping of edges in transit occurs with any supplier, and what matters is not that it can never happen but that there is a clear procedure for putting it right.

What should the supply include besides the units themselves?

A GFRC manufacturer supplies more than shells: the product includes cast-in fixings, fastening components, documentation and technical support for installation. The scope of supply should be checked against a list, otherwise the missing items will have to be bought in during the works.

  • working drawings of the units and marked installation layouts;
  • cast-in fixings and, for stud-frame panels, a steel frame with a protective coating;
  • fasteners and connection details for the subframe or the load-bearing structure;
  • batch certificates and test reports;
  • a repair compound matched to the colour of the units and guidance on making good chips;
  • instructions for unloading, storage and installation;
  • installation supervision, or installation by the manufacturer.

If the cladding is hung on a subframe, it matters who calculates it: the weight and fixing arrangement of the panels determine the bracket spacing and the section of the rails. How such a system is built up is described in the article on the ventilated facade with GFRC cladding.

Corrosion protection of the metal is a subject in its own right. Cast-in fixings and frames work inside the facade for decades and cannot be inspected after installation, so the contract states the material: galvanised or stainless steel, and the type of coating. A saving on these parts is not visible from outside, but it determines the service life of the fixing.

A manufacturer in another region: how logistics affects the choice

Distance from the factory is not a limitation in itself: GFRC units are thin-walled and relatively light, and they are carried by road over long distances. What matters more is how packaging, the make-up of loads and storage on site are organised.

When assessing a distant supplier, clarify whether the units are dispatched in sets by work section, who is responsible for the goods in transit and how damage is recorded on receipt. Large panels and long profiles require stillages and rigid restraint, so the cost of delivery depends not only on weight but also on dimensions. TECHNOFORMA has its production in the Moscow Region and warehouses from Novorossiysk and Makhachkala to Yekaterinburg and Novosibirsk, which makes it possible to run projects in different regions.

Proximity to the factory is useful in another respect: it is easier to attend the acceptance of samples and the first batch, and quicker to reorder a damaged or modified unit. If the manufacturer is far away, these matters are settled in advance through the approval of reference samples and a time allowance for reorders.

Which mistakes are made when choosing a GFRC supplier

Mistakes in selection usually stem from judging the material by a sample and the price per square metre without understanding the technology. The most frequent are:

  • Comparing prices without a common schedule: one supplier prices with the frame and cast-in fixings, another the shell alone.
  • Ordering from a factory that does not make its own moulds: every change to the ornament drags on for weeks.
  • Approving colour from a photograph or a small sample instead of a full-size reference.
  • Confusion over terms: concrete with polymer or steel fibre, whose properties are different, is offered under the name GFRC. The differences are examined in the article on GFRC and fibre concrete.
  • No check of capacity: a factory with a small stock of moulds cannot keep pace with installation on a large project.
  • A contract with no requirements for packaging and the acceptance procedure.

Most of these mistakes can be avoided if a schedule of units is prepared and the sample requirements are formulated before prices are requested. The range and capabilities of a factory are conveniently assessed from the section on GFRC products.

How to organise work with the manufacturer after selection

Once the contract is signed, the quality of the result depends on how steadily the parties exchange information. The work is usually organised in the following order:

  1. handing over the design and agreeing the division of the facade into units;
  2. approving the working drawings and reference samples;
  3. making the moulds and the first batch, and accepting it at the factory;
  4. series production and dispatch to a programme tied to installation;
  5. checking deliveries on site and placing reorders.

On the client's side, one responsible person is needed to approve drawings and samples within the agreed time: a delay in approval pushes back the start of mould-making and the whole programme.

On a large project, regular short meetings involving the designer, production and installation are useful. They are used to compare the production programme with actual progress on the work sections and to identify in advance the items that could hold up the works.

FAQ

How do I choose a GFRC manufacturer?

Check in-house full-cycle production including moulds, test reports for the mix design, completed projects of comparable scale and plant capacity.

What documents should a GFRC manufacturer provide?

A product passport, test reports (strength, frost resistance, water absorption), fire safety documents and glass fibre documentation.

What is GFRC made of?

Glass fibre, sand, cement and water; according to TECHNOFORMA, high-grade cement is used along with admixtures to improve frost resistance.

How long does it take to make GFRC decoration?

It depends on complexity and the number of unique moulds. The manufacturer gives an exact lead time after studying the design.

Can I visit the plant before signing a contract?

A site audit is normal practice for large projects; arrange the visit with the manufacturer in advance.

How does sprayed GFRC differ from cast GFRC?

With spraying there is more fibre and it lies in the plane of the shell, so thin, large units are stronger in bending. Premix casting is more convenient for small ornament with complex relief. The method is chosen to suit the specific unit.

How can one be sure that alkali-resistant fibre is used in the units?

Ask the manufacturer for the glass fibre supplier's documents stating the fibre type and check them against the mix design declared in the product certificate. During a factory visit the fibre marking can be seen in the raw material store.

Can units be ordered from the manufacturer without installation?

Yes, this is a normal supply arrangement. In that case the contract should include working drawings, installation layouts and installation supervision, so that the main contractor's team works under the manufacturer's technical direction.
