Acceptance of Facade Works: What to Check

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

Contents

  3. Geometry

Acceptance of facade works is a check that the facade has been built to the design, within permissible deviations and with a complete set of as-built documentation. It is carried out in stages: concealed works are accepted as they are completed, cladding by work zones, and the whole facade by a final certificate.

The main rule: do not look only at the appearance. Most ventilated facade defects that will show up in a few years lie in the substructure, anchoring, insulation and junctions, and by the time of final acceptance they are already covered by the cladding.

This article is for technical supervisors, developers, general contractors and private clients. Related topics are in the section Facade Design and Selection.

Stages of Acceptance

Acceptance of a ventilated facade is divided into stages that follow the installation sequence:

StageWhat is acceptedDocument
Base preparationSetting out, anchor pull-out testsAnchor test report
BracketsAnchoring, spacing, projection, isolating padsCertificate of concealed works
Insulation and wind barrierThickness, tight fit, dowel fixing, membrane overlapsCertificate of concealed works
RailsVerticality, flatness, thermal gaps at jointsCertificate of concealed works, as-built drawing
Fire barriersPresence and execution per the designCertificate of concealed works
Cladding by work zonesGeometry, joints, fixing, no damageInterim acceptance certificate
Whole facadeCompliance with the design, junctions, documentation setFinal certificate

Each subsequent stage starts only after the previous one has been accepted. If a crew has covered the insulation with cladding before the certificate of concealed works was signed, the client is entitled to demand that it be opened up.

Concealed Works

Concealed works are everything that will be inaccessible for inspection once the cladding is installed. On a ventilated facade these are:

  • Bracket anchoring: anchor type per design, embedment depth, distance to the edge of the floor slab or masonry, number of anchors per bracket.
  • Brackets: vertical and horizontal spacing per the layout, thermal break pads.
  • Insulation: brand and thickness per design, tight fit of boards without gaps, staggered joints in two-layer insulation, number of dowels per board.
  • Wind barrier membrane: overlaps, fixing, no tears.
  • Rails: connection to brackets, fixed and sliding points, gaps at rail joints.
  • Embedded parts for decoration: position, fixing to structures.
  • Fire barriers around openings and at each floor — if provided for in the design.

Each type of concealed work is documented with an inspection certificate and photographs. Photos referenced to grid lines and levels should be kept with the certificates — they will be needed in any dispute years later.

Geometry

Cladding geometry is checked with instruments: straightedge, spirit level, plumb line, level or total station. What is measured:

  • flatness — deviation of the cladding surface from the straightedge;
  • verticality — deviation from vertical per storey and over the full height;
  • horizontality of courses and decorative lines — cornices, bands, sill courses;
  • joint width and straightness, consistency of joints across the facade;
  • lipping between adjacent panels — the step between face surfaces;
  • position of decoration relative to design levels and axes, symmetry of surrounds.

Permissible deviations are stated in the design, the facade system manufacturer's documents and the applicable Russian codes of practice for finishing works. They should be fixed in the contract in advance to avoid arguments about criteria at acceptance. The product surface is inspected separately: chips, cracks, installation marks, colour variation between elements, soiling.

Documents

As-built documentation for a facade is the set of documents confirming that the work was carried out to the design. Without it, acceptance is incomplete. The set includes:

  1. working documentation marked up with changes made;
  2. certificates of inspection of concealed works for each stage;
  3. as-built survey drawings;
  4. anchor pull-out test reports;
  5. quality documents for materials and products: certificates or declarations, passports, test reports;
  6. suitability documents for the facade system;
  7. work logs and, if kept, the design supervision log;
  8. interim acceptance certificates for work zones.

Check for consistency: material brands in the quality documents must match those in the design and certificates, and quantities in the certificates must match the actual ones.

Defects and Comments

Comments at acceptance are made in writing — as a list of defects stating the location (grid line, level, elevation), a description, photos and a deadline for rectification. Typical facade comments:

  • chips, cracks, damage to products during installation;
  • uneven joints, lipping between panels;
  • colour variation between elements from different batches;
  • unfixed or loose elements;
  • leaky junctions with windows and parapets;
  • missing sills, trims, end caps;
  • soiling, traces of mortar and sealant.

After rectification, a repeat inspection is carried out and the comment is closed with a signature. It is not advisable to sign the final certificate before significant comments are closed: afterwards the contractor has less incentive to fix them. Minor cosmetic comments can be transferred to warranty obligations, but with the deadline recorded in the certificate.

Disagreements over comments are resolved according to the design and contract: if the criterion — permissible deviation, joint type, colour reference — is fixed in the documents, the dispute comes down to a measurement. If not, the parties have to negotiate, and the side with the better evidence wins: photos, log entries, interim acceptance certificates.

For colour variation in natural stone and glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) products, the basis for assessment is the approved reference. Elements should be compared in natural light and from a normal viewing distance, not up close: slight differences in shade inherent to natural stone are not a defect if they fall within the agreed reference range.

FAQ

How is a ventilated facade accepted?

In stages: anchor tests, brackets, insulation and wind barrier, rails, cladding by work zones and final acceptance with a set of documents.

What concealed works are there on a ventilated facade?

Anchoring and brackets, insulation, wind barrier membrane, rails, embedded parts for decoration and fire barriers.

What does facade as-built documentation include?

Working documentation with changes, certificates of concealed works, as-built drawings, anchor test reports, quality documents for materials and work logs.

How is cladding geometry checked?

With a straightedge, level, plumb line or survey instrument: flatness, verticality, horizontality of lines, joint width and lipping between panels.

Can a certificate be signed with outstanding comments?

It is better to close significant comments before signing; minor ones can be transferred to warranty with the deadline recorded in the certificate.
