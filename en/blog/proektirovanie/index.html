Facade design and selection

What is a facade? A facade is the exterior face of a building, and in construction terms the whole outer envelope: cladding, insulation, substructure, windows and ornament. A dimensioned facade drawing shows divisions, levels, materials and details — appearance, cost and schedule all depend on it.

This section has articles on how to choose cladding and compare options, what stages and documents make up a facade design, what shop drawings (KMD) and a facade passport are, how approval of a building's architectural appearance (AGO) works in Moscow, how to write a tender specification, check an estimate and choose a contractor. Separately — facade composition, choosing materials for climate and seismic conditions, facades in the Caucasus, BIM modelling, designer's supervision and acceptance of works.

Readers also come with private questions: how to finish the facade of a bathhouse, a garage or an aerated concrete house, and whether the facade or the windows should come first. We answer these too, with a caveat: the final decision depends on the wall construction and climate.

Facade design stages: what the client receives at each one

Facade design moves from the general to the particular: from the image of the building to the drawings from which each product is manufactured. Each stage produces its own result, and skipping any of them usually leads to rework on site. A detailed breakdown is given in the article on design stages and documents.

StageResultWhich questions it settles
ConceptSketches, visualisations, material optionsAppearance of the building, budget range
Design documentationElevations, sections, principal decisions on the systemApprovals and expert review
Working documentationDetails, schedules, cladding layoutQuantities for the cost estimate and procurement
Shop drawings (KMD)Drawings of each product and its fixingManufacture and installation without adjustment on site
As-built documentationDrawings of the actual position, certificatesHandover and subsequent operation

What to prepare in order to start facade design

To begin, it is enough to have data from which the geometry of the building, the loads and the expectations regarding appearance can be assessed. The fuller the set, the fewer assumptions in the first assessment and the more accurate the preliminary estimate.

  • architectural drawings: plans, elevations, sections; for an existing building — a measured survey;
  • the construction and material of the external walls, floor slab levels;
  • the construction region: wind and snow zone, seismicity of the site;
  • the height and function of the building — fire safety requirements depend on them;
  • references or a sketch of the desired appearance, material preferences;
  • planned dates and the phasing of construction.

If some of the information is missing, this does not prevent a start: the missing data are clarified in the course of the work, and the assumptions are recorded in the design brief.

Which design decisions affect the budget and the programme most?

The greater part of the cost of a facade is determined at the early stages — by the choice of system, cladding material and the degree to which the mouldings are bespoke. Once the working documentation has been issued, the scope for savings narrows considerably.

The budget is affected by the number of standard sizes, the share of bespoke elements, the complexity of the details and the height of the building. Large-format GFRC panels reduce the number of joints and installation operations, but require a carefully considered layout and lifting equipment. For a ventilated facade, the subframe and the bracket spacing are determined in advance — this sets both the metal consumption and the pace of work.

Common mistakes at the facade design stage

Most installation problems are the consequence of decisions not carried through to the end in the design. The mistakes that occur most often are listed below.

  • the facade is designed after the walls have been erected, when the embedded parts and levels can no longer be changed;
  • the cladding is chosen from a visualisation without checking the weight, the fire safety requirements and the availability of the material;
  • the working documentation contains no junction details for windows, roof and plinth;
  • the cost estimate is calculated by area, without a schedule of products and accessory elements;
  • the shop drawings are issued to the design dimensions without an as-built survey;
  • no control sample or facade mock-up is provided for agreeing the colour and texture.

It is useful to check the design against this list before a tender is announced: the contractors receive the same input data, and their offers become comparable.

FAQ

At what stage of construction should the facade design be commissioned?

Ideally in parallel with the design of the building, before the external walls are erected. The embedded parts, floor slab levels and openings can then be coordinated with the facade system in advance. For a building that already exists, design begins with a measured survey and an inspection of the substrate.

Is a facade design needed for a private house?

Yes, but in a reduced scope. As a rule, a sketch with a visualisation, a layout of the cladding and mouldings, fixing details and a schedule are sufficient. This is enough to calculate the quantities and avoid adjusting products on site.

What should be done if the actual dimensions of the building differ from the design ones?

Before the shop drawings are issued, an as-built survey of the walls and openings is carried out. Deviations are taken into account in the product drawings and compensated for by adjusting the subframe and the joint width; putting products into production to the design dimensions without a check is risky.
