Design Supervision of Facade Works

Design supervision on a facade is control by the author of the design to ensure that the built facade matches the design decisions: materials, divisions, decoration profiles, colour, fixing details. It is carried out by the design author or an organisation authorised by them; comments are recorded in the design supervision log and must be addressed.

The main purpose of supervision is to prevent deviations from the design that change the appearance or reduce the facade's reliability, and to resolve issues that arise on site quickly. This is especially important for facades with decoration and natural stone, where 'substitution with an equivalent' is immediately noticeable.

This article is for developers, technical clients, architects and technical supervisors. Related topics are in the section Facade Design and Selection.

What Design Supervision Is

Design supervision is a service provided by the designer during construction. Its procedure is governed by the Russian standard SP 246.1325800 'Regulations on Design Supervision of the Construction of Buildings and Structures'. Supervision is carried out under a contract with the client; for some projects its necessity is set by the design brief or project requirements.

It is important to distinguish three types of control on a facade:

Type of controlCarried out byWhat it checks
Design supervisionThe design authorCompliance with design decisions — appearance, materials, details
Technical supervision (client's construction control)The client or an organisation it hiresQuality of work, quantities, compliance with standards and the design
Production controlThe contractorIncoming, operational and acceptance control of its own work

Design supervision does not replace technical supervision but complements it: technical supervision is responsible for quality and quantities, design supervision for faithfulness to the design intent.

Who Carries It Out

Design supervision is carried out by the organisation that developed the design or by the architect whose design the building follows. Responsible specialists are appointed: the chief architect or chief project engineer and specialists from each discipline. On a facade, these are the architect responsible for the appearance and the structural engineer responsible for the substructure and fixings.

If the facade contractor developed the working documentation for the substructure and cladding itself, its designers can also supervise their own solutions: they know the details and permissible deviations better than anyone. TECHNOFORMA Group offers design supervision as part of its natural stone services; the company employs 12 designers led by 2 architects.

The frequency of site visits is set by the contract and the construction schedule. The most important moments to be present are the start of substructure installation, the first cladding zone, installation of the first elements of each type of decoration, and junction details.

What Is Checked

On facade works, design supervision checks that what is built matches the design. The main points:

  • Materials: cladding type, stone type, texture and colour match the approved references; no unapproved substitutions.
  • Layout and divisions: position of joints, alignment of junctions with axes and divisions, placement of make-up pieces.
  • Decoration: profiles of cornices and surrounds, projections, heights of elements, junctions at corners.
  • Details: junctions with windows, parapets and plinth; movement joints; sills.
  • Substructure and fixings: compliance with the bracket layout, anchor type, rail spacing.
  • Colour: compliance with the colour passport, no colour variation between batches.

Design supervision also approves changes that have to be made as work proceeds: material substitutions, changes to a detail due to the actual position of structures, layout adjustments where walls deviate.

For natural stone, supervision has a separate task — controlling slab matching. Stone from different blocks and batches differs in pattern and shade, and if slabs are laid without matching, patches will appear on the facade. Supervision checks that slabs are installed according to the agreed layout and that transitions between batches fall at corners, bands or other natural boundaries.

For glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) decoration, checking the first products of each mark is important: if a profile or size does not match the design, the error is corrected in the mould before the whole series is produced.

Documents

The main design supervision document is the log, in which specialists record identified deviations from the design, instructions for rectifying them and deadlines. The contractor and client record in the log that instructions have been carried out.

In addition to the log, the following are used:

  • the design supervision contract and the order appointing responsible persons;
  • approved references for materials, texture and colour;
  • revisions to working documentation issued as a result of supervision;
  • certificates of inspection of concealed works, which a representative of the author may co-sign;
  • interim and final acceptance certificates.

Log entries are evidence in quality disputes: if a deviation was recorded and the contractor did not fix it, the document shows this.

Benefits

The benefit of design supervision for the client is measured by the errors that never reach the finished facade:

  • the appearance matches what was approved, and there is no need to re-approve changes with the architecture authority;
  • unapproved material substitutions are identified before purchase or immediately after the first batch;
  • problems that cannot be solved without the designer — mismatches with structures, wall deviations — are resolved in days rather than weeks;
  • details that affect durability are built as designed;
  • at acceptance there is a documentary basis for claims.

The cost of supervision is incomparably lower than the cost of reworking a facade: dismantling and reinstalling cladding, let alone remanufacturing decoration, is far more expensive.

For the architect, supervision is a chance to see how the solutions work in reality and, if necessary, adjust details before they are repeated across the whole building.

How to organise design supervision of a facade: the sequence of steps

Design supervision of facade works is set up before installation begins, not after the first remarks appear: the contract, the team and the visit schedule should be in place by the time the contractor starts setting out the axes. The very first decisions on site are then taken with the author of the design involved.

The working procedure usually looks like this:

  1. The client signs a contract with the designer and hands over the construction programme.
  2. The design organisation appoints the responsible specialists by order and passes their contact details to the client and the contractor.
  3. The parties agree a visit plan tied to stages rather than calendar dates: a shift in the installation programme automatically shifts the visits.
  4. Before work starts, reference samples are approved: stone samples, GFRC textures and colours, and a control area of cladding.
  5. At each visit the specialist inspects the completed work, checks it against the working drawings and makes an entry in the log.
  6. The contractor rectifies the remarks, and this is noted in the log at the next visit.
  7. When the works are complete, the log is handed to the client and becomes part of the project records.

If the working drawings for the units and the subframe were issued by the facade contractor, it is worth defining in advance who is responsible for which set: the architect for the appearance, the author of the shop drawing package for unit marks and fixings.

At which stages of facade works is a visit by the designer needed?

A visit is needed at the points where a decision is replicated across the whole facade or will soon be covered by the next layers. Permanent presence is not normally required: control visits tied to stages are sufficient.

Setting out of axes and wall surveyActual deviations of the substrate, alignment of divisions with axes and openingsDecision to adjust the layout or the bracket projection
Installation of brackets and railsBracket layout, anchor type, compliance with the results of anchor pull-out testsEntry confirming compliance, instructions on strengthening
Insulation and wind barrierThickness and number of layers, fixing of slabs, air gapParticipation in the inspection of concealed works
First cladding sectionJoints, flatness, colour and texture compared with the reference sampleApproval of the control area as the benchmark
First units of each type of decorationProfile, projection, corner joints, levelsPermission to install the whole series
Junctions with windows, parapet, plinthSills and flashings, sealing, movement jointsRemarks on details before the scaffolding is struck

The last row of the table matters more than it seems: once the scaffolding is down, rectifying even a minor remark at height requires a hoist or a cradle.

Typical deviations from the design identified by design supervision

Most deviations arise not from carelessness but from an attempt to speed up the work or to work around a clash on site. Below are the situations that occur on projects more often than others.

  • Replacing the cladding with an "equivalent". A different manufacturer, a different slab thickness or a different batch of stone produces a difference in shade and pattern that is visible from the ground.
  • Shifted joints. Installers start the layout from a convenient corner rather than from the axis set by the design, and the cut slabs end up on the main elevation.
  • Trimming decoration on site. A cornice or a surround is shortened on the scaffolding to get around a wall deviation; the cut is left exposed and breaks the profile.
  • Changed bracket spacing. A fixing is moved because of reinforcement or voids in the substrate without the loads being recalculated.
  • Simplified junctions. A flashing is replaced with sealant, a movement joint is covered with continuous cladding.
  • Ignored marking. Stone slabs are installed without regard to the agreed selection and layout scheme, and the pattern on the facade falls apart.

What these cases have in common is that the decision was taken on site without the designer. The task of supervision is not to forbid a change but to bring it into a managed procedure: assess the consequences and document it.

How to agree a material substitution or a change to a detail?

Any change follows a single route: a request from the contractor, an assessment by the author of the design, a written decision and a revision of the documentation. A verbal agreement on the scaffolding is not enough — at handover the facade is compared with the drawings, not with what the participants remember.

For a material substitution the contractor submits a sample, quality documents and the reason for the change. The designer compares the sample with the reference in daylight and checks whether the properties used in the calculation are preserved: weight, thickness, fixing method, combustibility class. For stone, the frost resistance and water absorption of the rock are assessed as well. If the substitution affects an appearance approved by the architectural authority, a new approval may be required — this is for the client to decide.

A change to a detail is issued as a revision sheet to the working documentation. Where the load on a fixing or the projection of a unit changes, the structural engineer carries out a verification calculation. The log entry refers to the revision number, so the as-built documentation remains coherent.

Response times for requests are worth writing into the contract: a crew standing idle while waiting for an answer costs more than the supervision itself.

What to include in a design supervision contract

The contract determines whether supervision will be a working tool rather than a formality. Apart from the fee and the dates, it is useful to fix several conditions in it.

  • The specialists by discipline: architectural solutions, subframe structures, GFRC and stone units.
  • The list of stages at which a visit is mandatory and the procedure for calling an unscheduled visit.
  • The response time for contractor requests and the form of the response.
  • The procedure for issuing revisions to the documentation and the allocation of their cost, depending on who initiated the change.
  • Participation in the inspection of concealed works and in final acceptance.
  • The right of the specialist to require work to be suspended on an area where a deviation affects load-bearing capacity.

On projects in other regions some issues are resolved remotely, by photographic records and video calls, while visits are planned for the key stages. This format should also be described in the contract.

Installation supervision by the manufacturer and design supervision: the difference

Installation supervision is technical guidance of the works by the manufacturer of the units, whereas design supervision is a check of compliance with the design by its author. The supervising engineer trains the crew, demonstrates the sequence of operations and checks the slinging, placing and adjustment of units; he is on site considerably more often and answers the question "how to install".

Design supervision answers the question "has the right thing been installed". In practice the two functions complement each other: when large decorative units are installed — for example GFRC cornices or columns — the supervising engineer eliminates workmanship errors, and the architect confirms the profile and the levels. When the manufacturer itself carries out the facade design, both types of control sit within one organisation and remarks reach the workshop without intermediaries. The installed details are then checked according to the rules described in the article on junctions with windows and the roof.

How design supervision of facade works is concluded

Supervision ends not with the last visit but with the closing of every entry in the log and participation in final acceptance. Until then the specialist checks that each remark has either been rectified or withdrawn by a written, reasoned decision.

By the end of the works the client should be left with three groups of documents. The first is the log with notes on completion. The second is the working documentation with all revisions incorporated, that is, drawings which match the facade as built. The third is the approved reference samples: they will be needed if a damaged slab or decorative unit has to be replaced in service and matched for colour and texture.

On projects built in phases it is useful to hold a short review after the first block: which details raised the most questions, which units had to be reworked. These conclusions are carried into the documentation for the following blocks, and the number of remarks normally falls noticeably from phase to phase.

FAQ

How does design supervision differ from technical supervision on a facade?

Design supervision checks compliance with the design and its intent; the client's technical supervision checks quality, quantities and compliance with standards.

Who carries out design supervision of a facade?

The design author — architect and structural engineer; for the facade contractor's own solutions, its designers.

Which document governs design supervision?

The Russian standard SP 246.1325800 'Regulations on Design Supervision of the Construction of Buildings and Structures'; terms are set in the contract.

What is recorded in the design supervision log?

Identified deviations from the design, instructions for rectification, deadlines and confirmation of completion.

When is it important for design supervision to be on site?

At the start of substructure installation, the first cladding zone, installation of the first decorative elements and at junction details.

How often should the design supervision specialist visit the site?

The frequency is set by the contract and tied to work stages rather than the calendar. Visits are usually mandatory at the start of the subframe, at the first cladding section, when the first units of each type of decoration are installed and before the scaffolding is struck; between these, visits are arranged as required.

Can design supervision of a facade be carried out remotely?

Partly. Day-to-day questions and simple remarks are resolved through photographic records and video calls, but the control area of cladding, the colour, the texture and the first decorative units are assessed on site: a photograph distorts shade and flatness. The remote format should be described in the contract.

Who pays for design changes that arise during supervision?

It depends on the cause. If the change results from an error in the documentation, the designer normally issues it at its own expense; if the cause is a deviation of the structures, a decision by the client or a contractor's request to substitute a material, the cost is borne by the initiator. It is best to fix the procedure in the contract in advance.
