How to Choose Facade Cladding: Criteria and Comparison

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

How to choose facade cladding: start not with the material but with the conditions: building type and height, load-bearing capacity of the walls, climate, fire safety requirements, planning regulations, budget and service life. Only then are materials compared: natural stone, GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete), porcelain stoneware, fibre cement and composite panels, clinker, render, metal cassettes.

The main criterion for multi-storey buildings is the system as a whole: cladding, substructure, insulation and fixings must be compatible and permitted for a building of that height and fire hazard class. A beautiful panel on an unsuitable substructure is a risk, not a saving.

This article is for architects, developers, general contractors and private homeowners. TECHNOFORMA designs, manufactures and installs facades in GFRC, natural stone and porcelain stoneware, so the comparison below is the view of practitioners rather than sellers of a single material. More on choosing a facade is in the section Facade design and selection.

Selection criteria

Cladding selection criteria are best divided into mandatory ones (without which a solution is unacceptable) and desirable ones (which affect quality and cost).

Mandatory:

  • Fire safety. For multi-storey and public buildings, requirements for the combustibility of the cladding and the system as a whole are set by the current Russian fire safety codes (SP). Non-combustible materials (natural stone, porcelain stoneware, GFRC, which at TECHNOFORMA is non-combustible class NG according to the company) give more freedom.
  • Loads. The weight of the cladding and the wind load must be carried by the wall and substructure; for lightweight concrete and hollow brick this is often the limiting factor.
  • Climate. Frost resistance, water absorption, resistance to UV and temperature changes.
  • Regulations. In some cities the appearance of a facade must be approved, and materials and colours are fixed in the facade passport or in the decision on the architectural appearance.

Desirable:

  • service life and repairability;
  • freedom of form: whether three-dimensional ornament or curved elements are needed;
  • speed of installation and ability to work in winter;
  • maintenance in operation: washing, impregnation, element replacement;
  • cost per finished square metre including substructure and installation.

Materials

The main facade cladding materials and what they offer:

  • Natural stone: granite, marble, limestone, travertine, sandstone, dolomite, shell limestone. An inimitable texture and the durability of dense stones; heavy, needs a strong substructure; the cost depends heavily on the stone.
  • GFRC: thin-walled products of a cement matrix with glass fibre; at TECHNOFORMA it has 4 components: glass fibre, sand, high-grade cement and water. It makes it possible to produce cornices, columns, window surrounds, relief and large panels of any shape; lighter than stone; imitates stone and concrete.
  • Porcelain stoneware: uniform, consistent, light; the main material of mass-market rainscreen facades. Limited to flat shapes.
  • Fibre cement panels: light, coloured or textured; installed on a substructure.
  • Composite (aluminium) panels: light, bent shapes, contemporary look; the fire performance of the specific product requires particular attention.
  • Clinker tiles and brick: traditional look, low water absorption.
  • Render systems (ETICS): economical for low- and mid-rise housing; require careful workmanship and maintenance.
  • Metal cassettes and profiled metal: for industrial and retail buildings.

Ventilated facades clad with composite panels, porcelain stoneware, stone or GFRC are built in the same way: brackets on the wall, insulation, a wind barrier if needed, rails and cladding with an air gap. What differs is the loads, element spacing and fixings.

Budget

A facade budget is made up of material, substructure, insulation, installation, scaffolding or cradles and design. So materials should be compared on the cost per finished square metre on a specific building, taking operation into account.

  • A cheap material on an expensive substructure can cost more than it seems. Conversely, light cladding saves on brackets and anchors.
  • Complex ornament (cornices, pilasters, window surrounds) is expensive in natural stone, whereas GFRC is cast in moulds, and the unit cost falls on repeated elements.
  • Combining materials is a common way of managing the budget: stone or GFRC on the plinth, entrances and ornament, porcelain stoneware on standard areas.
  • Operation: render is periodically repaired and repainted, porous stone is impregnated, while porcelain stoneware and GFRC mainly need washing.

Mistakes

Typical mistakes when choosing facade cladding:

  1. Choosing from a picture. The material is approved from a catalogue photo without samples in daylight and without a visualisation on the building.
  2. Separately from the substructure. The cladding is chosen without checking whether there is a permitted substructure for it for the height and type of building.
  3. Ignoring the substrate. Heavy cladding is fitted to a wall that cannot hold anchors with the required load.
  4. Combustible materials at height. Panels without confirmed fire performance on multi-storey buildings.
  5. Skimping on details. Reveals, sills, parapets and junctions are improvised on site, leading to leaks and efflorescence.
  6. Different batches. Cladding is bought in parts, and the shade differs between adjacent sections.
  7. No shop drawings. Installation without working drawings of the substructure and cladding layout.

FAQ

Which facade is best for a multi-storey building?

A ventilated rainscreen facade with non-combustible cladding (porcelain stoneware, GFRC or natural stone) on a substructure permitted for the building height. The choice between them is determined by appearance, weight and budget.

Which cladding materials should I choose for a private house?

Natural and artificial stone, clinker, render, fibre cement, porcelain stoneware and GFRC all suit a private house. Consider the walls, climate, desired appearance and maintenance budget.

Which is cheaper, porcelain stoneware or GFRC?

On flat areas, usually porcelain stoneware. On complex ornament (cornices, columns, window surrounds) GFRC is often more cost-effective than the alternatives, especially natural stone.

Can several materials be combined on one facade?

Yes, this is a common solution. For example, on Dinastiya and RUBLEVO BUSINESS PARK TECHNOFORMA combined GFRC with porcelain stoneware, and on Dinastiya with granite as well.

Where should cladding selection start?

With the input data: building height and type, wall material, climate, regulatory requirements, budget. Then the concept, visualisation of options and design.
