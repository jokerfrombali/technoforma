Facade Tender: How to Write a Specification and Compare Bids

A facade works tender gives a fair comparison only when all bidders price the same scope. To achieve this, the client prepares a technical specification with the design, an element schedule and material requirements, and compiles the bids into a single table line by line — rather than comparing the totals.

The main criterion for choosing the winner is not the lowest price but the completeness of the bid, realistic deadlines and the contractor's proven ability to manufacture and install the cladding. Price differences between bidders are almost always explained by different scopes of work.

This article is for developers, technical clients and general contractors running a tender for a ventilated facade or cladding in glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC), natural stone or porcelain stoneware. Related topics are in the section Facade Design and Selection.

Contents of the Specification

A facade specification should answer the questions that each bidder would otherwise answer in its own way. The minimum contents:

  1. General project data: address, use, number of storeys, top level, wind and snow region, site seismicity.
  2. Design documentation: architectural drawings (elevations, sections, details), structural data on the load-bearing walls, the facade passport or approved appearance, if any.
  3. Scope of work: design of the substructure and shop drawings, supply, installation of substructure, insulation, wind barrier, cladding, junctions, sills, reveals.
  4. Material requirements: cladding type by zone, texture, colour, combustibility class, requirements for the substructure and fixings.
  5. Site conditions: schedule, work zones, availability of scaffolding or lifts, storage area, power supply.
  6. Bidder requirements: experience, own production, installation crews, documents.
  7. Bid form: a single table of items in which each bidder must enter prices.

The more detailed the 'scope of work' section, the fewer 'hidden items' will appear after the contract is signed.

Element Schedule

An element schedule is a list of all cladding products with dimensions and quantities. For flat panels it comes down to area by type, while for GFRC or stone decoration it consists of individual items, because the cost of each profiled element is calculated separately.

ItemWhat to specifyExample unit
Flat panelsSizes, thickness, texture, colourm², pcs
Cornices, bands, string coursesProfile (cross-section), length, quantitylinear m
Columns, pilastersHeight, diameter or section, capital and basepcs
Window surroundsOpening size, surround profile, keystoneset

Without a schedule, bidders will price from the picture, and discrepancies in the number of elements will produce price differences unrelated to quality. The schedule can be prepared by a design organisation or the facade contractor at the preliminary design stage.

Comparing Bids

Bids are compiled into a comparison table by specification item. The procedure:

  • check that every bidder has filled in every line; an empty line means the item is not included;
  • compare quantities: if a bidder's area or number of elements differs from the schedule, find out why;
  • compare materials: substructure brand, insulation thickness, fixing type, cladding manufacturer;
  • compare deadlines by stage and how they fit your schedule;
  • record payment terms: advance share, stage payments, retentions.

Separately, assess who does the work: if a bidder manufactures the cladding itself and installs it with its own crews, the risk of a break in the chain is lower than with an intermediary who buys products and hires installers as it goes.

It is useful to reduce all bids to unit rates: cost per square metre of cladding by type, per linear metre of cornice, per window surround set, per square metre of substructure with insulation. This shows which items drive the difference. A sharply lower rate on one item while the rest are at market level usually means an error in quantity or a different material; a sharply higher one means the bidder has priced in the risk of incomplete input data.

If bidders ask many clarifying questions, send the answers to everyone so that bids remain comparable.

Hidden Items

Hidden items are works and materials that were left out of a bid but will inevitably be needed. They are what turns the cheapest bid into the most expensive. Most often omitted:

  • substructure design, calculations and shop drawings;
  • pull-out tests of anchors in the base wall;
  • junctions with windows, sills, reveals, parapets;
  • trim and corner elements, fixings, embedded parts for decoration;
  • delivery, unloading, storage;
  • scaffolding, lifts, aerial platforms, cranes;
  • protection of products on site, cleaning;
  • as-built documentation.

The safeguard is a clause in the specification: 'the bidder must include in its bid all works and materials required to complete the facade according to the design; costs not stated in the bid will not be paid'. And a mandatory question to each bidder: what is not included in its price.

Choosing the Winner

The winner is chosen on several criteria, not price alone. A reasonable evaluation scheme:

  1. compliance of the bid with the specification — bidders with incomplete bids either complete them or drop out;
  2. full cost including hidden items;
  3. realism of the schedule and production capacity;
  4. experience on comparable projects, confirmed by addresses;
  5. own production and installation team;
  6. payment and contract terms.

Before the final decision, it is useful to negotiate with the two or three best bidders and visit their production. The price of facade works can only be finally fixed after inspecting the site: this is exactly how TECHNOFORMA determines the cost of construction and installation works.

Once the winner is chosen, its bid and answers to questions become an annex to the contract. This fixes the scope of work and prevents disputed items from later being treated as extras.

How a facade tender runs: stages and indicative timing

A tender for facade works runs through six stages: preparing the documents, inviting bidders, the site visit and queries, receipt of bids, levelling of the calculations, and negotiations followed by the award. For a project of medium complexity the whole cycle usually takes four to eight weeks. Compressing the programme almost always results in incomplete bids.

  1. Preparing the package. The client assembles the design, the schedule of elements and the bid form, and appoints a person responsible for answering queries.
  2. Inviting bidders. Four to six companies are normally enough: with fewer there is no real comparison, with more the volume of analysis grows while the depth of each bid falls.
  3. Site visit and queries. Bidders are given the chance to see the building, the condition of the substrate and the access routes. One to two weeks are usually allowed for queries.
  4. Submission of bids. All bidders submit on the same day and in the same form, together with a programme and a list of assumptions.
  5. Levelling. The client clarifies discrepancies and asks bidders to revise their figures to a common scope.
  6. Negotiations and decision. The programme, payment terms and technical alternatives are discussed with the shortlisted bidders.

Pricing a facade with GFRC or stone ornament usually takes a bidder at least two to three weeks: the facade has to be broken down into units, the number of moulds estimated and the load on the subframe assessed. If the submission period is shorter, bidders add a contingency for the unknown and prices rise across the board. What makes up the programme of the works themselves is covered in the article on planning facade installation time.

At what design stage should a facade works tender be launched?

A tender produces comparable prices once there are at least architectural drawings with facade elevations and outline details. The earlier the stage, the wider the spread of bids and the more qualifications they contain.

StageWhat bidders can priceAccuracy and risks
Sketch, conceptA budget cost based on area and comparable projectsA guide for the budget; too early to fix the price
Design documentationAreas by cladding type, the main ornamental elements, the subframe from standard detailsA price with tolerances; quantities are refined after the working drawings
Working drawings and shop drawingsEvery item in the schedule and specificationA firm price that changes only if the design changes

A common compromise is a two-stage tender. At the first stage two or three bidders and a budget benchmark are selected on the basis of the concept; at the second, the shortlisted bidders price the facade from the developed documentation. Sometimes design is placed under a separate contract: the contractor produces the facade shop drawings, and the cost of manufacture and installation is then fixed against them.

For facades with complex ornament, involving the manufacturer early is also useful for a technical reason. The division into units, the panel sizes and the fixing method all affect the joint pattern. If these decisions are taken after the tender, part of the drawing set will have to be reworked.

One lot or several: how to divide the facade works

The facade can be awarded to a single turnkey contractor or split into lots: design, supply of cladding, subframe with insulation, and installation. Splitting sometimes lowers the price of individual items, but it transfers coordination and responsibility for the interfaces between contractors to the client.

ArrangementAdvantagesWhat to bear in mind
Single lot: design, manufacture, installationOne point of responsibility for the result, one programme, changes resolved within one companyThe pool of bidders is smaller: companies with their own production and installation teams are needed
Supply and installation separatelyA strong manufacturer and a local installer can be chosenInstallation supervision and a clear boundary of responsibility for damage and geometry are required
Separately by material: stone, GFRC, porcelain stonewareSpecialist contractorsInterfaces between systems, shared scaffolding and the sequence of work sections remain with the client

With separate lots, the brief sets out the boundaries in advance: who carries out the survey, who accepts the substrate, who is responsible for the units after unloading, and whose scaffolding the other trades use. For the installation of ventilated facades the boundary between subframe and cladding is especially important: the load-bearing capacity of the brackets is calculated for a specific panel weight and fixing arrangement.

Samples and a control mock-up: why include them in the tender brief

Material samples and a control mock-up of the facade turn appearance requirements from words into a benchmark against which deliveries are later compared. Without them, a dispute about colour, texture or joint width is settled subjectively.

At tender stage it is usual to request cladding samples in the required texture and colour, a section of the ornamental profile and a sample of the fixing detail. For GFRC facade panels it is worth asking for a sample with an exposed edge, so that the shell thickness and cast-in fixings can be assessed. After the award and before series production begins, reference samples are agreed, and a control area is installed on site: several panels with a corner, a window abutment and joints.

  • the brief states the size and number of samples, the deadline for providing them and the party that pays for their manufacture;
  • the reference sample is signed by both parties and kept until the end of handover;
  • colour and texture tolerances are described in advance: natural stone and concrete do not give a completely uniform tone;
  • the control mock-up is accepted before mass installation begins.

Which contract terms should be stated in the tender documents?

Commercial terms affect the price no less than quantities do, so they are announced to all bidders in advance. If one prices with an advance for materials and another with payment on completion, the totals cannot be compared.

  • Payment procedure. The size of the advance, payment for units manufactured and held in store, and settlement for completed work sections.
  • Retention and the period for its release, and the requirements for the facade warranty.
  • Indexation. Whether the price is fixed for the whole period or may be revised if material costs change on long projects.
  • Liability for time on both sides: for handing over the work area and for the supply and installation programme.
  • Variation procedure. Additional quantities are valued at the unit rates given in the bid.
  • Handover. The content of the as-built documentation and the interim certificates for concealed works.

Unit rates are best requested as a separate table. They will be needed for any design change and will make checking easier when the winner submits a detailed cost estimate for the facade works.

How to assess alternative solutions in a facade tender

An alternative bid is an option in which the bidder changes the material, the fixing system or the division into units compared with the design. It is considered only alongside the base calculation to the brief, otherwise comparison with the other bidders loses its meaning.

Substitutions can be useful: the manufacturer sees better than the client where panels can be enlarged, the number of unique moulds reduced or a solid stone profile replaced with a thin-walled unit. The saving is then achieved through technology rather than at the expense of appearance. To assess such an option, the bidder is asked for:

  • a description of the substitution and a list of the drawings it affects;
  • the difference in price and programme compared with the base option;
  • confirmation of performance: fire classification, weight per square metre, frost resistance;
  • a sample or a building where the solution has already been used;
  • the opinion of the design author, if the appearance changes.

An accepted alternative is formalised as a design change before the contract is signed. If this is not done, a discrepancy between the documentation and the as-built facade will arise at handover.

Typical client mistakes when running a tender

Most tender problems are created not by the bidder but by the organiser. Listed below are the mistakes that most often lead to delays and supplementary agreements.

  • Launching the tender on renderings without elevations and sections: bidders price different facades.
  • Too short a pricing period and no site visit.
  • Changing the design during the tender without issuing the new version to all bidders.
  • Allowing alternative materials without rules: substitutions should be submitted as a separate line next to the base option.
  • Evaluating on price alone, without checking production capacity and the workload of the installation teams.
  • No requirement in the brief to test the substrate: the results of anchor pull-out tests change the bracket spacing and the cost of the subframe.
  • Verbal agreements with the preferred bidder that are not recorded in the minutes and the contract.

A simple self-check: if three bidders who received the same package asked the same questions, there is a gap in the documentation, and it is better to close it before bids are submitted.

FAQ

What should a facade specification include?

Project data, design documentation, scope of work, material requirements, site conditions, bidder requirements and a single bid form.

How do I compare bids for facade works?

Compile them into a table by specification item, check quantities and materials, identify excluded items, and only then compare totals.

Why is an element schedule needed in a tender?

So that all bidders price the same number of products. Without it, price differences reflect different scopes, not different quality.

Why does the cheapest tender bid often turn out more expensive?

It usually omits design, fixings, junctions, equipment or delivery, and these costs appear after the contract is signed.

Can the price be fixed after the tender?

Yes, if the bid is complete and was prepared after studying the design and inspecting the site; otherwise the price will be refined.

How many bidders should be invited to a facade tender?

Four to six companies are usually enough. With two or three bidders it is hard to gauge the market level, and with ten the client has no time to examine each bid in depth, so the comparison is reduced to the bottom line.

Can a tender be held without working drawings?

Yes, but the price will be indicative. From a concept or design documentation bidders price by area and comparable projects, so the contract should provide for quantities to be refined once the working drawings and shop drawings have been produced.

Who pays for the samples and the control mock-up?

The client sets the terms in the brief. Small material samples are generally provided by bidders at their own expense, while the control mock-up on site is usually included in the contract with the winner as a separate item.
