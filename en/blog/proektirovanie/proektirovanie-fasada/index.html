Facade Design: Stages and Documents

Facade design is the development of a set of solutions and documents from which the facade will be manufactured and installed: from the architectural concept and choice of materials to working drawings of the substructure, cladding layout, details and specifications. For a ventilated rainscreen facade the design also includes calculations: wind load, cladding weight, anchor capacity, thermal performance.

The main criterion of a good design is that it can be built without improvisation on the scaffold. If junctions, reveals and parapets are not detailed, installers resolve them on site, and that is exactly where leaks, cracks and efflorescence later appear.

This article is for developers, general contractors, client-side technical managers and architects planning the schedule and budget of facade works. At TECHNOFORMA the design department consists of 12 designers led by two architects, as well as 3D visualisers; the design is developed together with manufacturing and installation. More on selection and design is in the section Facade design and selection.

Stages

Facade design usually follows this sequence:

  1. Collecting input data. The architectural design or a survey of the existing building, wall structure, construction area (wind, snow, climate), client and regulatory requirements.
  2. Concept. The image of the facade, articulation, materials, colours; sketches and 3D visualisations of several options.
  3. System selection. Cladding type, substructure, insulation, fixing method, with fire safety requirements checked for the building's height and class.
  4. Calculations. Structural calculation of the substructure and fixings, thermal calculation, checking the load-bearing capacity of the substrate (often with pull-out tests of anchors on the wall).
  5. Working documentation. Architectural drawings of the facade, shop drawings of the substructure, cladding layout, details, specifications.
  6. Production documentation. Product drawings: for GFRC these are models and moulds, for stone cutting and finishing sheets.
  7. Designer supervision during installation and revision of the documentation to reflect actual deviations.

At TECHNOFORMA, after the design department, the work is taken over by the company's own mould shops (models and moulds are made in-house), production with quality control, logistics and installation teams, so the transition from drawing to product happens within one company.

Architectural drawings and substructure shop drawings

The architectural section (AR in Russian practice) answers the question of what the facade should look like: articulation, proportions, materials, colours, architectural details, levels. It is based on the concept and, for buildings subject to state expert review, forms part of the design documentation.

The shop drawings for the metal structures (KMD in Russian practice) answer the question of how it is held up: detailing of the substructure, every bracket, rail, clip and anchor, their position and spacing, assembly diagrams. The substructure is manufactured and assembled from these drawings.

  • The architectural section sets the cladding layout; the shop drawings adapt the rail grid to it.
  • The architectural section fixes the material and panel thickness; the shop drawings calculate the loads and select the fixings.
  • The architectural section shows projecting elements (cornices, pilasters, bay windows); the shop drawings resolve their embeds and brackets.

If the two sections are produced by different organisations without coordination, discrepancies arise: a joint does not land on a rail, a cornice has nothing to hang on. When both are developed by one team together with the cladding manufacturer, there are fewer such discrepancies.

Approvals

Facade design approvals take place at several levels:

  • With the client and architect: concept, materials, samples, visualisations, layout. Decisions are recorded in minutes.
  • With the lead designer: compatibility with the structure, building services, windows, roof.
  • State expert review: for projects whose design documentation is subject to review, facade solutions are reviewed as part of the design.
  • City authorities: in some cities, including Moscow, the appearance of a facade is approved separately (architectural appearance, facade passport, etc.). The procedure and documents are set by each city's regulations.
  • Technical supervision: checks that installation complies with the working documentation.

Changes after approval (a different material, colour or articulation) often require re-approval, so materials are best chosen and checked on samples before documents are submitted.

Timeframes

The time needed to design a facade depends on the area and complexity of the building, the number of unique details and elements, the completeness of input data and the number of approval iterations. There is no universal figure; the timeframe is set for each project after analysing the input data.

What really speeds up a design:

  • a full set of input data at the start: architectural design, structure, survey results;
  • an early decision on materials and system: every revision sets back the shop drawings and layout;
  • parallel work: while the working documentation is in progress, production prepares models and moulds for standard GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) elements;
  • one team for architectural drawings, shop drawings and products: fewer letters and less waiting for replies.

For large projects, the design is split into phases by building or facade, so that installation of the first sections can start before the design of the whole building is finished.

What input data are needed before facade design begins

The designer needs information about the building, the construction site, and the client's expectations regarding appearance and budget. If some of the data are missing, work can begin, but decisions will have to be taken on assumptions that are checked later and often revised.

The minimum package is as follows:

  • architectural drawings — plans, elevations, sections, external wall details;
  • structural solutions — material of the walls and frame infill, position of floor slabs, columns, shear walls and movement joints;
  • site address and characteristics — climatic zone, wind zone, design seismicity, terrain type;
  • thermal performance requirements for the external wall, or a completed calculation from the building design;
  • data on windows and curtain walling — type, position in the opening, fixing method;
  • preferences for cladding material, colour and texture; samples or references;
  • a budget and programme guideline, and the sequence in which the blocks are to be built;
  • for existing buildings — measured surveys, results of the wall survey and fixing test reports.

The last item deserves particular attention. On refurbishment projects the actual condition of the walls almost always differs from the archive drawings: the masonry material changes, and there are voids, blocked-up openings and weakened areas. Designing without a survey in such a case is working blind.

What calculations are carried out at the stages of facade design

The calculations confirm that the facade will withstand the loads, will not suffer cold spots or accumulate moisture, and that its construction meets fire safety requirements. Without them the drawings remain a picture that can neither be justified to the approving body nor built safely.

CalculationWhat it determinesWhat it affects
Structural (strength)Forces in brackets, rails and anchors from the self-weight of the cladding, wind and iceSpacing and size of subframe components, quantity of fixings
ThermalReduced thermal resistance of the wall, allowing for thermally conductive inclusionsThickness and grade of insulation, use of thermal breaks under brackets
MoistureRisk of moisture accumulation in the insulation, adequacy of the air gapSize of the gap, need for a membrane
Thermal movementElongation of rails and cladding elements under temperature changesLength of rails, design of joints, joint width
Special actionsBehaviour of the system under seismic loads, where the construction region is seismicType of fixings, additional ties, limits on cladding weight

Wind load is distributed unevenly over a facade: in corner zones and at the parapet it is considerably higher than in the middle of the wall, and it increases with height. The fixing spacing in the design therefore cannot be the same over the whole area. The method is set out in detail in the article on ventilated facade calculation.

A thermal calculation that ignores the brackets gives an overstated result: each of them conducts heat past the insulation. How these losses are allowed for and reduced is explained in the article on thermal bridges. For projects in the Caucasus and other seismic regions the fixings are checked separately — see the article on facades in seismic areas.

Stages of designing a GFRC or stone facade: what is added

Where the cladding is GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) or natural stone, steps connected with the manufacture of the units are added to the standard sequence. Here the design ends not with a fixing detail but with a drawing of each type of element, from which the mould is made or the slab is cut.

  1. Division into units. Continuous architectural forms — cornices, string courses, surrounds, columns — are divided into elements that can be cast, transported and lifted. The position of the joints is agreed with the architect.
  2. Standardisation. Elements of similar size are reduced to a single type: the fewer different moulds, the lower the cost and the shorter the production time.
  3. Design of inserts. For each unit the position of the cast-in inserts, their load-bearing capacity and the method of connection to the subframe are determined.
  4. Unit drawings. Formwork drawings are issued with dimensions, profile, texture of the face and marking.
  5. Samples and references. Fragments are produced for approval of colour and texture before the production run starts.
  6. Installation scheme. The installation sequence, the slinging method and temporary fixings for heavy and projecting elements are specified.

For stone, the equivalent steps are cutting the slabs to suit the actual slab sizes from the quarry, pattern matching, drawings of special pieces and the position of kerfs for fixings. The thickness of the slab and its format are checked by a bending calculation under wind load.

Such work is effective when the designer knows the constraints of production: the maximum dimensions of moulds, minimum thicknesses, permissible undercuts. Within TECHNOFORMA the design department works alongside the workshop where GFRC products are made, so questions of buildability are resolved before the drawings are issued.

How the design affects the estimate for facade works

Most of the cost of a facade is determined at the design stage, not at the procurement stage: by the time prices are negotiated with suppliers, the material, the format, the subframe spacing and the number of unit types have already been set. It is therefore sensible to optimise the budget while the decisions still exist on paper.

The final figure is most affected by:

  • the choice of cladding material and format — this governs both the price per square metre and the load on the subframe;
  • the number of bespoke elements — each new mould or special piece adds production preparation;
  • the proportion of complex details — corners, reveals, bay windows and curved areas require more metal and labour than the field area;
  • the subframe material and the insulation thickness;
  • the layout, which determines the percentage of waste in cutting.

An accurate bill of quantities appears only after the working documentation. Until then the assessment is based on aggregated rates, and the more complex the facade, the greater its margin of error. The cost structure is examined in the article on the estimate for facade works.

Proper optimisation does not change the appearance: the number of sizes is reduced, divisions are aligned to standard formats, solid parts are replaced with lightweight hollow ones. Substituting a cheaper material without recalculating the subframe and obtaining fresh approval is not optimisation — it is a change to the design, with all the consequences.

Which design errors cost the most during installation

The most expensive errors are those discovered after the cladding has been manufactured. A drawing can be corrected in a day, whereas recasting a batch of units or reordering stone means weeks and direct costs.

  • The design was based on architectural drawings without a survey. The actual deviations of the walls are not allowed for, the bracket projection is insufficient, and the unit dimensions do not match the openings.
  • The layout is not coordinated with the windows and floor slab levels. Narrow make-up slabs appear, and joints do not fall on the centre lines of openings.
  • Details have not been worked out. Junctions with the roof, curtain walling and entrances are left to the installers' discretion and resolved on site.
  • Thermal movement has not been allowed for. After one season, slabs are pushed out and joints are damaged.
  • The weight of the decorative elements was not fed into the calculation. The subframe was designed for flat cladding, yet projecting elements are hung on it.
  • Adjacent disciplines have not been coordinated. Rainwater pipes, lightning protection, lighting and signage fixings clash with the subframe and cladding.
  • There are no requirements for the installation sequence. Heavy elements are set in an arbitrary order, and the last one does not fit the gap left for it.

The common feature of these situations is a gap between those who draw and those who manufacture and install. It is closed either by regular coordination between the parties or by entrusting the whole cycle to a single contractor.

How the client should accept a facade design: what to check

Accepting a design means making sure that materials can be purchased and installation carried out from it without further questions to the author. The client does not have to check the calculations personally, but it is worth working through a checklist.

  • The set corresponds to the design brief in scope and stages.
  • Calculations are included, stating the loads and the construction region for which they were made.
  • The system used has valid approval documents, and the materials in the schedule comply with them.
  • The cladding layout has been approved by the architect.
  • There is a detail for every non-standard area; references to the details are shown on the elevations.
  • The schedules contain grades, quantities and a breakdown by phase.
  • Installation tolerances and acceptance requirements are stated.
  • The documentation is signed by the author and the checker, and the sheets carry revision numbers.

FAQ

What does a facade design include?

An explanatory note, elevations with the cladding layout, substructure shop drawings, details, calculations, specifications and product drawings. For city approvals, visualisations and a colour scheme.

Who develops the facade design?

The lead designer as part of the architectural section, or a specialist facade company. TECHNOFORMA develops the design with its own design department, working together with production and installation.

How long does a facade design take?

It depends on the area, complexity and completeness of input data. The timeframe is set for each project; an early choice of materials and a full set of input data speed up the work.

Can a ventilated facade be installed without a design?

No. Without calculations and shop drawings it is impossible to confirm that the fixings are reliable and that fire requirements are met, and details are improvised on site with a risk of leaks.

Can facade design begin before the building frame is complete?

Yes: the concept, the layout and the preliminary calculations are carried out from the design drawings of the building. However, the working documentation and unit drawings are properly issued after an as-built survey of the erected structures: the actual deviations of the walls and floor slabs affect the bracket projection and the dimensions of the cladding elements.

Does a facade design require expert review?

Facade solutions form part of the design documentation for the building and undergo expert review together with it, where such review is mandatory for the project. Working documentation for the subframe is not usually subject to separate state review, but the client may commission an independent check — this is sensible for tall buildings and heavy cladding.

Who owns the rights to the facade design after payment?

This is determined by the contract. Usually the client receives the right to use the documentation for the construction of the specific building, while the terms for reuse, transfer to third parties and making changes without the author's involvement are stipulated separately.
