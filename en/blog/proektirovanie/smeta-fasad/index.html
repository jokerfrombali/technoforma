Facade Works Estimate: Structure and Review

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

Contents

  3. Labour

A facade works estimate is a document that breaks down the cost of a facade into materials, labour, equipment, overheads and the contractor's profit. It should be checked in two respects: completeness (are all design items included?) and justification (do quantities match the design and rates match the market?).

The main mistake when checking is to look at the total or the price per square metre. Two estimates with the same total can describe different facades: one includes the substructure, insulation and junctions, the other only the cladding.

This article is for developers, cost estimators, technical supervisors and private clients who have received an estimate from a contractor. Other design-stage questions are covered in the section Facade Design and Selection.

Sections of the Estimate

The structure of an estimate for a ventilated rainscreen facade usually follows the installation sequence. Typical sections:

SectionWhat it includesWhat to look for
Design workSubstructure calculations, shop drawings, cladding layout, detailsIs it there at all; who issues it
Preparatory workScaffolding, lifts, hoarding, anchor testsWho provides equipment — client or contractor
SubstructureBrackets, rails, anchors, isolating padsMaterial (aluminium, galvanised or stainless steel), spacing
InsulationInsulation, dowels, wind barrier membraneThickness and density per thermal calculation
CladdingPanels, decoration, fixings, clips, embedded partsConsistency with the element schedule
Junctions and trimsReveals, sills, parapets, corners, fire barriersHave any been omitted
LogisticsDelivery, unloading, storageIs distance accounted for

If a section is missing, it does not mean the work is free: it will have to be paid for separately, possibly to another contractor and at a higher price.

Materials

In the materials section, three things are checked: description, quantity and price.

Description. An item called 'facade cladding' without the material, thickness, texture and manufacturer cannot be checked. For glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC), the element type and dimensions must be stated; for natural stone, the rock type, thickness and finish; for porcelain stoneware, the format, thickness and fixing method. The substructure should be named with its brand and material: aluminium and steel systems differ in both price and load-bearing capacity.

Quantity. Cladding area is calculated from the facade elevations minus openings but including reveals. For panel materials, a cutting allowance is added; its size depends on the format and layout complexity. For GFRC decoration, quantities are pieces and linear metres from the element schedule; the allowance here is minimal because products are made to size.

Price. Compare with current price lists or commercial offers from several suppliers. For custom-made products — GFRC decoration, stone with non-standard finishing — there is no market price, so such items are checked by requesting an alternative quote from another manufacturer against the same schedule.

Labour

The labour section describes the work involved: substructure installation, insulation, cladding installation, junctions. What to check:

  • Units of measurement match the type of work: substructure and cladding in m², cornices and string courses in linear metres, columns and complex decoration in pieces.
  • Quantities match the materials section: if materials show 8,000 m² of cladding and labour 7,000 m², one of the figures is wrong.
  • Separate rates for complex areas: installing large decoration, working at height from an aerial platform and curved surfaces are usually more expensive than a standard flat area.
  • No duplicates: the same work should not be counted twice — for example, fixing insulation both in the insulation section and in the substructure section.

If installation and production are done by one company, the cost of labour is often priced as a package. In that case, ask for a breakdown so it is clear how the price will be recalculated if quantities change.

Overheads

Overheads and estimated profit are the contractor's costs of organising the work and its income. In estimates prepared under Russian state methodologies they are charged at standard percentages of the wage fund; in commercial offers they are often included in unit rates.

Besides the overheads themselves, other 'mark-ups' in an estimate include:

  • costs of temporary buildings and structures;
  • winter surcharges if the schedule falls in the cold season;
  • travel and accommodation for crews working in another region;
  • a contingency reserve;
  • taxes.

Check the base on which each percentage is charged. A typical error is charging overheads on the cost of materials when the methodology uses labour costs as the base, or charging the winter surcharge twice. The contingency reserve is worth discussing separately: it is usually better for the client to pay for additional work as it arises than to include a fixed percentage.

How to Check an Estimate

A review procedure that suits both technical supervisors and clients without cost-estimating training:

  1. Match the list of sections against the design: are all facade structures included?
  2. Recalculate the main quantities from the drawings — area of cladding, substructure, insulation, length of cornices.
  3. Cross-check quantities of materials and labour against each other.
  4. Check that material names and brands match the design.
  5. Compare key rates with alternative offers.
  6. Check the mark-ups: the base and the size of the percentages.
  7. List everything missing from the estimate and ask the contractor for a written answer.

Separately, find out what happens if actual quantities turn out different: a fixed-price estimate and an estimate based on actual quantities require different controls. A contractor that prices after inspecting the site and from working documentation gives a more accurate figure than one that estimates from the facade area by eye.

FAQ

What should a facade works estimate include?

Design, preparatory work, substructure, insulation and wind barrier, cladding, junctions and trim elements, logistics, overheads and profit.

How do I check a facade estimate?

Match sections to the design, recalculate quantities, cross-check materials and labour, check rates and the base for mark-ups, and list missing items.

Why do ventilated facade estimates vary so much between contractors?

Usually because of different scopes of work and materials: substructure, insulation thickness, junctions, equipment and delivery are treated differently.

How can I check the price of custom GFRC decoration?

Request an alternative quote from another manufacturer against the same element schedule.

When can a contractor give an accurate estimate?

After studying the design and inspecting the site; at TECHNOFORMA, the cost of construction and installation works is determined after the inspection.
