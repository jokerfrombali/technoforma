Facades in Seismic Zones: Fixing the Cladding

A facade in a seismic zone is designed so that in an earthquake the cladding does not tear away from the building and fall on people. To achieve this, the cladding weight is reduced, fixings and substructure are calculated for seismic loads, gaps are left so that elements can move with the frame, and the building's movement joints are repeated in the cladding.

The main criterion is the reliability of the mechanical fixing of every element at the design seismicity of the site. Lightweight materials such as glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) make the task easier, but any cladding needs calculation.

This article is for architects, structural engineers and developers working in the North Caucasus, Crimea, the Far East, Siberia and other areas of high seismicity. Other design issues are covered in Facade design and selection.

Loads

In an earthquake, a facade is subjected to loads that do not occur in normal conditions:

LoadNatureHow the risk is reduced
InertialThe cladding tends to stay in place while the building moves; the force is proportional to the element's massLight cladding, reliable fixings
Inter-storey driftFloors shift relative to each other and the wall deformsMovable fixings, gaps between elements
Vertical componentUp-and-down shocks, especially dangerous for cantilevered decorFixings designed for pull-out, secondary safety fixings
PoundingNeighbouring elements strike each other as they moveSufficient joint width

The inertial load on an element is greater the heavier it is and the higher it sits on the building: upper floors oscillate with greater amplitude. So large cornices and parapet decor at the top of a tall building are the most critical details.

Seismic loads are added to the usual ones: dead load, wind and thermal movement. The calculation is made for their combination.

As a guide, site seismicity is expressed in points on the MSK-64 scale, and seismic design is mandatory for sites with a design seismicity of 7 points and above. In Dagestan, Chechnya, Ingushetia, Kabardino-Balkaria and other North Caucasus republics, a large part of the territory falls into areas of seismicity 7–9 points, so seismic calculation is a standard part of facade design there.

Requirements

The main code for design in seismic areas of Russia is the Russian standard SP 14.13330 'Construction in seismic areas'. Site seismicity is determined from the general seismic zoning maps and refined by site investigations. Requirements for facade systems follow from the general provisions of the code and from the suitability documents of the particular system.

In practice this means:

  • the rainscreen substructure must be approved for use in seismic areas and calculated for the specific site;
  • cladding elements are fixed mechanically; adhesive-only fixing of heavy cladding at height is not permitted;
  • anchors in the substrate are pull-out tested and their capacity is confirmed;
  • decor and attached elements are calculated for seismic action together with their fixings;
  • the cladding must not bridge the building's movement and seismic joints.

Specific coefficient values and requirements for details are determined by the designer according to current codes and the substructure manufacturer's documents.

Fixing Methods

The cladding fixing method in a seismic zone is chosen according to the element's mass and size and the substrate:

  • Slabs on a rainscreen substructure (porcelain stoneware, stone, GFRC panels) are fixed with clips or concealed fixings to rails; the substructure has movable connections that accommodate displacement.
  • GFRC decor such as cornices, window surrounds and pilasters is fixed through cast-in fixings embedded in the element to the substructure or to the load-bearing structure. Thin-walled GFRC elements are much lighter than stone ones, which reduces the inertial load.
  • Thick natural stone is fixed with anchors, dowels and brackets of corrosion-resistant steel; adhesive may be used only as an additional measure.
  • Cantilevered decor (cornices with a large projection, balcony elements) is backed up by secondary safety fixings.

Fixings are designed together with the elements: the position, number and type of cast-in fixings depend on the element's shape. It is therefore more convenient when one organisation develops both the elements and the details. At TECHNOFORMA Group, the design department produces working documentation for the substructure and cladding, and the elements are made at the company's own production facility.

Movement Joints

The building's movement joints (thermal, settlement and seismic) divide the frame into independent blocks. In an earthquake the blocks move relative to each other, and if the cladding runs continuously across a joint, it will be destroyed.

  • The cladding and substructure are broken on the axis of every building movement joint.
  • The joint is closed with a compensating element that allows movement: a flexible profile or a cover strip fixed on one side.
  • Decor such as cornices and string courses is divided into separate elements at the joint.
  • Gaps are left between cladding elements, with a width set by calculation; they must not be filled with rigid mortar.

Architecturally, it is convenient to combine a movement joint with a compositional division: a pilaster, a change of material, the corner of a projecting bay. Then it does not read as a defect.

Junctions between cladding and windows or curtain walls need particular attention. Window units and cladding are fixed to different structures and move differently during oscillations. The junction is designed with a gap and an elastic seal so that a GFRC or stone window surround does not press on the frame or spall when the opening racks.

The width of joints between slabs on the substructure is also set with the design storey drift in mind.

Documents

For a facade in a seismic zone, the client and technical supervision need:

  1. a suitability document for the facade system (technical certificate or equivalent) stating that it may be used in seismic areas;
  2. a calculation of the substructure and fixings for the combination of loads, including seismic, for the specific site;
  3. working documentation: substructure shop drawings, cladding layout, fixing and movement joint details;
  4. pull-out test reports for anchors in the substrate;
  5. data sheets and test reports for cladding elements;
  6. as-built documentation: records of concealed work on anchoring and substructure, as-built drawings.

These documents will be needed both for design review and for acceptance of the facade.

Typical mistakes found when inspecting facades in seismic zones: heavy elements fixed only with adhesive; cladding bridging a movement joint; anchors in hollow blocks or weak brick without testing; rigid mortar in joints; cantilevered decor without safety fixings; a substructure not approved for seismic areas. After an earthquake the facade is inspected: fixings, cracks in elements and displacement of elements are checked, and damaged details are restored before the next season.

How does the design seismicity of the site affect the choice of cladding?

The higher the design seismicity, the tighter the limits on element mass, fixing method and joint width. At intensity 7 almost any material is feasible with a proper calculation; at intensity 8–9 the choice narrows towards light thin-walled products and subframes with a confirmed scope of application.

The final seismicity depends not only on zoning maps but also on the ground: following site investigations the design intensity may turn out higher or lower than the background value. Facade decisions are therefore taken once the survey report is available, not on the basis of the region's name.

SeismicityWhat usually changes in the facade designWhat to watch
Intensity 7Seismic load is included in the fixing calculation; the subframe must be approved for seismic regionsAnchor tests, gaps at openings and corners
Intensity 8More brackets and anchors, large elements are divided, safety fixings are added for cantilevered decorMass of each element, building height, details on upper floors
Intensity 9Preference for light cladding; heavy stone is used to a limited extent and under an individual calculationScope of application in the system's approval document, height limits

The table describes the general logic rather than regulatory limits: specific restrictions are set out in the facade system documents and determined by calculation. For more on how regional climate and terrain affect a project, see the article on facades in the Caucasus.

Ventilated facade subframe in a seismic region: which connections are made movable

The subframe must hold the cladding without transferring the deformations of the structural frame to it. To achieve this, some connections are rigid and carry the weight, while others are sliding and allow the rail to move relative to the bracket.

  • Load-bearing and support brackets. The weight of the rail with its cladding is taken by one load-bearing bracket; the others resist wind and out-of-plane seismic action and let the profile move along its axis.
  • Rail length. As a rule, a vertical profile is no longer than one storey and is interrupted at slab level so that inter-storey drift does not accumulate in a long rigid mullion.
  • Gap between rails. The joint between profiles is left open by the amount set in the calculation; a cladding element must not bridge this joint by resting on two adjacent profiles.
  • Cladding fixings. Clips, concealed anchors and cast-in fixings are designed for alternating loads; resilient pads are placed under brittle panels.
  • Material. Aluminium and steel systems differ in stiffness and thermal movement, and the choice is linked to the cladding mass.

The arrangement of mullions, brackets and sliding connections is covered in the article on the ventilated facade subframe. Installation of ventilated facades in seismic regions strictly follows the design: moving a bracket 'to suit the site' changes the structural model.

What to fix the facade to: substrate, frame infill and anchors

In framed buildings, which are typical of seismic regions, it is most reliable to transfer facade loads to structural members — slab edges, columns and shear walls. Block or brick infill deforms differently from the frame during an earthquake, and its capacity as an anchor substrate is noticeably lower.

The fixing scheme is chosen from the results of a wall survey and on-site tests. General guidance:

  • In-situ reinforced concrete is the preferred substrate; anchors approved by the manufacturer for cracked concrete and seismic action are used.
  • Solid brick is acceptable after testing; the result depends heavily on the quality of the masonry and joints.
  • Aerated blocks and hollow masonry units give low anchor capacity, so heavy cladding and decor are switched to a floor-to-floor scheme fixed into the slabs.
  • Insulation and membrane take no part in the structural action, but insulation fasteners are also selected to suit the wall material.

The number of test points and the reporting procedure are described in the article on anchor pull-out tests. Test results from the actual site matter more than catalogue values: they show the real condition of the wall.

Cornices, parapets and columns: how large decor is designed for seismic conditions

Large projecting decor is designed as a structure in its own right with its own calculation, not as a finish. The aim is to reduce the mass and projection of the element, duplicate the fixings and rule out a situation in which the failure of one anchor causes a piece to fall.

  • Division. A long cornice is split into sections, usually from one to several metres long, with a gap between them; each section is fixed independently of its neighbours.
  • Thin-walled section. A hollow GFRC element with a wall thickness of around 10–20 mm is several times lighter than a solid stone or concrete piece of the same profile.
  • Internal frame. Elements with a large projection are given a metal frame through which the load passes to the brackets.
  • Redundancy. Cantilevers and parapet pieces are provided with at least two independent lines of fixing and, where necessary, safety ties.
  • Columns and pilasters. Column casings are assembled from shells with horizontal joints over the height so that the element follows the storey drift.

Sections and versions can be seen on the page for GFRC cornices, and the approach to estimating product mass is given in the article on how much GFRC weighs. For high-rise blocks the mass of each element type is recorded in a schedule, and the structural engineer checks the connections against it.

Which facade solutions are used on different building types in seismic regions

The set of solutions depends on the height and purpose of the building: the taller the block and the more people there are near its walls, the stricter the approach to cladding mass and to decor above entrances and pavements. Below are typical combinations found in practice; they do not replace a calculation for the specific project.

Building typeTypical solutionSpecial attention
Multi-storey residential complexVentilated facade with GFRC panels or porcelain stoneware, GFRC decor on the lower and crowning floorsCornices and parapets at upper levels, repeatability of details across all blocks
Hotel, serviced apartmentsRich decor: columns, arches and window surrounds made of thin-walled elements; stone at plinth and entrance levelCanopies and portals above entrances, areas with constant pedestrian flow
Business centreLarge-format panels combined with glazed screensJunctions with glazed structures, different movements of facade and glazing
Public building (university, school, hospital)Cladding on a subframe with an increased fixing reserveEscape routes: no heavy hung items above exits without duplicated fixings
Low-rise and private houseStone or GFRC on a subframe or on anchors to the load-bearing wallSubstrate quality, fixing of cornices and balustrades

The general principle for all types is that heavy materials go lower and light ones higher. Natural stone is appropriate on the plinth and first floors, where it also protects against mechanical damage, while above that its texture is reproduced by GFRC panels. This combination reduces the load on the subframe and structural frame and simplifies installation at height.

For areas above entrances, walkways and playgrounds the designer usually specifies additional measures: a reduced element format, safety fixings and avoidance of projecting pieces with a large overhang. It is worth discussing these decisions with the architect at concept stage so that they do not look like a forced correction. Equipment hung on the facade is assessed separately — outdoor air-conditioning units, signage and lighting: these are fixed to the load-bearing structure through their own brackets, not to the cladding or the subframe rails, unless the calculation provides for it.

Facade design procedure for a seismic region

Seismic requirements are taken into account from the first sketch: correcting the mass and division of the cladding at working-drawing stage is expensive. The sequence of work is usually as follows:

  1. Obtain from the client the design seismicity of the site, the survey data and the structural layout of the building with the position of seismic joints.
  2. Select the cladding and subframe with regard to the scope of application in the approval document.
  3. Agree with the building's structural engineer the design storey drifts and the points to which the facade may be fixed.
  4. Develop the division of cladding and decor, aligning joints with the axes of movement joints and slab levels.
  5. Calculate the subframe and the fixings of cladding and decor for the load combination.
  6. Carry out anchor tests and adjust the bracket spacing to the actual results.
  7. Issue working and shop drawings with details, element schedules and installation requirements.

When concept, calculation and manufacture are in the same hands, changes are processed faster: at TECHNOFORMA Group this work is done by 12 design engineers and 2 architects, and the products are made at the company's own plant in the Moscow region.

What should be checked when installing cladding in a seismic region?

On site, the first things to check are those that cannot be seen once the cladding is in place: anchors, brackets, sliding connections and gaps. Concealed work is recorded in inspection certificates with photographs.

  • the type and grade of anchors match the design, and embedment depth and tightening torque are observed;
  • load-bearing and support brackets are in their correct positions, and the slotted holes of sliding connections are not locked by rivets;
  • rails are interrupted at the levels shown in the design, and gaps are not filled with sealant or mortar;
  • every decorative item is fixed at all the specified cast-in points, and safety ties are installed;
  • cladding and subframe are interrupted along the axes of the building's movement joints;
  • deviations from the design are agreed with the designer in writing before they are carried out.

The site diary, certificates and as-built drawings are kept until the end of the facade's service life: during an inspection after a seismic event they make it possible to locate the critical connections quickly.

FAQ

How is a rainscreen facade made earthquake-resistant?

Use a substructure approved for seismic areas, calculate it and its fixings for seismic action, fix the cladding mechanically and leave gaps and movement joints.

Which cladding is best for a seismic zone?

Light cladding with reliable mechanical fixing: GFRC panels and decor, porcelain stoneware on a substructure; heavy stone with reinforced fixing according to calculation.

Can stone be bonded to a facade in a seismic zone?

Heavy cladding at height must not be bonded without mechanical fixing; adhesive is acceptable only as an additional measure.

Which code governs construction in seismic areas?

The Russian standard SP 14.13330 'Construction in seismic areas', and for the facade system, its suitability documents and calculation.

What happens to the cladding at a building movement joint?

The cladding and substructure are broken on the joint axis and the joint is closed with a compensating element.

Should a facade be inspected after an earthquake?

Yes. After noticeable tremors, the fixings of cladding and decor, cracks in elements, displacement of elements and the condition of joints are checked. Damaged details are restored, and elements with failed fixings are temporarily fenced off or removed.

Can natural stone be used on a facade at seismic intensity 8–9?

Yes, provided the facade system's approval document permits it and a calculation for the specific site and building height confirms it. Usually the format and thickness of the slabs are reduced, each slab is fixed mechanically with stainless-steel fixings, and on upper floors and above entrances heavy stone is replaced with lighter cladding of a similar texture.

Does building height affect the requirements for cladding fixings?

Yes. Oscillation amplitude and wind load increase with height, so on upper floors and in corner zones brackets and anchors are usually spaced more closely and the requirements for large decor are stricter. Height limits are set out in the facade system documents.

Who is responsible for the seismic calculation of the facade?

The calculation of the subframe and fixings is carried out by the facade designer on the basis of input data from the lead designer: design seismicity, storey drifts and the layout of movement joints. The results are agreed with the building's structural engineer, because facade loads are transferred to the load-bearing structure.
