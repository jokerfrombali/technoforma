Facades in Seismic Zones: Fixing the Cladding

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

A facade in a seismic zone is designed so that during an earthquake the cladding does not detach from the building and fall on people. To achieve this, the cladding's mass is reduced, fixings and the substructure are designed for seismic loads, gaps are left so that elements can move together with the frame, and the building's movement joints are carried through the cladding.

The main criterion is the reliability of the mechanical fixing of each element at the site's design seismicity. Lightweight materials such as glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) make the task easier, but calculation is needed for any cladding.

This article is for architects, structural engineers and developers working in the North Caucasus, Crimea, the Far East, Siberia and other areas of elevated seismicity. Other design questions are covered in the section Facade Design and Selection.

Loads

During an earthquake, a facade is subjected to loads that do not exist under normal conditions:

LoadNatureHow the risk is reduced
InertialThe cladding tends to stay in place while the building moves; the force is proportional to the element's massLightweight cladding, reliable fixings
Inter-storey driftFloors shift relative to each other, the wall deformsMovable fixings, gaps between elements
Vertical componentUp-and-down shocks, especially dangerous for cantilevered decorationFixings designed for pull-out, safety restraints
PoundingAdjacent elements strike each other as they moveSufficient joint width

The inertial load on an element is greater the heavier it is and the higher it sits on the building: upper floors sway with greater amplitude. That is why large cornices and parapet decoration at the top of a high-rise are the most critical details.

Seismic loads are added to the usual ones: self-weight, wind, thermal movement. The calculation is made for their combination.

Requirements

The main regulatory document for design in seismic zones in Russia is the Russian standard SP 14.13330 'Construction in Seismic Areas'. Site seismicity is determined from general seismic zoning maps and refined by surveys. Requirements for facade systems follow from the general provisions of the standards and from the suitability documents for the specific system.

In practice, this means:

  • the ventilated facade substructure must be approved for use in seismic zones and designed for the specific site;
  • cladding elements are fixed mechanically — adhesive-only fixing of heavy cladding at height is not permitted;
  • anchors in the base wall are pull-out tested and their load-bearing capacity is confirmed;
  • decoration and attached elements are designed for seismic action together with their fixings;
  • the cladding must not bridge the building's movement and seismic joints.

Specific coefficient values and requirements for details are determined by the designer according to current standards and the substructure manufacturer's documents.

Fixing Methods

The fixing method for cladding in a seismic zone is chosen according to the element's mass and size and the base structure:

  • Panels on a ventilated facade substructure (porcelain stoneware, stone, GFRC panels) are fixed with clips or concealed fixings to the rails; the substructure has movable details that compensate for displacements.
  • GFRC decoration — cornices, window surrounds, pilasters — is fixed via embedded parts cast into the product, to the substructure or to load-bearing structures. Thin-walled GFRC elements are significantly lighter than stone ones, which reduces the inertial load.
  • Thick natural stone is fixed with anchors, pins and brackets of corrosion-resistant steel; adhesive may be used only as an additional measure.
  • Cantilevered decoration (cornices with a large projection, balcony elements) is backed up with safety restraints.

Fixings are designed at the same time as the products: the position, number and type of embedded parts depend on the element's shape. So it is more convenient when one organisation develops both the products and the details. Movement Joints

A building's movement joints — thermal, settlement and seismic — divide the frame into independent blocks. During an earthquake the blocks move relative to each other, and if the cladding continuously bridges a joint, it will be destroyed.

  • The cladding and substructure are broken along the axis of each building movement joint.
  • The joint is closed with a compensating element that allows movement: a flexible profile, or a cover plate fixed on one side only.
  • Decoration — cornices, bands — is divided into separate elements at the joint.
  • Gaps are left between cladding elements, their width set by calculation; they must not be filled with rigid mortar.

Architecturally, it is convenient to align a movement joint with a compositional division: a pilaster, a change of material, the corner of a projection. Then it does not read as a defect.

Junctions between cladding and windows or curtain walls need particular attention. Window units and cladding are fixed to different structures and move differently during oscillations. The junction is designed with a gap and a flexible seal so that a GFRC or stone window surround does not press on the frame or crumble when the opening is distorted.

Documents

For a facade in a seismic zone, the client and technical supervisor need:

  1. a suitability document for the facade system (technical certificate or other) stating that it may be used in seismic zones;
  2. calculations of the substructure and fixings for the combination of loads, including seismic, for the specific site;
  3. working documentation: substructure shop drawings, cladding layout, fixing and movement joint details;
  4. pull-out test reports for anchors in the base wall;
  5. passports and test reports for the cladding products;
  6. as-built documentation: certificates of concealed works for anchoring and the substructure, as-built drawings.

These documents will be needed both for the design review and for acceptance of the facade.

FAQ

How is a ventilated facade made earthquake-resistant?

Use a substructure approved for seismic zones, design it and its fixings for seismic action, fix the cladding mechanically and leave gaps and movement joints.

Which cladding is best for a seismic zone?

Lightweight cladding with reliable mechanical fixing: GFRC panels and decoration, porcelain stoneware on a substructure; heavy stone with reinforced fixings by calculation.

Can stone be glued to a facade in a seismic zone?

Heavy cladding at height must not be glued without mechanical fixing; adhesive is acceptable only as an additional measure.

Which document governs construction in seismic zones?

The Russian standard SP 14.13330 'Construction in Seismic Areas', plus the suitability documents and calculations for the facade system.

What should be done with the cladding at a building movement joint?

Break the cladding and substructure along the joint axis and close it with a compensating element.
