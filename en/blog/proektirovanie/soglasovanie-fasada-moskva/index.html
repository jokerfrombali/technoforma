Architectural Appearance Approval (AGO) in Moscow

Facade approval in Moscow is the procedure in which the city's architecture authority reviews and approves the external appearance of a building: its massing, materials, colours and architectural details. In Moscow it has historically been called approval of the architectural and urban planning solution (AGR) and is carried out by Moskomarkhitektura; the Russian Town Planning Code uses the term architectural and urban planning appearance (AGO) of a building. In practice both mean the same thing: without an approved appearance you cannot build a new building or significantly change the facade of an existing one.

The key point for the client is to build approval into the schedule and choose materials before submitting documents. The materials, colours and divisions fixed in the approved appearance become mandatory; replacing the cladding on site without re-approval is a violation.

This article is for developers, technical clients, building owners and architects working in Moscow. The exact list of documents, timeframes and procedure are set by current Moscow regulations and the service regulation on mos.ru, and must be checked before submission. More on design is in the Facade design and selection section.

When AGO approval is required

Appearance approval in Moscow is required in typical cases:

  • New construction: capital construction projects for which approval of appearance is required by city regulations. Some categories (for example, some individual houses, linear and certain industrial facilities) may be exempt; this is checked against current rules.
  • Reconstruction that changes the external appearance of the building.
  • Major repairs and facade changes: replacing cladding, changing colour or glazing, adding new entrances, shop windows or extra storeys, if this changes the approved appearance.
  • Amendments to a previously approved appearance: for example, a change of cladding material during construction.

Cultural heritage sites and buildings in their protection zones are subject to additional requirements and approvals by heritage protection authorities.

The first practical step is to check, using the land plot's town planning plan (GPZU) and current Moscow regulations, which procedure applies to the building.

The document package for appearance approval generally includes text and graphic materials. The specific list and formatting requirements are set by the service regulation.

The quality of visualisations and the accuracy of the finishes schedule directly affect the outcome: the architecture authority assesses the appearance based on the materials shown in the package.

For work on existing apartment buildings, minutes of the owners' general meeting are also required, since the facade is common property. For changes affecting the structure, a technical report on the condition of the facade and the feasibility of the work is prepared.

Typical mistakes when compiling the documents: materials in the finishes schedule not matching the visualisations, no catalogue colour references, outdated surveys of the existing building, visualisations without surroundings.

Material requirements

Cladding materials are fixed specifically in the approved appearance: type, texture, colour. So material requirements are set at two levels.

Architectural level: the appearance must fit the urban context, including building heights, the style of the surroundings and the compositional role of the site. Requirements for cladding quality are higher in the centre and in landmark locations.

Technical level: materials must comply with current Russian codes of practice (SP) on fire safety and loads for the type and height of the building. For rainscreen facades on multi-storey buildings, this means non-combustible cladding and systems with proven performance.

  • Natural stone, porcelain stoneware and GFRC (non-combustible, NG class, according to TECHNOFORMA) are non-combustible materials widely used in Moscow construction.
  • The finishes schedule identifies each material unambiguously; colour is given by the manufacturer's catalogue or a colour system.
  • Material samples are worth preparing in advance: they help the architect justify the choice and the client decide before submission.

Timeframes

The approval timeframe has three parts:

  1. Preparing the package: concept, visualisations, elevations, finishes schedule. Depends on the building and how ready the architectural solution is.
  2. Review by the architecture authority: the period is set by the public service regulation; check it on mos.ru on the date of submission, as regulations change.
  3. Revisions following comments: if the appearance is not approved first time, the package is corrected and resubmitted, and the review period starts again.

Careful work at the start helps shorten the overall timeframe: accurate visualisations with real materials, a considered colour scheme, no contradictions between the elevations and the schedule. Preliminary consultations with the architecture authority on the concept also reduce the risk of refusal.

A practical tip: build approval into the schedule before cladding is procured. Ordering material before the appearance is approved is risky: if there are comments on colour or texture, the batch will have to be changed. A sensible sequence is a concept with real samples, then approval, then the rainscreen facade design and procurement.

Changes to the appearance

Architectural appearance approval applies to a specific solution. If the appearance changes during design or construction, you need to assess whether an amendment is required.

  • Replacing the cladding material, for example natural stone with porcelain stoneware or GFRC, usually changes the appearance and requires an amendment.
  • Changing the colour, divisions or layout pattern also affects the appearance.
  • Changing the manufacturer while keeping the material, colour and texture may not require re-approval, but this is assessed case by case.
  • Changes to an existing building such as new entrances, signage, balcony glazing or replacing cladding during repairs also fall under the rules on changing appearance.

Changing the appearance without approval can lead to enforcement orders and problems when the building is commissioned. So if the idea of substituting a material to save time or money comes up on site, first assess whether the substitution fits within the approved appearance.

Altering a facade without approval can result in administrative liability, an order to restore the facade to its previous state and difficulties registering the changes. Fine amounts and procedures are set by current Russian legislation and must be checked on the date of the work.

How to prepare a facade design for appearance approval: what to work out before submission

Before the documents are submitted, the facade design must be developed to a level at which it can be built without changing its appearance. The fewer provisional materials and 'approximate' colours the package contains, the lower the risk that the approved appearance will diverge from the working documentation.

  • Division and module. The size of panels and slabs, the joint pattern and the position of rustication and string courses are matched to production capabilities and material formats.
  • Material for each zone. Plinth, main wall plane, opening surrounds, crowning part, entrance areas — for each zone the material, texture and colour are named.
  • Decor. The profiles of cornices, architraves and mouldings are shown at a scale sufficient to assess the modelling of the facade.
  • Hung equipment. Places for outdoor air-conditioning units, signs, lighting and downpipes are provided for in the composition rather than left to the tenants' discretion.
  • Key details. The building corner, the window junction, the parapet and the joint between different materials are drawn at least at sketch level to confirm buildability.

This work belongs to the stage of the architectural concept of the facade. Involving the cladding manufacturer at this stage makes it possible to check formats, element mass and the fixing method in advance, and so avoid changing the design after approval.

Who takes part in preparing the facade approval and how to allocate the work

The package for appearance approval is prepared by the architect, but its content depends on several participants. If their roles are not defined in advance, information on materials and details arrives late and is contradictory.

ParticipantWhat they doWhat they contribute to the package
Client, client's technical representativeSets the requirements for appearance and budget, takes decisions on materialsDesign brief, initial permit documents
Architect, author of the designDevelops the concept, elevations, visualisations and finishes schedule; manages the submission and responses to commentsSet of materials for approval
Structural engineerChecks whether the frame can carry the chosen cladding and defines the fixing pointsLimits on mass and details
Facade manufacturer and designerConfirms formats, textures, colours and the fixing method, prepares samplesSamples, album of elements, preliminary calculation
Main contractorAssesses buildability within the programme and the sequence of workComments on the organisation of installation

It is convenient to appoint one person responsible for the finishes schedule — as a rule, on the architect's side. All changes of material pass through that person, and only one current version reaches the package. The same specialist later checks the specifications of the working documentation against the schedule.

The work is best planned backwards from the date by which the approved appearance is needed: time for preparing samples, for visualisations with real materials, for internal approval by the client. Making texture and colour samples takes time, so the request to the manufacturer is sent at the start of work on the concept, not on the eve of submission. It is also useful to agree in advance who answers comments on materials: if a question concerns texture, shade or element format, the manufacturer together with the architect prepares the answer faster and more accurately.

How should materials be described in the facade finishes schedule?

A material is described so that it cannot be replaced by a visually different one without changing the wording of the schedule. Four parameters are enough: type of material, surface texture, colour by catalogue or reference sample, and element format.

Glazing and framesType of glazing, tint, profile colourGlass and profile samples

For materials with natural variation — stone, or GFRC with a stone-like texture — it is useful to show the acceptable range of shades straight away rather than a single ideal sample. What the approved architectural and urban appearance fixes and what is refined in the working documentation

The approved appearance fixes what the observer sees: volume, proportions, materials, colour, the pattern of divisions and the character of the details. The engineering content of the facade — subframe, insulation, fixings — is, as a rule, not part of it and is developed later.

In practice this means that at working-documentation stage the following are usually refined without changing the appearance:

  • the brand and material of the subframe and the bracket spacing;
  • the cladding thickness and its fixing method, provided it does not alter the look of the joints;
  • the division of large decor into installation units, provided the joints are concealed or coincide with those shown;
  • the construction of junctions, sills and parapet cappings within the dimensions shown.

By contrast, a change in slab format, joint width and pattern, relief depth or shade is noticeable from outside. The line between refinement and a change of appearance is not always obvious, so borderline cases are assessed with the author of the design and, where necessary, with the architecture authority — before the material is ordered.

Replacing a material with an equivalent: how to preserve the approved appearance

A material can be replaced without loss of appearance only when the new cladding reproduces the texture, colour, format and joint pattern of the original. The formal question of whether the approval needs to be amended still remains and is resolved under the current procedure.

A typical situation is the replacement of solid stone decor with GFRC with a stone-like texture in order to reduce mass and lead time. To justify such a replacement, the following are prepared:

  1. comparative samples of the original and the proposed material, photographed under identical lighting;
  2. a fragment of the elevation with the new division and joints;
  3. data on properties: combustibility, frost resistance, mass;
  4. the opinion of the design author on conformity with the architectural intent;
  5. an updated finishes schedule and visualisation, if the appearance is being amended.

How the materials differ in look and in behaviour on the facade is examined in the article GFRC or stone. The decision on replacement is taken before production starts: tooling and stone purchased for a design that has already changed cost more than any pause.

How to take the approved facade through to construction without distortion

Between the approved image and the finished building lie working documentation, production and installation, and at each step the appearance may change. Three tools hold it in place: reference samples, a mock-up and author supervision.

  • Reference samples. Texture and colour samples signed by the client and the architect are kept by the parties and serve as the basis for accepting batches.
  • Mock-up. A full-size area of facade with cladding, joints and one or two details is installed before mass work begins; it is used to assess colour in daylight and the quality of joints and junctions.
  • Author supervision. The architect checks that the work carried out conforms to the design and agrees unavoidable deviations.
  • Single schedule. The finishes schedule from the approved package passes into the working documentation and specifications without items being renamed.

The architect's work on site is described in the article on author supervision of the facade. When the concept, working drawings and products come from one company, there are fewer points at which the design intent can be lost: at TECHNOFORMA Group 2 architects and 12 design engineers work on projects. The scope of work is described on the facade design page.

Checklist before submitting documents for appearance approval in Moscow

Before submission the package should be checked for internal consistency: a considerable share of comments relate not to the architecture but to discrepancies between sheets.

  • materials and colours are named identically on the elevations, the visualisations and in the schedule;
  • visualisations are made from the points from which the building is actually perceived and show the surrounding development;
  • colours are specified by catalogue, not descriptively;
  • all elevations are shown, including courtyard and end elevations;
  • the roof, entrance areas, shopfronts and the locations of signs and equipment are shown;
  • the plot restrictions under the land-use plan and any requirements arising from nearby cultural heritage sites are taken into account;
  • the current requirements for the content and format of materials have been checked against the service regulations as at the submission date.

If the building is constructed in phases or blocks, it is decided in advance whether the appearance is submitted for the whole complex or by stage, and care is taken that the first-phase materials remain available when the following phases are built. For stone this means reserving blocks from the same quarry; for GFRC it means keeping the tooling and the colour mix design.

FAQ

Who approves facade appearance in Moscow?

Moskomarkhitektura, the Moscow Committee for Architecture and Urban Planning. For cultural heritage sites, heritage protection authorities are also involved.

Does replacing cladding during repairs need approval?

If the replacement changes the approved appearance (material, colour, divisions), it usually does. This is determined under the current rules for the specific building.

How long does architectural appearance approval take?

The review period is set by the service regulation and should be checked on mos.ru. Add time for preparing the package and possible revisions.

Which facade materials get approved in Moscow?

What matters most is how the appearance fits the surroundings and meets technical requirements. Natural stone, porcelain stoneware and GFRC are non-combustible materials widely used in Moscow construction.

Should the cladding manufacturer be involved before the appearance is approved?

There is no mandatory requirement, but it is practical. The manufacturer confirms that the stated formats, textures and colours can actually be produced, provides samples for the package and helps avoid a situation in which the approved material turns out to be unavailable or fails the calculation.

Can GFRC be used in an approved appearance instead of natural stone?

Yes, if the material is stated explicitly in the finishes schedule. GFRC is a non-combustible material used for both flat panels and three-dimensional decor with a stone-like texture. If the approved appearance specifies stone, switching to GFRC is treated as a change of material and the procedure established for amendments is followed.

What should be done if the approved material has been discontinued?

An equivalent with the same texture, colour and format is selected, samples are compared with the reference and the replacement is agreed with the design author. It is then assessed whether the replacement requires the approval to be amended under the current rules. To reduce this risk, materials for the whole project are reserved or purchased in a single batch.
