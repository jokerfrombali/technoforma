Portals and Entrances: Decorative Design

An entrance is the part of the facade that greets people: porch or steps, landing, doors, canopy, opening surround, lighting and signage. On a residential complex, business centre or hotel, the entrance shapes the first impression of the building, so it is treated more richly than the rest of the facade — with a portal, columns, a pediment or stone.

The main criterion in entrance design is combining expressiveness with durability in use: the entrance endures the heaviest wear from people, cleaning, water and de-icing salts. Decor must be robust at ground level, and the canopy must shed water correctly.

This article is for architects, developers and property managers designing or refurbishing entrances. Individual decorative elements are described in the section Architectural Facade Details.

What an Entrance Includes

Entrance design starts with a list of its functional parts — the decor is then distributed across them.

  • Porch and steps — with a slip-resistant surface, handrails and a ramp where required for people with reduced mobility.
  • Entrance landing — its size is determined by door swing and accessibility standards.
  • Door assembly — doors, lobby, glazing.
  • Canopy — protects the landing and doors from precipitation.
  • Surround — portal, architraves, pilasters, columns, pediment.
  • Plinth and wall cladding near the entrance — materials resistant to impact and soiling.
  • Lighting, address plate, signage — their positions are set in the design in advance, so as not to drill into finished decor.

A typical mistake is to complete the functional part first and then hang the decor on it. It is better to design the entrance as a whole: then columns sit on the porch without rework, the canopy is coordinated with the portal, and cables for light fittings are laid before the cladding is installed.

Portals with Columns

An entrance portal is an architecturally emphasised surround of a doorway. In the classical version it is a pair of columns or pilasters carrying an entablature and pediment; in a more restrained version, a profiled frame with a hood or attic above the door.

In a portico, the columns often act as cladding for the posts that support the canopy or slab above the entrance. In this case the load-bearing posts are designed by a structural engineer, and GFRC or stone columns are fitted over them. Portal proportions are chosen by door and storey height: the colonnade should not be lower than the second-floor window openings or cut across them.

Canopies and Pediments

A canopy is a functional element without which an entrance quickly loses its appearance: water from the roof and walls falls on the steps and doors, and ice forms in winter. Decoratively, a canopy is treated in several ways:

  • Pediment above the portal — triangular or segmental, on columns or pilasters; inside is a roof or a waterproofed slab.
  • Cantilevered slab on brackets — a reinforced concrete or steel slab with decorative corbels beneath and a profiled cornice on its edge.
  • Glass canopy on tie rods — a modern solution that does not overload a classical surround.

The canopy structure is designed for snow load, including drifts against the wall, and for wind. Decorative elements — cornice, pediment, corbels — are usually hung on the load-bearing slab or frame. Drainage is provided by a fall on the top surface, a drip along the edge and, if necessary, an outlet routed so that water does not pour onto the steps.

At the bottom, where impact, cleaning and de-icing salts act, dense stone such as granite or basalt performs best. Higher up, where modelling and low weight matter, GFRC is used: according to the manufacturer it is non-combustible (NG class) and frost-resistant, and it readily reproduces classical profiles. Approvals

Changing the entrance of an existing building changes its external appearance, and in many cases requires approval. The procedure depends on the region, the building's status and the nature of the works, so the points below are general guidelines for Russia rather than an exhaustive list.

  • Apartment building. The entrance and facade are common property of the owners; changes generally require a decision of the owners' general meeting and approval by the management company.
  • Non-residential premises in a residential building (a separate entrance to a shop or office) — usually a design, owners' consent and approval of the architectural appearance by the authorised local authority are required.
  • Building in a historic centre or listed heritage site — works are approved by the heritage protection authority, with stricter requirements for materials and form.
  • New construction — entrances are part of the design and the architectural and urban planning solution for the building.

Before ordering decor, check the local authority's requirements: many cities have landscaping rules and design codes regulating signage, canopies and facade colours. How to match the scale of a portal to the type of building?

The scale of an entrance is set by the height of the ground floor, the flow of people and the role of the entrance in the composition of the facade. The main entrance should read from a distance; secondary ones should not compete with it. For this reason a building usually has one developed portal and several simplified surrounds in the same profile.

Type of buildingCharacter of the entranceWhat to allow for
Residential complexentrance portals one or two storeys high, identical across the blocksrepeatability of units, damage resistance of the lower zone, access for residents with reduced mobility
Business centrelarge portal with glazing, restrained profilesdoor width for the flow, lobby, space for wayfinding and signage
Hotelcanopy for vehicle drop-off, columns, expressive lightingvehicle clearance, drainage from a large canopy
Public buildingportico with a wide flight of stepsseveral doors, escape routes, winter clearing of the steps
Private housecompact portal with a pair of columns or pilastersproportion to the house, space for light fittings

A common approach for multi-block buildings is to mark the main entrance with a large two-storey portal and to give the other entrances a frame with a pediment head. This preserves the hierarchy and reduces the number of unique moulds, and therefore the cost of tooling. The width of the portal is related to the window spacing: its edges should fall on the piers and not cut across the first-floor openings.

It is convenient to tie the height of the portal to existing horizontals: the top of the entablature to the string course above the ground floor, the top of the pediment to the sills of the first or second floor. The depth is determined by the width of the landing and the pavement: projecting columns must not narrow the passage, and on the building line the projection is, as a rule, limited by planning rules.

Lighting and service provisions: what to allow for before the decoration is installed

Everything that passes through the cladding of the portal is built into the design before the units are made: drilling a finished column or cornice for a cable means spoiling the surface and weakening the unit. The list of built-in items is drawn up jointly by the architect, the electrical engineer and the manufacturer of the decoration.

  • Lighting: fittings in the column bases, behind the cornice, in the canopy soffit; recesses and ducts for them are formed in the units in advance.
  • Intercom, card readers, call buttons for visitors with reduced mobility — with back boxes in the pilaster or wall.
  • CCTV and sensors — on brackets fixed to the load-bearing substrate, not to thin-walled decoration.
  • Signage and house number — cast-in fixings and a concealed power feed.
  • Heating of steps and drainage, an air curtain in the lobby — where provided for in the design.
  • Rainwater pipe from the canopy — it is concealed in a column or behind a pilaster only if there is an access point for cleaning.

Light changes the perception of relief: grazing light from below emphasises fluting and rustication, while floodlighting flattens the modelling. Techniques and types of fitting are described in the article on architectural facade lighting. For hollow columns it is decided in advance where the cables will run and how they can be reached for repair.

Where does an entrance deteriorate fastest and how can this be avoided?

The most vulnerable places are the junction of the porch with the building, the column bases by the steps and the lowest band of cladding. Water, salt, impacts and differential settlement of the structures all meet here.

The porch, as a rule, has its own foundation and settles differently from the building. A movement joint is formed between them, and the cladding of the portal is not rigidly tied to the cladding of the steps. If a column stands on the porch while the entablature is fixed to the wall, the capital detail allows for slight movement — otherwise a crack will run through the decoration.

The bases of columns and pilasters are not sunk to the level of the landing surface. They are set on a plinth block of dense stone and the junction is sealed: meltwater with de-icing agents destroys porous materials within a few seasons. The steps and landing are made with a fall away from the door and with a slip-resistant finish — details are in the article on natural stone steps and porches. For the most heavily loaded zone, granite with a flamed or bush-hammered surface is usually chosen.

The lowest band of the walls next to the door suffers from trolleys, bicycles and cleaning machines. Here the cladding is made of slabs of increased thickness with mechanical fixing, and external corners are protected with a chamfer or rounding: a sharp stone arris chips in the very first season.

Sequence of work: from the entrance sketch to installation

Work on a portal follows the same sequence as on the facade as a whole, but with a greater share of bespoke units. The duration of each stage depends on the complexity of the forms and is determined by the design.

  1. Measurement and survey: actual levels, condition of the wall and porch, location of services.
  2. Sketch and three-dimensional model: proportions, profiles, coordination with the windows and the ground-floor cornice.
  3. Structural solutions: load-bearing posts and canopy slab, porch foundation, fixing details for the decoration.
  4. Shop drawings of the units with division into elements, cast-in fixings and marking.
  5. Manufacture of moulds and units, trial assembly of complex details at the factory.
  6. Installation: first the load-bearing structures and waterproofing, then the stone of the lower zone, and afterwards the GFRC elements in the sequence set by the method statement.
  7. Sealing of joints, protective treatment, installation of light fittings and signage.

At TECHNOFORMA the stages from concept to shop drawings are carried out by the in-house design department — the composition of the documentation is described on the facade design page. Variants of orders for portals are shown in the section on GFRC facade columns and capitals.

When an occupied building is refurbished, one more condition is added — the entrance must remain in use during installation. The work is divided into phases, a temporary canopy and protective decking are provided, and large elements are lifted at the hours of lowest footfall.

Typical mistakes in entrance design

Mistakes are more often connected with proportions and water than with the material. The list below is convenient to go through as a checklist before the sketch is approved.

  • The portal is not tied to the facade: its cornice does not align with the ground-floor string course, nor its axis with the axis of the windows above the entrance.
  • The columns are too slender for their height or stand without bases directly on the tiling.
  • The canopy is shorter than the landing: water and snow fall on the top step.
  • There is no drip on the cornice and pediment — streaks remain on the wall below them.
  • The doors open into a column or railing; the ramp was added later and obscures the decoration.
  • Light porous stone is laid on the steps without protection and darkens quickly.
  • The signage covers the entablature because no place was provided for it.

Most of these shortcomings are already visible on a three-dimensional model if people, doors in the open position and the signage are shown on it. Approving a portal from the elevation drawing alone is therefore risky.

Care of the portal and porch through the year

An entrance gets dirty faster than the rest of the facade, so it is washed more often — as a rule once or twice a year, and the steps as required. Water and neutral detergents are used; acid cleaners damage limestone, marble and concrete surfaces.

In winter, ice is not chipped off the steps and bases with a crowbar: plastic scrapers and de-icing agents acceptable for stone are used. In spring the joints, the sealant at junctions and the lowest metre of the columns are inspected, and the water repellent is renewed if necessary. Methods and limitations are described in the piece on cleaning stone and concrete facades.

The canopy is monitored separately: outlets and drips are cleaned in autumn, after leaf fall, and snow is removed from a flat slab before ice forms on the edge. Streaks on the columns under the canopy almost always mean that water is going over the edge rather than through the outlet — the cause is remedied at the top, not by washing off the marks.

FAQ

What does entrance design include?

Porch and steps, landing, doors, canopy, surround (portal, columns, pilasters, pediment), plinth cladding, lighting and signage.

What is an entrance portal?

An architecturally emphasised surround of a doorway: columns or pilasters with an entablature, pediment or hood, or a profiled frame.

What should entrance steps be made of?

Dense stone — granite or basalt — with a slip-resistant surface finish.

Do changes to an entrance need approval?

In most cases, yes: in an apartment building, by the owners; in many cities, by the authority responsible for architectural appearance; for heritage sites, by the heritage protection authority.

Can portal columns support a canopy?

The load is carried by posts designed by a structural engineer; GFRC or stone columns are fitted over them as cladding.

Can a portal be installed on an insulated facade?

Yes. The elements are fixed to the load-bearing wall on brackets or anchors passing through the insulation layer, not to the render. The projection and weight of the units are allowed for in the fixing calculation; the heavy parts of a portal are, as a rule, supported on the porch or on separate posts.

What does the manufacturing time of a portal depend on?

On the number of unique moulds, the complexity of the capitals and profiles, the volume of stone parts and the readiness of the input data. The time is stated after the shop drawings are developed, when the number of units and the scope of tooling are clear.

How can the lower part of columns at the entrance be protected?

The base is set on a plinth block of granite or another dense stone, the junction with the landing is sealed, and the surface is treated with a water repellent. Where trolleys or vehicles may strike, bollards or guards are provided, or the height of the stone plinth is increased.
