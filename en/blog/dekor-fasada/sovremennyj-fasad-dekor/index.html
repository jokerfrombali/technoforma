Contemporary Facade: Sculpted Forms, Fins, Large Panels

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

A contemporary facade is a building's appearance built not on historical decor but on the sculpting of the volume, rhythm, texture and quality of materials. Instead of cornices and window surrounds — large-format panels with a minimum of joints, vertical fins and ribs, deep reveals, relief and perforated surfaces, carefully designed lighting.

The main selection criterion is precision of execution: in minimalist architecture every uneven joint and step between surfaces shows, so the materials and substructure must deliver strict geometry. Such facades are chosen for office centres, residential complexes, public buildings and private houses. A general overview of decorative solutions is in the section Architectural facade decor.

Trends

The main directions shaping contemporary facades of houses and public buildings:

  • Sculpting instead of decor. Expressiveness comes from folds, cantilevers, bay windows and deep loggias rather than applied elements.
  • Vertical rhythm. Fins, piers and ribs set the rhythm and act as solar shading.
  • Large format. Panels a storey high or more, a minimum of visible joints.
  • Texture. Relief, reeded and 'parametric' surfaces, play of light and shadow.
  • Natural tones. Stone and stone-look materials, concrete, warm metals, wood and its substitutes.
  • Curved forms. Rounded corners, wavy parapets, complex three-dimensional panels — where the technology allows.
  • Integrated services. Air-conditioning enclosures, ventilation grilles and light fittings are built into the cladding.

A minimalist facade does not mean an absence of detail — the details become technical: thin joints, concealed fixing, neat junctions.

Fins and ribs

Fins are narrow vertical or horizontal elements installed at intervals in front of the facade plane or windows. Ribs are piers and lesenes projecting from the plane. They solve several tasks at once:

  • set rhythm and scale, hiding the irregularity of windows and balconies;
  • act as solar shading, reducing overheating;
  • create privacy without blank walls;
  • change the facade's appearance with viewing angle and light.

Fins are made of aluminium, steel, wood and its composite substitutes, fibre cement and glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC). GFRC concrete fins look more massive than metal ones, allow complex cross-sections (trapezoid, triangle, rounded) and are non-combustible. They are heavier than metal, however, so support spacing and fixing are calculated for self-weight and wind load, especially with a large projection.

Fins on high-rise buildings need particular attention to wind: not only strength must be checked, but also freedom from vibration and noise.

Fin spacing is chosen according to facade orientation: on the south side horizontal elements block the high sun more effectively, on east and west facades vertical ones, including rotating fins. The fin rhythm is best coordinated with the window grid, otherwise the elements will cover openings randomly.

Large-format panels

Large-format panels are the basis of the contemporary look: the fewer the joints, the more monolithic and expensive the facade appears. Rainscreen facades use:

The advantage of GFRC is the ability to make a large panel of complex shape: with a corner, reveal, relief or perforation. Such products are made in custom moulds, so they pay off when repeated across a large facade. The properties of a specific mix and permissible sizes are stated in the manufacturer's technical documentation.

Materials

Cladding houses in contemporary materials is usually done as a ventilated rainscreen facade: substructure, insulation, air gap and cladding. One building often combines 2–3 materials — for example, GFRC panels for sculpting and accents, porcelain stoneware for plain areas, metal for fins and frames.

What to consider when choosing:

  • Fire safety. On multi-storey buildings the cladding and insulation must comply with current codes of practice. According to TECHNOFORMA, GFRC is rated non-combustible (Russian class NG).
  • Durability of colour and surface. Mass-coloured and natural materials age more evenly than surface-painted ones.
  • Repairability. In a rainscreen system a damaged panel can be replaced.
  • Compatibility. Different materials have different thermal movement, which is allowed for in the joints.

GFRC's freedom of form makes it possible to produce both flat cladding and three-dimensional elements in one material, which simplifies junctions and keeps the colour consistent.

How not to date

Fashionable facade solutions quickly become a hallmark of their decade. To keep a contemporary facade from looking dated in a few years, architects follow several principles:

  1. Proportions matter more than tricks. A well-found window rhythm and scale of divisions do not age, unlike random 'trendy' inserts.
  2. A limited palette. Two or three materials and tones last longer than a patchwork of cladding.
  3. Natural textures. Stone, concrete and materials with a natural texture age more gracefully than gloss and bright colours.
  4. Quality of details. Neat joints, junctions and flashings keep the facade clean; streaks and stains age a building faster than any fashion.
  5. Room for renewal. A rainscreen system allows part of the cladding to be replaced in future without touching the walls.

A minimalist facade is especially demanding in execution: without decor, every defect becomes visible.

FAQ

What materials are used for contemporary house facades?

GFRC, porcelain stoneware, fibre cement, metal cassettes, natural stone — most often as a ventilated rainscreen facade.

What is facade minimalism?

Abandoning applied decor in favour of clean volumes, large-format cladding, rhythm and texture.

What are facade fins made of?

Aluminium, steel, wood and composites, fibre cement, GFRC. Concrete fins are more massive and non-combustible but need a fixing calculation.

Can large panels of complex shape be made?

Yes, in GFRC: panels with a corner, reveal, relief or curve are made in custom moulds.

How can a contemporary facade be protected from dating quickly?

Limit the palette, choose natural textures, and pay attention to proportions and the quality of details.
