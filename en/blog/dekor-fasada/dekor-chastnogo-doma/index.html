Private House Facade Decor: What Is Worth It

Private house facade decor means cornices, bands, window surrounds, sills, rusticated quoins, entrance columns, balusters and other elements that shape the look of the house and protect the walls from water. For a private house, what matters is not the amount of decor but its appropriateness: a few well-chosen elements do more than a full set from a catalogue.

The main selection criterion is the balance of durability, weight and budget: elements must withstand frost and precipitation, be securely fixed to the wall and not need repair every few years. This article is for private homeowners and architects designing houses. An overview of element types is in the section Architectural facade decor.

Which elements you need

Facade decor elements can be divided into functional ones — without which the facade suffers — and purely decorative ones.

ElementFunctionPriority
Crowning corniceSheds water, protects the wall head, completes the lookHigh
External sills and dripsProtect the wall below the window from waterHigh
Plinth claddingProtects the base of the wall from splashes, snow and impactHigh
Window surrounds and pedimentsFraming windows, protecting the head junctionMedium
Floor band, mouldingsDividing the facade, shedding waterMedium
Rusticated quoinsVisual strength of cornersDepends on style
Columns, portico, balustradeAccent for the entrance or terraceDepends on style and budget

A classical house logically gets the full set of basic elements; a contemporary one gets clean surfaces with expressive sills, reveals and plinth.

Budget

The honest answer to how to finish a house facade on a budget is: save on the number and complexity of elements, not on material and fixing. Cheap decor that has to be replaced in a few years ends up costing more.

How to allocate the budget:

  • Function first. Cornice, sills, plinth — always in durable materials.
  • Standardisation. One surround profile for all windows, one band profile — fewer product types, lower cost.
  • Focus on the main facade. Rich decor on the entrance facade, restrained on the side and rear.
  • Phasing. Secondary elements can be added later if their locations and fixings are planned in advance.
  • Standard elements. Catalogue decor is cheaper than custom: no model or mould is needed.

Decor prices depend on material, size, complexity and order volume; TECHNOFORMA determines the cost of installation after inspecting the building.

Materials

The material for cladding and decorating a private house facade is chosen according to the wall, climate and budget.

  • Glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC). Non-combustible (Russian class NG, according to TECHNOFORMA), frost-resistant, reproduces complex relief, light compared with stone. Suitable for cornices, window surrounds, columns, balusters and cladding panels.
  • Natural stone. Plinth, steps, sill slabs, wall cladding. TECHNOFORMA supplies granite, basalt, dolomite, limestone, marble, sandstone, travertine, shell limestone and other types; it carries out measurements, layout, 3D visualisation and installation.
  • Coated expanded polystyrene. The cheapest exterior facade decor, fixed with adhesive to a rendered facade. Downsides: combustible, vulnerable to impact, poor detail.
  • Polyurethane. Light, good detail, but needs painting and has fire safety restrictions.
  • Porcelain stoneware, fibre cement, clinker — for wall cladding, often as a ventilated rainscreen facade.

For houses of aerated concrete, SIP panels and timber, weight matters: heavy decor needs a sound substrate and a fixing calculation. On timber-frame and log walls, cladding is usually installed as a ventilated rainscreen facade, where elements are fixed to the substructure rather than directly to the wall.

Installing on a finished facade

Decor is often added to a house that is already finished. This is possible but requires preparation.

  1. Substrate survey. Wall material, presence and thickness of insulation, condition of the render. This determines where and with what the elements are fixed.
  2. Choice of fixing. Light decor with adhesive plus mechanical fixing; heavy decor only with anchors into the load-bearing wall, through the insulation, with thermal breaks.
  3. Junctions. There must be no gaps between decor and wall for water to get in; top surfaces must be sloped or flashed.
  4. Making good. After installation, fixing points and joints are filled and painted to match.

On a facade with an external insulation and render system, heavy elements must not be glued to the insulation — it is not designed for such loads. Fixing must reach the load-bearing wall. In a rainscreen facade, decor is fixed to the substructure or to separate brackets, which is built into the design.

Mistakes

Typical mistakes when decorating a private house facade:

  • Too much decor. Columns, balustrades, cartouches and rosettes on a small house make it feel overloaded.
  • Wrong scale. A cornice too thin for a tall house, or massive columns at a modest entrance.
  • Mixed styles. Classical window surrounds on a contemporary house with panoramic glazing.
  • 'One-season' material. Unprotected foam and plaster break down from moisture and frost.
  • Gluing heavy elements or fixing to a weak substrate.
  • No water shedding. Decor without slopes and drips collects water and dirt and leaves streaks on the walls.
  • No design. Elements chosen one by one, without an overall layout and visualisation.

A facade visualisation before buying decor helps avoid most mistakes — the manufacturer's designers and 3D visualisers produce it.

Another frequent problem is decor chosen without considering drainpipes, air conditioners and lighting. Pipes crossing the cornice or outdoor-unit brackets on window surrounds spoil the look more than having no decor at all. Locations for services are best planned in the layout from the start. And finally, don't forget colour: decor painted a contrasting white on a dark wall highlights every inaccuracy of installation.

How to match decor to the height and architecture of the house?

The set of decor for a private house is chosen from the height and shape of the building: the lower and more compact the house, the fewer elements it can carry without looking overloaded. The starting point is not a catalogue but the silhouette of the house, the roof form and the window layout.

House typeWhat is appropriateWhat is better avoided
Single-storey with a hipped or gable roofA light crowning cornice, sills, architraves of simple profile, a stone plinthString courses, tall columns, roof balustrades
Two-storey classicalCornice, string course, architraves with pediments on the first floor, rusticated corners, a portico or pilasters at the entranceDecor of a different profile on each floor, small-scale mouldings
House with an attic storeyA cornice along the eaves line, dormer surrounds, gable decor, sillsA heavy cornice competing with the roof overhang
Contemporary house with a flat roofParapet copings, portal frames around windows, large panels, a plinth in dense stoneProfiled architraves, balusters, keystones

On a two-storey house horizontal divisions work better than vertical ones: the string course between floors and the cornice visually hold the volume together. Vertical elements — pilasters and rusticated corners — are added when the facade is long and lacks rhythm.

The fence, gates, canopy and garage belong to the same system. If one cornice profile and one type of surround are used on the house, they are repeated in simplified form on the outbuildings rather than replaced with new ones.

Sizes of decorative elements: proportion guidelines

Decor sizes are tied to the storey height and the width of the openings rather than chosen on the principle that bigger means richer. Below are the guidelines used at the sketch stage; final values are refined on the visualisation.

ElementGuideline for a 1–2 storey houseWhat to check
Crowning corniceProfile height 200–400 mm, projection 150–350 mmFit with the roof overhang and the gutter
String courseHeight 120–250 mm, projection 40–80 mmPosition relative to the floor slab and windows
ArchitraveWidth about 1/6–1/5 of the opening width, usually 100–180 mmDistance between adjacent windows
External sillProjection beyond the wall plane 30–50 mm, fall away from the windowDrip groove, insertion under the frame
Corner rusticationBlock height 250–400 mm, alternating long and shortWhether it divides evenly into the storey height
Entrance columnsDiameter matched to height by the proportions of the orderProportion to the canopy and the door

The main check is the view from the street and from the gate rather than from the drawing: these are the points from which the house is seen most often. More on choosing the scale is given in the article on how to choose decor from a catalogue.

How to fix decor to different walls: brick, aerated concrete, timber frame

The fixing method is determined by the wall material and the presence of insulation: the same cornice is set on anchors on a brick wall, but on a timber-frame house only through a subframe. This question is settled before the units are ordered, because the embedded parts depend on it.

WallFeatureTypical solution for decor
Solid brick, in-situ concreteHigh load-bearing capacityAnchors and brackets directly into the wall
Aerated concrete, ceramic blocksLow pull-out strengthSpecial anchors for cellular materials, fixing into the ring beam and floor slabs, pull-out testing of anchors
Timber frame, SIP panels, solid timberThe wall is not designed for point loadsVentilated facade: decor is fixed to a subframe connected to the frame
Wall with a rendered insulation systemThe insulation carries no loadLight elements — on the reinforcing layer, heavy ones — on brackets through the insulation

GFRC units are thin-walled shells, so they are noticeably lighter than solid concrete and stone and place fewer demands on the substrate. This is especially important for aerated concrete houses, where a heavy crowning profile would require strengthening of the masonry. Standard solutions for profiles and details can be found on the pages GFRC cornices and architraves and window surrounds.

Whatever the wall type, one general rule applies: the weight of the element is transferred to the load-bearing structure, not to the finishing layer. For large cornices and columns the fixing is calculated separately, taking into account wind and snow loads on projecting parts. Steel components are galvanised or stainless so that rust does not show through on light-coloured decor over time.

In what order should facade decor be ordered and installed?

Decor is planned before the walls are finished and installed after the roof and windows. In this order the embedded parts, brackets and flashings end up in the right places, and the finished surface does not have to be opened up.

  1. Elevation sketches with the layout of elements tied to the windows, roof and plinth.
  2. A survey of the built shell: the actual dimensions of openings and corners almost always differ from the design.
  3. Shop drawings of units and details, approval of a colour and texture sample.
  4. Manufacture; in parallel — preparation of the substrate and installation of brackets.
  5. Plinth cladding, then sills and window surrounds, string courses, cornice.
  6. Sealing of joints, finishing of walls up to the decor, final protection.

Mechanically fixed installation is possible almost all year round, whereas adhesive and render work depends on temperature and humidity. If finishing falls in the cold season, the restrictions should be taken into account in advance — they are covered in the article on facade work in winter.

In practice most of the time is taken not by installation but by approval of the sketch and samples, so the decision on decor is best made while the shell is being built. The units then arrive on site by the time the roof is closed and the windows are fitted, and facade work proceeds without the scaffolding standing idle.

What to prepare for a decor quotation for a private house?

For a preliminary quotation, drawings or photographs of the elevations with dimensions are sufficient. The more complete the input data, the more accurate the schedule and the fewer make-up pieces needed during installation.

  • elevations and plans of the house with opening sizes, storey heights and the cornice level;
  • wall material, type and thickness of insulation, type of final finish;
  • photographs of the house if it is already built, including corners, the plinth and the roof junction;
  • style preferences, references, the desired colour of the decor;
  • a list of elements that are essential and those that can be postponed to a second stage;
  • the site address and access conditions for delivery.

The size of the order is most affected by the running length of cornices and string courses, the number of windows and the number of standard sizes. The latter can be reduced by standardising openings at the house design stage: three or four window sizes instead of ten considerably simplify both manufacture and installation.

How to combine decor with the wall finish: colour and texture

Decor is perceived together with the wall, roof and windows, so its colour is chosen last — when the other materials have already been selected. A three-tone scheme works most reliably: the main wall colour, decor that is lighter or close in tone, and a dark plinth.

  • rendered wall — decor one or two tones lighter than the wall, or white if the architecture is classical;
  • clinker brick — light surrounds and string courses of simple profile that do not compete with the brickwork pattern;
  • stone cladding — decor in the same stone, or GFRC with texture and colour matched to a sample of the stone;
  • dark contemporary facades — a minimum of profiles, large frames and reveals in a contrasting or graphite colour.

The colour of the window frames, gutters and soffits is considered within the same palette: a white cornice next to a brown gutter and grey frames looks accidental. Samples are assessed outdoors, held against the wall, in daylight and in overcast weather — indoors the shades are perceived differently.

Care of decor and plinth after installation

GFRC and stone decor does not require regular repair, but it is worth inspecting once a year. Joints, the condition of the sealant, drip grooves and junctions with the roof and windows are checked — this is where problems begin.

Dirt is washed off with water and a soft brush; a pressure washer is used with care, from a distance and without a narrow jet. The lower part of the house suffers most from splashes, snow and de-icing agents, so dense stones are chosen for it and a sloped perimeter paving is provided — details are in the article on plinth cladding in natural stone. Gutters are cleaned before the rainy season: an overflowing gutter is a common cause of streaks on the cornice.

Small chips on GFRC elements are repaired with a colour-matched compound without removing the unit. Climbing plants are better kept off the decor: the shoots hold moisture in the joints and make it difficult to inspect the fixings.

FAQ

What exterior house decor lasts longest?

Natural stone and GFRC: they withstand frost and precipitation and do not need frequent repair.

Can expanded polystyrene decor be used?

On rendered facades of private houses, yes, as a budget option, but it is combustible, vulnerable to impact and reproduces detail less well.

How should an aerated concrete house facade be finished?

With a render system and light decor, or a ventilated rainscreen facade clad in GFRC, porcelain stoneware or stone; heavy elements are fixed with anchors by calculation.

Is a ventilated rainscreen facade suitable for a private house?

Yes, including houses of aerated concrete and timber: the cladding is fixed to the substructure and the wall remains ventilated.

Which facade cornices and mouldings should I choose for a house?

According to the scale and style of the house: for 1–2 storeys, a moderate projection and simple profile, in a frost-resistant material with a drip.

Can decor be installed on a finished facade?

Yes, after surveying the substrate, and with heavy elements fixed through the insulation into the load-bearing wall.

When should decor be ordered — before or after the walls are finished?

Before. Brackets, embedded parts and flashings are installed on the bare wall or during insulation, and render or cladding is then brought up to the elements. If the facade is already finished, decor can still be installed, but part of the finish at the fixing points will have to be opened up and reinstated.

Does GFRC decor need painting?

Not necessarily. The colour can be set through the mass, in which case the elements need neither painting nor periodic renewal of the coating. If the decor must match the colour of a rendered wall exactly, it is painted with facade paint together with the wall.

Can decor be installed without a contractor?

Small light elements on a low house — yes, provided the manufacturer's fixing scheme is followed. Cornices, columns and other large units are better entrusted to an experienced team: anchor calculation, work at height and precise setting to levels all matter here.
