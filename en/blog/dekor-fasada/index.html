Architectural facade mouldings

Ornament defines a building's character: the cornice completes the volume, string courses and bands divide the facade by storey, columns and pilasters set the rhythm, and window surrounds and pediments bring the windows into a single system. This section has articles on each element: how it is built, which sizes and proportions are appropriate, what it is made of today and how it is fixed to the wall.

We also look at styles — classical, the neoclassicism of residential complexes, Art Deco, Eastern motifs and contemporary facades with fins and large panels — and at how to choose elements from a catalogue and when custom ornament to an architect's sketch is needed. Most elements today are made of GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete): it is light, non-combustible and reproduces complex profiles, and moulds for a custom element are made in the mould shop to the drawing.

The articles help you speak the same language as the architect and the manufacturer: to understand why a cornice has a particular projection, how baluster spacing is calculated and why an element's scale is checked on a visualisation before production.

Moulding production is covered on the GFRC: element manufacturing page.
