Pediments and Attics

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

A pediment is the crowning of a facade, building end, portico or entrance, bounded by the roof slopes above and by a cornice below. In classical architecture it is a triangle or segment above a colonnade; in private construction, the whole triangular area of the end wall under a pitched roof is often called a gable. An attic is a wall above the crowning cornice that hides the roof and completes the facade horizontally.

The main selection criterion is structure and loads: the pediment and attic sit at the very top of the building, are exposed to wind and snow, and their weight is transferred to the walls or the top-floor slab. The form is therefore chosen together with the structural scheme, and the decor by weight and fixing method.

This article is for architects, structural engineers and clients designing the top of a facade. An overview of all elements is in the section Architectural Facade Details.

Types of Pediments

Facade pediments are classified by shape and degree of closure.

TypeDescriptionWhere appropriate
Triangulara classical pediment with a horizontal and two raking cornicesporticoes, central projections, private houses
Segmentalthe upper cornice in the form of an arcprojections, entrances, alternating with triangular
Brokenthe raking cornices are interrupted at the apex, with an urn, cartouche or coat of arms in the gapBaroque, expressive accents
Steppeda gable with stepshistoric northern European styles
Gable endan end wall under a pitched roof without a horizontal corniceprivate construction

The inner field of the pediment — the tympanum — is left plain, clad or filled with relief, a coat of arms or a round window (oculus). The pitch of the raking cornices of a classical pediment is usually shallow — much lower than the pitched roofs of private houses — so a classical pediment and the gable of a house look different, although in Russian they share a name.

Attic and Parapet

An attic is a wall above the building's crowning cornice. In antiquity, inscriptions were placed on the attics of triumphal arches; in neoclassicism the attic serves to hide the roof, increase the facade height and provide a place for sculpture or urns.

Attics and parapets are often confused. The difference:

  • A parapet is a guard along the edge of a flat roof, functional: it protects against falls, conceals roof details and helps organise drainage. It may have no decoration.
  • An attic is an architectural element above the cornice that continues the facade composition: with its own divisions, pilaster-like piers, profiles and sometimes balustrade inserts.

On modern buildings the attic is usually combined with the parapet: the wall along the roof edge acts as a guard and is clad outside as an architectural attic. An attic may be continuous or interrupted by balustrades, giving the facade a light silhouette. Attic height is chosen by the building's proportions and coordinated with roof guarding requirements if roof access is provided.

Construction

Structurally, a pediment and attic consist of a load-bearing core and cladding.

  1. Masonry. The pediment is built of brick or blocks on the top-floor wall. Tall thin walls need reinforcement and ties to the roof structure, otherwise they lose stability under wind.
  2. Cast-in-place reinforced concrete. Attics and parapets of multi-storey buildings are often cast together with the top floor slab.
  3. Steel frame. A light pediment or attic is assembled on a steel frame fixed to the slab or walls; the cladding is hung on the frame.

Cladding may be GFRC panels and profiled elements, stone, render over insulation or rainscreen panels. With a framed scheme, the GFRC tympanum and pediment cornices are fixed to the frame on brackets, so the load on the walls below is minimal.

In every option the top of the pediment and attic is capped: raking cornices with roofing or a metal flashing, the top of the attic with a coping falling inwards towards the roof. The junction with the roof is made by turning the waterproofing up the vertical wall. Cladding joints on raking cornices are positioned so that water does not run into the joint from above.

For pediments and attics, cladding weight is especially important: it sits at the very top, and heavy elements increase the load on the walls and require robust fixing. That is why GFRC is more often chosen for cornices, tympana and attic balustrade inserts on multi-storey buildings. Stone is used on low-rise buildings and where the load-bearing core is designed for its weight. Properties are in the product data sheet.

Loads on the Roof

Pediments and attics affect the roof and upper part of the building in several ways.

  • Self-weight — transferred to the walls or top-floor slab. For heavy cladding, the load-bearing capacity of the substrate is checked.
  • Wind — pressure is higher at the top of the building, and increased peak loads act at corners and edges. Tall attics and pediments are designed against overturning and tied to the frame.
  • Snow — drifts form on the roof against attics and parapets: snow accumulates at the wall, creating additional load on the roof and on the wall itself.
  • Water — an attic prevents free run-off, so drainage is provided by internal outlets or spouts through the wall.

Design load values are set by the current code on loads and actions and depend on the construction region and building height. Calculations are carried out by a structural engineer; for refurbishment, after a survey of the roof and walls. FAQ

What is a pediment?

The crowning of a facade, portico or building end, bounded by the roof slopes and a cornice; a classical pediment is triangular or segmental.

What is an attic?

A wall above the crowning cornice that hides the roof and completes the facade; it is often combined with a parapet.

How does an attic differ from a parapet?

A parapet is a functional guard at the roof edge; an attic is an architectural element continuing the facade composition.

What types of pediments are there?

Triangular, segmental, broken and stepped; in private construction, gable ends under a pitched roof.

What should pediment cladding be made of?

GFRC, stone or render; for the upper parts of multi-storey buildings, low weight and non-combustibility are important.
