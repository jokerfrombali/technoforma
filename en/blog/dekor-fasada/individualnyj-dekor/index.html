Custom Decor to an Architect's Sketch

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

Made-to-order facade decor is elements produced to an architect's sketch or drawings: non-standard cornices, window surrounds, columns, bas-reliefs, ornamental panels, signature details. Unlike catalogue products, each such element gets its own master model and mould, and a sample is approved before the series.

The main criterion for choosing a contractor is the full cycle: design, model and mould making, production and installation in one pair of hands reduce the risk of discrepancies between sketch and result. Custom decor is ordered by developers, architectural practices, private homeowners and operators of historic buildings. All element types are in the section Architectural facade decor.

What is needed for a quote

To estimate the cost and lead time of custom decor, the manufacturer needs input data. The more complete the package, the more accurate the quote and the fewer iterations.

  • Facades — drawings, or at least photos with dimensions, marking where the elements go.
  • Sketches or drawings of elements — profiles, sections, ornament developments; 3D models if available.
  • Quantities — linear metres of linear elements, number of individual pieces, number of repeats of each type.
  • Substrate — wall material, whether there is insulation or a rainscreen facade system.
  • Surface requirements — colour, texture, a sample if one has been approved.
  • Scope of work — decor only, or also fixing design and installation.
  • Timing — when the building is ready for installation.

If the sketch is still rough, the manufacturer's design department can develop it into working drawings: divide it into products allowing for joints, weight and transport, and produce a 3D visualisation. At TECHNOFORMA this work is done by 12 designers led by two architects, plus 3D visualisers.

Master model

A master model is a full-size reference piece of the element from which the mould for serial production is taken. The quality of every product in the series depends on its accuracy.

Ways of making a master model:

  • CNC milling from a 3D model — precise geometry, suitable for profiles and geometric ornament.
  • Hand modelling and carving — for sculptural elements, floral ornament, figures.
  • Combined method — the base is milled, details are finished by hand.
  • Assembly from run profiles — for cornices and string courses whose profile is run with a template.

The model is approved with the architect before the mould is made: correcting relief on the model is easier and cheaper than remaking the mould. Moulds are made from various materials — silicone compounds, polyurethane, fibreglass, metal — depending on the complexity of the relief and the number of products to be cast from it.

TECHNOFORMA makes models and moulds in its own mould shops, without outsourcing this stage to third-party workshops.

For symmetrical elements a model of half or a quarter of the product is often made and then mirrored in the digital model. This reduces manual work and eliminates differences between the left and right parts of a surround or portal. For long profiles it is important to define the product length and joint positions in advance — the mould size depends on them.

Lead time

The lead time for custom decor is made up of several stages:

  1. developing the sketch, working drawings and 3D model;
  2. making and approving the master model;
  3. making the mould;
  4. producing and approving the first sample;
  5. serial production, including curing of the products;
  6. delivery and installation.

The duration of each stage depends on the complexity of the elements, the number of different types, the size of the series and the production workload, so there is no single lead time — it is determined after reviewing the documentation. What speeds up the work:

  • ready 3D models or accurate drawings from the architect;
  • quick approval of the model and sample;
  • standardisation — fewer different element types, more repeats;
  • parallel work: while moulds for some elements are being made, others are already in production.

For large projects production capacity matters. According to the company, TECHNOFORMA's production site covers more than 2 ha, and its GFRC production capacity exceeds 20,000 m² per month.

Cost of moulds

The cost of moulds is the main cost item that distinguishes custom decor from catalogue decor. It depends on:

FactorEffect
Element sizeThe larger the product, the more material and work for the model and mould
Relief complexitySculptural relief and undercuts need hand work and a more complex mould
Number of different typesEach type needs its own model and mould
Series sizeThe more products from one mould, the smaller its share in the price of each element
Mould materialChosen according to the number of casts required

The practical conclusion: standardise elements at the design stage. Three types of window surround instead of ten barely change the facade's appearance but noticeably reduce cost. The exact price is calculated after reviewing the drawings — there are no general rates for custom moulds.

Sample approval

The sample is the first product from the production mould. Its approval is the last check before the series starts, and changes after that are expensive.

What is checked when approving a sample:

  • Geometry — dimensions, straightness, angles, profile matching the drawing;
  • Relief — crispness of arrises and details, no blowholes or chips;
  • Texture and colour — in daylight, compared with the approved reference and the wall material;
  • Cast-in fixings — position, compliance with the installation scheme;
  • Fit — how the product joins its neighbours, with no break in the pattern.

The result of approval is recorded in writing: the approved sample becomes the benchmark for the whole batch. Colour and geometry tolerances are best agreed in advance. The material's technical properties are confirmed by the product data sheet and test reports. At TECHNOFORMA quality control runs at every production stage, and installation is carried out by our own installation team, so installers' comments can be taken into account straight away.

FAQ

Can facade decor be ordered to my own sketch?

Yes. Working drawings, a master model and a mould are made from the sketch, then a sample for approval and the series.

What should I send for a custom decor quote?

Facade drawings or photos with dimensions, element sketches, quantities, substrate details and colour and texture requirements.

Why is custom decor more expensive than catalogue decor?

Because of the master model and mould costs. In a large series their share in the price of each product falls.

How long does made-to-order decor take?

It depends on complexity, the number of element types, volume and production workload; the lead time is determined after reviewing the documentation.

What is a master model?

A full-size reference piece of the element from which the mould for serial production is taken.
