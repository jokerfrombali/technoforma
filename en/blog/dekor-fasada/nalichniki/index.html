Facade Window Surrounds and Window Framing

Facade window surrounds are profiled frames around window openings on the outside of a building. They cover the junction between wall and reveal, frame the window and, together with a pediment and a sill element, turn the opening into a finished architectural detail. On multi-storey buildings it is usually the window surrounds that decide whether a facade looks classical or merely rendered.

The main selection criterion is compatibility with the facade system and the windows: the surround must fit the window junction detail, must not obstruct casements and sills and must not create a water trap. Shape and material are chosen after that.

This article is for architects, general contractors and home owners deciding what to make exterior window surrounds from and how to install them. The overall picture is in the section Architectural Facade Details.

What Window Surrounds Do

Window framing on a facade solves several tasks at once.

  • Compositional. The surround highlights the opening, sets the rhythm of windows on the wall and helps build a hierarchy of storeys: on the principal floor the surrounds are richer, on the upper floors simpler.
  • Scale. A wide frame visually enlarges the window, a narrow one makes it more austere. This allows the proportions of openings dictated by floor plans to be adjusted.
  • Protective. A projecting surround, and especially the pediment above it, sheds water away from the head reveal and the window unit.
  • Technical. The framing conceals the window installation joint, the junction of insulation and reveal and any irregularities at the edge of the opening.

Window surround decor is designed together with the window detail, not applied on top of a finished facade. If windows and sills are installed first and surrounds are chosen afterwards, the profile has to be trimmed or moved away from the frame, and the surround loses its proportions.

Types of Window Surrounds

Exterior window surrounds are classified by shape and by their components.

Archivoltprofiled framing of an arched openingarched windows and entrances

What to make facade window surrounds from depends on the system: on a rendered facade, run-in-place render mouldings, GFRC or stone units are used; on a ventilated rainscreen facade, GFRC, stone or special profiles fixed to the substructure. GFRC surrounds are cast in bespoke moulds, so the profile can follow the architect's drawing — from a simple ledge to a multi-stepped ornamented frame.

The viewing distance is taken into account when choosing a type. Windows on the first two floors are seen close up, which justifies fine profiles, panels and crossettes. On upper floors the same details merge, so frames are made bolder and simpler, and the accent shifts to the pediment and cornice. A single facade usually uses two or three types of framing, separated by tier, rather than different frames on adjacent windows.

Window Pediments and Sill Elements

A full window surround consists of more than just the frame.

  • Pediment (hood) — a small projecting cornice above the window: straight, triangular or segmental. It sheds water and visually caps the opening.
  • Frieze — a plain or ornamented field between the frame and the pediment.
  • Sill element — a ledge or profiled sill below the window, often with brackets; sometimes a balustrade or panel is placed beneath it.
  • Brackets (corbels) — support the pediment or sill ledge visually and sometimes structurally.

Pediments and sill elements are the parts of the surround most exposed to water. Their top surfaces are sloped away from the wall, and a drip is formed along the lower edge so that water does not run onto the wall. The sill element does not replace the window sill flashing: the metal flashing remains part of the window detail, and the decorative ledge sits below it or over it with managed drainage. Pediments are covered in detail in a separate article in this section.

Installation Around Windows

Installation of window surrounds is coordinated with the windows at the working drawings stage.

  1. Sequence. Usually the windows are installed first and the junction detail completed (vapour barrier, installation foam, breathable tape), then insulation and cladding, and only then the surrounds. If a surround runs under the sill flashing or onto the frame, the order is changed per the design.
  2. Gaps. A joint is left between the frame and the window unit and filled with sealant: the window and the facade move differently.
  3. Casements. Check that the profile does not obstruct opening of casements or cover the frame's drainage slots.
  4. Fixing. GFRC units are anchored to the load-bearing wall through embedded fixings or to the rainscreen substructure without blocking the ventilation cavity.
  5. Head and sill. Above the window — a pediment with a fall and a drip; below the window — a flashing and a sill element with managed drainage.

At TECHNOFORMA Group, details are designed by the in-house design department and installation is carried out by the in-house installation division, which allows windows, insulation and decor to be coordinated in one design.

How do you work out the width and projection of a window surround on a facade?

The width of the frame is set by the dimensions of the opening and the pier, and the projection by the depth to which the window is recessed in the wall and by the thickness of adjacent elements. There are no rigid rules here, but there are stable proportions proven by classical architecture; they are given below as guidance for sketch design.

ParameterGuidelineComment
Frame widthgenerally from one sixth to one eighth of the opening widthrelatively wider on narrow windows, narrower on wide ones
Projection of the frame from the wall planeusually 30–80 mmno greater than the projection of the band the frame meets
Height of the pedimentroughly one to one and a half frame widthstaller on the principal floor
Projection of the pediment40–100 mm more than that of the framewith a drip along the lower edge
Gap between the frames of adjacent windowsno less than the frame widthotherwise the surrounds merge into a single strip

On a multi-storey building these proportions are adjusted for height: from about the sixth or seventh floor the frame width is usually increased by 15–30 %, and the number of members is reduced. If the floor plans leave a narrow pier, the frame is replaced by a shared surround for two windows or by a vertical recess uniting the openings of several floors.

The depth at which the window is set also affects the composition. With a window recessed by the thickness of the insulation, the reveal is deep and the surround reads as a portal; with a window in the plane of the facade the frame needs a greater projection of its own, otherwise there will be no shadow. Ways of handling the opening as a whole are discussed in the article on window decoration on the facade.

Fixing details for window surrounds on different facade systems

The fixing method is determined not by the surround itself but by what lies behind it: a load-bearing wall, a layer of insulation or a subframe. The general principle is that the load from the unit is transferred to the load-bearing substrate by mechanical fixings, while adhesive and sealant play a supplementary role.

Facade systemHow the surround is fixedFeatures of the detail
Rendered facade over insulationadhesive bed and anchors through the insulation into the wallanchor length is calculated to allow for the insulation thickness, and the penetration is sealed
Ventilated facadebrackets or subframe profiles, cast-in inserts in the unitthe ventilation gap is maintained, and a fire-stopping box is provided around the opening as designed
Stone or brick claddinganchors into the load-bearing layer, support anglesthe difference in thermal movement between the materials is taken into account
Uninsulated wall: in-situ concrete, brickanchors and adhesivethe strength of the substrate at the edge of the opening is checked

At the edge of an opening an anchor must not be placed too close to the face of the wall: the edge of the masonry or concrete may spall. Minimum distances are taken from the technical documentation for the anchor, and on existing buildings load-bearing capacity is confirmed by pull-out tests.

Thermal performance deserves separate attention. Metal brackets passing through the insulation next to a window add thermal bridges in the facade, so their number and cross-section are optimised by calculation. Typical junction solutions are described in the article on ventilated facade junction details.

Arched, corner and bay windows: how non-standard surrounds are handled

Non-standard openings are framed with the same profiles as ordinary ones, but using special pieces: curved, corner and transition units. The aim is to keep the section of the frame unchanged so that the facade is perceived as a whole.

  • Arched windows. The archivolt is assembled from several curved segments; the joints are arranged symmetrically about the centre line, and a keystone is often placed at the crown to cover the central joint.
  • Corner windows and bays. The frame is returned at the corner with a separate corner piece; where the glazing has no mullion, the pediment and the sill band run continuously around the corner.
  • Paired and ribbon windows. The openings are united by a shared frame with an intermediate pilaster strip, so that the facade is not broken up by small surrounds.
  • Windows with a balcony door. The side parts of the frame are of different heights; the bottom of the balcony door is usually left without a profile and resolved with a threshold.
  • Glazed shopfronts on the lower floors. Instead of a window surround, a portal surround with a larger section and its own plinth is used.

Curved pieces need a separate mould for each radius, so the number of different arches on a facade is normally limited to two or three.

How many surround sizes does a multi-storey building need?

A typical residential block usually needs three to five window types and, accordingly, three to five sets of surrounds. The fewer the sizes, the fewer the moulds, the simpler the kitting and the faster the installation.

A surround set consists of repeating parts: side jambs, head, corner pieces, pediment and sill band. Jambs of different heights are often produced from one mould with an insert that alters the length of the casting; tooling is covered in more detail in the article on moulds for GFRC. It is therefore useful to reduce window heights to a limited range as early as the floor-plan stage.

The other side of standardisation is tolerances. The actual dimensions of openings in an in-situ concrete or brick building differ from the design ones, as a rule, by up to 10–20 mm. The surround is not adjusted to each opening: deviations are absorbed by the width of the joint at the window unit and by adjustment of the fixings. Before a series is launched, several sections are surveyed and the dimensions of the units are corrected where necessary.

Colour and texture of surrounds: matching the wall or contrasting with it?

The choice between a tonal and a contrasting surround depends on what is meant to create the pattern of the facade: shadow or colour. A surround matching the wall looks calmer and needs a deeper profile; a contrasting one reads from a distance even with a small projection, but it highlights any inaccuracy in geometry and joints.

On light facades the surrounds are usually one or two tones lighter or darker than the wall. White frames on a dark wall give the strongest contrast, but streaks beneath pediments and sill bands show up on them sooner, so the requirements for drips and water-repellent treatment are higher here. Texture is chosen in relation to the wall material: next to smooth porcelain stoneware a fine-grained surface is appropriate, and next to stone or render an imitation of sawn limestone or sandstone.

Units coloured through the mix keep their colour when slightly chipped and need no repainting, but the shade of the batch must be approved on a sample before the series begins. With surface painting the colour is easier to adjust on site, although the coating will have to be renewed in service, usually when the whole facade is repainted.

What to prepare for an estimate of window surrounds

Elevations and a schedule of openings are enough to assess the scope and content of the work; details and setting-out are developed later in the working documentation.

  • elevations with levels and grid lines;
  • a schedule of window openings: types, dimensions, numbers per floor;
  • the window installation detail: depth of setting, flashing, type of reveal;
  • the external wall build-up and the type of facade system;
  • a sketch or photograph of the desired surround, with colour and texture requirements;
  • the number of storeys and information on facade access.

From this information the designer determines the number of types, draws up a schedule and suggests where a bespoke profile can be replaced by a standard one. Ready-made solutions and section options are collected on the page for GFRC window surrounds.

Typical mistakes in framing window openings

Most defects are related not to the material but to a lack of coordination between the window detail and the decoration. The mistakes listed below are the most common.

  • The frame covers the drainage holes of the window profile or obstructs the fitting of insect screens and external grilles.
  • A pediment without a drip or a fall: water runs down the wall and leaves streaks beneath the window.
  • A joint between pieces on the window centre line at the head, not covered by a keystone, so that the joint is in the most visible place.
  • A rigid joint between the surround and the window frame: under thermal movement the joint opens and lets water in.
  • The same small profile on every floor of a tall building.
  • Fixing with adhesive only, without anchors.
  • A sill band installed above the flashing: water from the flashing gets behind the decoration.

The check is simple: the window detail drawing should show the window unit, flashing, insulation, cladding and the surround with its fixings all together. If the decoration is shown only on the elevation and is absent from the details, the problems will appear during installation.

FAQ

What should exterior window surrounds be made of?

For multi-storey buildings, GFRC or stone; on rendered facades, also run render mouldings. Foam decor is restricted by fire safety requirements.

When are window surrounds installed — before or after the windows?

Usually after the windows, insulation and cladding, but the sequence is determined by the junction detail in the design.

Can GFRC window surrounds be installed on a ventilated facade?

Yes, they are fixed to the substructure or to the load-bearing wall through the insulation, keeping the ventilation cavity clear.

Is a sill flashing needed if there is a decorative sill element?

Yes. The metal flashing is part of the window detail; a decorative ledge does not replace it.

What does a full window surround include?

A frame (architrave), a pediment above the window, sometimes a frieze, a sill element and brackets.

How wide should a window surround on a facade be?

As a rule, the frame is between one sixth and one eighth of the opening width. On the upper floors of tall buildings it is increased so that the surround can be read from the ground; the exact dimension is checked on the facade elevation.

Can surrounds be installed when windows are replaced in an existing building?

Yes. The surround is designed for the new window detail, taking account of the setting depth of the unit, the flashing and the condition of the reveals. Before installation the substrate at the edge of the opening and the load-bearing capacity of the anchors are checked.

How many moulds are needed for the window surrounds of a residential building?

That depends on the number of window types and the make-up of the surround. A block usually requires three to five sets, and jambs of different heights are often cast in one mould with an insert, which reduces the amount of tooling.
