Plaster Mouldings or GFRC: Exterior and Interior

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

Plaster mouldings or GFRC — the choice depends primarily on where the element is installed. Gypsum plaster is the classic material for interior mouldings: it is workable, reproduces fine detail accurately and performs well in dry heated rooms. Outdoors, plaster does not work: it absorbs water, loses strength and breaks down when frozen.

GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) suits both facades and interiors. It is a cement-based material with alkali-resistant glass fibre — strong, moisture-resistant and frost-resistant, and, according to TECHNOFORMA Group, non-combustible (NG class under Russian standards). If decoration is needed outside or in a wet room, the choice is GFRC; for ceiling mouldings in a living room, plaster remains a sensible option.

For a general overview of GFRC, see GFRC: material and technology.

Why plaster is not for exteriors

Plaster is an excellent material for interiors, but not for facades. The reasons:

  • Solubility and water absorption. Set gypsum partially dissolves in water and absorbs it readily. In rain the surface washes out and details lose their sharpness.
  • Loss of strength when wet. Wet plaster is much weaker than dry.
  • Poor frost resistance. Water in the pores expands on freezing and destroys the material: cracks, delamination and crumbling appear.
  • Brittleness. Without reinforcement, plaster elements easily crack from impacts and vibration.
  • Metal corrosion. Gypsum in contact with moisture promotes corrosion of steel embeds and fixings.

Protective coatings and water repellents only postpone the problem: any damage to the layer lets water into the plaster. That is why facade mouldings were historically made of stone, lime mortars and concrete, and today of GFRC, less often of polymer materials.

GFRC in interiors

GFRC is used in interiors where plaster is not enough:

  • Large elements. Columns, pilasters, portals and arches in halls and lobbies where strength and impact resistance are needed.
  • Wet rooms. Swimming pools, spa areas, hammams, washrooms in public buildings. Here plaster quickly loses its properties, while GFRC is unaffected by moisture.
  • Entrance areas and transition zones with changes in temperature and humidity.
  • Areas with mechanical loads. Wall panels in corridors, stair halls and public spaces.
  • Decoration with a concrete or stone texture. Architectural concrete, relief wall panels, fireplace surrounds.

For ceiling mouldings, thin cornices and rosettes in dry rooms, plaster is usually simpler and cheaper: it is lighter, easy to finish on site and fills well. A combination with the facade and hall in GFRC and the ceilings in plaster is common.

Weight and installation

The weight of products depends on their design. Plaster mouldings are usually solid or have small cavities and are installed with gypsum adhesive or filler; large elements are additionally fixed with screws and studs. For ceilings, the weight of plaster cornices and rosettes is an important factor: heavy elements need a reliable substrate.

GFRC products are thin-walled (usually 10–20 mm), and three-dimensional elements are hollow. So a GFRC column or portal, despite the strong material, is moderately heavy. Installation:

  • in interiors — with adhesives and mechanical fixings to the wall or slab; large elements on embeds and brackets;
  • on facades — on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure or on anchor fixings with load calculations.

Texture and colour

Plaster gives a very smooth white surface and reproduces the finest ornamental detail, which is why restorers and interior decorators love it. It is painted with any interior paints after priming; joints are filled with gypsum filler and become invisible.

GFRC offers more texture options:

  • smooth surface for painting;
  • concrete texture with natural pores;
  • stone look — travertine, limestone, sandstone;
  • acid-etched or sandblasted surface with exposed aggregate;
  • relief built into the mould.

GFRC colour is set by integral pigmentation using pigments on white or grey cement, or by painting with vapour-permeable coatings. Integrally pigmented products do not need repainting, and chips do not reveal a different colour. In interiors it is important to provide a protective anti-soiling sealer, especially where the surface is frequently touched.

How to choose

A simple selection guide:

  1. Outdoors, facade, veranda, street furniture — GFRC only (or stone); plaster is ruled out.
  2. Wet rooms — pools, spas, washrooms in public buildings — GFRC.
  3. Large elements in public interiors — columns, portals, lobby cladding — GFRC for its strength and impact resistance.
  4. Ceiling mouldings and small decoration in dry living rooms — plaster or polyurethane.
  5. A unified style for facade and interior — one profile can be made in different materials from the same model: GFRC outside, plaster inside.

If facade mouldings are needed to a historical pattern, a model and mould are made from measurements or photos, and the product is then cast in GFRC.

Consider the cost of ownership too. Plaster outdoors will need constant repair and eventually replacement, and in a wet interior it will need restoring after leaks and condensation. GFRC costs more than plaster to buy but needs no regular repair in the same conditions. In dry living rooms, on the other hand, there is usually no point paying extra for GFRC.

When ordering GFRC products for interiors, agree the texture and colour on a sample with the manufacturer in advance, as well as the fixing method for the specific substrate (brick, concrete, plasterboard on studs) and the protective sealer. Heavy elements on partitions may require the substrate to be reinforced.

FAQ

What are facade mouldings made of?

Today facade mouldings are most often made of GFRC, less often of polymer materials or natural stone. Gypsum plaster is not suitable for outdoor use.

Can plaster mouldings be used on a facade?

No: gypsum absorbs water, loses strength when wet and breaks down when frozen. Protective coatings only delay the deterioration.

Is GFRC suitable for interiors?

Yes, especially for large elements — columns, portals, wall panels — and for wet rooms such as pools and spa areas.

Which is heavier — plaster mouldings or GFRC?

It depends on the design. GFRC products are thin-walled and hollow, so large elements can weigh about the same as plaster ones or less, while being stronger.

Which facade mouldings last longest?

Among available materials, natural stone and GFRC are the most durable; GFRC is lighter and allows complex profiles to be repeated serially.
