GFRC Composition: Cement, Sand and Alkali-Resistant Fibre

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

Glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) consists of four basic components: cement, fine sand, alkali-resistant glass fibre and water. Plasticisers and modifying admixtures are added to control workability, the rate of strength gain and frost resistance.

The key component is the fibre: it must be alkali-resistant, otherwise the cement environment gradually destroys it and the element loses flexural strength. So when choosing a supplier, what matters is not what goes into the mix but which materials the manufacturer uses, in what proportions, and how it controls them.

This article is for architects, technical supervision engineers and clients comparing GFRC manufacturers' offers. A general overview is in GFRC: material and technology.

Mix components

According to TECHNOFORMA Group, its GFRC is made from four components. The role of each:

ComponentRole in the materialWhat to look for
High-grade cementBinder, forms the matrix and compressive strengthGrade and batch consistency; white cement is used for light and integrally coloured elements
SandAggregate, reduces shrinkage, forms the surface structureFine grading, free of clay and silt particles
Alkali-resistant glass fibreDispersed reinforcement, bending and tension, crack resistanceFibre type (zirconia AR glass), length and proportion by weight
WaterCement hydration, mix workabilityLow water-cement ratio; excess water reduces strength

Unlike ordinary concrete, GFRC contains no coarse aggregate — crushed stone or gravel. This makes thin walls possible and allows the mould relief to be reproduced sharply.

Why alkali-resistant glass fibre is essential

Hardened cement paste is a strongly alkaline environment. Ordinary glass (E-glass) corrodes in it over time: the fibres lose strength and the element gradually becomes brittle. That is why GFRC uses alkali-resistant glass fibre (AR glass) containing zirconium dioxide. It is this fibre that distinguishes GFRC from any mix to which generic "glass fibre for concrete" has simply been added.

There is no universal answer to what GFRC is made of and in what proportions. In industry practice the fibre content is usually a few percent of the mix by weight (roughly 3–5% for spray-up and less for vibration casting), the cement-to-sand ratio is close to 1:1, and the water-cement ratio is low, around 0.3–0.4. The specific mix design depends on the forming method, the required properties and the manufacturer's materials; exact values are set in the process specification and confirmed by test reports.

The fibre comes as roving (a continuous strand chopped by the spray gun) or as ready-chopped fibre for premix. Fibre length is usually measured in centimetres and chosen to suit the production method.

Admixtures and plasticisers

Admixtures do not replace the main components; they adjust the properties of the mix and the finished element:

  • Plasticisers and superplasticisers reduce the water content without loss of workability. Less water means higher density and strength and lower water absorption.
  • Polymer admixtures (e.g. acrylic dispersions) improve curing conditions for thin walls, increase crack resistance and reduce water absorption.
  • Frost-resistance admixtures — according to TECHNOFORMA, the frost resistance of GFRC is improved with admixtures; this is especially important in climates with frequent freeze-thaw cycles.
  • Pigments for integral colouring; added in strictly controlled doses so as not to reduce strength.
  • Pozzolanic additives (e.g. metakaolin, silica fume) bind part of the free lime and lower alkalinity, giving the fibre additional protection.

The set and dosage of admixtures is part of the manufacturer's mix design, so it is worth discussing when agreeing the elements.

How composition affects strength

The strength of GFRC is determined not by one component but by their balance:

  • Fibre content and distribution are the main factors in flexural strength. Too little fibre reduces crack resistance; too much impairs placing and can cause balling.
  • Water-cement ratio — the lower it is while keeping workability, the denser and stronger the matrix.
  • Sand quality — dirty or overly coarse sand impairs bond with the fibre and the element's surface.
  • Compaction — in spray-up, layers are rolled; in vibration casting, the mix is compacted by vibration; air voids reduce strength.
  • Curing regime — thin elements lose moisture quickly, and without humidity control they do not reach full strength.

For comparison: in natural materials, properties are set by mineral composition. Shell limestone, for instance, is a limestone made largely of calcite from shells, and its strength depends on the porosity of the particular quarry. With GFRC, strength can be engineered through the mix design. In a ventilated rainscreen facade the GFRC panel is only the cladding layer; the system also includes the substructure, fixings, insulation and wind barrier, and the strength of the whole assembly is determined by the design of its connections.

What to ask the manufacturer about the mix design

Before ordering, it is worth asking the manufacturer a few specific questions:

  1. What glass fibre is used — alkali-resistant AR fibre, who makes it, and is there documentation for it?
  2. What is the fibre content by weight and what forming method is used (spray-up, vibration casting, premix)?
  3. What cement and sand are used, and how is batch consistency controlled?
  4. What admixtures are added for frost resistance and lower water absorption?
  5. Are control samples made from each batch, and are there test reports for flexural strength, frost resistance and water absorption?
  6. How is curing of thin-walled elements organised?

FAQ

What is GFRC made of?

Cement, fine sand, alkali-resistant glass fibre and water, plus plasticisers and modifying admixtures. GFRC contains no coarse aggregate (crushed stone).

What are typical GFRC mix proportions?

The cement-to-sand ratio is usually close to 1:1, fibre makes up a few percent of the mix by weight, and the water-cement ratio is low. Exact proportions depend on the technology and are set in the manufacturer's specification.

Can ordinary glass fibre be used in concrete?

Not for durable elements. Ordinary glass breaks down in the alkaline cement environment; GFRC uses alkali-resistant fibre containing zirconium dioxide.

Why are plasticisers added to GFRC?

They reduce the water content while keeping the mix workable, which increases density and strength and lowers water absorption.

What does a ventilated rainscreen facade with GFRC panels consist of?

Substructure brackets and rails, fixings, insulation, a wind barrier membrane where required, and GFRC cladding panels installed with a ventilated cavity.
