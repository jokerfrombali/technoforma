GFRC Street Furniture and Small Architectural Forms

Small architectural forms made of glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) are planters, plant pots, benches, parapets, railings, litter bins, fountains, decorative objects and other landscaping elements cast from a cement mix reinforced with glass fibre. The material combines the look of concrete or stone with a thin wall and low weight, so a large object can be delivered and moved without heavy machinery.

The main selection criterion is purpose and load: a decorative vase and a bench in a residential courtyard work under different conditions, and the design of the element (thickness, ribs, cast-in inserts, fixing to the base) must match actual use. The second criterion is the number of identical items: a series pays for the mould, while a one-off object to a custom sketch costs more.

Such elements are ordered by developers, property management companies, landscape architects and private owners. Material basics are in GFRC: material and technology.

What landscape elements are made of GFRC

Almost any outdoor form can be made of GFRC as long as it fits the logic of "thin skin + ribs + cast-in inserts". The main groups:

  • Planting — planters, plant pots, tree grilles, edging for flower beds and raised beds.
  • Seating — benches, seats, amphitheatres, cube seats, tables.
  • Railings and boundaries — parapets, balustrades, fence posts and caps, decorative walls.
  • Small structures — cladding for gazebos and canopies, fountains, basins, decorative arches, steles and wayfinding signs.
  • Décor and art objects — sculptures, abstract forms, playground elements.

Architectural GFRC makes it possible to design landscape elements in the same style as the building facade: the profiles, texture and colour of cornices and window surrounds carry over to planters, parapets and fence caps. This makes landscaping part of the architecture rather than a set of catalogue products.

Planters and plant pots

GFRC planters are the most common item. Compared with ordinary concrete, the thin wall reduces the element's weight several times over, so a large planter can be placed on a roof, terrace or podium where the floor load is limited.

What to look out for when choosing:

  • Drainage — drainage holes in the base and a drainage layer are essential; otherwise water in the soil loads the walls from the inside when it freezes.
  • Wall shape — walls that flare outwards at the top cope better with the expansion of frozen soil than tapering ones.
  • Internal protection — a waterproofing coating or liner reduces moisture saturation of the walls and efflorescence.
  • Stability — tall narrow planters are fixed to the base or given a weighted bottom.
  • Size and weight with soil — the floor load is calculated including wet soil and plants, not just the element itself.

Plant pots and tree surrounds are made modular: long flower beds and green dividers are assembled from identical elements, which reduces the number of moulds.

Benches and railings

Benches and railings are subject to mechanical loads, so they are designed differently from decorative vases. A GFRC bench is usually a hollow volume with internal ribs or a cast-in steel frame to which timber slats can be attached. The seat is designed for the weight of several people and dynamic loads, and the element is anchored to the base so it cannot be moved or tipped over.

Railings, balustrades and parapets resist horizontal loads from people leaning on the handrail. Where a railing has a protective function (level changes, stairs, terraces), load-bearing capacity is provided by a metal frame or cast-in inserts, with GFRC acting as cladding. Height and load requirements for railings are set by current Russian building safety codes (SP) and must be taken into account in the design.

Landscaping for residential complexes

In residential complex landscaping, GFRC elements help to solve several tasks: zoning the courtyard, planting on the podium, creating seating areas and linking the courtyard to the facade architecture. The trend towards "wellbeing architecture" — an environment that supports residents' health and comfort — means more greenery, shade, places to meet and quiet spots to relax. GFRC fits this as a material for large raised planters, amphitheatres, benches of complex shape and pergolas.

Practical recommendations for developers:

  1. Include landscape elements in the concept together with the facade to use shared profiles and colours.
  2. Choose modular solutions: two or three moulds assembled into different compositions.
  3. Consider snow clearing and lawn maintenance — elements should not have narrow inaccessible spots.
  4. Protect the surface with a water repellent and anti-graffiti coating where relevant.

At TECHNOFORMA, landscape elements are designed and made in the same cycle as facade products: design department, own mould shops, production with quality control at every stage, and delivery.

Resistance to loads

The outdoor durability of GFRC elements depends on three factors: mix design, construction and maintenance. Glass fibre takes tensile stresses, so the element does not shatter on impact the way ordinary unreinforced concrete of the same thickness would; but a thin wall is still vulnerable to point impacts from heavy objects, so thickness and ribs are increased in heavily used areas.

According to the manufacturer, frost resistance is high and is improved with admixtures; for outdoor elements it is especially important that water does not stand in bowls, on horizontal surfaces or in cavities. Water-repellent treatment reduces water absorption and soiling. Specific strength, frost resistance and water absorption values depend on the mix design and are stated in the product data sheet and test reports.

The material does not burn: according to the company, GFRC is non-combustible (Russian class NG), which matters for elements next to buildings and on roofs.

How to order GFRC landscape elements: input data and stages

To manufacture landscape elements for a particular courtyard, terrace or embankment, the producer needs a landscaping layout, sketches of the items with overall dimensions and information on the base they will stand on. The more precise the input data, the fewer corrections are needed at the model and mould stage and the sooner batch production begins.

A brief usually includes:

  • a site plan showing the position of each item and the paving levels;
  • sketches or 3D models with dimensions, radii and the desired texture;
  • the permissible load on the roof, podium or car park slab if the items do not stand on the ground;
  • the number of identical elements and the acceptable differences between them;
  • colour requirements: a facade sample, a catalogue reference or a physical master sample;
  • delivery and unloading conditions — width of access roads, availability of a crane or forklift.

The work then follows a clear sequence: working drawings with embedded parts and stiffening ribs, a master model and mould, a control sample of texture and colour, batch production, marking and packing. It is sensible to entrust the structural part to the same specialists who carry out facade design: the shell calculation and fixing details of facade decor and street furniture are similar. Which tooling suits one-off and serial items is explained in the article on moulds for GFRC.

Installation and fixing: what planters, benches and parapets rest on

A GFRC item is a thin shell, so it must rest on a level, rigid base over its whole bearing surface or on the bearing ribs provided. Point support on uneven paving or crushed stone creates local stresses and eventually leads to cracks.

BaseHow it is installedWhat to check
Soil, lawnOn a compacted sand and crushed stone bed or a concrete slabDrainage away from the foot, absence of settlement
Paving, slabsOn levelled paving using pads or a mortar bedFall, alignment of drainage holes with the outlet
Trafficable roof, podiumOn load-spreading pads over the protective layerIntegrity of the waterproofing, load on the slab
Concrete parapet, wallOn anchors through embedded partsLoad-bearing capacity of the base, sealing of the joint

Benches and litter bins in public areas are, as a rule, anchored to the base to prevent sliding and overturning. Large planters are held in place by their own mass together with the soil, but on roofs their position is coordinated with the structural grid of the building. The load calculation takes into account not only the shell but also wet substrate, the drainage layer and the plant itself; the procedure is set out in the article on how much GFRC weighs.

Guarding elements are installed in a way close to facade practice: an embedded part in the item, an anchor in the base, a sealed joint between sections. Balustrades and balusters on terraces and stairs are assembled in the same manner. Installation is carried out after paving and finishing works are complete, so that the face surface is neither soiled nor damaged.

How do GFRC landscape elements differ from concrete, granite and polymer ones?

GFRC occupies an intermediate position: in appearance and durability it is close to concrete and stone, while in mass and freedom of form it is closer to composites. The choice depends on what matters most on the project: weight, vandal resistance, plasticity of form or repeatability of a series.

MaterialStrengthsLimitations
Ordinary reinforced concreteMassiveness, stability, simple shapesLarge mass, thick wall, machinery needed for relocation
Natural graniteWear resistance, prestigious appearanceHigh mass, complex shapes are labour-intensive
Polymer composites and plasticMinimal weight, bright coloursCombustibility, ageing in sunlight, a 'non-architectural' look
Metal and timberSlender sections, a surface pleasant to the touchCorrosion or rot without regular maintenance
GFRCComplex form, low mass, non-combustibility, a single style with the facadeA bespoke mould requires time and a production run

In practice the materials are combined. The body of a bench is made of GFRC and the seat of thermally modified timber; planters are complemented with metal trays and liners. The shade of the bodies is agreed on samples, and the ways of obtaining colour are described in detail in the article on integral colouring and coatings.

Typical mistakes in selecting and installing outdoor items

Most defects in street furniture and planters arise not from the material but from decisions taken before manufacture. Below are the mistakes encountered most often.

  • No data on the slab load. Planters are ordered from a picture, and on site it turns out that the roof is not designed for the mass of soil and plants.
  • Drainage is not connected to the water outlet. Water from the planter runs onto the paving, leaves streaks and forms ice in winter.
  • The inner surface is not protected. Constantly wet soil and fertilisers accelerate efflorescence on the face side.
  • Sharp edges and thin projections in walkways. They chip under trolleys and cleaning machinery, so edges should be rounded.
  • Too many unique types. Each new mould increases the lead time and the budget, although the courtyard gains little visually.
  • Installation before finishing is complete. The items are soiled with mortar and caught by site machinery.

Some of the risks are removed at concept stage: it is enough to show the producer the landscaping layout before the design is approved and to receive comments on the structure, the division into parts and the bearing details.

Seasonal care and minor repairs

Care of GFRC items comes down to washing, checking the drainage and renewing the protective impregnation. The material does not require complex maintenance, but regular inspections noticeably extend the service life.

  1. In spring the surfaces are inspected, drainage holes are cleared and residues of de-icing agents are washed off.
  2. In summer the items are washed with water at moderate pressure and a neutral detergent; acidic products are not used.
  3. In autumn leaves and standing water are removed from bowls and horizontal ledges, and fountains are drained.
  4. Every few years, depending on the condition of the surface, the water repellent or protective coating is renewed.

Chips and small cracks are repaired with a compound on the same cement base, matched in colour. How visible the repair is depends on the texture: on a rough surface it is hardly noticeable, while a smooth one requires careful finishing. Graffiti is easier to remove from surfaces treated with a protective compound in advance, so for public spaces such treatment is specified from the outset.

Which landscape elements suit different types of project

The set of items and the requirements for them depend on the type of project: in the courtyard of a residential complex resistance to everyday wear matters, at a business centre a prestigious entrance area, on an embankment resistance to moisture and wind. For this reason the same planter is designed for different sites with a different wall thickness, protection and method of fixing.

Type of projectTypical itemsWhat to pay attention to
Residential complexModular planters, benches, edging elementsWear resistance, safe edges, ease of repair
Business centre, retail buildingLarge planters at entrances, parapets, wayfinding stelesThe same colour and texture as the facade, protection against vehicle impact
Hotel, resortVases, terrace balustrades, fountain bowlsResistance to moisture and salts, decorative detailing
Park, embankment, public spaceAmphitheatre benches, guarding, art objectsAnti-vandal treatment, anchoring, simple replacement of sections
Private housePlanters, pier caps, garden decorA style matching the house, ease of relocation

For projects in southern and coastal regions, solar exposure and salt mist are additionally taken into account: light shades with integral pigment are chosen and the water-repellent protection is renewed more often. In areas with a long winter the main concern is draining water from bowls and cavities before the frosts set in. In both cases the solution is built into the design of the item rather than corrected on site.

What determines the cost of GFRC landscape elements

The budget is determined primarily by tooling and the production run rather than by the mass of the item. A mould for one planter costs the same as a mould for fifty, so the unit price in a series is considerably lower than that of a one-off piece.

FAQ

What small architectural forms are made of GFRC?

Planters, plant pots, benches, parapets, balustrades, post caps, fountains, steles and art objects.

Do GFRC planters survive winter?

With the right design, yes: they need drainage, walls flaring outwards at the top and protection of the inner surface.

How strong are GFRC benches?

Benches are designed with ribs or a cast-in frame and anchored to the base; strength depends on the design and mix.

What is architectural GFRC?

GFRC used to make décor and forms for architecture: facade elements, landscape elements, columns, cornices.

Can landscape elements match the facade style?

Yes: they use the same profiles, texture and colour as the architectural GFRC décor on the building.

Can GFRC planters be moved after installation?

Yes, provided the item is not anchored. An empty medium-sized planter is usually moved by hand or on a trolley, a large one with a forklift or crane using the lifting points provided. Before relocation it is advisable to remove part of the soil so as not to load the walls and the bottom.

Is there a minimum quantity for bespoke landscape elements?

Technically a single item can be made, but it then bears the full cost of the model and mould. A series becomes economically justified when one mould is used many times, which is why landscaping projects tend to limit themselves to two or three types and assemble different compositions from them.

Can lighting or automatic irrigation be built into an item?

Yes, if this is provided for before manufacture. Channels and recesses for cables, luminaires and irrigation pipes, as well as embedded parts for fixing them, are formed in the shell in advance. Drilling a finished thin-walled item on site is undesirable, as the face surface is easily damaged.
