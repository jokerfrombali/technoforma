Pros and Cons of GFRC for Facades

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

The main advantages of glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) for facades are freedom of form, low weight with a thin wall, non-combustibility and weather resistance. The main disadvantages are the strong dependence of quality on the manufacturer, the cost of custom moulds in small runs and demanding design of fixing details.

In other words, the material itself rarely fails; the mix design, technology and installation do. So the decision "GFRC or not" almost always comes down to two questions: does the architecture justify the mould costs, and can the manufacturer be trusted.

This review is useful for architects and developers choosing cladding for a residential complex or public building, and for private clients with a classical facade design. A general overview is in GFRC: material and technology.

Advantages

The advantages of GFRC show above all where the facade is rich in architectural detail:

  • Freedom of form. Panels of any curvature, complex cornice profiles, columns, capitals, reliefs and ornaments. The shape is defined by the model, not by the limits of formwork and reinforcement.
  • Low weight. Thanks to fibre reinforcement, the element wall is usually 10–20 mm. Hollow columns and cornices weigh many times less than solid equivalents, reducing the load on the substructure, anchors and wall.
  • Non-combustibility. According to TECHNOFORMA Group, its GFRC is classed as non-combustible (NG). This matters for multi-storey housing and public buildings.
  • Frost and weather resistance. The mineral material is unaffected by UV, moisture and temperature swings; according to the company, frost resistance is improved by admixtures.
  • No corrosion. GFRC has no steel rebar that could rust if the cover were damaged.
  • Wide range of textures and colours. Smooth surface, stone effect, acid etching, integral colour.
  • One material for the whole facade. Panels, decoration and small architectural forms in a single style and colour.
  • Serial production. One mould yields dozens or hundreds of identical elements, which pays off on residential complexes with repeating sections.

Disadvantages and risks

The disadvantages of GFRC are more often related not to the material but to how it is produced and used:

  • Dependence on technology. Ordinary glass fibre instead of alkali-resistant, the wrong fibre content, excess water or drying out in the first day reduce strength. Defects may appear months or years later.
  • Loss of flexural strength over time. This property is taken into account in design by using long-term values with a margin.
  • Mould costs. A unique element needs a model and a mould; in a run of a few pieces this noticeably raises the element price.
  • Demanding details. Thin panels are sensitive to errors in fixing design, movement joints and water shedding. A poor detail can lead to cracking.
  • Efflorescence and soiling. Like any cementitious material, untreated GFRC can show efflorescence and absorb dirt.
  • Repairs. Chips and cracks can be repaired, but matching colour and texture perfectly is harder than replacing a standard tile.
  • Not load-bearing. GFRC is cladding and decoration, not structure.

Where GFRC is not the best choice

GFRC is not a universal solution. It loses out to other materials in several situations:

  • Large flat surfaces without decoration. If the facade consists of plain planes, porcelain stoneware or fibre cement boards are usually simpler and cheaper: they are mass-produced without custom moulds.
  • One-off unique elements in small numbers. One or two non-standard elements are cheaper to make from another material or to cut from stone.
  • When a genuine natural material is needed. If the architect or client wants actual granite, travertine or marble with a natural structure, GFRC remains an imitation.
  • Abrasion loads. Steps, floors and heavily trafficked paving are jobs for stone or special concretes.
  • Interior decoration in dry rooms. For ceiling mouldings, plaster or polyurethane is lighter and cheaper; GFRC is justified where strength and moisture resistance matter.

A combination is often optimal: planes in porcelain stoneware or stone, architectural details in GFRC.

How to reduce the risks

Most risks are eliminated at the stage of choosing the contractor and designing:

  1. Check the production. In-house mould shop, quality control at every stage, sample test reports for flexural strength and frost resistance.
  2. Request fibre documentation confirming alkali resistance.
  3. Commission a ventilated facade design. Layout, fixing details, movement joints, water shedding from horizontal elements, substructure load calculations.
  4. Approve a reference sample of colour and texture before the production run.
  5. Specify water-repellent treatment of the faces — it reduces water absorption and soiling.
  6. Entrust installation to one organisation. When design, production and installation are done by one contractor, responsibility for the result is not diluted.

FAQ

What are the pros and cons of a ventilated rainscreen facade?

Pros: moisture removal through the ventilated cavity, external insulation, a wide choice of cladding, easy repair. Cons: higher cost compared with a render-on-insulation facade and the need for an accurate design of the substructure and details.

What are the pros and cons of shell limestone?

Shell limestone is light, porous, easy to work and has a recognisable texture. Its drawbacks are modest strength, high water absorption and properties that vary by quarry, so on facades it is usually protected with water repellents.

What are the pros and cons of Dagestan stone?

Dagestan limestones and dolomites are valued for their warm tones and good workability. Properties depend on the specific quarry, so density, strength and frost resistance must be checked against the batch documents. TECHNOFORMA Group has extensive experience with Dagestan stones.

What is the main disadvantage of GFRC?

The strong dependence of quality on the manufacturer: the mix design, fibre alkali resistance and curing regime determine durability, and defects do not show immediately.

Is GFRC worthwhile for a private house?

Yes, if the facade is rich in decoration — cornices, columns, surrounds: GFRC gives a stone look without a heavy load on the walls. For a simple flat facade, other cladding options are more sensible.
