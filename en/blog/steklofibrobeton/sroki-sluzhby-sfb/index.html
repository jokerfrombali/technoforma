Service Life of GFRC on Facades

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

The service life of glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) on a facade is measured in decades and is determined not by one parameter but by a combination: the quality of the alkali-resistant fibre and mix design, correct design of the element and its fixings, drainage and maintenance. International experience with GRC spans more than half a century, and products made to the technology remain in service.

The main durability factor is water: wherever it stands and freezes, any concrete deteriorates faster. So when choosing a manufacturer, assess not only sample strength but also how falls, drip edges, joints and surface protection have been designed.

This article is for clients, technical supervision and facility management organisations. Basic information on the material is in GFRC: material and technology.

What service life depends on

The service life of GFRC elements is influenced by three groups of factors:

MaterialFibre type and contentAlkali-resistant glass fibre only, content as per mix design
MaterialWater-cement ratio, admixturesTest reports for strength, frost resistance, water absorption
ElementThickness, ribs, cast-in insertsCompliance with calculation and working drawings
ElementFalls, drip edgesNo horizontal surfaces where water can stand
InstallationFixing schemeFreedom for thermal movement, corrosion-resistant fixings
ServiceClimate, environmental aggressivenessSea salt, industrial emissions, freeze cycles

Climate deserves separate attention. A southern climate with frequent freeze-thaw transitions and wet winters can be harder on a facade than steady frost. A sea coast adds chlorides, which are dangerous above all for metal inserts and fixings. All these conditions are taken into account when choosing the mix design, cover thickness and fixing materials.

Ageing and carbonation

Two main processes take place in GFRC as it ages.

Composite ageing. Cement stone continues to hydrate, and hydration products gradually fill the spaces between glass filaments within the fibre strands. As a result, the material's ductility — its ability to carry load after the first crack — decreases over time, and flexural strength approaches the limit of proportionality. This effect has long been known, so element design relies on long-term rather than initial properties, and modern mixes with modifying admixtures slow the process down.

Carbonation. Carbon dioxide in the air reacts with the calcium hydroxide in cement stone, and the alkalinity of the surface layer drops. For glass fibre this is largely neutral, but for steel inserts located in a thin carbonated layer it means a risk of corrosion. That is why inserts and fixings in GFRC are made of stainless or protected steel, and cover is specified with a margin.

Both processes are slow and predictable; they are accounted for in design rather than dealt with after installation.

The practical conclusion for clients: comparing suppliers by "28-day strength" is not enough. It is more appropriate to ask how the mix design and calculations account for the material's long-term properties, what type of fibre is used and what steel the inserts are made of.

The role of water-repellent treatment

Water-repellent (hydrophobic) treatment is an impregnation that makes surface pores water-repellent while keeping them vapour-permeable. It extends service life through three effects:

  • water saturation of the material is reduced — less freeze-thaw damage;
  • salt migration is reduced — less efflorescence and staining;
  • the surface gets less dirty and is cleaned better by rain.

The effect of the sealer diminishes over time under UV and precipitation, so it is renewed at intervals specified by the product manufacturer. Before re-treatment the facade is cleaned, and on painted elements compatibility of the sealer with the paint is checked.

Important: water-repellent treatment does not fix design errors. If water stands on the horizontal surface of a cornice or a joint lets it into the element, the sealer will only slow the deterioration. Falls, drip edges and correct joints come first, then surface protection. Some products slightly deepen the colour of the element, so they are tested on a sample before the whole facade is treated.

Field experience

Worldwide experience with GFRC dates from the 1970s; since then the material has been used on facades in different climate zones — from the UK to the Middle East. The industry's conclusions over this time:

  1. Most problems are related not to the material itself but to design: rigid fixing that ignores movement, lack of drainage, insufficient thickness at insert zones.
  2. Production quality is decisive: incorrect fibre content, excess water and poor curing show up as cracks within the first few years.
  3. Regular inspection allows local defects to be spotted and fixed before they develop.

TECHNOFORMA's first GFRC projects were handed over in 2017 — Zolotye Klyuchi residential complex and Bogatyreva residential complex in Makhachkala. The company has been working with the material for more than 10 years.

Typical signs on a facade to look out for during inspection: hairline cracks at inserts and corners, delamination of the textured face layer, efflorescence and streaks below horizontal elements, traces of fixing corrosion, failed sealant in joints. Most of them do not threaten safety if detected in time and are dealt with by local repair.

How to extend service life

The service life of a GFRC facade can be extended at every stage:

  • Design — falls on top surfaces of at least a few percent, drip edges, sills, open or properly sealed joints, freedom for thermal movement, corrosion-resistant fixings.
  • Production — consistent mix design, control of fibre content, proper curing of elements.
  • Installation — installation without overstressing, protection from damage, covering top edges before the rains begin.
  • Service — inspecting the facade at least once a year and after strong winds, cleaning drainage, renewing water-repellent treatment and sealants, local repair of chips with repair compounds.

For systematic maintenance it helps to have as-built documentation: a layout plan with element marks and fixing details. It speeds up finding and replacing a damaged element.

The facility management organisation should keep an inspection log with photographs: this makes it easier to track how a defect develops and decide whether to repair or replace an element. When an element is replaced, the new one is cast in the same mould and colour-matched, so it makes sense to keep the moulds for standard elements.

FAQ

What is the service life of GFRC?

Decades, with the right mix design, element design and maintenance; worldwide experience with the material exceeds 50 years.

What does GFRC durability depend on?

Alkali resistance and content of the fibre, mix design, element design, drainage, fixings and maintenance.

Does GFRC need water-repellent treatment?

Yes, the sealer reduces water saturation, efflorescence and soiling; it is renewed periodically.

What is GFRC ageing?

A gradual loss of composite ductility as fibre strands fill with hydration products; it is accounted for in design calculations.

How often should a GFRC facade be inspected?

At least once a year and after strong winds or severe weather.
