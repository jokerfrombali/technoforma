GRC, GFRC and SFB: Names for Glass Fibre Reinforced Concrete

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

GRC panels are facade panels made of glass fibre reinforced concrete: the abbreviation GRC (Glassfibre Reinforced Concrete) is used internationally for the same material that in Russia is called SFB (steklofibrobeton). In the USA the variant GFRC (Glass Fiber Reinforced Concrete) is more common. In all cases it means fine-grained concrete reinforced with alkali-resistant glass fibre.

When choosing GRC panels, what matters is not the name but the mix design, forming technology and test-verified properties. Different terms in catalogues and projects do not mean different materials, but foreign projects often reference their own standards, which need to be correctly translated into Russian requirements.

This article is for architects working with international references and for procurement teams. For a general overview of the material, see GFRC: material and technology.

GRG deserves a special note: it is not concrete but gypsum with glass fibre. It is not used on facades. Confusing GRC and GRG in specifications leads to procurement mistakes.

Global experience

Glass fibre reinforced concrete has been used worldwide since the 1970s, following the development of alkali-resistant glass fibre containing zirconium dioxide. Today GRC is a familiar material for:

  • Facades of public and commercial buildings — large-format panels, including on steel frames.
  • Curved architecture — parametric facades, shells, perforated screens.
  • Middle Eastern architecture — in the Middle East GRC is widely used for carved mashrabiya screens, domes, arches and ornaments.
  • Restoration and reconstruction of historic facades — lightweight copies of stone decoration.
  • Infrastructure — tunnel linings, noise barriers, permanent formwork.
  • Landscaping — street furniture, planters, benches.

Global experience shows that with the right mix design, technology and details, GRC facades perform for decades. It has also shown where the mistakes lie: early panels made with ordinary glass, unaccounted-for loss of flexural strength and rigid fixing that ignored thermal movement led to cracking. These lessons formed the basis of standards and practical guidelines.

International standards

Abroad, the production and use of GRC are governed by standards and guidelines from industry bodies. The best known:

  • EN 1169 — European standard for factory production control of GRC products.
  • EN 1170 — a series of European standards for GRC test methods: density, water absorption, flexural strength, fibre content and other properties.
  • EN 15191 — classification of glass fibre reinforced concrete performance for precast products.
  • PCI MNL-128 — recommendations of the Precast/Prestressed Concrete Institute (PCI, USA) on the design, manufacture and installation of GFRC panels.
  • GRCA guidelines — from the international association of GRC manufacturers: production practice, quality control and design.

The common approach of these documents: control of fibre content in every batch, regular flexural testing of samples, design based on long-term strength values, and movement-tolerant fixings. If a foreign project references these documents, the requirements must be translated into the Russian regulatory framework when the project is adapted.

Why the terms matter in procurement

Knowing the terms helps avoid mistakes at several stages:

  1. Working with international references. An architect sees a project with “GRC cladding” or “GFRC panels” — this is glass fibre reinforced concrete, and it can be manufactured in Russia.
  2. Specifications. Do not confuse GRC and GRG: the latter is gypsum for interiors.
  3. Comparing offers. Imported GRC panels and Russian SFB are the same material; compare properties, not names.
  4. Technical requirements. If the brief cites a foreign standard, clarify which properties are required: flexural strength, fibre content, density, water absorption.
  5. Production method. Spray-up and premix give different properties; it is useful to state the method in the specification.

Logistics is a separate issue: large-format panels are expensive and risky to transport over long distances. Manufacturing closer to the site reduces the risk of damage and shortens lead times.

Russian equivalents

In Russia the material is called steklofibrobeton, or SFB. Production and use are based on manufacturers' technical specifications, current standards for concrete test methods and codes of practice for the design of facade systems, loads and fire safety. If a project requires compliance with specific foreign performance values, they are set out in the technical brief and confirmed by test reports.

In composition and technology, Russian SFB is in line with global practice: cement, sand, alkali-resistant fibre, water and admixtures; formed by spraying or vibration casting. According to TECHNOFORMA Group, which describes itself as one of the largest GFRC manufacturers in Russia and the CIS, its capacity is over 20,000 m² of GFRC per month and its production site covers more than 2 hectares. According to the company, its material is classified as non-combustible (NG class under Russian standards) and has high frost resistance.

A practical procurement tip: if tender documents mention “GRC” or “GFRC”, replace it in the Russian specification with “steklofibrobeton (SFB)” and list the required properties — forming method, wall thickness, flexural strength, water absorption, frost resistance, combustibility. This makes offers from different suppliers comparable and acceptance unambiguous.

FAQ

What is GFRC?

Glass Fiber Reinforced Concrete is the American name for concrete reinforced with alkali-resistant glass fibre. It is essentially the same material as GRC and SFB.

Are GRC concrete and SFB the same thing?

Yes. GRC (Glassfibre Reinforced Concrete) is the international designation for glass fibre reinforced concrete; in Russia the abbreviation SFB is used.

What is GRG and can it be used on a facade?

GRG is glass fibre reinforced gypsum. It is an interior material and is not suitable for facades.

Can GRC panels be made in Russia to a foreign design?

Yes, glass fibre reinforced concrete is produced in Russia. The foreign project's requirements are translated into a technical brief with specific values and confirmed by testing.
