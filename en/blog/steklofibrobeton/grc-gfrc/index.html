GRC, GFRC and SFB: Names for Glass Fibre Reinforced Concrete

GRC panels are facade panels made of glass fibre reinforced concrete: the abbreviation GRC (Glassfibre Reinforced Concrete) is used internationally for the same material that in Russia is called SFB. In the USA the variant GFRC (Glass Fiber Reinforced Concrete) is more common. In every case it means fine-grained concrete reinforced with alkali-resistant glass fibre.

When choosing GRC panels, what matters is not the name but the mix design, the forming method and properties confirmed by testing. Different terms in catalogues and designs do not mean different materials, but foreign projects often refer to their own standards, and these have to be translated correctly into Russian requirements.

This article is for architects working with international references and for procurement specialists. For a general overview of the material, see GFRC: material and technology.

GRG deserves a separate note: it is not concrete but gypsum with glass fibre. It is not used on facades. Confusing GRC and GRG in specifications leads to procurement errors.

Global experience

Glass fibre reinforced concrete has been used worldwide since the 1970s, after alkali-resistant glass fibre with zirconium dioxide appeared. Today GRC is a familiar material for:

  • Facades of public and commercial buildings: large-format panels, including those on a steel frame.
  • Curved architecture: parametric facades, shells, perforated screens.
  • Middle Eastern architecture: in the Middle East GRC is widely used for carved mashrabiya screens, domes, arches and ornaments.
  • Restoration and reconstruction of historic facades: lightweight copies of stone decoration.
  • Infrastructure: tunnel linings, noise barriers, permanent formwork.
  • Landscaping: small architectural forms, planters, benches.

Global experience shows that with the right mix design, process and fixing details, GRC facades perform for decades. It has also shown where the mistakes lie: early panels made with ordinary glass, an unaccounted loss of flexural strength and rigid fixing that ignored thermal movement led to cracking. These lessons formed the basis of standards and practical guidelines.

As a guide to how terms relate to product types in international catalogues: "GRC cladding panels" are facing panels; "GRC mouldings" or "cornices" are cornices and profiled decoration; "GRC screens" are perforated screens; "GRC columns" are hollow columns. In a Russian specification all of these are glass fibre reinforced concrete products with the element type stated.

International standards

Outside Russia, the production and use of GRC are governed by standards and by guidelines from industry bodies. The best known are:

  • EN 1169: the European standard for factory production control of GRC products.
  • EN 1170: a series of European standards on GRC test methods covering density, water absorption, flexural strength, fibre content and other properties.
  • EN 15191: classification of glass fibre reinforced concrete performance for precast products.
  • PCI MNL-128: recommendations of the Precast/Prestressed Concrete Institute (PCI, USA) for the design, manufacture and installation of GFRC panels.
  • GRCA guidelines: from the international association of GRC manufacturers, covering production practice, quality control and design.

The common approach of these documents: controlling fibre content in every batch, regular flexural testing of samples, design based on long-term strength values, and sliding fixing details. If a foreign design refers to these documents, the requirements must be translated into the Russian regulatory framework when the design is adapted.

Why the terms matter in procurement

Knowing the terms helps avoid mistakes at several stages:

  1. Working with international references. When an architect sees a design with "GRC cladding" or "GFRC panels", it is glass fibre reinforced concrete and can be manufactured in Russia.
  2. Specifications. GRC and GRG must not be confused: the latter is gypsum for interiors.
  3. Comparing offers. Imported GRC panels and domestic SFB are the same material; compare properties, not names.
  4. Technical requirements. If the brief cites a foreign standard, clarify which values are actually required: flexural strength, fibre content, density, water absorption.
  5. Production method. Spray-up and premix give different properties; it is worth stating the method in the specification.

Logistics is a separate issue: large-format panels are expensive and risky to transport over long distances. Production closer to the site reduces the risk of damage and shortens delivery.

Typical mistakes when translating foreign documentation:

  • copying properties from a foreign catalogue without test reports for the specific product;
  • incorrect unit conversion of inches and pounds per square foot in American documents;
  • specifying "GRC" without the forming method and wall thickness, so suppliers offer products that cannot be compared;
  • mixing up "panel" and "decorative element", which have different fixing requirements.

Russian equivalents

In Russia the material is called steklofibrobeton, or SFB. Its production and use rely on manufacturers' technical specifications, current Russian standards for concrete test methods and Russian codes of practice (SP) for facade system design, loads and fire safety. If a design requires compliance with specific foreign values, these are fixed in the technical brief and confirmed by test reports.

In composition and technology, Russian SFB follows international practice: cement, sand, alkali-resistant fibre, water and admixtures; forming by spraying or vibration casting. According to TECHNOFORMA Group, which describes itself as one of the largest GFRC manufacturers in Russia and the CIS, its capacity is more than 20,000 m² of GFRC per month and its production site covers more than 2 hectares. According to the company, its material is non-combustible (NG class) and highly frost-resistant.

A practical tip for procurement: if tender documents mention "GRC" or "GFRC", replace it in the Russian specification with "glass fibre reinforced concrete (SFB)" and list the required properties: forming method, wall thickness, flexural strength, water absorption, frost resistance, combustibility. This makes offers from different suppliers comparable and acceptance unambiguous.

A checklist of properties to state in the brief instead of a reference to a foreign standard:

What GRC grades mean in international specifications

In international documentation, glass fibre reinforced concrete is often identified not only by an abbreviation but also by a strength grade. Specifications drawn up to the guidance of the GRCA association refer to grades 8, 10 and 18: the number corresponds to the characteristic flexural strength in megapascals. The lower grades usually relate to premix, the highest to sprayed material.

Two abbreviations almost always accompany the grade, and strength figures cannot be read without them:

  • LOP (limit of proportionality) — the stress at which the first crack appears in the specimen;
  • MOR (modulus of rupture) — flexural strength: the maximum stress the specimen withstands before failure;
  • characteristic value — a statistically assured value, not the mean of a series of specimens.

When requirements are transferred into a Russian technical brief, the grade is spelled out: the required limit of proportionality and modulus of rupture, the age of specimens and the test method are stated. A bare entry of “GRC grade 18” without explanation tells a Russian laboratory or construction supervisor nothing. How these indicators relate to domestic documents is covered in the article on GOST and regulations for GFRC.

The age of specimens should also be taken into account. In international documents strength is generally related to 28 days, while reduced long-term values are used for design. If a Russian test report states a different age, the figures are compared only after the method has been clarified.

How to convert units from American GFRC documents

American GFRC specifications and catalogues are written in imperial units, and conversion errors occur more often than errors in translating terms. Not only dimensions need converting, but also loads, density and strength.

The basic ratios sufficient for reading a typical specification:

QuantityAmerican unitMetric equivalent
Length, thickness1 inch (in)25.4 mm
Length1 foot (ft)304.8 mm
Strength, stress1,000 psiabout 6.9 MPa
Surface load, cladding weight1 psfabout 4.9 kg/m²
Density1 pcfabout 16 kg/m³
Force1 pound-force (lbf)about 4.45 N

A practical example: an American design gives the weight of the panel skin in pounds per square foot. Multiplying the value by 4.9 gives kilograms per square metre, which can be compared with a Russian manufacturer's data. More on what makes up the mass of the cladding and how it is transferred to the building can be found in the piece on the weight of GFRC.

Rounding deserves separate attention. A half-inch wall thickness should not become 12.7 mm on the drawings: it is brought to the manufacturer's standard nominal value and verified by calculation.

Temperature in American documents is given in degrees Fahrenheit; a difference of one degree Fahrenheit corresponds to roughly 0.56 degrees Celsius. This matters when converting thermal movement and joint widths.

GRC, UHPC, GRP: how not to confuse related materials

A single international project may combine several composites with similar abbreviations. They differ in composition, product thickness and behaviour in fire, so substituting one for another without recalculation is unacceptable.

DesignationWhat it isHow it differs from GRC
UHPC / UHPFRCUltra-high performance fibre reinforced concreteDense matrix, usually steel or synthetic fibre, different strengths and cost
GRP / FRPGlass fibre reinforced plastic on polymer resinOrganic binder, different fire performance
Precast concretePrecast reinforced concreteBar reinforcement, thickness and mass several times greater
Cast stoneArtificial stone made of concreteSolid cast pieces without dispersed glass fibre reinforcement
SFRCSteel fibre reinforced concreteSteel fibre, used mainly in floors and structures

If a specification contains several such items at once, each is checked against the drawings: the architect often allows an alternative, but only with confirmed properties. The difference between types of fibre reinforced concrete is explained in the article on GFRC and fibre concrete.

In practice GRC and GRP are confused most often: both materials make it possible to produce thin shells of complex shape and look alike once painted. In documentation they can be told apart by the stated binder — cement or resin — and by the data on density and combustibility group.

What input data is needed to assess an international GFRC design?

For a preliminary assessment, the materials an architect or main contractor usually already holds are sufficient. The fuller the set, the fewer assumptions are made when calculating mass, the number of moulds and the make-up of the subframe.

DocumentWhat is taken from itIf the document is missing
Facade drawings (elevations, sections)Cladding area, panel types, levelsA survey or 3D model is used
SpecificationMaterial grade, forming method, tolerances, testing requirementsRequirements are set by the designer from loads and regulations
DetailsSupport arrangement, joint width, junctions with windowsDetails are developed afresh
Finish scheduleColour, texture, references to samplesSelected from the manufacturer's samples
Site dataConstruction region, building height, type of substrateClarified during the site survey

It is worth reading the specification in full, not only the section on the material: requirements for mock-ups, site testing, warranty obligations and packaging are often found in the general sections and noticeably affect the scope of work.

All departures from the original document adopted during adaptation are brought together in a separate table and agreed with the design author before production starts. This protects both parties at acceptance: it is clear which requirement was replaced, with what and on what grounds.

How to adapt an international GRC design for production in Russia?

Adaptation comes down to three tasks: turning the material requirements into verifiable indicators, recalculating panels and fixings for local loads, and bringing the documentation into the Russian format. The work usually proceeds in the following order.

  1. Collect the input data: architectural drawings, specification, 3D model, photographs or samples of the reference surface.
  2. Draw up a table matching terms and units, and decode the material grades and references to standards.
  3. Establish the climatic and wind loads for the site and the fire safety requirements for a building of the given use and height.
  4. Recalculate skin thickness, ribs, frame and fixing spacing; refine the joint width for the temperature range.
  5. Select the subframe and anchors for the actual substrate and carry out anchor tests on site.
  6. Produce texture and colour samples, agree the reference sample and, if required, a full-scale mock-up.
  7. Issue the working documentation and panel layout, and only then start making the moulds.

Frost resistance and drainage details most often need revising: designs created for a warm climate are not intended for repeated freeze-thaw transitions. The second typical change is re-dividing large curved panels to suit transport dimensions and the lifting capacity available on site. How Russian practice in the use of GFRC differs from international practice

The material is the same, but the conditions of use differ, and this is reflected in design solutions. The differences concern climate, the system for confirming fire safety and the organisation of work.

  • Climate. For most Russian regions frost resistance and protection from moisture are decisive, whereas in the Middle East thermal movement and solar exposure come first.
  • Wall construction. In Russia panels are more often hung on a ventilated facade subframe with insulation; elsewhere, steel stud-frame panels covering a whole storey are common.
  • Fire requirements. The assembled system is assessed, so an international report on the material does not replace Russian confirmations.
  • Division of roles. An international manufacturer usually supplies panels for installation by another contractor; in Russia a full cycle with single-point responsibility is in demand.

The architectural possibilities are nevertheless the same: double curvature, perforation and deep relief are all available from domestic production. Examples and limitations of such solutions are gathered in the article on parametric GFRC facades.

FAQ

What is GFRC?

Glass Fiber Reinforced Concrete is the American name for concrete reinforced with alkali-resistant glass fibre. It is essentially the same material as GRC and the Russian SFB.

Are GRC and SFB the same thing?

Yes. GRC (Glassfibre Reinforced Concrete) is the international designation of glass fibre reinforced concrete; in Russia the abbreviation SFB is used.

What is GRG and can it be used on a facade?

GRG is glass fibre reinforced gypsum. It is an interior material and is not suitable for facades.

Can GRC panels for a foreign design be manufactured in Russia?

Yes, glass fibre reinforced concrete is produced in Russia. The requirements of the foreign design are translated into a technical brief with specific values and confirmed by testing.

What does GRC grade 18 mean in a specification?

It is a material grade in international practice: the number indicates the characteristic flexural strength in megapascals, usually for sprayed glass fibre reinforced concrete. In a Russian technical brief the grade is replaced by specific strength values, specimen age and test method.

Can an international GRC certificate be used in Russia?

As a reference document, yes: it helps to understand the level of requirements in the design. For use on a project, test reports for the products actually supplied are needed, together with confirmations under current Russian regulations, including fire regulations.

What is a stud-frame GFRC panel?

It is a large-format panel in which a thin glass fibre reinforced concrete skin is fixed to a steel frame by flexible anchors. The frame carries the loads and is fixed to the building, while the skin is free to move as temperature and humidity change.
