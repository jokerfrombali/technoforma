GFRC Properties: Strength, Density, Frost Resistance

Updated: 2026-09-25 · TECHNOFORMA editorial team

The properties of glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) are described by several indicators: flexural and compressive strength, density, water absorption, frost resistance and combustibility. Flexural strength is what sets GFRC apart from ordinary concrete: it is provided by alkali-resistant glass fibre, which is why element walls can be thin.

Specific values depend on the mix design, the forming method and the age of the samples, so manufacturers should be compared not on advertising figures but on test reports. According to TECHNOFORMA Group, its GFRC is non-combustible (class NG under the Russian classification) and has high frost resistance, further improved by admixtures.

This article is for designers, technical supervision and clients who need to know which figures to ask for. A general overview is in GFRC: material and technology.

Key performance figures

Below are indicative ranges typical for the industry. Values depend on the mix design and technology; exact data are in the product data sheet and test report.

The indicators are interrelated. A dense matrix with a low water-cement ratio simultaneously gives high compressive strength, low water absorption and good frost resistance. Fibre content and orientation have the greatest influence on flexural strength and on the element's behaviour after the first crack. So when comparing manufacturers, look at a set of figures from one test report rather than a single favourable number.

Flexural and compressive strength

GFRC strength is described by two groups of indicators. Compressive strength is determined by the cement matrix and is close to that of high-class fine-grained concretes. For cladding it is rarely critical: the panel does not carry vertical loads from the building.

Flexural strength is the key indicator for facade elements. A panel is subject to wind load, its own weight between fixing points and thermal movement. For GFRC, two values are distinguished: the limit of proportionality (the stress up to which the material behaves elastically, without cracking) and the modulus of rupture (the failure stress). The latter is provided by the fibres, which hold the crack together and prevent brittle failure.

Age must also be taken into account: GFRC flexural strength can decrease over the years, so design uses long-term rather than initial values with safety factors. This is addressed by alkali-resistant fibre, admixtures and a correct mix design.

Frost resistance and water absorption

The frost resistance of GFRC is its ability to withstand repeated freezing and thawing in a water-saturated state without loss of strength or surface damage. It is directly linked to water absorption: the denser the matrix and the fewer the open pores, the less water enters the material and the lower the internal ice pressure.

According to TECHNOFORMA Group, its GFRC has high frost resistance, improved by admixtures. In practice, durability is increased by:

  • a low water-cement ratio and plasticisers;
  • polymer and air-entraining admixtures;
  • water-repellent treatment of the face;
  • correct detailing that prevents water ponding on horizontal elements (drips, cornice falls).

Trade names of admixtures for winter concreting that turn up in searches have nothing to do with the frost resistance of the finished element: the frost resistance grade is confirmed only by testing samples.

Fire performance

GFRC consists of mineral components — cement, sand, glass fibre and water — so according to the company it is classed as non-combustible (NG). This is an important difference from polyurethane, polymer and expanded polystyrene decoration.

For multi-storey residential buildings, public buildings and hotels, requirements for the combustibility of cladding and rainscreen facade systems are set by the applicable fire safety codes. A non-combustible cladding material makes these requirements easier to meet, but the fire safety of the facade as a whole is determined by the entire system: insulation, substructure, wind barrier, fire breaks. So the system must be assessed, not only the panels.

When heated, GFRC does not melt, does not produce burning droplets and does not support combustion. Under prolonged high-temperature exposure, like any cementitious material, it gradually loses strength and the surface may crack. For facades this means GFRC elements do not become a route for fire to spread across the cladding, but their fixings and substructure must also meet the project requirements.

How to verify declared properties

Declared properties are a promise; documents and tests confirm them. What to request from the manufacturer:

  1. Test reports on samples for flexural and compressive strength, density, water absorption and frost resistance — with the date, laboratory and description of the samples.
  2. Product data sheet or the technical specifications under which the products are made.
  3. Fibre documentation confirming alkali resistance.
  4. Combustibility data for the material.
  5. Control samples from your batch — these can be tested in an independent laboratory.

The same approach works for any facade material. The properties of shell limestone blocks or travertine depend on the quarry and the specific batch: density, strength and water absorption can differ noticeably between two quarries of the same rock. Insulation properties (for example, facade mineral wool boards) are taken from the technical documentation of the specific product from its manufacturer. In every case, compare the figures in the documents, not in the advertising.

FAQ

What is the density of GFRC in kg/m³?

Usually about 1,900–2,200 kg/m³, depending on composition and degree of compaction. The exact value is stated in the test report for the specific mix.

How strong is GFRC?

The flexural strength of sprayed GFRC is roughly 15–30 MPa and the compressive strength 40–80 MPa. Values depend on the mix design, forming method and sample age.

How frost-resistant is GFRC?

Highly: a dense matrix with low water absorption withstands freeze-thaw cycles well. The specific frost resistance grade is confirmed by testing samples.

Does GFRC burn?

No. According to TECHNOFORMA Group, its GFRC is classed as NG — non-combustible.

How can I verify a manufacturer's GFRC properties?

Request sample test reports, the product data sheet and documents for the alkali-resistant fibre, and if necessary have control samples from your batch tested in an independent laboratory.
