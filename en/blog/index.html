Articles on facades, GFRC and stone

In this section TECHNOFORMA engineers and designers write about what they work with every day: how GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) works, which mouldings suit a facade, how a ventilated facade is installed, how natural stone types differ, how facade design proceeds and what a facade needs in service.

Sections

  • GFRC: material and technology — composition, production, properties, comparison with other materials;
  • Architectural facade mouldings — cornices, columns, balustrades, window surrounds, styles;
  • Ventilated rainscreen facades — substructures, insulation, installation, standards;
  • Natural stone for facades and interiors — stone types, processing, import, layout;
  • Facade design and selection — stages, documents, cost estimate, choosing a contractor;
  • Facade maintenance, standards and repair — care, inspection, repair, energy efficiency.

The articles do not replace calculations for a specific building: decisions on materials and fixings are always made from the project drawings and current standards.

How the facade articles are structured and what they are based on

Each article answers one practical question and follows the same pattern: a short answer at the start, then the construction or properties, limitations, typical mistakes and what needs to be checked in the design. Figures are given as ranges, because exact values depend on the mix design, the type of stone, the height of the building and the construction region.

The articles draw on manufacturing and installation experience: the company has worked with GFRC for more than ten years and with natural stone for more than fifteen, and employs twelve design engineers and two architects. Where a matter is governed by standards, the article names the document and explains its meaning, but does not replace reading the document itself or carrying out the calculation.

The section contains four types of publication:

  • overviews — give a general picture of the topic and a glossary of terms;
  • comparisons — set two materials or two technologies side by side against the same criteria;
  • practical guides — describe the sequence of actions from input data to handover;
  • reference articles — explain performance figures and the requirements of standards.

How the topics of the section relate to each other

The topics of the section overlap because on a real facade the materials work together. GFRC mouldings are fixed to the same wall as the ventilated facade subframe, and the plinth stone meets the panels and the insulation. It is therefore useful to supplement an article on one material with an article on the adjacent detail.

A practical approach is to read in pairs: an article on a material and an article on its fixing, an article on design and an article on acceptance, an article on choosing a cladding and an article on its maintenance. This reveals constraints that are not visible when reading on a single topic: the weight of products, joint spacing, access for maintenance, requirements for the substrate.

What to do after reading: from an article to a facade estimate

An article helps to formulate the task, while the solution appears only on the drawings of a specific building. Once it is clear which material and which type of fixing are being considered, the next step is to submit the input data for assessment.

For a first estimate it is usually enough to provide elevations and plans in any format, the height of the building, the construction region and the wishes regarding appearance. If there is no sketch yet, work begins with facade design. Built solutions are shown in the section of completed projects: residential complexes, a hotel complex, a business centre and a university.

Topics for new publications come from the questions of clients and designers. If the required topic is not in the section, a question can be sent through the enquiry form and a relevant specialist will prepare the answer.

FAQ

Can the articles be used as the basis for a design decision?

No. The articles explain principles and help to ask the right questions, but they do not take into account the loads, the wall construction or the fire safety requirements of a specific building. A design decision is made on the basis of calculation and the standards in force.

Why do the articles not give prices for materials and works?

The cost of a facade depends on the area, the set of products, the share of bespoke elements, the height of the building and the region, so there is no single figure. The articles show what the price is made up of, while the estimate is prepared from the drawings of the project.

Are the articles suitable for private clients?

Yes, some articles directly answer questions about finishing a private house. However, most of the material is intended for developers, main contractors and architects and describes solutions for multi-storey and public buildings.
