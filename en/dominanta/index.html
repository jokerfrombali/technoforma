What a residential complex facade usually requires

A multi-storey residential facade has to combine a distinctive look with durability and fire safety: at this height the cladding is usually fixed to a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure, and decorative elements and panels are made from non-combustible materials. Repeating elements such as window surrounds, string courses and balcony balustrades are cost-effective to cast in series from a single mould, and installation is planned in sections so that it does not hold up finishing works and handover of the building.

The North Caucasus is a seismically active region, so low weight and reliable fixing are especially important for cladding and decorative elements here: lightweight thin-walled GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) products reduce the load on the substructure and the supporting structure.

How surfaces are divided between GFRC and porcelain stoneware on a high-rise building

On this project the quantities of the two materials are almost equal: 14,000 m² of GFRC and 13,000 m² of porcelain stoneware. This ratio is characteristic of combined facades, where slab cladding forms the background and cast elements create the architectural pattern of the building.

In general, porcelain stoneware is given to ordinary walls with a repeating module: it is dimensionally stable and quick to install on a standard frame. GFRC is used for elements with a profile and relief — facade panels, string courses, window surrounds. Both materials are non-combustible, which matters for an 18-storey building, and both are fixed to the frame of a ventilated facade, so the insulation and air cavity behind them are continuous.

How is facade installation organised across several blocks?

Four blocks allow the work to run as a flow: crews move from section to section with the same sequence of operations. For projects of this scale the following principles are usually applied.

  • Work sections. Each block is divided into areas by height and by elevation; frame, insulation and cladding follow one another one or two sections apart.
  • Series production. Repeating GFRC elements are produced in series from the same moulds and dispatched as sets per floor or section.
  • A single batch of slabs. Porcelain stoneware is purchased with shade and calibre controlled per block to avoid colour variation on one surface.
  • Access. The method of lifting materials and working at height is defined in the method statement, taking the weight of the elements into account.
  • Stage-by-stage acceptance. Concealed layers are accepted before the cladding is hung, and remarks are dealt with before moving to the next area.

General requirements for such buildings are collected in the article on the facade of a multi-storey residential building.

Coastal climate and wind: what the design takes into account

Makhachkala lies on the shore of the Caspian Sea, and for facades here wind load, humid salt-laden air and intense sun are significant. At the height of the upper storeys wind pressure is noticeably higher than at ground level, so the bracket spacing and the number of fixing points in corner and upper zones are, as a rule, increased by calculation.

For the frame and fasteners in coastal areas, materials with higher corrosion resistance are selected and contact between dissimilar metals is excluded. Porcelain stoneware and GFRC are resistant to moisture and ultraviolet; for GFRC the quality of the face layer and a protective impregnation that reduces soiling of the surface are important.

Thermal movement is also built into the calculation: in summer the cladding on sunlit elevations heats up considerably more than the air, so the joints between panels and slabs are left open or filled with an elastic material, and the frame rails are divided into lengths with expansion gaps.

What matters to the client of a similar residential complex

For the developer of a multi-storey complex the facade is at once the image of the project, the handover programme and a significant budget item. At the preparation stage the following should be decided.

  • The ratio of materials by zone: where decoration is needed and where slabs are enough — more on the porcelain stoneware facade cladding page.
  • The degree of standardisation of elements: the fewer the sizes, the faster production and installation.
  • The sequence of blocks and the date each will be ready for facade works.
  • Insulation and fire safety requirements for the adopted height.
  • The contractor's scope: supply only, installation, or the full cycle including design.

FAQ

Why are GFRC and porcelain stoneware combined on a high-rise residential building?

Porcelain stoneware covers large flat surfaces economically, while GFRC makes it possible to produce relief, surrounds and string courses that cannot be made from a slab. Both materials are non-combustible and are installed on a shared ventilated facade frame.

What work did TECHNOFORMA carry out at the Dominanta residential complex?

Design and installation of the facade: 14,000 m² of GFRC and 13,000 m² of porcelain stoneware on four 18-storey blocks. The works ran from November 2022 to October 2024.
