GFRC and Natural Stone Facades in Azerbaijan

TECHNOFORMA Group is a Russian manufacturer of GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) facades with its own design department, mould workshops and installation teams. For developers and architects in Azerbaijan the company offers design, manufacture of facade panels and decoration, supply of natural stone and technical support during installation — agreed individually for each project.

Architectural context: from Icherisheher to the oil boom

Baku's appearance was formed in layers. Icherisheher, the old walled city with the Maiden Tower and the Palace of the Shirvanshahs, sets the oriental tradition: stone masonry, pointed arches, carved portals, geometric ornament. The oil boom of the late 19th and early 20th centuries brought mansions and apartment houses in European eclectic, Neo-Gothic, Neo-Renaissance and Neo-Moorish styles: rich cornices, bay windows, balconies on brackets, moulded friezes. The Soviet period added neoclassical ensembles, and modern Baku is building high-rise residential complexes and hotels where classical and oriental motifs are in demand again.

Such facades need complex decoration in large volumes — columns, capitals, arched surrounds, ornamental panels and screens. GFRC suits this task: the company makes its own models and moulds, so the elements follow the architect's design rather than a catalogue and are identical across the whole building.

Absheron stone and 'kubik' limestone

The traditional building material of Baku and Absheron is the local sawn shell limestone (coquina) known as 'kubik', along with denser Absheron limestones, from which both the old city and many oil-boom houses were built. The warm light tone of this stone largely defines the colour of the city.

The company works with soft stones — shell limestone, limestone, dolomite, sandstone — and hard ones — granite, basalt, marble. Two approaches are possible: natural stone cladding from the company's warehouses or to order (imported from Turkey, Iran, India, China), or GFRC panels tinted and textured to match light limestone — consistent with the historic buildings but lighter in weight.

Climate: wind, heat and seismicity

  • Wind is the main factor for Baku facades: the northern khazri and the southern gilavar blow for most of the year, so the ventilated rainscreen facade substructure and panel fixings are designed for high wind loads, especially on high-rise buildings by the sea.
  • Hot dry summers, strong sun and mild winters with rare frosts; humid salty air on the coast, frosts and freeze-thaw cycles in the foothills and mountains.
  • Seismicity: much of Azerbaijan, including Baku, Shamakhi and Ganja, is seismically active; seismic loads are built into the design of the fixings.

According to the company, GFRC is a non-combustible material (Russian class NG) with high frost resistance; the panels are lighter than massive cladding, which reduces the load on the structural frame in seismic areas.

How we work with developers

  1. The architect or developer provides the project, concept or facade sketch.
  2. The design department — 12 designers led by two architects — prepares the solution, panel layout, details and 3D visualisation.
  3. After approval: estimate, manufacture of moulds and panels with quality control at every stage.
  4. Delivery and installation: supervision of a local crew, a visit by company specialists or installation by our own teams — by agreement.

GFRC panels are manufactured at the company's production site in the Moscow Region. The company's nearest warehouse to Azerbaijan is in Makhachkala. The route, customs clearance, delivery terms and the role of specialists on site are agreed separately for each project — the company has no representative office in Azerbaijan.
