GFRC and Natural Stone Facades in Sumgait and on the Absheron Peninsula

The Absheron Peninsula means Sumgait, the seaside settlements around Baku, the resort zone of the northern coast and new residential districts. For residential, resort and public buildings on Absheron, TECHNOFORMA Group offers facades in GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) and natural stone — design, production and supply by agreement.

The peninsula's climate

Absheron is the driest and windiest part of Azerbaijan: a semi-desert climate, hot summers with strong sun and short mild winters. The khazri and gilavar winds blow from the sea and the steppe for most of the year, carrying salt and sand. The area is seismically active. For a facade this means a substructure designed for wind and seismic loads, fixings chosen for sea air, and cladding resistant to UV and sand abrasion.

Sumgait and seaside development

Sumgait grew in the mid-20th century as an industrial city with Soviet neoclassicism in the centre — colonnades, arches, cornices — and standard housing estates. Today facades are being renovated, housing and a seaside boulevard are being built. For renovating Soviet buildings, a ventilated rainscreen facade with insulation and GFRC cladding makes it possible to insulate the building and restore its expressive classical details at the same time. Hotels and resort complexes are going up on the northern coast of the peninsula, where light stone facades are appropriate.

Absheron stone

The peninsula is the source of the famous Absheron limestone and 'kubik' shell limestone from which Baku and the Absheron settlements were built. The company processes soft stones — limestone, shell limestone, dolomite, sandstone — and can offer cladding in the same warm light palette: natural stone from its warehouses or to order, or GFRC with a limestone texture. The choice is made in the design based on loads, budget and the architectural brief.

How salt, sand and sun affect cladding and how it is protected

In the coastal zone of Absheron a facade ages mainly because of salt aerosol, sand-laden dust and ultraviolet light. The effect of these factors is reduced by the choice of material, surface treatment and detailing, not merely by the thickness of the cladding.

FactorWhat happensDesign response
Salt airCorrosion of fixings, salt staining on porous stoneStainless fixings, water-repellent treatment of stone, protected slab edges
Sand and dustAbrasion of coatings, soiling of reliefDense surface, through-coloured GFRC, textures without deep pockets
SunFading of pigments, heating of dark surfacesLight mineral pigments, movement joints
Occasional downpoursStreaking on a dusty facadeDrips, sills, projecting cornices

Porous stones, including shell limestone for facades, are usually treated with a water repellent near the sea, and the treatment is renewed following inspections.

GFRC or natural stone by the sea: which to choose?

For a coastal property in Absheron both materials are applicable, and the choice is determined by the height of the building, the complexity of the decor and the client's readiness to maintain the surface. They are often combined: stone below, glass fibre reinforced concrete above.

  • Weight. Thin-walled GFRC panels are noticeably lighter than stone slabs of the same area, so on upper storeys and in refurbishment the load on the walls and subframe is lower.
  • Shape. Cornices, arches and ornament are easier to cast in a mould than to carve from stone; flat wall cladding is covered by stone without tooling.
  • Surface. Natural limestone gives a lively pattern and a unique texture, but porous grades need water-repellent treatment; GFRC is uniform in tone within a batch.
  • Repair. A damaged panel or slab on a subframe can be replaced individually; with stone it is important to keep a reserve from the same batch.

A detailed comparison is given in the article on choosing between GFRC and stone.

Facade refurbishment in Sumgayit: where it starts

Refurbishment starts with a survey of the walls, not with the choice of cladding. The condition of the external panels or masonry determines which anchors can be used and what facade weight the substrate will carry.

  1. Survey: condition of walls, joints and balcony slabs, anchor pull-out tests.
  2. Measured survey and recording of wall deviations from the vertical, which the subframe compensates for.
  3. Thermal calculation and choice of insulation; non-combustible mineral wool is used in ventilated systems.
  4. Panel layout tied to window openings and balconies.
  5. Installation section by section, usually without rehousing residents.

Junctions with windows and balconies are detailed separately: this is where leaks and cold bridges most often occur. The scope of thermal insulation works is described on the facade insulation page.

What matters for the facade of a coastal resort property

The facade of a hotel or serviced-apartment complex by the sea has to keep its appearance with minimal maintenance, because repairs in season mean rooms out of use. At design stage, therefore, not only the appearance is assessed but also access to the facade for washing and inspection.

Open terraces, stairs and galleries are usually enclosed with GFRC balustrades: hollow balusters with cast-in fixings are lighter than stone ones and are not affected by moisture. The height and strength of guarding are taken from the building regulations in force for the building type concerned. For large light-coloured surfaces the permissible shade variation between batches is agreed in advance. The company's experience on the Caspian coast is the Alye Parusa residential complex in Dagestan.

What data are needed to estimate a facade in Absheron

Drawings and site information are sufficient for an estimate; a site visit refines the solution at the next step.

  • Elevations and plans of the building; for refurbishment, photographs and survey measurements.
  • Distance from the shoreline and number of storeys.
  • Material and condition of the walls.
  • Preferred materials, colour and programme.

On this basis the design department prepares the layout, fixing details and a schedule of products.

FAQ

Is GFRC suitable for seaside buildings on Absheron?

Yes, with a properly selected substructure and fixings that account for wind, salt and sand.

Can a Soviet-era building in Sumgait be insulated and reclad?

Yes, the company designs ventilated facades with insulation and cladding in GFRC, stone or porcelain stoneware.

Do you have an office in Azerbaijan?

No. Design, production and delivery are arranged from Russia for each specific project; terms are agreed individually.

How much does a facade cost?

The cost is determined after reviewing the project and inspecting the site. Send your drawings to sales@technoforma.ru or call 8 (800) 550-51-98.

How often should a facade by the sea be washed?

The frequency is set on the basis of inspections. In a coastal zone the facade is usually rinsed with clean water more often than inland, to remove salt and dust. Acid-based products are not used on limestone or GFRC.

Do residents have to move out while the facade is refurbished?

As a rule, no: a ventilated facade is installed from outside, from scaffolding or cradles. The sequence of works and the fencing of hazardous areas are agreed with the client.

Can existing balconies be kept when new cladding is installed?

The decision is taken after the balcony slabs have been surveyed. If their load-bearing capacity is adequate, the guarding and cladding are renewed; otherwise the slabs are strengthened or replaced under a separate design.
