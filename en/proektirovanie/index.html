Facade design

A building facade starts with a design. TECHNOFORMA's design department — 12 design engineers led by two architects, plus 3D visualisers — develops facades in GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete), natural stone and other materials and supports them through to installation.

What a facade design includes

  • architectural concept and 3D visualisation;
  • selection of materials and textures;
  • working documentation: elevations, layouts, fixing details, specifications;
  • GFRC element drawings for production;
  • natural stone layout with slab marking;
  • design of the ventilated rainscreen facade substructure and insulation;
  • designer's supervision during installation.

Why design with the manufacturer

Our design engineers work alongside the mould shops and production, so elements are designed to be manufacturable from the start: division into elements, weight, embedded fixings and fixing method are all taken into account. This reduces rework between design, factory and installation.

How the work proceeds

  1. Drawings and brief. You send architectural drawings, a sketch or photos of the facade; if there is no design, our design department will prepare one.
  2. Quote. We calculate the range of elements and volumes and agree the scope of work and delivery schedule.
  3. Design. Design engineers and 3D visualisers issue the working documentation, layout and fixing details.
  4. Production. Models and moulds are made in our own mould shops, with quality control at every stage.
  5. Delivery. Shipping from the production base near Moscow, via company warehouses if needed.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division of more than 100 specialists fits the substructure, insulation and cladding; installation supervision is also available.

Building facade design: from input data to shop drawings

A request for a building facade design usually means one of three things: an appearance concept for approval, working documentation for tender and construction, or a cladding design for an already approved facade. We work at any of these stages.

  • Concept. Composition, rhythm, proportions, materials and colour — visualised in the context of the surrounding buildings; in Moscow this package is needed to approve the building's architectural appearance (AGO).
  • Working documentation. Elevations and sections, cladding layout, element specifications, junction details at windows, roof and plinth.
  • Structural part. Ventilated rainscreen facade substructure, calculation of cladding and wind loads, fixing of large mouldings — cornices, columns, balustrades.

If the building already has an architectural design, our engineers adapt it to GFRC or stone technology: they divide the ornament into elements, select thicknesses and embedded fixings, and agree changes with the original architect.

What drives the cost

The cost of design depends on the facade area and complexity, the amount of unique ornament, the stage (concept or working documentation) and the availability of input data.

Historical note

In the eclectic and Art Nouveau periods, facade design was often a separate commission for an artist or architect: the facade of the Tretyakov Gallery, for example, was rebuilt in 1902–1904 to a design by the artist Viktor Vasnetsov. Today a facade design covers not only appearance but also fixing, load and fire safety calculations.

What calculations are part of facade design

Facade design is not only drawings of the building's appearance but also a calculated justification: the structure must withstand its own weight, wind and thermal movement, and meet the requirements for thermal protection and fire safety. The set of calculations depends on the system and the height of the building.

Typical mistakes in a facade design and what they lead to

Most installation problems are built into the documentation. Five mistakes occur most often, and each of them leads to rework on site.

  • Design without a survey. The layout is based on the architectural drawings, while the actual walls deviate by several centimetres; the result is trimming on site and joints of varying width.
  • Decoration that ignores the technology. A product is drawn as a single piece but cannot be demoulded or lifted to height; the division has to be changed after approval.
  • No junction details. Windows, parapets, the plinth and movement joints are left to the discretion of the installers.
  • Related disciplines not coordinated. Fixings clash with service penetrations, downpipes, lighting or signage structures.
  • Schedule without marking. Production and site work with different designations, and kitting drags on.

To avoid this, the working documentation is taken to the level of fabrication drawings with every element marked.

How does a facade design change with the type of building?

The set of sections is the same, but the emphasis differs. For residential complexes the priority is standardisation and serial production, for business centres the combination of cladding and glazing, and for hotels and public buildings bespoke decoration and entrance areas.

  • Residential buildings. A limited set of product types, coordination with balconies and outdoor air-conditioning units, phased issue of drawings by construction phase.
  • Office and retail buildings. Large-format panels, interfaces with curtain walling, space for signage and lighting.
  • Hotels and club houses. Complex modelling, columns, cornices, portals and a larger number of unique moulds; an example is the Al-Qasr hotel complex.
  • Refurbishment. Inspection of the walls, measured survey and a check of the substrate's load-bearing capacity before design work begins.

What input data speed up facade design

The speed and accuracy of facade design depend directly on how complete the input data are. A minimum set makes it possible to assess the scope; an extended set allows working drawings to be issued straight away.

  • architectural plans, sections and elevations with levels and opening positions;
  • structural solutions: wall material, position of floor slabs and columns;
  • an as-built or measured survey if the building shell has already been erected;
  • thermal protection and fire safety requirements from the design documentation;
  • information on adjacent systems: windows, curtain walling, downpipes, lighting.

Missing data are obtained by the design department on site, through measured surveys and anchor tests.

In what form the design documentation is issued

The result is a package from which the products can be manufactured, the subframe purchased and installation carried out without further questions to the author. It usually includes drawings in PDF and an editable format, schedules and specifications in spreadsheets and, where required, a building information model.

Working in a three-dimensional model helps to find clashes with structures and building services before going to site; the approach is described in the piece on BIM design of facades. The cladding material determines the set of drawings: for GFRC products, drawings of moulds and embedded parts are issued; for natural stone, cutting plans and the slab layout for each elevation.

FAQ

Can I order just the facade design?

Yes, design can be ordered separately from production and installation.

What do you need to get started?

Architectural drawings, a sketch or photos of the existing facade, and a brief on materials.

Do you provide 3D visualisation?

Yes, our design department includes 3D visualisers.

How long does facade design take?

The time depends on the area, the amount of unique decoration and the stage. A concept generally takes several weeks, working documentation from several weeks to several months. An exact period is given once the input data have been reviewed.

Is a measured survey needed if an architectural design exists?

Yes, a measured or as-built survey is advisable for working documentation: actual walls almost always differ from the designed ones. Without it, the cladding layout has to be corrected on site.

Can the design be changed after production has started?

It can, but this affects time and cost: moulds and products already made for the altered areas will have to be redone. For this reason the appearance, layout and details are agreed before mould-making begins.
