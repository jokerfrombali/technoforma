GFRC and Natural Stone Facades in the Northwestern Federal District of Russia

The Northwestern Federal District stretches from Kaliningrad on the Baltic to Naryan-Mar in the Arctic. Here a facade has to withstand damp, wind, sea air and northern frosts.

Climate

St Petersburg, Kaliningrad, Pskov and Novgorod have mild but damp winters with thaws and high humidity all year round. Karelia, Vologda and Arkhangelsk have long snowy winters. Komi, the Murmansk Region and the Nenets Autonomous Okrug are subarctic and Arctic, with frosts, blizzards and hurricane-force winds, plus permafrost in the Nenets Okrug. The common requirements are minimal water absorption of the cladding, high frost resistance and fixings designed for wind loads.

Architecture and stone

The district is St Petersburg classicism and Art Nouveau, medieval Novgorod and Pskov, the German heritage of Kaliningrad and the timber architecture of Kizhi and Vologda. It also has its own famous stones: Vyborg rapakivi granite, Putilovo limestone slab, Karelian gabbro-diabase and Ruskeala marble, Novgorod and Pskov flagstone. The company complements them with granite, marble and travertine from India, Turkey, China and Iran.

Which facade solutions suit the North-West

In the North-West, the solutions that perform best are those that shed water quickly and do not let it accumulate in the material: a ventilated facade with an air gap, dense types of stone and GFRC with a protective impregnation. Rendered facades with moulded plaster decoration generally need more frequent repair here.

On plinths and embankments the traditional choice is granite for facades and plinths; slabs and panels are hung using the technology of ventilated facade systems.

How can a facade be protected against moisture, efflorescence and biological growth?

The main protection is in the detailing: water must not linger on horizontal surfaces or run behind the cladding. Chemical treatments only supplement correct details.

  • Falls and drips on every projection; GFRC cornices and string courses are covered with flashings or designed with a fall on the top face.
  • Materials with low water absorption, particularly at plinth level and beneath windows.
  • Water-repellent treatment of porous stone and concrete surfaces, renewed periodically.
  • Joint inspection: failed sealant is a common cause of white streaks; causes and removal are covered in our article on efflorescence on stone.
  • Algae and moss may appear on north-facing and shaded walls; they are removed by gentle washing and biocidal treatments.

Decoration for a historic setting and the facade works season

In cities with classical and Art Nouveau buildings, decoration is usually reproduced from measured surveys: the profiles of mouldings, window surrounds and brackets are transferred to models and cast in GFRC. Thin-walled products place little load on old walls and are not affected by damp; the approach is described in our article on the restoration of a historic facade.

The season for wet trades in the North-West is short and depends on rainfall, so mechanically fixed cladding is easier to plan than adhesive and rendering work. It makes sense to complete design and manufacture by spring, so that the dry months can be used for installation, joint sealing and protective treatment. In the northern parts of the district the light, warm period is shorter still, and the delivery schedule is tied to it with a margin.

Facade care in a damp climate: what to check

In a humid climate a facade lasts longer if it is inspected regularly and minor defects are put right before winter. Inspections are usually carried out in spring, once the snow has gone, and in autumn.

  • The condition of the sealant in joints and at window junctions.
  • The integrity of flashings, parapet copings and the rainwater system: most leaks behind the cladding start there.
  • Streaks, dark patches and white deposits on the surface, which indicate that water is not following its intended path.
  • Chips and cracks in decorative elements, particularly on cornice edges.
  • The condition of the water-repellent treatment: if water no longer beads on the surface, it is time to renew the impregnation.

Stone and concrete are best washed with water at moderate pressure and neutral cleaning agents; acidic products are used only after a trial on an inconspicuous area.

FAQ

Where are deliveries to Northwest Russia shipped from?

From the Moscow Region — the company's production base closest to the district.

Do you have an office in St Petersburg?

No, the office is in Moscow; we work from drawings, and site visits are arranged by agreement.

Are travertine and limestone suitable for a facade in St Petersburg?

Porous stones can be used with reservations: above plinth level, with water-repellent treatment and with the frost resistance of the specific batch confirmed. For the plinth and for areas exposed to splashing and de-icing agents, granite is the more reliable choice.

Do buildings by the sea need special fixings?

In coastal cities, stainless steel is generally chosen for embedded parts and fixings. The subframe material is determined by calculation, taking the corrosivity of the environment into account.
