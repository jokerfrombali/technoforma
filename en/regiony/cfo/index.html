GFRC and Natural Stone Facades in the Central Federal District of Russia

The Central Federal District is home ground for TECHNOFORMA Group: the company's office is in Moscow, and its production base and stone warehouses are in the Moscow Region. From here, GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) elements and natural stone are delivered to all 18 regions of the district.

Climate and facade requirements

The district's climate is moderately continental but varied. In the west (Smolensk, Bryansk) winters are mild and damp, with frequent thaws. In the north-east (Kostroma, Yaroslavl, Ivanovo) they are longer, snowier and colder. In the south, in the Black Earth region (Belgorod, Voronezh, Kursk, Tambov), summers are hot, with winds and icing. The common problem is frequent freeze-thaw cycles, so a facade needs frost resistance, low water absorption and well-designed drainage.

Architecture

The district preserves the white-stone architecture of Vladimir, the classicism of Kostroma, Kaluga and Tver, the constructivism of Ivanovo and the post-war Stalinist Empire style of Voronezh and Moscow. Reconstruction and new buildings in historic settings call for decoration made to bespoke models — a task for GFRC. Local stone is mainly limestone and dolomite (Moscow Region, Staritsa, Tula, Lipetsk); imported granite is used for plinths.

Logistics

Production is located in the Moscow Region, so most regional capitals are 180 to 700 km away by federal highways. This allows elements to be delivered in batches to match the installation schedule. In Moscow the company delivered RUBLEVO BUSINESS PARK.

Which facade solutions suit the Central Federal District, by building type

In the Central District the most common approach combines a ventilated facade on the main wall plane with GFRC decorative elements on cornices, surrounds and entrance areas. Stone is used where resistance to moisture, de-icing agents and mechanical impact is needed: on plinths, entrance steps and ground floors.

When are facade works carried out in Central Russia?

Ventilated systems can be installed in the district all year round, whereas wet trades are tied to the warm season. The subframe, insulation and mechanically fixed cladding are also installed in winter, provided that wind and temperature stay within the conditions set for the works. Jointing, adhesives, painting and water-repellent treatment are usually scheduled for the period of consistently above-zero temperatures, roughly from April to October; in the north-eastern regions this period is shorter.

The programme is therefore built around the season: design and manufacture are moved to winter, and finishing operations to the warm months. The constraints of the cold period are covered in our article on facade works in winter, and the sequence of operations in the section on ventilated facade installation.

What to consider when working in a historic setting

In historic centres the appearance of a building is, as a rule, approved by the architecture or heritage protection authorities, and this affects the timescale more than manufacture does. Materials, colour and the pattern of the decoration are best fixed before the documents are submitted, with texture samples to support the proposal.

  • In Moscow, new and refurbished buildings go through approval of the architectural appearance.
  • To replicate lost profiles, a measured survey is carried out and models are made, from which GFRC products are cast.
  • For white-stone facades, limestone of a similar tone and texture is selected.

A sample panel on the wall helps to judge the colour in natural light next to the neighbouring buildings.

Typical mistakes when ordering a facade in the Central District

Most facade problems are built in before installation begins, at the design and procurement stage. Four mistakes are the most common.

  1. Decorative elements are chosen from an image without checking the scale on the building: a small profile is lost at height, and a large one makes the facade look heavy.
  2. Drainage from cornices and string courses is not worked out, and after a few seasons streaks and efflorescence appear on the wall.
  3. Porous stone is placed on the plinth, where moisture and de-icing agents break it down.
  4. Products are ordered before the anchors have been tested and the walls surveyed, so fixing details have to be reworked on site.

Detailed design drawings with fixing details and a sample approved before series production help to avoid this.

FAQ

Where is your production located?

In the Moscow Region; the stone warehouses are there too.

Do you work in all regions of the Central Federal District?

Yes. Estimates are based on drawings; installation or installation supervision is agreed case by case.

Do walls need strengthening to carry GFRC decorative elements?

As a rule, no: thin-walled products are considerably lighter than their concrete and stone counterparts. The load-bearing capacity of the substrate is nevertheless checked by calculation and by anchor pull-out tests on site.

Can we order the decorative elements only if another contractor installs the ventilated facade?

Yes. The products are manufactured to the client's drawings, and the fixing details are coordinated with the contractor's subframe at the design stage, so that no rework is needed on site.
