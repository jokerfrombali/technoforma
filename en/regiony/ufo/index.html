GFRC and Natural Stone Facades: Ural Federal District

The Ural Federal District runs from the mining and industrial Urals with their granites and marbles to Western Siberia and the Arctic. The climate is sharply continental and harsh: frosts and heat in the south, winters lasting over half the year and permafrost in the north. Architecturally it is the constructivism of Yekaterinburg, Stalin-era Chelyabinsk, fast-growing Tyumen and modern northern cities.

Regions of the district

The district includes:

  • Sverdlovsk Region — Yekaterinburg
  • Chelyabinsk Region — Chelyabinsk
  • Tyumen Region — Tyumen
  • Kurgan Region — Kurgan
  • Khanty-Mansi Autonomous Okrug — Yugra — Khanty-Mansiysk
  • Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug — Salekhard

Supply and working with clients

The Urals are stone country: Ural granites and marbles, including the well-known Koelga marble, have historically been used to clad buildings across the country. The company works both with domestic stone to order and with imported stone from its warehouses. For the northern territories — Yugra and Yamal — the focus is on lightweight GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) panels on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure: lower loads on piles and foundations and faster installation in the short building season. For all regions of the district the design accounts for severe frost, wind and temperature swings.

The company has a warehouse in Yekaterinburg serving the whole district, including Yugra and Yamal; project-specific GFRC elements are manufactured in the Moscow Region.

We work from the client's drawings: facade design, production, delivery, installation or installation supervision — by agreement. Services: GFRC facades, natural stone, design and construction. Completed projects are on the projects page.

Which climatic loads govern a facade in the Urals and Western Siberia

A facade in the Ural District is designed first and foremost for frost, frequent freeze-thaw transitions and wind. These factors determine the frost-resistance requirements for the cladding, the spacing of the subframe brackets and the insulation thickness.

TerritoryConditionsWhat the design allows for
Middle and Southern Urals, Trans-Uralscold winter, hot summer, wide annual temperature rangemovement joints, allowance for thermal expansion of the rails, cladding with low water absorption
South of Tyumen Regionlong winter, weak soils in river floodplainslightweight cladding, a thicker insulation layer
Yugra and Yamallong winter, blizzards, permafrostminimum facade weight, large panels, corrosion-resistant fixings

In industrial cities polluted air adds to the climate: dust and soot show up sooner on light cladding, so the texture and protective impregnation are chosen with future cleaning in mind. For more on the link between conditions and construction, see the article on how climate affects a facade.

Which facade solutions suit the cities of the district?

For high-rise residential and commercial development in Yekaterinburg, Tyumen and Chelyabinsk the usual choice is a ventilated facade: the insulation is protected from moisture and the cladding can be installed at any time of year. Such a system carries GFRC panels and decorative elements, which are non-combustible and noticeably lighter than stone slabs of the same area.

In historic quarters and in the refurbishment of mid-twentieth-century buildings there is demand for cornices, string courses and window surrounds that reproduce the original profiles. Natural stone cladding is used mostly on plinths, entrance areas and lower storeys, where resistance to impact and de-icing agents matters; granite is the usual choice for these zones. The subframe and insulation design is selected as part of the facade works.

How to plan delivery and installation around the season

The length of the warm season varies greatly across the district, so the programme is built backwards from the date by which the facade must be closed. The further north the project, the earlier the design must be completed and production started.

  • It is convenient to finish the design and the panel layout in winter: facade design does not depend on the weather, and by spring the elements are ready for dispatch.
  • For Yugra and Yamal the delivery route is clarified in advance: some settlements can be reached only by winter roads or by water during the navigation season.
  • Dry installation on a subframe is possible in frost, but joint sealing and work with mortars must respect the temperature limits set by the material manufacturers; see the piece on facade works in winter.
  • Large panels reduce the number of lifts and fixings, which matters when daylight is short and work is carried out in wind.

Elements are batched and marked by work section at the factory, so that they do not have to be stored on site under snow for long.

What to prepare for a facade quotation in the district

Architectural drawings and information about the site are enough for a preliminary quotation. The construction area immediately determines the wind and temperature actions that the designer allows for in the calculation.

  • elevations, plans and sections of the building, material of the load-bearing walls;
  • the site address and number of storeys, from which the wind zone and height zones are determined;
  • preferences for materials: panels, decorative elements, stone, texture and colour;
  • the construction programme and the desired start date for installation;
  • the scope of work: supply only, installation supervision or turnkey installation.

From these data a list of elements, the principal details and a programme tied to the season and the delivery route are prepared.

FAQ

Is GFRC suitable for the harsh climate of the Urals and the North?

Yes, provided the mix is correctly specified. The material is non-combustible and frost-resistant, and thin-walled panels place little load on the frame and foundations. Requirements for frost resistance and fixings are set out in the design for the specific construction area.

Can a facade be installed in winter?

The subframe and cladding are installed all year round; the restrictions mainly concern sealants, adhesives and mortars. In northern areas wind and short daylight hours are also taken into account.
