What TECHNOFORMA did

TECHNOFORMA's scope on the project covered design and installation supervision. Design means developing facade cladding solutions by the company's own design department: design engineers and 3D visualisers. Under installation supervision, the company's specialists provide technical management and control of the installation, which is carried out by another contractor.

What a public building facade usually requires

A university building should look solid and enduring, and its cladding must meet the requirements for public buildings, including fire safety. A large cladding area on a low-rise building means long facades, where the rhythm of divisions, the quality of joints and a uniform tone of the products are particularly noticeable.

The North Caucasus is a seismically active region, so low weight and reliable fixing are especially important for cladding and decorative elements here: lightweight thin-walled GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) products reduce the load on the substructure and the supporting structure.

What does facade installation supervision include, and when is it justified?

Installation supervision is an arrangement in which the cladding is installed by the client's or main contractor's crew, while the component manufacturer is responsible for technical guidance. It is justified when there is already a contractor on site with labour and plant but no experience with the specific material and details.

The supervising engineer's tasks usually include:

  • induction briefing of the crews and a walk-through of the installation drawings;
  • checking the subframe and the setting out of axes before hanging begins;
  • inspection of the first installed areas — these set the quality benchmark;
  • resolving questions on details together with the designers;
  • taking part in interim and final acceptance.

The limits of each party's responsibility are fixed in the contract: the installer is responsible for following the method, and the supervising organisation for the correctness of design decisions and instructions. The full cycle with the company's own crews is described on the page "construction, installation and facade works".

Long facades: how to achieve even joints and a uniform tone

On a long, low building the eye readily picks up any deviation from the horizontal and any difference in shade between adjacent components. For such projects, tolerances, the inspection procedure and the rules for making up batches are therefore defined in advance.

TaskHow it is usually handled
Uniform tonecomponents for one plane are produced in consecutive batches, and a reference sample is approved before production starts
Even jointsthe layout is tied to axes and openings, the joint width is set in the design and checked with gauges
Thermal movementmovement joints and sliding fixings are provided
Repeatability of ornamentidentical elements are cast from the same moulds

How the supply of a large volume of GFRC is planned for one season

With a volume of thousands of square metres and work confined to a single construction season, production and installation run in parallel: components are dispatched in phases in the order in which they are hung. To make this possible, the casting schedule is drawn up from the installation drawing, not the other way round.

The key conditions are early approval of samples and working documentation, a time allowance for making models and moulds, and steady deliveries without components piling up on site. TECHNOFORMA's production site is designed for more than 20,000 m² of GFRC per month, which makes it possible to run large projects without long pauses between batches.

Under installation supervision, this is supplemented by coordinating the programme with the organisation carrying out the works: deliveries are planned to match the actual pace of its crews, and production must learn of any slippage on site in advance. Otherwise components either accumulate in the site store or the crews stand idle waiting for the next batch.

What matters to the client of a public building facade

For universities, institutions and other public buildings, documents for materials, a predictable programme and ease of maintenance come first. These requirements are best set out in the brief before a contractor is chosen.

  • confirmation that the cladding is non-combustible and that the system meets fire requirements;
  • an agreed programme linked to funding and commissioning dates;
  • as-built documentation and a clear procedure for acceptance of facade works;
  • resistance of the lower zone to mechanical impact and ease of cleaning;
  • care and inspection instructions for the facilities management team.

FAQ

Who is responsible for quality under installation supervision?

The organisation carrying out the installation is responsible for the execution of the works and for following the method. The supervising organisation is responsible for design decisions, briefing and technical instructions. The exact division is set out in the contract.

Is GFRC suitable for educational buildings?

Yes. The material is non-combustible (NG class), frost-resistant and allows both large panels and ornament of complex shape to be produced. Compliance of the facade system with the requirements for public buildings is confirmed by the design and by the documents for the materials used.

Why is a reference area of the facade needed?

On the reference area the actual appearance of joints, the tone and the quality of installation are agreed before bulk work begins. The remaining work zones are then accepted against it, which reduces the number of disputes at acceptance.
