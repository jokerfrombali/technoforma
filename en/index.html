What a GFRC manufacturer produces in series and what is made to bespoke moulds

The factory range falls into two groups: repeating elements cast in series from a single mould, and one-off pieces made to the architect's sketch. The ratio between the two determines both the facade budget and the lead time before production starts: the more repeats there are, the sooner the tooling pays for itself.

Serial items usually include GFRC facade panels, window surrounds, string courses and cornices of constant section. Capitals, portals, bas-reliefs, corner pieces and make-up elements remain one-off items. The products are thin-walled shells, typically 10–20 mm thick, with cast-in fixings, so on high-rise buildings they are hung on a subframe without strengthening the load-bearing walls.

A separate group comprises small architectural forms and landscaping elements: planters, benches, guarding. They are cast using the same technology as the facade decoration, so the grounds of a development can be finished in the same material and texture as the building.

GFRC, natural stone or porcelain stoneware: which to choose for a facade

There is no single right material: the choice is driven by the architecture, the number of storeys, the budget and the amount of relief required. Materials are often combined on one building — stone at the plinth and entrances, glass fibre reinforced concrete for the decoration and upper storeys, porcelain stoneware on the plain wall areas.

A detailed comparison is given in the article on how to choose facade cladding. If the design is built around stone, it is best to start with the type of stone: for example, travertine for a facade gives a warm light tone, whereas granite performs better at the plinth.

Which buildings suit full-cycle facades

Most orders are for multi-apartment residential complexes, business centres, hotels and public buildings. Each type has its own priorities, and the make-up of the facade solution changes accordingly.

  • Residential complexes. Many repeating elements and a tight handover schedule for each block; serial production and delivery in work sections matter most.
  • Business centres. Large-format panels, precise geometry, restrained relief.
  • Hotels. A distinctive image and resistance to intensive use and, in resort areas, to humid air.
  • Public buildings. Prestigious architecture with stricter requirements for fire safety and durability.
  • Boutique and private houses. Small volumes with a high share of bespoke decoration.

An example of a large residential project is the Dubai Houses residential complex in Grozny: 30 blocks and 70,000 m² of GFRC.

What to prepare for a facade estimate

For a preliminary estimate, dimensioned architectural drawings of the elevations and an idea of the intended materials are sufficient. The more complete the input data, the fewer assumptions go into the estimate and the closer the first figure is to the final one.

  1. Elevation drawings, plans and sections in DWG or PDF; a 3D model if available.
  2. Sketches or visualisations showing the decoration, with the desired textures and colours.
  3. Information on the load-bearing walls and frame: material, condition, number of storeys.
  4. The region and site address, so that wind and seismic loads and logistics can be taken into account.
  5. The scope of work: products only, products with installation, or the complete facade with subframe and insulation.
  6. The desired programme and the sequence of blocks.

If there are no working drawings yet, they are developed by the facade design department on the basis of a sketch or an architectural concept.

How do you check a GFRC manufacturer before signing a contract?

The most reliable way to assess a factory is by three things: its actual capacity, an in-house design department and completed buildings that can be seen in person. A palm-sized sample says little about how the supplier will cope with tens of thousands of square metres.

It is worth visiting the factory and looking at the moulding area, the finished goods store and the way dimensional control and product marking are organised. Ask for the material test reports and a reference texture sample that will serve as the benchmark at acceptance. A separate question is who is responsible for installation: when the products, the subframe and the works are covered by a single contract, there are fewer disputed areas at the interface between contractors. The full list of criteria is set out in the article on choosing a GFRC manufacturer.

When comparing quotations, make sure they cover the same scope: mould making, cast-in parts, packing, delivery, subframe and installation. A quotation without tooling or fixings looks cheaper, but the missing items will appear in the budget later anyway. The most convenient approach is to compare the cost of the finished facade per square metre and, separately, the cost of linear and one-off products.

FAQ

How does GFRC differ from architectural concrete?

GFRC is reinforced with glass fibre, so elements can be thin-walled and light while remaining strong. Architectural concrete is a broader term; for facade mouldings and panels GFRC is more practical thanks to its low weight and freedom of form.

How much do architectural concrete and GFRC mouldings cost?

The price depends on the range of elements, complexity of shapes, surface texture, volume and scope of work. We prepare an exact quote based on the project drawings.

Do you make small items such as pillar caps or fireplace surrounds?

Our main focus is building facades, but custom GFRC items can be discussed on request.

Do you work outside Moscow?

Yes, most of our completed projects are in the North Caucasus; supply and installation are possible anywhere in Russia.

Can GFRC products be ordered without installation?

Yes, each line of work can be ordered separately. In that case the installation contractor receives the layout drawings, the product marking and the fixing details. If installation is carried out by a third-party contractor, installation supervision is available — technical guidance and control by the manufacturer.

Is a finished design needed in order to request an estimate?

No. A sketch, a visualisation or dimensioned architectural drawings of the elevations are enough for a first estimate. The working drawings, with the product layout and fixing details, are prepared by the company's design department.

What does the production lead time depend on?

On the volume, the number of different moulds and the complexity of the relief. At the start, most of the time goes on design and on making the models and moulds, after which repeating products are turned out in a steady flow. The dispatch schedule is tied to the installation sequence on site.
