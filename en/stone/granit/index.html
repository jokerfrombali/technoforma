Granite for Facades and Plinths

Granite is the strongest and most durable of all cladding stones. TECHNOFORMA Group supplies granite for facades, plinths, steps and landscaping and carries out installation, including ventilated granite facades.

Why granite

Granite is an igneous rock composed of quartz, feldspar and mica. It is dense, has low water absorption and is frost- and abrasion-resistant, which is why it is chosen for plinths, porches, steps, paving and facades in high-wear areas. Colours include grey, pink, red, black and greenish; finishes include polished, honed, flamed, bush-hammered and split.

Ventilated granite facades

Large granite slabs are installed on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure: the stone acts as a protective screen with insulation behind it. The weight of the granite is taken into account when calculating the substructure and anchors; this is done by our design engineers. More about installation on the ventilated facade installation page.

Applications

Plinths

The most vulnerable part of the facade, exposed to splashes, de-icing agents and mechanical damage; in terms of durability, granite has practically no alternative here.

Facades

Cladding of lower floors, entrance areas or the entire building.

Landscaping

Steps, porches, kerbs, setts.

Flexible and liquid granite

Flexible granite for exterior facades and liquid granite are finishing coatings, not natural stone. The company supplies and installs natural granite.

Stock and supply

How ordering works

  1. Drawings and brief. You send facade or interior drawings, photos of the building and your preferences for colour and texture.
  2. Estimate. We select stone from stock or to order and calculate areas, elements and logistics; our surveyors visit the site if needed.
  3. Design. 3D visualisation, a layout with pattern matching and slab marking, fixing details.
  4. Production. Sawing and surface finishing on our own equipment, quality control, marking according to the layout.
  5. Delivery. From the stone yards in the Moscow Region or via the company's regional warehouses.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division fixes the stone with adhesive or on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure; designer's supervision is provided.

What affects the price

  • origin and grade of the stone: from stock or supplied to order;
  • slab thickness and format;
  • surface finish: polished, honed, bush-hammered, sawn, split;
  • number of non-standard elements: corners, reveals, profiles, steps;
  • installation method and working height;
  • volume and logistics.
  • slab weight and requirements for the ventilated facade substructure.

We calculate the exact cost from drawings and quantities; installation is priced after a site inspection.

Properties of granite for facades

Granite is valued for facades because it combines high strength with near-zero water absorption: the stone takes up almost no moisture, so it is not damaged by frost and soils only slightly. Typical ranges are given below; figures for a particular quarry may differ and are confirmed by the batch test report.

The class of the stone by specific activity of natural radionuclides is checked separately: first-class granite is used for residential and public buildings, as stated in the batch documents.

Which granite finish suits walls, plinths and steps?

The finish determines safety as well as appearance: any treatment is acceptable on vertical surfaces, but only a slip-resistant one on horizontal surfaces. The colour of the same stone changes noticeably with the finish, so samples are viewed in the finish actually chosen.

FinishSurfaceWhere it is used
Polishedmirror-like, saturated colourwalls, portals, columns, interiors

Rock-facedsplit, in reliefplinths, rustication, retaining walls

Two or three finishes are usually combined on one building: a textured plinth, calm walls and polished accents at the entrance. The options are examined in more detail in the article on types of stone finishing.

Slab thickness and fixing methods for granite

Because of its considerable weight, granite above plinth level is almost always fixed mechanically, and slab thickness is set by calculation. As a guide: 20–30 mm for the plinth and lowest tier on adhesive with additional fixing, 30–40 mm for a ventilated facade, and 30–50 mm for steps and parapet copings.

In a ventilated system the slabs are set on clips or on concealed fixings — in kerfs in the edges or on undercut anchors at the back. The load from the stone is transferred through brackets to the load-bearing wall, so the substrate is checked and anchor pull-out tests are carried out before work starts. The scope of work and substrate requirements are described on the page installation of ventilated facades.

Weight is also taken into account when comparing options: for the upper parts of tall buildings heavy stone is sometimes replaced with lighter cladding, with granite kept on the lower storeys. The article natural stone or porcelain stoneware helps to assess the difference in properties and cost of ownership.

Where granite is used on facades: building types

Granite is used on facades where the cladding has to withstand heavy use and keep its appearance without frequent maintenance. The quantity of stone and its position on the building depend on the purpose of the project.

  • Residential complexes — plinth, ground and first floors, entrance areas and porches; higher up, stone usually gives way to lighter materials.
  • Business centres and hotels — a ventilated facade of large slabs, polished portals, cladding of columns and lobbies.
  • Public buildings — staircases, podiums, ramps and forecourts, where resistance to wear and de-icing agents matters.
  • Private houses — plinth, steps, sills and parapets, paving at the entrance.

On projects with heavy footfall granite justifies itself through its service life: its upkeep and repair costs are minimal compared with soft stones.

A granite plinth: how to detail it

A granite plinth lasts for decades provided water does not get behind the slabs or stand in the joints. Durability here is governed by the detailing, not only by the properties of the stone.

  • The top of the plinth is covered with a flashing or a projecting course with a drip.
  • The bottom row does not bear on the perimeter paving: a movement gap is left so that ground movement is not passed on to the cladding.
  • Joints are filled with a flexible frost-resistant compound rather than rigid mortar.
  • For adhesive fixing, compounds designed for heavy stone with low water absorption are used.
  • Next to the pavement a flamed or bush-hammered surface is chosen — it shows fewer traces of de-icing agents.

Construction options and the choice of stone are covered in the article plinth cladding in natural stone. Where a dark, uniform stone is needed, basalt cladding is considered as an alternative.

Common mistakes when choosing granite

Granite forgives a great deal in service, but mistakes at the ordering stage are costly to put right. The most common are these:

  • the quantity is ordered with no allowance for trimming and tone matching, and the top-up arrives from another batch in a different shade;
  • polished slabs are laid on a porch and become slippery in rain and frost;
  • the weight of the cladding is not included in the calculation of the subframe, or of the existing wall in a refurbishment;
  • the slab format is not coordinated with the layout of windows and joints, which produces narrow make-up pieces;
  • light grades are fixed with unsuitable adhesive and stain around the slab edges.

A layout with numbered slabs prepared before cutting and an approved reference sample help to avoid this. An example of stone combined with ornament is the Dinastia club house in Kislovodsk, where granite complements GFRC cladding.

Maintaining granite cladding

Granite does not need regular protection in the way porous stones do: periodic washing with water and a neutral cleaner is sufficient. A water repellent is applied selectively — on light grades and in areas where oil or rust stains are possible.

Every few years the joints, fixings and flashings are inspected: damaged sealant is made good, and chipped slabs in a ventilated system are replaced one at a time without dismantling adjacent rows. For this purpose a small reserve of slabs from the same batch is included in the delivery.

FAQ

How much does granite facade cladding cost?

The price depends on the quarry, colour, thickness, finish and volume; installation is priced after a site inspection.

Can a ventilated facade be made from granite?

Yes, on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure designed for the loads from the stone.

Which granite finish is suitable for steps?

Non-slip finishes: flamed or bush-hammered.

What thickness of granite is needed for a plinth?

Usually 20–30 mm for adhesive fixing with additional mechanical fixing, and 30 mm or more in a ventilated system. The exact figure depends on slab format and is set by the design.

How much does granite cladding weigh?

At a density of about 2.6–2.7 t/m³, a 30 mm slab weighs roughly 80 kg/m² and a 40 mm slab about 105–110 kg/m². This load is included in the fixing calculation.

Does granite change colour over time?

Hardly at all: the stone is resistant to ultraviolet light and moisture. Its appearance is altered by dirt and streaks, which is why flashings, drips and periodic washing matter.
