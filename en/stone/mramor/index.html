Marble for Facades and Interiors

Marble is the stone of prestigious facades, entrance areas and interiors. TECHNOFORMA Group supplies marble for exterior facade cladding and interiors and carries out layout design and installation.

Marble on the facade

Marble is a metamorphic rock of recrystallised calcite or dolomite. It is striking on a facade but requires careful selection: a polished surface loses its gloss outdoors over time, and some varieties are sensitive to acid rain and temperature swings. For exterior cladding, dense varieties with a matt finish (honed, ground, bush-hammered) are therefore chosen, while strongly veined marble is more often used on entrance areas, portals and sheltered surfaces.

Flexible and liquid marble

Flexible marble for facades is a thin layer of stone chips or veneer on a flexible backing, and liquid marble is a decorative plaster with marble chips. These are finishing materials, not natural stone: they have a different durability and a different look. The company works with natural stone; if lightweight decoration with a stone texture is needed, we use glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC).

Marble panels and inlays

Marble panels and Florentine mosaic (pietra dura) are one-off work to a sketch: stones of different colours are selected and cut to the pattern. Such elements are made to order for interiors, entrance areas and accent surfaces.

Interiors

Floors, walls, stairs, window sills, fireplace surrounds, worktops: this is where marble reveals itself fully. The layout and pattern matching are shown in a 3D visualisation before purchase.

Stock and supply

TECHNOFORMA Group has been working with natural stone for more than 15 years: it imports stone from India, Turkey, China and Iran, keeps stock from these countries and supplies stone to order from all over the world. The stone yards and production base are located in the Moscow Region, and the company's warehouses in Novorossiysk, Makhachkala, Yekaterinburg and Novosibirsk make delivery to the regions easier. The equipment processes both soft stones (sandstone, shell limestone, dolomite, limestone) and hard ones. Measurements are taken by the company's own surveyors, while layouts and 3D visualisation are prepared by the design department.

How ordering works

  1. Drawings and brief. You send facade or interior drawings, photos of the building and your preferences for colour and texture.
  2. Estimate. We select stone from stock or to order and calculate areas, elements and logistics; our surveyors visit the site if needed.
  3. Design. 3D visualisation, a layout with pattern matching and slab marking, fixing details.
  4. Production. Sawing and surface finishing on our own equipment, quality control, marking according to the layout.
  5. Delivery. From the stone yards in the Moscow Region or via the company's regional warehouses.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division fixes the stone with adhesive or on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure; designer's supervision is provided.

What affects the price

  • origin and grade of the stone: from stock or supplied to order;
  • slab thickness and format;
  • surface finish: polished, honed, bush-hammered, sawn, split;
  • number of non-standard elements: corners, reveals, profiles, steps;
  • installation method and working height;
  • volume and logistics.

We calculate the exact cost from drawings and quantities; installation is priced after a site inspection.

Properties of marble for facades in figures

In density and water absorption marble is close to granite, but it is noticeably softer and sensitive to acids, so it behaves differently outdoors. The figures depend on the quarry and are confirmed by the batch test report.

Density2.6–2.8 t/m³a 30 mm slab weighs about 80–85 kg/m²
Water absorptionusually 0.1–0.7%the stone takes up little water, but the surface reacts to precipitation

The table shows that the limitations of marble stem not from strength but from chemical composition: calcite gradually reacts with acid rain. The deciding factors are therefore the choice of grade and finish and the protection of the surface, which are discussed below.

Which marble should be chosen for external cladding?

Marble for facades is selected by structure: the finer and more uniform the grain and the fewer the cracks and veins, the more stable the stone is outdoors. Dolomitic marbles are generally more resistant to weathering than calcitic ones.

  • White and grey fine-grained grades — the main choice for walls, portals and columns.
  • Coloured grades with pronounced veining — appropriate in sheltered areas: under canopies, in loggias, in entrance recesses.
  • Brecciated and heavily fissured varieties — for interiors only.

With certain grades, thin slabs may bow after years of temperature swings, so a track record for the specific stone and a margin of thickness matter on a facade. The general principles of material selection are described in the article on choosing a facade for the regional climate.

Which finish to choose for outdoor use

For external planes a honed or ground surface is normally used rather than a polished one. Under rain and urban dust, a polish dulls unevenly over time, and patches of differing gloss appear on the facade.

A matt finish ages more quietly and needs no restoration of gloss. For steps and landings a bush-hammered or sandblasted treatment is used, which gives a slip-resistant surface. Polishing is kept for interiors and for elements under cover. The options are compared in the piece on types of stone finishing.

Slab thickness and fixing of marble cladding

Marble slabs on a facade are generally fixed mechanically: on a ventilated subframe or on anchors with an air gap. Thickness is set by calculation — usually 30 mm and above for a ventilated facade, and 20–30 mm for the plinth and lowest tier on adhesive with additional fixing.

Adhesive and grout are chosen specifically for light stone: ordinary cement compounds can stain and alter the shade around the slab edge. Stainless steel fixings are used to rule out rust streaks. The construction of the system is examined in the article on ventilated facades in natural stone.

Pattern layout and slab selection

The veining of marble changes from slab to slab, so the cladding is assembled to a layout approved in advance. Two techniques are common: book-matching, where adjacent slabs from one block are opened symmetrically, and a continuous pattern with veins running on across the joints.

For prominent areas — portals, lobbies, staircases — the slabs are photographed, laid out digitally, and each one is numbered. The allowance for pattern matching is usually larger than for uniform stones and is included in the estimate. The procedure is described in the article stone layout and slab numbering.

Marble care and frequent mistakes

Marble lasts a long time if it is protected from water and not cleaned with aggressive chemicals. After installation, external cladding is treated with a vapour-permeable impregnation, and cleaning is done with neutral products; more detail is given in the article on protecting natural stone.

  • polished marble is used on exposed steps;
  • acidic products are used for washing and leave dull patches;
  • there are no flashings above the cladding, and water runs down the face;
  • the grade is chosen from a photograph, without inspecting the slabs or test data.

Where a light stone with calmer outdoor behaviour is needed, travertine for facades or dense limestone is considered as an alternative.

FAQ

Can marble be used for exterior cladding?

Yes, provided a dense variety, a suitable finish and stone protection are chosen.

Is flexible marble natural stone?

It is a finishing material with a thin stone layer on a flexible backing, not solid stone.

Do you make marble panels?

Yes, to a sketch, made to order.

What thickness of marble is needed for a facade?

Usually 30 mm and above for a ventilated system, and 20–30 mm for the lowest tier on adhesive with additional fixing. The exact figure depends on slab format and stone grade and is determined by calculation.

Why does polished marble go dull outdoors?

Calcite reacts with acid rain and is abraded by dust, so the gloss gradually disappears, and does so unevenly. A honed or ground surface is normally chosen for external planes.

Can marble be selected with a continuous pattern across a whole wall?

Yes. Slabs from one block are laid out to a scheme with book-matched or running veining and numbered before cutting; a larger allowance of stone is provided for this.
