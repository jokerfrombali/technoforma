Natural stone for facades: supply and installation

TECHNOFORMA supplies natural stone for facades, plinths and interiors and carries out turnkey cladding. The company has worked with stone for more than 15 years: it imports stone from India, Turkey, China and Iran, keeps stock from these countries and brings in other stones to order from anywhere in the world. The production base and stone warehouses are near Moscow.

Stone types available

For natural stone facade and plinth cladding we select the stone to suit the task, climate and style of the building:

  • Travertine — a warm, porous texture, a classic for feature facades;
  • Limestone and shell limestone (coquina) — soft stones that are easy to work;
  • Dolomite and sandstone — for walls and plinths;
  • Marble — for portals, panels and interiors;
  • Granite and basalt — hard stones for plinths, steps and paving;
  • Onyx — a decorative stone for backlighting;
  • Dagestan stone — the company has extensive experience with it.

Our equipment processes both soft stones (sandstone, shell limestone, dolomite, limestone) and hard ones.

Natural or artificial stone

Artificial stone, flexible stone and stone-effect cladding tiles cost less up front, but they are different materials, with different durability, texture and frost behaviour. Natural stone does not fade and keeps its appearance over the years. If you need lightweight stone-effect mouldings, an alternative is GFRC with a stone texture.

How an order works

  1. Drawings and brief. You send architectural drawings, a sketch or photos of the facade; if there is no design, our design department will prepare one.
  2. Quote. We calculate the range and volumes and agree the scope of work and delivery schedule.
  3. Design. Design engineers and 3D visualisers issue the working documentation, layout and fixing details.
  4. Production. Stone is selected from stock or ordered from a supplier; slabs are processed and marked according to the layout, with quality control at every stage.
  5. Delivery. Shipping from the production base near Moscow, via company warehouses if needed.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division of more than 100 specialists fits the substructure, insulation and cladding; installation supervision is also available.

What drives the cost

The price of natural facade stone per m² and the cladding rate in the estimate depend on:

  • stone type and origin;
  • slab thickness and surface finish;
  • layout complexity and the number of non-standard elements (corners, reveals, profiles);
  • installation method — adhesive or ventilated rainscreen facade substructure;
  • building height and site conditions;
  • volume and logistics.

The installation cost is determined after a site inspection.

How to choose natural stone for a facade by its properties

Natural stone for a facade is chosen on three indicators: density, water absorption and frost resistance. The denser the stone and the less water it absorbs, the better the cladding withstands freeze-thaw cycles and the lower it can be placed — right down to the plinth and the perimeter paving.

Frost resistance is denoted by the F grade — the number of freeze-thaw cycles the stone withstands without loss of strength. For central and northern regions, where the temperature crosses zero many times in a winter, this indicator matters more than in the south; there, resistance to sun, sea air and sharp daily temperature swings comes to the fore.

Finish and slab thickness: what affects appearance and reliability

The same stone looks different depending on the surface finish. For facades, matt finishes are chosen more often: they produce less glare, hide dust and do not emphasise unevenness in the plane.

  • Polishing brings out the colour and figure, but outdoors it is justified on hard stones; marble and limestone lose their gloss over time.
  • Honing and grinding give an even matt surface — the most versatile option.
  • Flaming and bush-hammering make granite and basalt rough and lighter in tone; these finishes are used on plinths and steps.
  • Split face and rock face create a pronounced relief for plinths, rustication and corners.

The thickness is selected to suit the method of fixing. For adhesive fixing, slabs of 20–30 mm are usually used; for a suspended system, generally 30 mm or more, so that the fixings can be securely accommodated in the edge or on the rear face. A detailed analysis is given in the article on the thickness of cladding slabs.

Adhesive or subframe: how is stone cladding installed?

Stone is fixed in two ways: the 'wet' method — on an adhesive with additional mechanical restraint — and the 'dry' method — on the subframe of a ventilated facade. The choice depends on the height of the building, the slab format, the insulation requirements and the condition of the substrate.

Adhesive fixing is used on plinths, low-rise buildings and small formats. It requires a flat, sound substrate, above-zero temperatures and observance of curing intervals. The suspended method suits multi-storey buildings and large slabs: each slab bears on its own clips, pins or undercut anchors, a ventilated gap remains behind the cladding and the wall is insulated. The slabs do not transfer load to one another, so a damaged element can be replaced individually. The construction of the system is described in the article on the natural stone ventilated facade.

In both cases joints are left between the slabs: they accommodate thermal movement of the stone and the substrate. In seismic areas the fixings are additionally designed for horizontal loads, and the bearing capacity of the anchors is confirmed by on-site testing.

Which stone suits different types of building?

The range of stones and the method of installation depend on the purpose of the building, the number of storeys and the budget. Below are the solutions that most often prove justified.

  • Residential complexes. Stone on the plinth, entrance areas and lower floors, with GFRC or porcelain stoneware above. Less of the expensive material is used, while the zone at eye level looks substantial.
  • Business centres and hotels. Large-format slabs on a subframe, a strict layout, granite for the plinth and steps.
  • Public buildings. Durable stones with a matt finish that withstand intensive use and cleaning.
  • Private houses. Light limestones, dolomites and travertine for walls, dense stone for the plinth and entrance steps.

What to prepare for a stone cladding estimate

For a preliminary estimate, dimensioned elevations and an idea of the stone and colour are sufficient. An exact estimate is drawn up after the layout and, where necessary, a site measure.

  • elevations, plans and sections with levels, window and plinth details;
  • the wall material and the required insulation thickness;
  • the desired stone, finish and slab format, or reference photographs;
  • a list of special elements: corners, reveals, sills, cornices, steps;
  • the region and height of the building, and site access conditions;
  • whether installation is required or supply only, with layout and marking.

Stone is purchased with an allowance for cutting and tone matching; the size of the allowance depends on the figure of the stone and the complexity of the layout.

What mistakes are made when cladding a facade in natural stone?

Most defects in stone cladding are related not to the stone itself but to design and installation decisions. They are easier to prevent at design stage than to remedy on a finished facade.

  • Porous stone on the plinth. Soft limestone or shell limestone in the zone of splashing and de-icing agents quickly darkens and flakes.
  • Installation without movement joints. Slabs laid tight against one another press on each other under temperature changes and spall at the edges.
  • Unsuitable adhesive or mortar. Ordinary cement-based products leave efflorescence and stains on light stone; materials intended for natural stone are required.
  • Fixings in ordinary steel. Corrosion causes rust staining and weakens the fixing, so stainless steel is used.
  • No layout. Without prior selection of the slabs, the figure and tone on the facade turn out patchy.

Looking after a stone facade: protection and care

Stone cladding lasts for decades provided it is protected from prolonged wetting. The main measures are built into the design: flashings and drips on all projections, a damp-proof course at the plinth and drainage of water away from the wall.

Porous stones are treated after installation with a water repellent, which reduces water absorption without altering vapour permeability; the treatment is renewed periodically. Dirt is removed with water and neutral agents; acidic products are not used on limestone, marble or travertine. More detail can be found in the article on protecting natural stone.

FAQ

How much does natural facade stone cost per m²?

The price depends on stone type, thickness, finish and volume; installation is priced after a site inspection. Send us your drawings and we will prepare a quote.

Can I buy plinth cladding stone without installation?

Yes, stone is supplied without installation too; layout and measurement can be ordered separately.

Which stone is best for a plinth?

Dense stones with low water absorption suit plinths — granite, basalt, dense limestones and dolomites.

Do you import stone to order?

Yes. Besides stock from India, Turkey, China and Iran, the company supplies stone to order from anywhere in the world.

What thickness of stone is needed for a ventilated facade?

As a rule, 30 mm or more: this thickness allows the fixings to be securely accommodated in the edge or on the rear face of the slab. The exact value depends on the stone, the slab format, the wind load and the fixing system used, and is established by calculation.

Can a facade be clad in stone in winter?

Installation on a ventilated facade subframe is a dry process and is carried out all year round, subject to restrictions for wind and icing. Adhesive fixing requires above-zero temperatures of the substrate and the air, or the erection of heated enclosures.

How is a uniform tone of stone achieved across the whole facade?

Stone for a project is selected from a single batch and purchased with an allowance. Before installation the slabs are laid out, sorted by shade and figure and marked according to the layout drawing, so that changes in tone are gradual rather than random.
