Dagestan Stone for Facades

Dagestan stone is the collective name for cladding limestones, dolomites and sandstones from the Republic of Dagestan. TECHNOFORMA Group has extensive experience with Dagestan stones and supplies them for facades in Moscow and the regions together with cladding design and installation.

Which stones are called Dagestan stone

Most often these are dense light-coloured limestones and dolomites (white, cream, beige, pinkish, grey), as well as shell limestone and sandstone. Soft stones are easy to cut and profile, so Dagestan stone is used not only for tiles but also for cornices, window surrounds, balusters and carved decoration. Colour and density depend on the quarry, so the stone is selected from samples.

Facade cladding with Dagestan stone

Tiles and slabs

Sawn tiles of various thicknesses for adhesive fixing, or large slabs for ventilated facades.

Textured cladding

Split, rock-face, bush-hammered and rusticated surfaces.

Decoration

Profiled elements, carving, balustrades. Where there is a lot of repeating decoration, some elements can be made from glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) with a stone texture.

Dagestan stone in Moscow

Dagestan stone can be bought in Moscow from the stone yard in the Moscow Region or to order. For projects in the North Caucasus, supply via the company's warehouse in Makhachkala is more convenient. Dagestan stone is installed by the company's own installation division, with adhesive or on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure.

Climate and protection

Soft limestones absorb water, so for central Russia it is important to choose a dense stone, treat it with a water repellent and plan drainage properly; the plinth is best clad with a denser stone.

How the types of Dagestan stone differ: a comparison

Stones with very different properties are sold under a single name — from dense dolomite, which is close to marble in durability, to light, porous shell limestone. Dagestan stone for a facade is therefore chosen not by its trade name but by the type of rock and the density and water absorption of the specific batch.

The stone is usually named after the place of extraction: descriptions mention, for example, Mekegi dolomite, Akusha limestone or Derbent shell limestone. Beds within a single deposit also differ, so it is more reliable to rely on samples and test results. General guidelines are given in the table.

Shell limestoneyellow, golden, pinkishlight, with large pores, high water absorptionwalls in a mild climate, interiors, boundary walls

Formats, thickness and finishes of the tiles

The most common format is sawn tile of fixed height and random length: it is laid in bonded courses, like masonry. This format is economical, because the block is sawn with almost no waste, and it suits private houses and low-rise buildings well.

  • Random-length tile, as a rule 150–300 mm high and 20–30 mm thick — for adhesive fixing.
  • Modular slabs of a set size, 30 mm thick or more — for laying to a grid and for suspended systems.
  • Thick slabs and blocks — for plinths, corners, steps and blanks for profiled details.

The surface is left sawn, or is ground, bush-hammered or split to a rock face. Only dense dolomites and limestones take a polish, and outdoors the gloss fades over time. For light stone, calibration also matters: the more accurately the thickness and height of the tile are maintained, the thinner and more even the joints.

When calculating quantities, account is taken not only of the wall area but also of reveals, corners, and the sill and plinth courses. Random-length tile is ordered with an allowance for cutting and rejection on tone; for a layout with matched figure the allowance is increased. Corner elements are best ordered in one piece or with edges mitred at 45°, so that the edge of the tile is not visible on the arris.

Adhesive or ventilated facade: how is Dagestan stone installed?

For houses of one to three storeys, adhesive fixing is usually chosen; for multi-storey buildings and insulated walls, a suspended system. The decision depends on the height, the condition of the substrate and whether a layer of insulation is needed behind the cladding.

For adhesive fixing the substrate is levelled and primed, and for light stones white adhesives intended for natural stone are used: grey cement adhesive can cause stains that show through porous tile. Large formats and upper courses are additionally secured with stainless steel anchors or cramps. Movement joints are left every few metres in height and length.

On a ventilated facade the slabs bear on clips or concealed fixings in kerfs cut into the edges. Soft stone withstands point loads less well than granite, so the slab thickness, kerf depth and fixing spacing are selected by calculation and checked on samples. In Dagestan and the neighbouring republics the fixings are additionally designed for seismic actions — this is covered in the article on fixing cladding in seismic areas.

Which Dagestan stone should be chosen for a facade in different climates?

The more often the temperature crosses zero in a winter and the damper the climate, the denser the stone needs to be. In the south porous stones are acceptable; in central Russia and further north the choice narrows to dense limestones and dolomites.

  • The North Caucasus and southern Russia. Practically all the stones are suitable, including shell limestone; the main loads are sun, sharp daily temperature swings and heavy rain.
  • Coastal cities. Damp, salty air and wind accelerate the weathering of friable stone, so dense varieties are preferable and water-repellent treatment is essential.
  • Moscow and central Russia. For walls — dolomite and dense limestone with confirmed frost resistance; shell limestone is appropriate in interiors and in sheltered areas.
  • The plinth in any region. The zone of splashing, snow and de-icing agents is best covered with granite, basalt or the densest dolomite.

Regardless of region, the stone needs protection by design — cornice overhangs, flashings, drips — and a damp-proof course that prevents moisture rising from the ground into the cladding.

On projects in Moscow and the regions of central Russia, the stone is stored under cover on pallets before installation and is not laid on adhesive at sub-zero temperatures: moisture remaining in the pores and in the adhesive bed destroys both the tile and its bond to the substrate when it freezes.

Carved ornament: when stone and when GFRC

Window surrounds, cornices, columns, balusters and ornamental panels are carved from the soft Dagestan stones — a craft traditional to the region. Stone carving is justified when the elements are few, unique and seen at close range: at the entrance, on the ground floor, in the interior.

When the same profile is repeated dozens or hundreds of times and the details are raised to a height, it is more rational to cast them in GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete). The mould is taken from the model once, the products are identical, they weigh several times less than stone ones and are fixed on cast-in inserts. Colour and texture are matched to a sample of the stone, so that a surface of natural tile and, for example, GFRC window surrounds read as one material. This combination reduces the load on the walls and shortens the time needed to produce the ornament.

The opposite situation is restoration and one-off commissions: where several lost details of a historic facade have to be reproduced or a bespoke panel made, carving in natural stone remains the most accurate method.

What mistakes are made when cladding in Dagestan stone?

Problems with this stone are almost always related to moisture and the wrong choice of rock. Before purchase and installation, several points are worth checking:

  • porous limestone or shell limestone is laid on the plinth and parapets without protection — the stone becomes wet, darkens and breaks down on freezing;
  • the tile is bonded with grey adhesive or ordinary mortar — stains and efflorescence appear on the light surface;
  • there are no flashings above windows, on string courses or on the parapet — water runs down the wall and leaves streaks;
  • the stone is bought from different batches without an allowance — bands of different tone are visible on the facade;
  • water repellent is applied to damp or dirty stone — moisture is trapped inside;
  • the cladding is laid without joints or anchoring — when the substrate moves, the tile debonds.

How these situations are avoided on completed buildings in the region can be seen on the company's projects in Dagestan, for example the Dominanta residential complex in Makhachkala.

Care: water-repellent treatment, cleaning and repair

Light limestone and dolomite acquire a patina over time, and this is a normal property of natural stone. The aim of care is to prevent deep wetting and biological soiling, not to preserve the as-sawn whiteness.

After installation and complete drying, the cladding is treated with a water repellent; the treatment is renewed once water stops beading on the surface — usually every few years. Dirt is removed with a soft brush, water and neutral agents. Acidic cleaners dissolve carbonate rocks, so they are not used on limestone or dolomite. Small chips are made good with repair compounds matching the colour of the stone, and badly damaged tiles are replaced. The choice of impregnations is covered in the article on protecting natural stone.

FAQ

How much does Dagestan facade stone cost per m²?

The price depends on the stone type, quarry, thickness, finish and volume; installation is priced after a site inspection.

Where can I buy Dagestan stone in Moscow?

From the company's stone yard in the Moscow Region or to order.

Do you install Dagestan stone?

Yes, with our own installation division.

Is Dagestan stone suitable for the Moscow climate?

Yes, provided a dense stone is selected and protected from moisture.

What thickness of Dagestan stone is needed for facade cladding?

For adhesive fixing, tile 20–30 mm thick is usually used; for a ventilated facade, slabs of 30 mm or more, and for soft stones the thickness is often increased. The exact value depends on the density of the stone, the slab format and the method of fixing, and is established in the design.

Does Dagestan stone darken over time?

Yes, light limestones and dolomites gradually acquire a warmer tone and a patina. The areas where water runs down the wall darken most, so flashings, drips and timely water-repellent treatment help to keep the colour of the facade even.

Can Dagestan stone be combined with GFRC ornament?

Yes. The wall surfaces are clad in natural tile, while repeating cornices, window surrounds and string courses are cast in GFRC with colour and texture matched to a sample. The ornament is lighter and quicker to produce, and the facade reads as a whole.
