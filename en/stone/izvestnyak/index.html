What limestone is

Limestone is a sedimentary rock composed mainly of calcite. It forms from the remains of marine organisms and chemical deposits, so it can be dense and uniform or porous with shell inclusions. Soft limestone is easy to cut and profile, which makes it convenient for decoration.

Is shell limestone a limestone?

Yes. Shell limestone (coquina) is a limestone made up mainly of shells and shell fragments cemented with calcium carbonate. It is of marine origin, formed on the beds of former warm seas. It is lighter and more porous than dense limestone; see the shell limestone page for details.

Types of facade limestone

White limestone

A light stone for classical and neoclassical facades; it combines well with GFRC decoration.

Dagestan limestone

Dense light and cream limestones from the North Caucasus; the company has extensive experience with them, see the Dagestan stone page.

Imported limestones

From stock of stones from India, Turkey, China and Iran, or to order.

Formats

Limestone facade tiles of various thicknesses, large slabs for ventilated facades, profiled elements such as cornices, string courses, window sills and steps. For plinths, dense varieties with low water absorption are selected and treated with a water repellent.

  1. Drawings and brief. You send facade or interior drawings, photos of the building and your preferences for colour and texture.
  2. Estimate. We select stone from stock or to order and calculate areas, elements and logistics; our surveyors visit the site if needed.
  3. Design. 3D visualisation, a layout with pattern matching and slab marking, fixing details.
  4. Production. Sawing and surface finishing on our own equipment, quality control, marking according to the layout.
  5. Delivery. From the stone yards in the Moscow Region or via the company's regional warehouses.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division fixes the stone with adhesive or on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure; designer's supervision is provided.

Properties of limestone for facades: what to look for in the stone documents

Limestone for facades is selected on three figures — density, water absorption and frost resistance. The spread between quarries is wider for this stone than for granite or marble, so stone of the same colour may suit a plinth in the Moscow region or be fit only for interiors.

For temperate and northern regions, dense grades with a confirmed frost rating are generally used. What lies behind that rating and how to read the report is explained in the article on frost resistance of facade materials.

Which limestone suits plinths, walls and ornament?

Different demands are made on stone within one building: the plinth works in damp conditions and under impact, the walls under rain and sun, and ornament with thin sections. The grade is therefore selected zone by zone rather than once for the whole project.

  • Plinth and porch — only dense limestone with low water absorption; where there is doubt, this zone is made in granite or dense dolomite, keeping the overall tone.
  • Wall planes — stone of medium to high density, uniform in colour within a batch.
  • Cornices, string courses, architraves — fine-grained grades without large shell voids, which hold a sharp profile edge.
  • Interiors and sheltered areas — more porous and decorative varieties are acceptable.

Where a warm sandy shade with pronounced bedding is wanted, sandstone for facades is considered alongside limestone: the stones are close in colour range but differ in composition and behaviour.

How limestone is fixed: adhesive, anchors or a ventilated system

The fixing method depends on building height, slab thickness and the presence of insulation. On low-rise projects, tiles 20–30 mm thick are bonded with adhesive for natural stone, with mechanical fixing added above ground-floor level. For insulated walls and large formats a ventilated facade with slabs of 30 mm and above is used.

The weak point of soft stone is the fixing zone: a kerf for a clip or a hole for an anchor weakens the edge. For limestone, therefore, the thickness and the distance from the kerf to the edge are set with a margin, and the capacity of the fixing is confirmed by calculation or testing. Massive profiled pieces bear on the wall or are fixed to stainless steel embedded parts.

A comparison of the arrangements and their limitations is given in the article facade cladding in natural stone.

Which surface finishes are used

Limestone takes mechanical finishing well, but only the densest grades hold a mirror polish. Matt and textured finishes are more common on facades: dirt and the natural ageing of the stone are less noticeable on them.

  • Sawn and ground — an even matt surface for walls and reveals.
  • Honed — a smooth finish that brings out the pattern, without gloss.
  • Bush-hammered — a rough, slip-resistant surface for steps and plinths.
  • Antiqued — rounded edges and soft relief for facades in a historical idiom.
  • Rock-faced — a split, textured finish for rustication and plinth courses.

How limestone ages and how to look after it

Over time limestone darkens slightly and acquires a patina — a normal characteristic of the stone, and one that many architects build into the character of a building. The problem is not patina but streaks, biological growth and efflorescence, all of which are linked to water.

Protection rests on three measures: flashings and drips on all projecting elements, treatment with a vapour-permeable water repellent after installation, and its renewal at the interval stated by the manufacturer. The stone is cleaned with water at moderate pressure and neutral products; acidic cleaners are unsuitable for carbonate stone. If a white deposit has appeared on the surface, the causes and removal methods are described in the piece on efflorescence on stone and concrete.

Limestone and GFRC on one facade

Combining natural stone on the flat areas with ornament in glass fibre reinforced concrete reduces the load on the walls and shortens the production time for complex pieces. Thin-walled GFRC elements are several times lighter than stone profiles of the same section and reproduce the texture and tone of limestone.

This approach is justified where there are many identical elements — window surrounds, cornices, string courses between storeys. For example, GFRC mouldings and string courses are cast in moulds in series, while the wall plane is clad in stone tiles. Colour is agreed against samples of both materials before production begins.

Common mistakes in limestone cladding

Most complaints about limestone on facades arise from the wrong choice of grade or from simplified detailing. The main mistakes are:

  • porous stone is placed on the plinth or next to the pavement, where moisture and de-icing agents break it down;
  • the horizontal surfaces of profiles have no fall and no drip;
  • slabs are bedded in cement mortar without regard to the risk of efflorescence and staining;
  • water repellent is applied to damp stone, or a film-forming product is chosen;
  • stone from different batches is not laid out by tone before fixing.

Three steps before purchase help to avoid these errors: requesting the test report for the chosen grade, approving a reference sample in the required finish, and preparing a slab layout with numbering. For plinths and parapets the frost rating is checked separately and the top faces are protected from water.

FAQ

Is limestone frost-resistant?

Dense varieties are; frost resistance depends on the quarry, so the stone is selected to suit the climate.

What is the difference between limestone and shell limestone?

Shell limestone is a type of limestone made of shells; it is more porous and lighter.

Do you supply Dagestan limestone?

Yes, the company has extensive experience with Dagestan stones.

What thickness of limestone is needed for a facade?

Usually 20–30 mm for adhesive fixing, 30 mm and above for a ventilated system, and 30–40 mm or more for plinths and steps. The exact figure depends on the density of the grade and the slab format and is set by the design.

Does limestone need to be treated with a water repellent?

For external cladding, generally yes. A vapour-permeable impregnation reduces water absorption and soiling; the treatment is repeated at the interval set by the product manufacturer.

Can limestone be polished?

Dense fine-grained grades take a polish; porous ones do not. On facades a ground, honed or bush-hammered surface is more usual.
