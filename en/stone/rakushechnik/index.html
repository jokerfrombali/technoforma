Shell Limestone for Facades

Shell limestone (coquina) is a warm, textured stone with visible shells, popular in southern Russia. TECHNOFORMA Group supplies shell limestone for facade and interior finishing and carries out the cladding.

What shell limestone is

Shell limestone is a type of limestone formed from the shells of marine molluscs and their fragments, cemented with calcium carbonate. Descriptions of the stone usually start with its porosity: it is light, retains heat well and is easy to saw and work. Colours range from white and light cream to beige and golden.

Where it is quarried

Shell limestone is quarried where ancient warm seas once were: in Crimea, the Caucasus, Central Asia (including around Aktau), Turkey and other countries. Each quarry gives its own shade and density, so the stone is selected from samples.

Use in construction

Facade cladding

Shell limestone facade tiles, sawn or split, in various thicknesses; fixed with adhesive or on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure.

Blocks

Shell limestone wall blocks are used in low-rise construction in the south; tiles are used for facade cladding.

Interiors and landscaping

Walls, fireplaces, stairs, paths and retaining walls.

Special considerations

Shell limestone is porous and absorbs water, so on facades exposed to freeze-thaw cycles it needs a water-repellent treatment and proper drainage, and the plinth is better clad with a denser stone such as granite. Shell grit for poultry, aquariums or feed is a different product; the company supplies cladding stone.

  1. Drawings and brief. You send facade or interior drawings, photos of the building and your preferences for colour and texture.
  2. Estimate. We select stone from stock or to order and calculate areas, elements and logistics; our surveyors visit the site if needed.

  5. Delivery. From the stone yards in the Moscow Region or via the company's regional warehouses.

Properties of shell limestone: density, strength, water absorption

The properties of shell limestone depend on the quarry more than those of most cladding rocks: a stone with the same name may be a lightweight walling material or a dense slab suitable for external finishing. Selection is therefore based on the test results of the batch rather than on averaged reference data.

Densityroughly 1,100 to 2,300 kg/m³loose grades go into blocks, dense ones into cladding tiles

The values are given as orders of magnitude and are confirmed by the supplier's documents. The page on limestone for facades helps to compare shell limestone with other carbonate rocks.

Which shell limestone suits a facade and which does not?

Dense, fine-pored grades with a uniform structure, free of large cavities and clay seams, are suitable for external cladding. Loose, coarse-pored stone that crumbles easily in the hand is left for interiors, decorative inserts under a canopy and landscaping.

Several signs are checked when a batch is selected:

  • the fracture is dense, the shells are firmly cemented and do not fall out;
  • the slab rings clearly when tapped, with no dull areas;
  • the edges are even after sawing, without crumbling;
  • a drop of water is not absorbed instantly;
  • colour and pore size are similar within the batch.

Dense varieties from the North Caucasus are described on the Dagestan stone page: some of these rocks are close to shell limestone in structure and have long been used in local construction. Wall tile thickness is usually taken as 20–40 mm, and more for profiled elements, allowing for the strength of the specific grade.

The choice of face finishes is small, but it changes the look of the wall noticeably. Sawn tiles give an even plane with an open shell pattern, honed tiles look calmer and collect less dust, and split-face tiles emphasise relief and suit accent areas. As a rule, this stone does not take a polish because of its porosity.

How shell limestone tiles are fixed to the wall

Facade finishing with shell limestone is carried out in two ways: with adhesive on a solid substrate or on a subframe with an air gap. Porous stone has one particular feature — it bonds well with adhesive but resists point loads poorly, so fixing details are chosen more cautiously than for granite.

  1. Substrate preparation. The wall is levelled, cleaned and primed; adhesive-fixed stone cladding is, as a rule, not applied over insulation.
  2. Layout. Slabs are sorted by shade and pore size, and joints and make-up pieces are set out.
  3. Installation. Adhesive for natural stone is applied to both the wall and the tile; large formats are additionally secured with anchors.
  4. Joints. They are filled with flexible grout or left open in a ventilated system.
  5. Protection. Once dry, the surface is treated with a water repellent.

In ventilated systems for soft stone, fixings with an enlarged bearing area and thicker slabs are used. The general principles are set out in the overview of stone cladding installation methods.

Moisture protection: what goes into the design

The durability of shell limestone is determined by how much water enters the pores and how quickly it leaves. The task of the design is to prevent the stone from remaining wet before frost and to rule out constant wetting from below and above.

  • Cornice overhangs, flashings and drips above every horizontal projection.
  • Raising the cladding above the perimeter paving and a damp-proof course; below this, a plinth in dense stone is provided.
  • Coping elements on parapets with a fall and an overhang.
  • A vapour-permeable impregnation that is renewed during service.
  • Drainage from air conditioners, balconies and downpipes kept away from the wall plane.

A white deposit on new cladding is most often caused by salts from masonry mortars and adhesives. Causes and remedies are examined in the article on efflorescence on stone.

Typical mistakes when working with shell limestone

Problems with this stone almost always arise because it is treated like a dense rock. Below are the mistakes encountered most frequently.

  • Buying the cheapest walling grade for external cladding.
  • Installing it down to ground level, in the splash zone and where snow is piled.
  • Film-forming lacquers instead of vapour-permeable products: moisture is trapped inside and the surface peels.
  • High-pressure washing, which knocks out weak inclusions.
  • Thin large-format slabs on point fixings.
  • No movement joints on long walls.

Most of the risks listed are removed at design stage: zones where porous stone is permissible are assigned, the adhesive and impregnation grades are specified, and drainage details are shown. On site it remains to monitor the moisture of the substrate and not to work in rain or at sub-zero temperatures.

Where shell limestone is appropriate: building types and material combinations

Shell limestone for facades is chosen more often in southern regions — for hotels, health resorts, low-rise residential developments and private houses, where a warm tone and an appearance traditional for the place are important. In temperate climates it is used more selectively: on sheltered wall areas, in loggias, at entrances under a canopy.

On a single building the stone is usually combined with other materials. The plinth and steps are made of granite or basalt, the wall planes of shell limestone, and the profiled decoration of GFRC toned to the stone. For instance, GFRC window surrounds give a crisp profile and protect the reveal from water without loading the wall. Indoors there are fewer restrictions: shell limestone is used to finish lobbies, staircase walls and fireplaces.

What is needed for an estimate and how the budget is formed

The cladding estimate is made up of the stone, cutting, fixings, protective products, delivery and installation. The share of the material itself is usually lower for shell limestone than for granite or marble, but batch selection and an allowance for trimming and rejects need more attention.

For an estimate it is sufficient to send dimensioned elevation drawings and to state the wall material, building height and region of construction. Photographs of the site and a sample or reference of the desired shade are also useful. If the cladding is combined with decoration and insulation, they are calculated together so that details and delivery dates are coordinated.

Before the main volume is launched, it is useful to approve samples from the batch that will go to the site and to lay a control area of wall. The shade, joint width and colour, and the appearance of the surface after impregnation are approved on it. Such a fragment serves as a benchmark at handover and avoids disputes over permissible tonal variation.

FAQ

How much do shell limestone facade tiles cost?

The price depends on the quarry, density, thickness and finish; the estimate is based on drawings.

Is shell limestone a limestone?

Yes, it is a limestone composed of shells.

Is shell limestone suitable for cold climates?

Yes, provided a dense stone is selected, treated with a water repellent and protected from standing water.

How thick should shell limestone tiles for a facade be?

Tiles of 20–40 mm are usually used for walls. The specific value depends on the density of the grade, the slab format and the fixing method: in a ventilated system the thickness is, as a rule, greater than for adhesive fixing.

How should a shell limestone facade be cleaned?

With a soft brush, water at moderate pressure and neutral cleaners for limestone rocks. Acid products dissolve calcium carbonate and are unsuitable for this stone. After cleaning it is advisable to renew the protective impregnation.

Can shell limestone be combined with wall insulation?

Yes, as part of a ventilated facade: the insulation is fixed to the wall and the slabs are hung on a subframe with a cavity. Dense stone of increased thickness and fixings designed for porous rock are selected for this purpose.
