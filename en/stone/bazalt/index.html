Properties

Basalt is an extrusive igneous rock and one of the densest and strongest cladding stones. It has low water absorption and high frost and wear resistance. Its colour ranges from dark grey to almost black; after flaming or bush-hammering the surface becomes lighter and rough, while polishing gives it a deep dark tone.

Applications

Plinths and lower floors

Where cladding suffers from moisture, de-icing agents and impacts.

Facades

Accent surfaces and contrasting elements combined with light-coloured stone or glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC).

Paving and steps

Setts, slabs, kerbs and steps with an anti-slip finish.

Working with basalt

Basalt is a hard stone and more difficult to process than soft limestones; the company's equipment also handles hard stones. The weight of the slabs is taken into account when designing the ventilated rainscreen facade substructure and anchoring.

Basalt and other materials

Basalt insulation and basalt fibre are different products made from molten basalt. This page is about natural basalt cladding stone.

How ordering works

  1. Drawings and brief. You send facade or interior drawings, photos of the building and your preferences for colour and texture.
  2. Estimate. We select stone from stock or to order and calculate areas, elements and logistics; our surveyors visit the site if needed.
  3. Design. 3D visualisation, a layout with pattern matching and slab marking, fixing details.
  4. Production. Sawing and surface finishing on our own equipment, quality control, marking according to the layout.
  5. Delivery. From the stone yards in the Moscow Region or via the company's regional warehouses.
  6. Installation. Our own installation division fixes the stone with adhesive or on a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure; designer's supervision is provided.

Properties of basalt for cladding in figures

Basalt for cladding is valued for its combination of high density, almost zero water absorption and resistance to abrasion. On these indicators it is comparable to granite, and in uniformity of colour it often surpasses it: basalt has no coarse grain, and the surface looks even and calm.

The values in the table are indicative. Stone from different deposits varies in porosity and shade, so test results are requested for the specific batch.

Which finish and thickness of basalt should be chosen?

The finish is selected according to where the stone is used: on vertical surfaces appearance matters, on horizontal ones slip resistance. The thickness depends on the slab format and the method of fixing.

  • Honed and ground — a matt dark-grey surface for facades and plinths; dust and streaks are less noticeable on it than on a polished one.
  • Polished — a deep black tone for portals, inserts and interiors; outdoors it requires more frequent washing.
  • Flamed and bush-hammered — rough and lighter in tone; used on steps, landings and ramps.
  • Sawn and split — for setts, kerbs and plinths with a pronounced relief.

For wall cladding, slabs 20–30 mm thick are usually used with adhesive and 30 mm or more on a subframe; for steps and paving they are thicker, depending on the load and the base. All the surface options are described in the article on types of stone finish.

How basalt slabs are fixed on facades and plinths

Because of its considerable weight, basalt cladding on a facade is more often installed on the subframe of a ventilated facade, and on a plinth with adhesive and additional mechanical restraint. In both cases the load from the stone is allowed for in the calculation in advance.

Dense stone absorbs almost no water, so the adhesive must be designed for substrates with low water absorption, and the rear face of the slabs is cleaned of slurry after sawing. On a subframe the slabs bear on clips, pins or undercut anchors; the bracket spacing and anchor type are determined by calculation and pull-out tests. The bottom course of the plinth is separated from the perimeter paving by a joint so that ground movement is not transferred to the cladding. More detail can be found in the article on cladding a plinth in natural stone.

Basalt or granite: which to choose for plinths and steps?

Both stones are suitable for the most heavily loaded zones of a building; the choice is usually determined by colour, figure and the availability of the required format. Basalt gives a uniform dark tone without grain, while granite offers a wide palette from light grey to red and black with a pronounced crystalline structure.

  • where a neutral dark surface is needed to set off a light facade, basalt is chosen;
  • where a light or coloured plinth, large slabs or a polished finish are needed, granite for facades and plinths is more often suitable;
  • both stones are suitable for steps and paving, provided they have a slip-resistant finish.

The requirements for treads, risers and finish are examined in the article on stone steps and entrance platforms, and the formats for paths and open areas in the article on natural stone setts.

Caring for basalt cladding

Basalt does not require protective impregnation to the extent that porous stones do, but dust, lime streaks and efflorescence are more noticeable on a dark surface. On a facade the most important thing is therefore to shed water: to provide flashings, drips and sealed abutments.

Dirt is washed off with water and neutral detergents. De-icing agents do not harm the stone but leave a whitish film, which is removed in spring during routine washing.

FAQ

Is basalt suitable for plinths?

Yes, it is one of the most durable stones for plinth cladding.

What surface finishes are available for basalt?

Polished, honed, flamed, bush-hammered, sawn and split.

Can basalt be combined with GFRC?

Yes, dark basalt on the plinth with light GFRC decoration above is a common combination.

How much does a square metre of basalt cladding weigh?

At a density of 2,700–3,000 kg/m³, a slab 30 mm thick weighs roughly 80–90 kg/m², and one 20 mm thick about 55–60 kg/m². The weight of the subframe or the adhesive bed is added to this; the resulting load is taken into account in the calculation of the wall and the anchors.

Does basalt fade in the sun?

The colour of natural basalt is stable: the stone contains no pigments and does not fade. Over time a polished surface outdoors may become more matt, and dust is more noticeable against a dark background, so a honed or flamed finish is more often chosen for facades.

Is basalt slippery on steps?

Polished and honed basalt is slippery when wet, so a flamed or bush-hammered surface, or anti-slip strips on the treads, are used for steps, landings and ramps.
