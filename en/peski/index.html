What a residential complex facade usually requires

A multi-storey residential facade has to combine a distinctive look with durability and fire safety: at this height the cladding is usually fixed to a ventilated rainscreen facade substructure, and decorative elements and panels are made from non-combustible materials. Repeating elements such as window surrounds, string courses and balcony balustrades are cost-effective to cast in series from a single mould, and installation is planned in sections so that it does not hold up finishing works and handover of the building.

The North Caucasus is a seismically active region, so low weight and reliable fixing are especially important for cladding and decorative elements here: lightweight thin-walled GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) products reduce the load on the substructure and the supporting structure.

What layers make up a GFRC facade on a residential building

The rainscreen facade of a multi-storey building is not only the visible cladding but a system of several layers, each designed and accepted separately. A mistake in any layer shows up on the face: as steps in the plane, opening joints or streaks.

LayerPurposeWhat to look at
Anchors and bracketsTransfer the load to the wall and floor slabsAnchor tests, type of substrate
Insulation and membraneThermal protection and protection against wind washingNon-combustibility, tight fit of the slabs
RailsForm the plane of the facadeAllowance for thermal movement
GFRC productsCladding and decorationEmbedded parts, joint width

The system is examined in detail in the article on the GFRC ventilated facade, and the requirements for the thermal layer are set out on the facade insulation page.

How are junctions with windows, balconies and the parapet resolved?

In service, most questions arise not from the wall planes but from the places where the cladding meets other structures. This is where water and wind find weak points, so the junction details are worked out before production starts.

  • Window reveals. Surrounds are designed with the window installation joint and the sill flashing in mind; the gap for ventilating the cavity is retained.
  • Balconies and loggias. Products must not rest on the balustrades; the load is transferred to the slab or the wall.
  • Parapet. The top is closed with a coping that has a drip edge and a fall towards the roof.
  • Plinth. The lower edge of the cladding is raised above the perimeter paving and protected from splashes.

Typical solutions are shown in the piece on facade junction details. Linear elements for such places — for example, GFRC mouldings — are selected by profile and fixing method.

Operation and care: what the facade needs after handover

GFRC does not require complicated maintenance: the material is non-combustible, frost-resistant and not subject to corrosion. Periodic inspection and timely repair of minor defects are sufficient.

  • visual inspection, as a rule once a year and after strong winds;
  • checking the sealant in the joints and the condition of the flashings;
  • cleaning with water, without abrasives or aggressive compounds;
  • renewing the protective coating as recommended by the manufacturer.

The procedure is described in the article on caring for GFRC products.

How facade works are spread across the seasons in a year-long cycle

When facade works take up almost the whole calendar year, they are arranged so that weather-sensitive operations fall in the warm period. Mechanical installation of the subframe and hanging of products can continue all year round, while work with sealants and paints depends on temperature and humidity.

PeriodWhat is usually done
WinterWall survey, installation of brackets and rails, manufacture of products
Spring and summerInsulation, hanging of cladding and decoration, joint sealing
AutumnCompletion of the upper tiers, junctions, flashings, snagging

This order is indicative and is refined according to the main contractor's programme and the weather of the particular season. The reference sample of texture and colour is kept until the end of construction and used when accepting each batch.

FAQ

Does the wall behind GFRC cladding need to be insulated?

Whether insulation is needed, and how thick, is determined by a thermal calculation for the specific region and wall construction. Rainscreen systems on residential buildings generally use non-combustible mineral wool.

How wide are the joints between products?

Joint width depends on the size of the products and the design movements; it usually ranges from a few millimetres to about 10–15 mm. The exact value is set in the working documentation.

Can a single damaged product be replaced without dismantling the facade?

In most rainscreen systems, yes: the product is removed from its fixings and a new one, cast in the same mould, is installed. This is why it is important to keep the design documentation and the marking of the elements.
